Chapter 2: Do You Like the Go-Home Club? 2

"Hey, Kuze. You and Rin, you know each other?"

First period hadn't started yet. Haruto Amamiya was standing beside Kuze's desk.

It was the first time he'd ever come over on his own. Kuze noted the date, the way you'd note an earthquake.

"Not really."

"Not really? She was sitting on your desk."

"She was."

"So you know each other."

"We talked yesterday. For about two minutes."

"Two minutes, and she sits on your desk?"

"Apparently."

"Huh."

Amamiya scratched the back of his head. He was smiling, the same easy smile he wore for everyone. But his eyes kept drifting across the room, to where Rin sat with her chin on her fist, glaring at the blackboard.

"She's kind of a handful, you know? Rin. She says stuff she doesn't mean. So if she said something rude, don't take it personally, okay? That's just how she is."

"...Okay."

"She's actually really nice. Like, *really*. People don't get that."

"I see."

"Did she say something to you? About me, or...?"

"No."

"Nothing at all?"

"She said I was late."

"Ha! Yeah, that's Rin. She says that to me every morning."

"Are you late every morning?"

"...Kind of, yeah."

"Cool, cool. Just wanted to say."

He knocked twice on Kuze's desk, a friendly little rap, and wandered back to his seat. Hina grabbed his sleeve at once and started telling him something with both hands.

Kuze looked at the spot where the knuckles had landed.

*That's just how she is.*

It was a nice thing to say. The kind of thing a good friend says, smoothing things over before there's anything to smooth. On its own, you'd think: *what a considerate guy.*

But Kuze kept snagging on one small word.

*She.*

Amamiya hadn't asked what she wanted. He hadn't asked if everything was all right. He'd come to explain Rin, the way an owner apologizes over the fence for a dog that barks at strangers.

Was it kindness or ownership? From the outside, was there a difference?

Kuze didn't know. But nobody explains a person they don't, on some level, consider theirs.

The sediment settled a little thicker.

Then the bell rang. The math teacher came in. Kuze spent fifty minutes not learning anything about quadratic functions.

1

"Mina! Mina, Mina, Minaaa!"

At the break after second period, a large, damp-haired boy filled the doorway of 2-3. He waved both arms as if landing a plane.

Kuze sighed and stood up.

Daichi Enomoto had been his friend since they were five. Their kindergarten had put them together on pool day. Daichi cried because the water was cold. Kuze didn't cry, because he refused to get in. They'd been inseparable ever since.

Now Daichi was a head taller, the swim club's best freestyler, and he smelled permanently of chlorine. He'd spent twelve years getting in the water for both of them.

"You don't have to say it four times."

"I have to say it four times. You never come out on the first one."

"That's not true."

"Mina. It's scientifically true. I have data."

Daichi slung an arm around Kuze's neck and dragged him into the hallway. Break-time traffic roared past them.

"Okay. Okay. So. I have heard a rumor."

"I'm sure you have."

"A rumor that a certain *Rin Tachibana* of the kendo club, famous for snapping a first-year's shinai in one hit, sat on a certain *someone's* desk this morning."

"It's been two hours."

"The swim club group chat is very efficient."

"You're in a different class."

"The swim club group chat has members in every class. We're everywhere. We're like water."

"That's the worst thing you've ever said."

"Three points?"

"Two."

"*Two?!* That was a water joke from the *swim club!*"

"That's why it's two."

Daichi clutched his chest like he'd been shot. A passing first-year gave him a wide berth.

Then he dropped the act, the way he always did. His voice came down half a step.

"So what's the deal? She's not gonna hurt you, right?"

"She wants to talk. At lunch. On the roof."

"The *roof?*"

"The roof."

"Mina. The roof is where people confess. Or fight. That's it. Those are the two roof things. It's in every manga."

"Neither is going to happen."

"How do you know?"

"Because she's in love with Amamiya."

It came out flat and simple, like the weather forecast. He only heard it afterward. The certainty. The small, sour note underneath.

Daichi heard it too. He always did.

"...Huh."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Daichi looked at him a second longer than necessary. Then he grinned, and the moment passed, or pretended to.

"Well, if it's a duel, I'll carry your body home. Swimming shoulders."

"I'm touched."

"If it's a confession, I need *all* the details."

"It's not a confession."

"Details, Mina. Every one."

"Go to class."

"Every one! What she says, what she eats, what the weather's like--"

"The weather will be weather."

"What's she even like up close? Is she scary?"

"She clicks her tongue."

"At you?"

"At everything."

"Mina, that's so romantic."

"How is that romantic?"

"You noticed!"

"Mina, bring me back *one* sentence. One. I'll live off it for a week."

"Goodbye, Daichi."

"Every one!"

The warning bell rang. Daichi ruffled Kuze's hair like he was still five, dodged the swat, and jogged off toward 2-5.

Kuze fixed his hair and went back inside.

*The roof is where people confess. Or fight.*

He had a feeling it would be neither. He had a stronger feeling it would somehow be worse.

2

Seiryou's roof was technically off-limits.

The door at the top of the east stairwell had a sign that said *STUDENTS MAY NOT ENTER*. It also had a lock that had been broken since before Kuze was born. Every student knew about both.

He pushed it open.

Creeeak.

Wind hit him in the face. Clean April wind, carrying the last cherry petals over the chain-link fence like confetti nobody had remembered to stop throwing. Beyond the fence, the town spread out gray and green.

Rin sat against the water tank with her back straight and her knees together. There were two lunch boxes in her lap.

Two.

"You're late."

"It's been four minutes since the bell."

"Three minutes too many."

"The stairs are long."

"Then walk faster."

"Did anyone follow you?"

"Why would anyone follow me?"

"Because I sat on your desk, idiot! People *talk!*"

"You said *roof* very loudly."

"I did *not* say it loudly!"

"Forty people heard."

"...Thirty. Maybe."

She patted the concrete beside her once, hard, like a judge with a gavel.

Kuze sat. He left a precise meter between them. Rin looked at the meter, then at him, and seemed to approve.

For a while, neither of them spoke. Somewhere below, a volleyball smacked against the gym wall.

"..."

"..."

"So. What's--"

"Eat this."

She shoved one of the lunch boxes into his chest.

It was the navy-blue cloth. The same careful bow, a little crumpled now, as if it had been held too tightly on the stairs.

Kuze looked at it. Then at her.

"This is Amamiya's."

"It's *not* Haruto's! It's a... it's a *test* one!"

"A test one."

"A prototype!"

Red started at her ears and spread inward, like ink in water.

"I've never made one before. Okay? And I'm not giving Haruto something that tastes like garbage. So somebody has to taste it first. And you already know. So it's efficient."

"So I'm a taste tester."

"You're a *sacrifice.*"

"That's much worse."

"Just eat it!"

He untied the cloth.

A two-tier lacquered box, the kind that gets handed down. Inside: rice with a pickled plum, three pieces of karaage, green beans, potato salad, and a rolled omelette in four slices.

The omelette was the color of a traffic cone at dusk. Its outside was very, very brown.

"...Itadakimasu."

He picked up a slice. Rin watched him like a cat that hadn't decided about the bird.

He put it in his mouth.

It was sweet. Aggressively sweet. Sweet like a threat. Under the sugar was a bitter crust where the pan had run too hot. Somewhere under *that* was the ghost of egg.

He chewed.

"..."

"Well?"

"..."

"*Well?!*"

He swallowed.

"How much sugar did you put in?"

"...The recipe said one spoon."

"How big was the spoon?"

"It was a *spoon!* A normal spoon!"

"A teaspoon or a tablespoon?"

"..."

Rin looked at the green beans.

"There's a difference?"

Kuze put down the chopsticks and breathed the clean April air. At the moment, it tasted faintly of burnt sugar.

"Tachibana. A tablespoon is three teaspoons."

"*Three?!*"

"And your heat was too high. The outside burned before the inside set. So you rolled it thicker to hide it, which cooked the outside even longer."

"H-How do you know all that?!"

"My mother works late. I make my own lunch."

"...You do?"

"Since middle school."

She stared at him as if he'd revealed he could fly.

"What do you make?"

"Rice. Something grilled. Whatever's left in the fridge."

"Every day?"

"Every day."

"...Is it good?"

"It's edible."

"That's not what I asked!"

"It's better than this omelette."

"*Everything's* better than this omelette! That's not a real bar!"

"The karaage is good, though."

"...Really?"

"The coating's a little thick. But the marinade's right. Ginger, garlic, soy sauce. You didn't rush it."

"I let it sit overnight."

"It shows."

"Rate it. Out of ten."

"The omelette?"

"The *whole thing!*"

"Six."

"*Six?!*"

"The karaage is carrying it."

"The karaage is a *team player!*"

Something happened to Rin's face. It wasn't a smile. It was a smile being held down with both hands, and pushing back.

"...Tch. Of course it's good. I'm not an idiot."

"Did you make it this morning?"

"At five. Before practice."

"Five?"

"Don't say it like that! It's normal! Athletes wake up early!"

"For omelettes?"

"For *discipline!*"

"You're not. You just can't tell spoons apart."

"*Shut up!*"

She hit his arm. It wasn't hard. For someone who reportedly snapped bamboo, it was almost gentle.

Kuze ate the rest of the karaage. The beans. The rice. The potato salad, which had too much mayonnaise but was otherwise sound. Then he ate the other three slices of the terrible omelette, one by one. He didn't know why. Leaving them felt like breaking something.

Rin watched the whole time, arms crossed, her own lunch untouched.

"You don't have to eat all of it."

"I'm hungry."

"You have your own lunch. I can see it in your bag."

"I'll eat it later."

"..."

"..."

She looked away, toward the fence and the petals blowing out over the town.

"...Thanks. For eating it."

It was so quiet the wind nearly took it.

"You're welcome."

"Don't say *you're welcome!* That makes it a *thing!*"

"What should I say?"

"Nothing! Just... eat!"

"I finished."

"Then *sit!*"

"I'm sitting."

"Then sit *quieter!*"

3

Kuze wrapped the empty box in its cloth and retied the bow. Better than it had been.

"...You tied it better than me."

"It's a bow."

"It's *my* bow! Don't fix my bow!"

"The left loop was bigger."

"It was *artistic!*"

"So. Why me? Really."

"I told you. Because you already knew."

"You could've practiced on your mother. Or a friend."

"..."

Her fingers tightened on her own lunch box.

"My mom's in Osaka for work. Until summer."

"And friends?"

"..."

She didn't answer. The not-answering was the answer.

He'd suspected it. The hallway crowds didn't only part for her. They closed behind her, fast, the way water closes behind a stone. Girls who talked to Hina for twenty minutes went quiet when Rin passed. He'd assumed she liked it that way.

He was starting to suspect he'd assumed a lot of things.

"The girls in my class think I'm scary. The girls in kendo think I'm stuck up. Because I'm better than the third-years, and I don't pretend I'm not. And the guys..."

She made a face.

"The guys look at me like I'm a *poster*."

"A poster."

"Like I'm something on a wall. Something to *look at*. It's gross."

"Amamiya doesn't?"

It was out before he could stop it. Rin's head snapped toward him.

"Haruto's different."

"How?"

He knew it was a mistake the moment he said it. It was the question he'd carried for nine days, and it had slipped out in the wrong place, too sharp.

But Rin didn't hit him.

She looked down at her untouched lunch. For a long moment, she said nothing.

"In middle school, I was in kendo too. There was this senpai. A third-year. The captain."

"..."

"He kept saying things. That girls shouldn't be stronger than boys. That I should lose on purpose so the boys wouldn't feel bad. Then he started doing it in practice. Hitting too hard when my guard was down. Calling it *training*. Nobody said anything. The advisor didn't see. The other girls were scared of him. I didn't want to cry in front of him. So I just... kept getting hit."

The wind pushed her bob across her cheek. She didn't brush it away.

"Then one day, Haruto walked into the dojo. He wasn't even in the club. He was returning somebody's textbook. He saw the senpai hit me after the call. And he walked right onto the floor in his socks, which is super against the rules, and said, *Hey, that's not fair. That wasn't a point.*"

She laughed, a short, surprised huff through her nose.

"He didn't even know what a point *was*. He just knew it wasn't fair."

"..."

"He got in so much trouble. The senpai shoved him. Haruto shoved back. They both got dragged to the staff room. But after that, the advisor started watching. And the senpai stopped."

She finally looked at him. Her gray eyes were very steady.

"So yeah. He's different."

"..."

Kuze had nothing to say to that.

He'd spent nine days building a theory. One observation at a time, like stacking stones. The drinks. The folder. *Some guy from class.* The owner apologizing over the fence. Every stone was real. He hadn't made any of them up.

But he'd been staring at the wall. He'd never once asked what was on the other side.

*He walked onto the floor in his socks.*

Kuze tried to picture himself doing it. A room full of strangers. A senpai twice his size. A girl he didn't know. A rule he didn't understand.

He couldn't. He'd have seen it, counted it, and entered it in the ledger. He'd have thought about it very carefully for a very long time.

And he wouldn't have walked onto the floor.

The sediment didn't go away. It never did. But something stirred it from underneath. For the first time, it didn't settle back in quite the same shape.

"...I see."

"That's it? *I see?*"

"What else should I say?"

"I don't know! Something! I just told you a whole *thing!*"

"It was a good thing. Thank you for telling me."

Rin opened her mouth. Closed it. Turned bright red again.

"D-Don't say it all *sincerely* like that! It's weird!"

"You told me to say something."

"Not *that!*"

She punched his arm. Again, not very hard.

"Tachibana."

"What."

"Does Amamiya know? That you're making this for him?"

"...No. And he's not going to. Not until it's perfect."

"What if it's never perfect?"

"Then he'll never know! That's the whole point of perfect!"

4

Rin finally opened her own lunch. The same food, only neater. She ate the karaage with the brisk efficiency of a soldier.

"Okay. So here's the deal."

"There's a deal?"

"You taste-test. Every day, until I get it right. Then I give Haruto a real one."

"Every day?"

"Until I get it *right.*"

"What if you never get it right?"

"Then every day forever."

"That seems like a bad deal for me."

"It's a *great* deal for you. You get free lunch."

"I get free experimental lunch."

"Free is free!"

"Do I get to choose the menu?"

"No."

"Can I request less sugar?"

"You can *request*."

"Will you listen?"

"...Maybe. If you say it nicely."

"Less sugar. Please."

"That wasn't nicely! That was like a teacher!"

Kuze considered it.

The rational answer was no. Every day on the roof meant every day of whispers in 2-3. More of Amamiya's eyes on his neck. More of Kujou's faint, unreadable smile. He'd spent a year perfecting the art of not being noticed.

"...What do I get? Really."

"I just *said*. Lunch."

"Lunch is payment for tasting. What do I get for keeping it a secret?"

Her chopsticks stopped halfway to her mouth.

"You said you wouldn't tell. Yesterday. You said it wasn't yours to tell."

"I did."

"So you *shouldn't* get anything. You already promised."

"I did."

"Then why are you asking?!"

He didn't know, exactly. It was the ledger. A habit so old he didn't notice it running anymore. Every exchange had two sides. He didn't want anything from her. He just wanted to know the terms.

"...Forget it. Lunch is fine."

Rin was looking at him strangely.

"You're weird, you know that?"

"I've heard."

"Like, you keep score. Of everything. I can see you doing it."

"I'm not."

"You *are.* Right there. Right now. Your eyes go all..."

She squinted, badly. He assumed it was an impression of him.

"...Like a cashier."

"I don't look like a cashier."

"You look *exactly* like a cashier."

"..."

He said nothing. The terrible thing was that she was right.

The second terrible thing was that she'd noticed. Nobody noticed. Daichi did, but Daichi had had twelve years. Rin had needed two days and one terrible omelette.

"...I don't like owing people. Or being owed. It's easier when things are even."

Rin chewed on that.

"Me neither. That's why I'm making you lunch. So I don't owe you for keeping quiet."

"..."

"What?"

"Nothing."

*She keeps a ledger too.*

He looked at her properly, maybe for the first time. The straight back. The crossed arms. The glare that went up like a shield the moment anyone got close. A girl alone against a water tank with two lunch boxes, because she had nobody else to practice on.

She wasn't scary. She was careful. She'd figured out early that the world keeps accounts. She'd decided never to end up on the wrong side of them.

He knew that feeling. He'd just never seen it from the outside.

"Deal."

"Huh?"

"Taste-testing. Every day until you get it right."

Rin blinked. Then she stuck out her hand, very formally, like they were signing a treaty.

"Deal."

"One more rule. You can't tell Hina."

"Why not?"

"She'll cry. Happy crying. It's *worse.*"

He shook it. Her grip was hard, her palm calloused from the shinai.

"And the tablespoon thing. You're going to *show* me. Tomorrow. With a real spoon."

"You want me to bring a spoon to school?"

"Bring *two.* So I can see the difference."

"..."

"What?! It's efficient!"

"Anything else?"

"Give me your phone."

"Why?"

"So I can tell you if lunch is cancelled! Obviously!"

"You could tell me in class."

"In *class?* In front of *everyone?*"

"You sat on my desk in front of everyone."

"That was *different!* That was an *emergency!*"

"What was the emergency?"

"...Give me your phone!"

He handed it over. She typed her number in with both thumbs, fast and furious, frowning at the screen.

"What's your first name?"

"Minato."

"...Too long. I'm saving you as *you*."

"That's not a name."

"It is now."

"What did you save yourself as? On mine?"

"...*Tachibana.*"

"That's boring."

"It's *informative!*"

Far below, faint through the concrete, the bell rang.

Ding-dong-dang-dong.

Rin let go of his hand as if it had burned her. She snatched up both lunch boxes and fled for the stairwell. The door shrieked open and banged shut.

Kuze sat by the water tank a moment longer.

His own lunch was still in his bag. His stomach was full of terrible omelette.

He didn't feel as bad about it as he'd expected.

5

He took the west stairs down, so he wouldn't arrive thirty seconds after Rin.

It was a rational plan. It failed on the third-floor landing.

Sayo Kujou stood by the window with a book open in one hand, as if she'd happened to pick that exact spot to read.

"Good afternoon, Kuze-kun."

"...Kujou-san."

"My. Still *Kujou-san*. You really are stubborn."

She closed the book without marking the page.

"You were on the roof, weren't you?"

"Was I?"

"You have a cherry petal in your hair."

Kuze reached up. There was a cherry petal in his hair. He removed it. Kujou watched with the mild pleasure of someone watching a cat fall off a sofa.

"Rin-san has been very *energetic* this week. Watching cooking videos at kendo practice. Asking the home economics teacher about measuring spoons. I wondered who she was practicing for. Now I wonder who she's practicing *on*."

"..."

Kuze said nothing. It was the only safe thing to say.

*How does she know about the spoons?*

Rin had only learned about spoons twenty minutes ago.

Unless she hadn't. Unless she'd asked last week, gotten the answer, and still brought the wrong omelette.

Or unless Kujou was guessing, and had just watched him react to the guess.

He kept his face very still.

Kujou smiled.

"You're interesting, Kuze-kun. You notice things. I noticed you noticing, at the vending machine yesterday."

"..."

"Haruto didn't buy you a drink. You counted the cans."

The silt in his chest went cold.

"Don't worry. I don't think anyone else saw. Everyone else was looking at Haruto."

She started down the stairs, unhurried, black hair swaying like a curtain. On the landing below, she looked back up at him.

"Then again, I suppose you'll say you weren't counting."

"I wasn't."

"Of course not. You just happened to buy one can, after four, and look at the fifth button for three seconds."

"..."

"Two point eight, perhaps. I rounded up."

"You measured that?"

"I measure everything, Kuze-kun. It's a family habit."

"Do you always watch people this closely, Kujou-san?"

"Only the ones who watch back."

"That sounds exhausting."

"Oh, it is. But the view is lovely. One more thing, Kuze-kun. Was it good? The omelette?"

"What omelette?"

"My. Well done."

"That's the thing about Haruto. Everyone always is."

"..."

"Everyone except you."

Then she was gone. Her footsteps faded down the stairwell, soft and even as a metronome.

Kuze stood alone on the landing.

*She saw me counting.*

He'd been so sure he was the only one watching. He'd sat in the audience for a year. It had never occurred to him to check who was sitting behind him.

"...What *is* she?"

The stairwell didn't answer.

6

He'd barely sat down when Gon leaned across the aisle.

"Kuze-dono. Thou returnest from the roof. Alive, verily."

"Apparently."

"The whole class awaited news. There was a betting pool."

"On what?"

"Confession, duel, or disappearance. Disappearance was favored three to one."

"Who bet on confession?"

"...I shall not betray my sources."

"Also, verily. Tachibana-san returned before thee. Red of face."

"Was she?"

"As a tomato in August."

"You bet on confession."

"A man must support the underdog."

"Umyu~ Kuze-kun~"

Momo had drifted over from the front row, phone in hand, smile bright.

"Where did you go at lunch~? Everyone was so curious~"

"The library."

"Ehh~? But the library's on the first floor~ And your hair smells like wind~"

"It was a windy library."

"Hehe~ Kuze-kun's funny~ Okay~ The library~"

She drifted away again. Kuze watched her go.

"Gon. Does Shirasagi usually talk to you?"

"Never, verily. Not once in a year."

"..."

"Why dost thou ask?"

"No reason."

"Kuze-dono. When a girl who never speaks to thee suddenly speaks to thee..."

"Yes?"

"In the visual novels, it is either a love flag or a death flag."

"Which is it?"

"I have not yet finished the route, verily."

"That's not helpful."

"Neither is the route."

The afternoon passed the way afternoons do when something happens at lunch. Slowly. Loudly. With eyes.

Rin didn't look at him once. She was so careful not to look that it was worse than looking. When their eyes met by accident during roll call, she went so red that the girl in front of her asked if she had a fever.

Amamiya looked at him three times during classical Japanese. Kuze counted.

He hated that he counted.

Kujou didn't look at him at all. That was the worst of the three. He could feel her attention anyway, like frost.

*Everyone except you.*

What had she meant? That he didn't watch Amamiya? He watched Amamiya constantly. That was the whole problem. Or that he watched him *wrong*, counting instead of admiring?

Or had she been telling him, very politely, that she watched too?

After school, Kuze exercised his rights as a go-home club member and tried to go home.

He got as far as the shoe lockers.

"Kuze! Hey, Kuze, hold up a sec."

Amamiya jogged up the corridor alone. No girls at his elbows. No chatter trailing after him. He looked smaller, the way a stage does when the house lights come up.

"You heading home?"

"Go-home club."

"Ha! Right, right."

Amamiya leaned against the lockers beside him, arms folded, easy.

"So, uh. Some of us are doing karaoke on Saturday. Club thing, kinda. Not really. Just hanging out. You should come."

Kuze paused with one shoe half off.

"...Me?"

"Yeah! You. We're classmates, right? It's weird we've never hung out. Everybody should get along, you know?"

"..."

*Everybody should get along.*

He said it like a law of physics. It was a nice sentence. The kind that wins class-rep elections.

And Kuze couldn't stop turning it over.

*Why now?*

For nine days, he'd been invisible four rows back. *Some guy from class.* The fifth can nobody bought. And today, the same day Rin sat on his desk, Amamiya had come over twice. Once to explain her. Now to invite him somewhere she would be.

Or: *I want to see what you two are like in front of me.*

Or: *It's easier to watch something when it's sitting next to you.*

Or, and this one made the sediment shift, maybe Amamiya was just a nice guy who'd noticed a classmate existed. Maybe the whole theory was a bitter boy in the back row building a villain out of spare parts.

He thought of socks on a wooden floor. *That wasn't a point.*

He genuinely didn't know. That was becoming a pattern.

"I'll pass."

"Just come for one song!"

"Which song?"

"Any song! Your choice!"

"Then I choose none."

"That's not a song!"

"Aw, come on. Why?"

"I don't sing."

"Saturday's your go-home day too, right? So you're free."

"That's not how it works."

"Sure it is! Go home, then come out! Two-step process!"

"Nobody *sings* at karaoke anyway. Everyone just yells. Sayo does enka. It's hilarious. Hina does the idol songs with the dances. Rin--"

He stopped. Something crossed his face and was gone.

"Rin usually doesn't come. But she might this time."

"..."

*She might this time.*

He'd said it lightly. He was watching Kuze's face as he said it.

"I'll pass."

"What do you even do on Saturdays?"

"Laundry."

"*Laundry?*"

"It's a two-step process."

"Okay, okay. Got it. Offer stands, though."

Amamiya pushed off from the lockers. Then he hesitated, one hand on the locker door.

"What?"

He didn't finish. He seemed to be waiting for Kuze to finish it for him.

Kuze didn't.

The silence stretched between the lockers. Outside, the baseball club was running laps.

"...So be nice to her. That's all."

"I'm not anything to her. I eat her omelettes."

It slipped out. He'd meant it to sound dismissive. It came out sounding like a confession.

Amamiya blinked.

"Her *what?*"

"..."

*Idiot.*

"Nothing. I'm leaving."

"Wait, what omelettes? Kuze! What omelettes?!"

"Rin can't even make omelettes! She burned toast at camp!"

"Kuze!"

Kuze shoved his indoor shoes into the locker and jammed his feet into his outdoor ones without undoing the laces. He walked out before Amamiya could say anything else.

He didn't look back. He could feel Amamiya standing in the entrance, watching him go.

*Be nice to her.*

As if it were Amamiya's to ask. As if he were the one who'd sat on the roof with the terrible omelette and the story about the dojo.

Kuze stopped himself there. That wasn't fair either. Amamiya *had* been in the dojo. That was the whole point of the story.

The sediment settled into a shape he didn't recognize. Heavier on one side.

8

The east stairwell had a fire door one floor below the roof. It had a small square window of reinforced glass at head height.

From the landing, you could see through it to the top step. And past that, through the broken door that never quite closed, to a slice of roof. A water tank. Two figures sitting a precise meter apart against it.

Momo Shirasagi had been standing on that landing at 12:41.

Now, at 9:12 that night, she lay on her stomach on a pink bedspread, heels kicking slowly in the air, scrolling through photos.

Rin Tachibana, holding out a lunch box with both hands, red to the ears.

A boy from the back row with something yellow and brown halfway to his mouth.

The two of them, shaking hands.

"Hmm~"

She zoomed in on the handshake. On Rin's face. On the boy's. She tilted her head and hummed through her nose.

"Kuze-kun, was it? From the back row~"

She hummed a little more.

"Haruto-kun would be *so* surprised~"

She opened Chirp. Not her main account, with its four hundred followers and its strawberry parfait profile picture and its cheerful posts about cute cafés.

The other one.

No picture. No name. Only a string of random letters. Eighty-three followers, and every one of them went to Seiryou.

She picked a photo. She cropped it carefully, so you couldn't quite see the boy's face. You could see Rin's very well.

Her thumb hovered over the button.

"Umyu... not yet, I think~"

She saved the draft. She rolled onto her back and looked up at the glow-in-the-dark stars on her ceiling.

"Rin-chan's got a secret~"

The empty room didn't answer.

"And secrets are only fun..."

She smiled. It wasn't the smile she used for Haruto.

"...when you pick the right time to tell them."
