# Annotated Passages

> **Purpose:** Show the voice working at full strength in eight original passages, each annotated line by line so you can see exactly which technique produces which effect.
> **Load when:** Calibrating the voice before drafting, when a draft feels "close but off", or when you need a concrete model for a specific scene type.
> **Related:** [Style Bible](../core/style-bible.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md), [Before & After](before-after.md), [Sample Chapter 01](sample-chapter-01.md), [Anti-Robotic Prose](../revision/anti-robotic.md)

## How to read this file

Each passage is original prose using the library's default cast (Minato Kuze and company at Seiryou Private High School). Lines are numbered in brackets, like `[3]`, so the annotation table can point to them. The numbers are not part of the prose. After each table there is a **"What to steal"** list: the transferable moves.

The eight passages cover the voice's main registers:

1. The opening and the portrait
2. The asymmetry spiral
3. Group chatter and the step back
4. Banter snap-back
5. The night-time post spiral
6. The monologue-and-silence duet
7. The cut-away
8. The quiet earnest peak

## Passage 1: Opening and portrait

> [1] *Ding-dong-dang-dong.*
> [2] The last bell of the day, cheerful as ever, rang out over Seiryou Private High School as if something worth celebrating had happened.
> [3] Nothing had.
> [4] Kuze had class duty.
> [5] He was halfway through the blackboard when the girl assigned to duty with him (attendance number fourteen, directly after his thirteen) finally showed up, sliding the door open with her hip because both her hands were full of somebody else's notebooks.
> [6] "Sorry, sorry! I'll help now, so let's finish super fast, 'kay?"
> [7] "Sure."
> [8] Hina Matsuri.
> [9] A honey-brown ponytail that swung like it had its own opinions, big round eyes that went even bigger whenever something interested her, and a height that made her look younger than she was right up until she beat the entire track team at the sports festival.
> [10] The most popular girl in their year, by any measurement Kuze could think of.
> [11] Also, as far as he could tell, the only person in Class 2-3 who said good morning to everyone. Including him. Including the class goldfish.
> [12] "Haruto made me carry these," she said, dumping the notebooks on the teacher's desk. "Isn't that mean? Isn't he the worst?"
> [13] She was smiling when she said it.
> [14] Of course she was.

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SFX opening line** | Instantly signals the genre, like a manga panel; the reader is in a Japanese school before a single sentence |
| 2 | **Long exhale** with ironic personification ("cheerful as ever") | Establishes the narrator's dry voice by giving the bell more enthusiasm than the POV |
| 3 | **Two-word verdict** | Undercuts line 2. The short-after-long rhythm is the voice in miniature |
| 4 | **School-system texture** (class duty) | A concrete situation, stated plainly |
| 5 | **Long exhale with specific trivia** (attendance numbers) | Human writers note stupid specifics; the numbers explain *why* they're paired without exposition |
| 6 | **Genki dialogue** ("Sorry, sorry!", "'kay?") | Voice identifies her before the name does |
| 7 | **Protagonist's one-word reply** | The gap between her energy and his is the comedy |
| 8 | **Name as its own paragraph** | The camera stops for the portrait |
| 9 | **Portrait formula**: hair, eyes, build, plus a contradiction (small but athletic) | Anime-visual and economical; the sports-festival detail is characterization, not decoration |
| 10 | **Reputation** | A standard portrait beat |
| 11 | **The crack**, delivered as a joke ("Including the class goldfish") | Humanizes her, and reveals Kuze counts himself somewhere near the goldfish |
| 12 | **Dialogue with an embedded relationship** | Introduces Amamiya without a portrait; he's the center of her orbit before he's on screen |
| 13-14 | **Observation, then a bitter two-word verdict** | The first seed of Kuze's core question (why him?), planted without stating it |

**What to steal:**
- Open on sound, then undercut the sound.
- Explain pairings through system trivia (attendance numbers, seat numbers, duty rotas) instead of narration.
- End every portrait with a crack, ideally a joke at the POV's own expense.
- Let the harem lead appear first in *other people's* dialogue.

## Passage 2: The asymmetry spiral

> [1] "We're heading to the club room," Amamiya said, slinging his bag over one shoulder. "Want me to grab you guys a couple of drinks on the way back?"
> [2] You guys. A couple.
> [3] Meaning Kuze was included.
> [4] "Ah, wait, I'm coming too!" Hina dropped her eraser. "Kuze-kun, can I leave the rest to you? I'm so sorry! I'll pay you back, promise!"
> [5] "It's fine. Go."
> [6] She went.
> [7] Amamiya went with her.
> [8] Nobody brought up the drinks again.
> [9] Kuze finished the blackboard, emptied the trash, and filled in the class log in handwriting neater than it needed to be, and the whole time, the drinks sat at the bottom of his mind like a pebble in a shoe.
> [10] Two drinks, when Hina was staying.
> [11] Zero drinks, the moment she wasn't.
> [12] Obviously they weren't close. Obviously Amamiya had club. Obviously it was stupid to care about a vending-machine can that had never existed in the first place.
> [13] But.
> [14] Was the offer for Hina all along, and Kuze just happened to be standing in its blast radius?
> [15] Or was it something smaller and meaner than that: the kind of kindness that only switched on when someone was watching?
> [16] Or, and this was the worst one, had Amamiya simply never noticed that Kuze was there at all?
> [17] The sediment at the bottom of his chest shifted, slow and silty.
> [18] ...Or was he just jealous?
> [19] He closed the class log.
> [20] Probably both.

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Harem-lead voice**: casual generosity | Seems kind; the reader should like him a little here |
| 2 | **Quoting back** exact words as fragments | The analytical engine starts: Kuze is *parsing* |
| 3 | Plain inference | Sets up the asymmetry |
| 4-5 | Genki apology, protagonist's minimal reply | Keeps the dialogue half of the page alive; "Go." is terse but kind |
| 6-8 | **Three short one-line paragraphs** | Time passes in three beats; line 8 is the asymmetry, landed flat |
| 9 | **Long exhale** that folds three chores into one sentence, then a simile | The chores are routine, so they get merged; the simile is homely (pebble in a shoe), not purple |
| 10-11 | **Parallel fragments with numbers** | Presents the evidence like a ledger |
| 12 | **Anaphora ("Obviously")** as self-argument | He tries to talk himself out of it, which makes him sympathetic |
| 13 | **One-word paragraph** | The pivot |
| 14-16 | **Hypothesis chain**, three questions escalating in pettiness and pain | The core of the voice. Note the third hypothesis hurts *him*, not Amamiya |
| 17 | **Leitmotif** (the sediment) | The private image; it recurs across the story and grows |
| 18 | **Self-suspicion** | The move that keeps the cynic likable |
| 19-20 | **Action, then a wry verdict** | The tilt: he refuses to choose between "Amamiya is fake" and "I'm jealous" |

**What to steal:**
- Find one tiny asymmetry per chapter and let the POV chew on it.
- Present the evidence in parallel fragments, like a ledger.
- Order hypotheses so the last one wounds the narrator.
- Always follow suspicion of others with suspicion of self.

## Passage 3: Group chatter and the step back

> [1] The club room door was already open.
> [2] "--so I said, 'Haruto, that's not a cat, that's a *bag*--'"
> [3] "It moved! I swear it moved!"
> [4] "My. You screamed like a little girl, Haruto-kun."
> [5] "I did not scream!"
> [6] "You kinda screamed~"
> [7] "Fueee, I have a video of it~"
> [8] "Delete that! Momo, delete that right now!"
> [9] Four girls. One boy. Zero people doing anything a Literature and Broadcast Club was supposedly for.
> [10] Behind them, two first-years in headphones hunched over the mixing board, adjusting levels on a recording nobody else seemed to care about. One of them glanced up at the noise, then back down, with the practiced face of someone who had stopped expecting anything.
> [11] Kuze knew that face.
> [12] He held up the CD case Ogata-sensei had given him.
> [13] "Delivery for Matsuri."
> [14] "Oh! Kuze-kun! Come in, come in!"
> [15] "I'm fine here."
> [16] "Thanks, Kuze." Amamiya reached across and plucked the case from his hand before Hina could stand up. "Hina, Ogata-nee says this is for you."
> [17] Kuze let go of it.
> [18] He took one step back into the hallway, and the room closed over the space where he'd been, like water.

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **In medias res dialogue with `--` on both ends** | We walk into an ongoing conversation, as Kuze does |
| 2-8 | **Untagged group burst** of seven lines | Each speaker is identifiable: Rin's bluntness is implied, Sayo's "My.", Hina's `~`, Momo's "Fueee" |
| 9 | **Ledger-style summary** (four, one, zero) | Kuze's analytical habit turned into a joke |
| 10 | **Long exhale** on a background character | Humanizes the room's margins; the reader shares Kuze's sympathy for the ignored |
| 11 | **Four-word identification** | Reveals Kuze's loneliness without saying "lonely" |
| 13 | **Protagonist's terse line** | Formal-ish (surname, no honorific), which is how he keeps distance |
| 14-15 | **Invitation and refusal** | A recurring pattern: Hina includes, Kuze declines |
| 16 | **The intercept** | Amamiya takes the CD *before Hina can stand*. The reader notices; Kuze will analyze it later. It's shown, not explained |
| 16 | **"Ogata-nee"** | Honorific texture: Amamiya calls the teacher "big sister", revealing a closeness Kuze didn't know about |
| 17 | **Minimal action** | A two-word surrender |
| 18 | **Physical step back and a simile** | The emotional exclusion is made spatial; the simile is plain and exact |

**What to steal:**
- Enter group scenes mid-conversation.
- Use 5-8 untagged lines when voices are distinct enough.
- Always narrate the POV's *physical* position relative to the group.
- Show suspicious behavior (the intercept) without commenting. Let the POV comment later, or never.

## Passage 4: Banter snap-back

> [1] "Hey. You."
> [2] Kuze looked up from his textbook.
> [3] Rin Tachibana was standing at his desk with both palms flat on it, which, in his limited experience of Rin Tachibana, was never a good sign.
> [4] Short silver-ash bob. Gray eyes, sharp enough to cut paper. A kendo club bag slung across her back like a sheathed weapon, which, technically, it was.
> [5] "You told me to behave myself yesterday."
> [6] "No, I didn't."
> [7] "You did! You said I should 'think about what my name means'!"
> [8] "I said you *might want to* think about what your name means. That's a suggestion. Not an order."
> [9] "That's the same thing!"
> [10] "It's not."
> [11] "It's the same thing, you jerk!"
> [12] "It's really not."
> [13] Her grip tightened on the edge of the desk. Somewhere behind her, Amamiya half rose from his seat, the way a lifeguard does when he sees a swimmer waving in a way that might be fun or might be drowning.
> [14] "Rin, whoa, whoa. What's going on?"
> [15] "Stay out of it, Haruto!"
> [16] She didn't look at him. Her ears had gone pink anyway.
> [17] Interesting, Kuze thought.
> [18] Then, because he was not a good person: "Shouldn't you go back to Amamiya? He looks worried."
> [19] "W-What!? Wh-why would I care what Haruto--that idiot--I don't--!"
> [20] *Ding-dong-dang-dong.*
> [21] Saved by the bell. Her, not him.
> [22] "This isn't over!" She stomped back to her seat. "After school! Don't you dare go home!"
> [23] "I'm in the go-home club," Kuze said to nobody. "It's literally my only activity."

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rough address** ("You.") | Tsundere address form; she refuses his name, which is a relationship stage marker |
| 3 | **Signature gesture** (palms on desk) and **dry commentary** | Gesture as speaker tag; the aside is the POV's voice |
| 4 | **Portrait in fragments** plus a joke that turns literal | A fragmentary portrait fits her sharpness; the literal twist ("which, technically, it was") is comic |
| 5-12 | **Snap-back rhythm**: accusation, flat correction, outrage, flatter correction | The central banter pattern. His lines get shorter as hers get louder |
| 13 | **Long exhale with an apt, homely simile** | A breather in the rapid exchange; characterizes Amamiya as the self-appointed rescuer |
| 14 | **Harem-lead tic** ("whoa, whoa") | Consistent voice |
| 16 | **Contradiction between words and body** | Tsundere tell, *shown* (pink ears) and not explained |
| 17 | **Two-word inner reaction** | Kuze notices and files it, which is character through restraint |
| 18 | **Self-aware cruelty** ("because he was not a good person") | The cynic is honest about his pettiness, and that makes it charming instead of mean |
| 19 | **Stutter and cut-off cascade** | Fluster rendered in punctuation |
| 20 | **SFX as scene punctuation** | A comic rescue by bell |
| 21 | **Deadpan correction** | Wit in four words |
| 23 | **Punchline aside to nobody** | Ends on a character-revealing joke, which counts as a tilt |

**What to steal:**
- In a snap-back, shorten the calm character's lines as the angry one escalates.
- Contradict dialogue with body language, and don't explain it.
- Let the POV admit his own pettiness in narration.
- End comic scenes on a punchline nobody in-world hears.

## Passage 5: The night-time post spiral

> [1] 9:42 p.m.
> [2] Kuze was lying on his bed, not doing homework, scrolling through Chirp with the specific joylessness of someone who knew he should stop.
> [3] He didn't mean to end up on Amamiya's account.
> [4] He ended up on it anyway.
>
> ```
> Haruto
> Went out for food with Rin and Kuze today! Looks like those two weren't getting along, but I got them to make up (^^)
> (Tap to view photo)
> ```
>
> [5] "..."
> [6] He tapped the photo before he could stop himself.
> [7] Carbonara. Napolitan. Two parfaits, one strawberry, one chocolate, arranged on the same side of the table with their spoons crossed.
> [8] And in the bottom corner, cut off at the edge like something that had wandered into frame by accident: a hamburger steak, a bowl of rice, and a cup of black coffee.
> [9] His hamburger steak.
> [10] Nobody had asked if they could take the photo.
> [11] *I got them to make up.*
> [12] Not "they made up." Not "we talked it out."
> [13] *I* got them to.
> [14] Kuze read the sentence four times. It didn't get better. It got worse, in the way a word gets worse when you repeat it until it stops meaning anything and starts meaning everything.
> [15] Two replies had already appeared underneath.
>
> ```
>     Momo
>     Haruto you're such a good guy~ (*´ω`*)
>
>     Hinacchi
>     You did THAT yesterday!?
> ```
>
> [16] He dropped the phone face-down onto the blanket.
> [17] The ceiling was the same as always.
> [18] The sediment wasn't.

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Time stamp opening** | A clean scene start; the late hour implies obsession |
| 2 | **Long exhale** with self-aware diagnosis | "specific joylessness" is precise and human |
| 3-4 | **Denial, then admission** in two one-liners | Comic self-contradiction |
| Post | **In-world post formatting** with a kaomoji | Characterizes Amamiya's public face; the emoticon is a nice touch of cheerfulness |
| 5 | **Silence line** | The reaction is a blank, and that's stronger than any description |
| 7 | **Evidence catalog**, including crossed spoons | Specific, petty, damning detail |
| 8 | **Long exhale** that ends on the key image | The POV's exclusion, literalized in the photo's crop |
| 9 | **Four-word possessive** | Emotion carried by a noun phrase |
| 10 | **Plain factual grievance** | Understated anger |
| 11-13 | **Quoting back and dissecting** a caption | The analytical engine, with italics for emphasis on the pronoun |
| 14 | **Reflection on semantic satiation** | A universal experience made specific, which lifts the scene above pure resentment |
| Replies | **Two public faces** | Momo's sugariness and Hina's genuine surprise (she wasn't there), both characterizing |
| 16-18 | **Action, stasis, change** in three one-liners, ending on the **leitmotif** | The tilt: something has shifted inside him, and it isn't named |

**What to steal:**
- Social posts are for contrasting public and private faces.
- Put the POV's exclusion into a concrete image (the crop).
- Dissect captions word by word.
- End on the leitmotif changing state.

## Passage 6: The monologue-and-silence duet

> [1] Behind the equipment shed, the ground was still damp from the morning's rain.
> [2] Daichi sat down next to him without asking, which was the kind of thing only Daichi could do.
> [3] "So. Mina."
> [4] "..."
> [5] "You wanted to help her. Right?"
> [6] "..."
> [7] "You don't have to know why. I don't know why either. Maybe you like her. Maybe you just hate it when thirty people gang up on one. Maybe it's something else. Doesn't matter."
> [8] "..."
> [9] "You keep saying 'rational' like it's a password. Like if you say it enough, nobody can get in." Daichi flicked a pebble at the fence. *Clink.* "Including you."
> [10] "..."
> [11] "You're allowed to be selfish, you know. You're allowed to want stuff. You're not a calculator, Mina. You're a guy who drinks black coffee to look cool and cries at dog movies."
> [12] "...I don't cry at dog movies."
> [13] "Hachikō. Third grade. I was there."
> [14] "..."
> [15] Something was stuck in Kuze's throat. It had been stuck there for days, for weeks, maybe for a year, and now it was trying to come out all at once, and it was too big.
> [16] He coughed. Coughed again.
> [17] "Damn it," he said, to no one.
> [18] Daichi didn't look at him. He just stayed, the way a wall stays, the way the shed stayed, the way he had always stayed.
> [19] "Damn it... damn it..."

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-line setting**, damp and specific | Scenery economy; the rain is literal, not symbolic |
| 2 | **Characterization through permission** | Defines their friendship in one clause |
| 3 | **Nickname** ("Mina") | Address form signals intimacy; only Daichi uses it |
| 4-14 | **Monologue-and-silence duet**: Daichi speaks, Kuze answers `"..."` | Kuze's silence *is* his breakdown. Each "..." carries more weight |
| 7 | **Hypothesis chain in dialogue** | The confidant mirrors Kuze's own analytical habit back at him, with compassion instead of suspicion |
| 9 | **Metaphor from the character's world** ("password"), plus SFX | The confidant speaks plainly; *Clink.* punctuates |
| 11 | **Specific affectionate teasing** (black coffee, dog movies) | Warmth through ribbing, not therapy-speak (see [Anti-Robotic §4](../revision/anti-robotic.md#4-therapy-speak-and-modern-internet-register)) |
| 12-13 | **The one denial and the evidence** | A laugh inside the cry; the specific memory (third grade) is the human detail |
| 15 | **Long exhale** of escalating time ("for days, for weeks, maybe for a year") | The prose opens up exactly where the emotion does |
| 16-17 | **Simple physical action**, then a curse to nobody | At the emotional peak the prose gets *simpler* |
| 18 | **Anaphora** ("the way... the way... the way") | A rhythmic embrace; the confidant's presence rendered as repetition |
| 19 | **Repetition with trailing ellipses** | Breakdown in dialogue form; no narration explaining the tears |

**What to steal:**
- At the low point, let the protagonist answer only in silence lines.
- The confidant uses specific shared history, never counseling vocabulary.
- Put one laugh inside the crying scene.
- At the peak, simplify: cough, curse, repetition.

## Passage 7: The cut-away

> [1] That night, in a room with a view of the entire city, Sayo Kujou uncapped a red pen.
> [2] On the desk in front of her lay a single sheet of drawing paper. Eight names, written in her small, precise hand.
> [3] Haruto Amamiya, in the center.
> [4] Around him: Rin. Hina. Momo. Her own name, which she had written slightly larger than the others, and then, after some consideration, slightly smaller.
> [5] She drew an arrow from Rin to Haruto. From Hina to Haruto. From Momo to Haruto, and then, after a pause, a second, thinner arrow from Momo to somewhere off the edge of the page.
> [6] "My, my."
> [7] Near the bottom corner, a little apart from everyone, was a name she'd almost forgotten to include.
> [8] Minato Kuze.
> [9] She tapped the pen against her lips.
> [10] Then she drew one arrow, from Rin to Kuze, very lightly, the way you'd sketch a line you weren't sure you wanted to keep.
> [11] And she circled his name.
> [12] Twice.
> [13] "Things are finally getting interesting, aren't they?"

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Omniscient cut-away** at a section break, with a status detail (city view) | Signals wealth and a shift of POV; the red pen is ominous |
| 2 | **Precise, controlled imagery** | Her handwriting is her character |
| 4 | **Comic micro-vanity** (larger, then smaller) | Humanizes the mastermind; ambiguity about her own place |
| 5 | **The relationship map drawn in real time** | Shows the web without exposition; the thinner arrow is a mystery seed |
| 6 | **Signature interjection** ("My, my.") | Voice consistency |
| 7-8 | **The protagonist from outside**, as an afterthought on the page | Dramatic irony: the reader sees how peripheral he looks, and yet |
| 10 | **Hesitant stroke** | Plants a romantic possibility without committing to it |
| 11-12 | **Escalating one-liners** (circled, then "Twice.") | A classic LN teaser rhythm |
| 13 | **Signature teaser line** | Chapter-ending hook through another character's anticipation |

**What to steal:**
- Cut-aways are short (under 250 words), visual, and end with a teaser line.
- Show the mastermind *doing* something with paper, a phone, or a chessboard.
- The POV character should appear in the cut-away as seen from outside, looking smaller than he feels.
- Hide one unresolved detail (the thin arrow) in every cut-away.

## Passage 8: The quiet earnest peak

> [1] Rin was waiting at the station.
> [2] Not at the gate, where people wait for someone. Off to the side, by the vending machine, where people stand when they want it to look like they aren't waiting for anyone.
> [3] "...You're late."
> [4] "We didn't have plans."
> [5] "I know that."
> [6] She held something out without looking at him. A can of coffee. Black. The expensive kind, the one Kuze never bought because it cost forty yen more than the kind that tasted exactly the same.
> [7] He took it.
> [8] It was still warm. She must have bought it more than once. She must have been standing here long enough for the first one to go cold.
> [9] He didn't say that.
> [10] "Thanks."
> [11] "It's not a thank-you or anything. For yesterday. It's not."
> [12] "I know."
> [13] "Good."
> [14] They stood by the vending machine while two trains came and went.
> [15] Neither of them got on.

| Line | Technique | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plain declarative opening** | Earnest scenes start quiet |
| 2 | **Analytical monologue turned tender**: the location parsed as evidence | The same engine that fuels his cynicism now reads her *care* |
| 3 | **Tsundere opening line**, with an ellipsis | A small accusation to cover the gesture |
| 4-5 | **Snap-back, softened** | The banter rhythm from Passage 4, slowed down; the callback is structural |
| 6 | **The gift**, specified to the yen | A callback to his black-coffee personality; she's been paying attention |
| 8 | **Deduction ending on an inference of care** | The analytical engine produces warmth, and the reader feels the shift |
| 9 | **Restraint** ("He didn't say that.") | The most human line in the passage: what he chooses not to say |
| 11 | **Tsundere denial** that explicitly names what it denies | Classic, earned, and not explained |
| 12-13 | **Two-word exchange** | Emotional climax in minimum words |
| 14-15 | **Time passing through trains**, and a one-line ending | The tilt is an *absence* of action; the unsaid is the point |

**What to steal:**
- At earnest peaks, turn the cynical engine toward reading kindness.
- Pay off established tics (black coffee) as proof someone was paying attention.
- Put the most important line in what the POV *doesn't* say.
- End on stillness.

## Passage 9 (bonus): Genre transfer, the same engine in a fantasy guild

The voice isn't limited to school settings. Here the same mechanics run in a fantasy adventurers' guild (see [Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md)).

> [1] *Clang.*
> [2] The guild's front door bell had been broken for three years, and nobody had fixed it, because every adventurer who walked in assumed someone else would.
> [3] "Welcome back, Leon-sama~! Another dragon? You're amazing!"
> [4] The receptionist's voice went up a full octave.
> [5] Kai, who had just returned from a two-week escort mission with a torn cloak and exactly one surviving boot, stood behind Leon and waited.
> [6] "Ah, and Kai-san. Please file your report at the side window."
> [7] The side window.
> [8] The side window was closed.
> [9] Was it because Leon had killed a dragon and Kai had escorted a cabbage merchant? Or was it because Leon was tall, blond, and smiled at everyone as if he'd been paid to? Or was it, and Kai was starting to suspect this was the real answer, that the receptionist had never once learned his face?
> [10] "..."
> [11] He sat down on the bench to wait for the side window to open.
> [12] It was a very familiar bench.

| Line | Technique | Transfer note |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | SFX, then a long exhale of dry world detail | Worldbuilding through a petty institutional failure, not exposition |
| 3 | The receptionist's `~` and "-sama" | Honorific texture works in fantasy too |
| 5 | A bathetic specific (one surviving boot) | A humanizing move ([Anti-Robotic §7](../revision/anti-robotic.md#7-the-humanizing-moves)) |
| 6-8 | Asymmetry noticed and quoted back | The same engine as Passage 2 |
| 9 | Three-hypothesis chain, with the last one self-wounding | Identical structure; only the nouns changed |
| 10-12 | Silence line, action, wry verdict | The tilt |

**Lesson:** Genre changes the nouns. The engine stays the same.

**The one-sentence lesson:** in this voice, *every* technique is in service of one overthinking person noticing small things. When a passage stops noticing, it stops being a light novel.

## Calibration exercise

Before writing a new chapter, pick the two passages closest to your chapter's register (for example, 2 and 4 for a comedy chapter, or 5 and 6 for a drama chapter). Reread them, then write your opening section and compare line by line:
- Does your section have a long exhale followed by a short verdict?
- Does the POV notice at least one asymmetry?
- Can each speaker be identified without tags?
- Does the last line tilt?

If you answer "no" twice or more, revise the opening before continuing. A strong first section sets the rhythm for the whole chapter.
