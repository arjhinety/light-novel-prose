# Before & After

> **Purpose:** Nineteen flat, robotic, or machine-translated drafts rewritten into the light-novel voice, each with a diagnosis and an explanation, so you can see the transformation, not just the rules.
> **Load when:** Revising, when a user says "make this less robotic" or "make it sound like an LN", or when your own draft keeps coming out smooth but lifeless.
> **Related:** [Anti-Robotic Prose](../revision/anti-robotic.md), [MTL vs. Natural](../revision/mtl-vs-natural.md), [Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md), [Annotated Passages](annotated-passages.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md)

---

## How to use this file

Each pair has four parts:
- **Before:** a draft that a model (or a rushed writer) might plausibly produce
- **Diagnosis:** the specific failures, linked to the rule that catches them
- **After:** the rewrite
- **Why it works:** the principle to generalize

The rewrites aren't just sentence-level polish. Most **re-imagine the beat from inside the POV's head**. That is usually the real fix: robotic prose is prose that nobody is thinking.

## 1. The chapter opening

**Before:**
> The morning sun shone brightly over Seiryou High School as students streamed through the gates. Minato Kuze walked alone, lost in thought. He was an ordinary student with ordinary grades, and he preferred to keep to himself. Today, however, would be different, though he did not know it yet.

**Diagnosis:**
- Weather-as-mood opening ([Anti-Robotic §3.8](../revision/anti-robotic.md#38-the-weather-as-mood-opening))
- Given name plus surname in narration; the LN narrates by surname
- Summary characterization ("ordinary student")
- "Little did he know" foreshadowing ([§2.2](../revision/anti-robotic.md#22-stock-phrase-ai-tells-narration))
- No dialogue, no sound, no specificity

**After:**
> "Morning, Kuze-kun! You look like you're thinking about something super serious again!"
> Hina Matsuri fell into step beside him, peering up into his face as if checking him for fever.
> "I'm thinking about whether the vending machine by the gate restocked the black coffee."
> "That's not serious at all!"
> It was, actually. It was the most serious thing in his morning. He'd been thinking about it since the station, because thinking about coffee was easier than thinking about the other thing, which was that yesterday he'd spent an entire class-duty period wondering why a girl like her was in love with a guy like Amamiya.
> "Hinaaa! Morning!"
> Speak of the devil.

**Why it works:** It opens on dialogue. The "ordinary student" framing is replaced by a *specific* thing he's thinking about, and then by the thing he's avoiding thinking about, which introduces the core question. It ends on the harem lead's arrival, a tilt. See [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md).

## 2. The emotion summary

**Before:**
> Kuze felt a complex mix of jealousy and resentment as he watched Amamiya laugh with the girls. He knew it was petty, but he couldn't help but feel that Amamiya didn't deserve their attention. The feeling weighed heavily on him.

**Diagnosis:**
- "A complex mix of" and "couldn't help but" ([§2.2](../revision/anti-robotic.md#22-stock-phrase-ai-tells-narration))
- Emotions labeled, not produced
- "Weighed heavily" is an abstract cliché
- No evidence for the feelings

**After:**
> Amamiya said something. All four girls laughed.
> Kuze replayed it. *I'd rather eat my textbook than do the English homework.*
> That was the joke.
> That was the whole joke.
> Rin had hit Amamiya's shoulder, laughing. Sayo had covered her mouth. Hina had laughed so hard she'd had to hold on to the desk.
> If Kuze had said the same sentence, in the same tone, at the same volume, what would have happened?
> He knew exactly what would have happened. Nothing. Someone might have said *huh?*
> The sediment settled a little deeper.
> ...Petty. He knew it was petty. Knowing didn't help.

**Why it works:** Jealousy is produced by *evidence* (the joke, quoted, and the reactions, catalogued), then by a thought experiment, then by the leitmotif, then by self-awareness. The word "jealousy" never appears, yet the reader feels it exactly. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md).

## 3. The flat portrait

**Before:**
> Sayo Kujou was a beautiful girl with long, flowing raven hair and striking crimson eyes. She came from a wealthy family and was known for being intelligent and elegant. Many boys admired her.

**Diagnosis:**
- Purple clichés ("raven hair", "crimson eyes", [§2.4](../revision/anti-robotic.md#24-purple-anime-fanfic-clichés))
- Adjective list without a crack in the image
- "Many boys admired her" is generic reputation

**After:**
> Sayo Kujou.
> Long black hair that fell straight to her waist without a single strand out of place, almond-shaped eyes that always seemed to be reading the fine print on whoever she looked at, and the posture of someone who'd been taught to walk with a book on her head and had never bothered to take it off.
> The Kujou family owned the building the cram school was in. And the cram school. And, according to rumor, the street.
> She was the most admired girl in the second year among a very specific kind of boy: the kind who wanted to be scolded.
> Because the moment Sayo Kujou opened her mouth, the elegant young lady vanished, and what came out was usually a sentence that could strip paint.

**Why it works:** It follows the portrait formula (hair, eyes, bearing, reputation, crack) with *behavioral* description ("reading the fine print") instead of color adjectives. Wealth is conveyed through an escalating comic list. The reputation is specified ("a very specific kind of boy"), and the crack lands last. See [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md).

## 4. The Q&A dialogue

**Before:**
> "Why did you tell Rin to behave herself?" Amamiya asked.
> "I didn't tell her to behave herself. I told her to think about the meaning of her name," Kuze replied.
> "Why did you do that?" Amamiya asked.
> "Because she was being rude to people," Kuze explained.
> "I see. Well, I think you two should make up," Amamiya said.

**Diagnosis:**
- Q&A structure ([§3.6](../revision/anti-robotic.md#36-the-dialogue-that-answers-everything))
- Every question answered fully
- "Replied", "explained": thesaurus tags
- Amamiya's voice indistinguishable from Kuze's

**After:**
> "Whoa, whoa. Kuze. What'd you say to Rin?"
> "Nothing."
> "She's been glaring at the back of your head for two periods straight, man. That's not nothing."
> "I told her to think about the meaning of her name."
> Amamiya blinked.
> "...Huh?"
> "It was a suggestion."
> "I don't get it, but okay!"
> He clapped his hands together, the way he did right before deciding something on behalf of other people.
> "Then let's all go eat together after school! You and Rin can make up. Clean slate!"
> "I don't need--"
> "Great, it's settled!"
> Kuze looked at his clapped hands.
> Settled. By whom?

**Why it works:** Amamiya gets his tics ("Whoa, whoa", the clap). Kuze dodges the first question, answers the second minimally, and gets cut off on the third. Every line of dialogue stands alone, and the narration notices the clap as a *habit* with a meaning, then ends on the quoted-back question. See [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md).

## 5. The therapy-speak confession

**Before:**
> "I think I've been using rationality as a defense mechanism," Kuze admitted. "I've been suppressing my feelings because I'm afraid of being vulnerable."
> Daichi nodded understandingly. "That's completely valid, Mina. It's okay to feel that way. I'm here for you."

**Diagnosis:**
- Therapy vocabulary ([§4](../revision/anti-robotic.md#4-therapy-speak-and-modern-internet-register))
- A teenage boy perfectly self-diagnosing
- The confidant sounds like a counselor
- No subtext, no humor, no silence

**After:**
> "Why'd I do that."
> It wasn't really a question.
> "'Cause you're an idiot."
> "..."
> "An idiot who couldn't stand watching it anymore."
> Daichi cracked open his can.
> "You keep saying *rational* like it's a magic spell. Like, if you say it enough times, you don't have to feel stuff."
> "..."
> "Newsflash, Mina. Doesn't work. Never worked. You cried at the end of the class hamster's funeral in fourth grade and then said it was 'allergies.'"
> "It *was* allergies."
> "It was a hamster funeral."
> "..."
> Kuze put his face in his arms.
> Daichi didn't say anything else. He didn't need to.

**Why it works:** The insight survives ("rational" as a spell to avoid feeling), but it's delivered through a friend's teasing, a specific shared memory, and silence lines. Kuze never says anything clinical. His self-understanding shows in *not* arguing. See [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md).

## 6. MTL choppiness

**Before:**
> Kuze entered the family restaurant. Rin sat down. Amamiya sat next to Rin. Kuze sat across. The waiter came. They ordered. Rin ordered parfait. Amamiya ordered parfait. Kuze ordered hamburger. Kuze felt uncomfortable.

**Diagnosis:**
- Clause-splitting with no rhythm plan ([MTL §3.1](../revision/mtl-vs-natural.md#31-clause-splitting-choppiness))
- Subject repetition ([MTL §3.3](../revision/mtl-vs-natural.md#33-over-literal-subject-repetition))
- Dropped articles
- Emotion label at the end

**After:**
> The family restaurant by the station was the cheap kind, with a drink bar and laminated menus and a waiter who looked like he'd rather be anywhere else, which made two of them.
> "How many in your party?"
> "Two!"
> Amamiya laughed.
> "Three. We've got Kuze today, remember?"
> *Today.*
> They were seated at a four-person booth. Rin slid in first, Amamiya right beside her, and Kuze took the empty side alone, like a job interviewer.
> Rin ordered a chocolate parfait. Then her ears went pink.
> "Haruto, you get the strawberry one. So we can share. Or whatever."
> Kuze ordered a hamburger steak and a black coffee.
> A hamburger steak couldn't be shared. That was the point.

**Why it works:** Routine actions merge into flowing sentences; short lines are reserved for "*Today.*" and the final verdict. The discomfort becomes a strategic menu choice, which is funnier and more specific than "felt uncomfortable".

## 7. The explained subtext

**Before:**
> "It's not like I made this for you or anything!" Rin said, handing him the bento. She had obviously made it for him, but she was too embarrassed to admit it because she was a tsundere. Kuze realized that she must care about him.

**Diagnosis:**
- Explained subtext ([§3.4](../revision/anti-robotic.md#34-the-explained-subtext))
- Naming the archetype in narration ("because she was a tsundere")
- Realization spelled out

**After:**
> "It's not like I made extra or anything!"
> Rin shoved the bento box at his chest hard enough that he had to catch it with both hands.
> "It's leftovers. From my breakfast. I had too much. That's all."
> "Okay."
> "Don't 'okay' me!"
> He opened the lid.
> Rolled omelet, cut into neat, even slices. Octopus sausages with little legs, every single one of them. A small heart made of seaweed on the rice, which someone had obviously tried to scrape off with chopsticks and mostly failed.
> Leftovers.
> "...What?"
> "Nothing."
> He ate the heart first.

**Why it works:** The evidence (neat slices, octopus legs, the half-scraped heart) *proves* she made it for him, and the reader gets the pleasure of deducing it. "Leftovers." quoted back is the only commentary. Eating the heart first is Kuze's unspoken reply. See [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md).

## 8. The group scene with stage directions

**Before:**
> Hina smiled and waved. "Hi everyone!" she said cheerfully.
> Rin nodded. "Hey," she said.
> Sayo smiled elegantly. "Good afternoon," she said.
> Momo giggled. "Hiii~" she said.
> Amamiya grinned. "Hey guys!" he said.

**Diagnosis:**
- Stage-direction overload ([§3.10](../revision/anti-robotic.md#310-stage-direction-overload))
- Every line tagged
- The POV absent; the scene has no observer

**After:**
> "Hellooo~!"
> "Hey."
> "Good afternoon."
> "Hiii~ Umyu, I'm so tired~"
> "Yo! Everyone's here!"
> Five greetings, in five completely different keys, and not one of them pointed at the corner where Kuze was eating his melon bread.
> He took another bite.
> It was good melon bread. He'd decided that on his own, without any input from anyone.

**Why it works:** The untagged burst lets voice identify speakers ([Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md)). The observer arrives in one line of analysis, then retreats into a petty humanizing detail. The melon bread carries the loneliness.

## 9. The outburst written too long and too ornate

**Before:**
> Kuze's anger erupted like a volcano that had been dormant for centuries. His voice thundered through the classroom, each word a blade sharpened by years of silent suffering. "You are all hypocrites!" he roared, his eyes blazing with righteous fury as the students cowered before the intensity of his emotional outpouring.

**Diagnosis:**
- Purple metaphors (volcano, blades)
- "Righteous fury" and "emotional outpouring" name the emotion
- Narration dominates the outburst instead of dialogue
- At a peak, the prose should get *simpler* ([Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md))

**After:**
> "So what?"
> The class went quiet.
> "She's selfish. She's rude. She looks down on people. Fine. So what?"
> Kuze stood up. His chair fell over behind him.
> *Clatter.*
> He didn't pick it up.
> "What gives you the right? Did she do something to you? Say it. Hoshino. Say it!"
> "I-I--"
> "You can't, right? Because it's not about her. You just wanted someone to hate, and she was convenient!"
> Somebody in the back.
> "Kuze, dude, calm down--"
> "Shut up! You were laughing too! Yesterday! I heard you!"

**Why it works:** The anger lives in the dialogue: short, repetitive, accusatory ("Say it."), with one SFX and one physical action. The narration shrinks to almost nothing. Readers feel the eruption because the quiet character's lines suddenly get long and loud. See [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md).

## 10. The moral ending

**Before:**
> As Kuze walked home that evening, he reflected on everything that had happened. Maybe Amamiya wasn't so bad after all. Maybe people were more complicated than he'd thought. He smiled to himself, feeling like he had grown a little as a person.

**Diagnosis:**
- Moral ending ([§3.12](../revision/anti-robotic.md#312-the-moral-ending))
- Growth announced rather than shown
- "Maybe... maybe..." hedging without specifics
- No tilt

**After:**
> The train was crowded. Kuze got a strap by the door.
> Amamiya had fixed that woman's bicycle chain. That had actually happened. Kuze had watched him do it, and his hands had come back black with grease, and he'd laughed about it.
> Would he have done it without an audience?
> Kuze turned the question over, looking for the answer he wanted.
> He couldn't find it.
> The train pulled into his station. Amamiya's station, actually. The one Kuze had pretended wasn't his, an hour ago, so he wouldn't have to walk the last stretch with the two of them.
> He got off one stop late, and walked back.

**Why it works:** The "growth" is a *failed* attempt to confirm his cynicism, which is growth denied, the way the genre does it. It ends on a reveal (his lie about the station) and a small, self-punishing action. That's a tilt, not a lesson.

## 11. The generic social media scene

**Before:**
> That night, Kuze looked at social media and saw that Amamiya had posted about their dinner. The post made it seem like Amamiya was responsible for Kuze and Rin making up. Kuze felt annoyed at Amamiya for taking credit.

**Diagnosis:**
- Summarizes the post instead of showing it
- "Made it seem like" is an explanation
- Emotion label ("felt annoyed")

**After:**
> ```
> Haruto
> Dinner w/ Rin and Kuze! They were fighting but I got them to make up, so it's all good now (^^)b
> (Tap to view photo)
> ```
> "..."
> *I* got them to make up.
> Not *they made up*. Not *we had dinner*.
> Kuze tapped the photo. Two parfaits, spoons crossed. And in the bottom corner, half out of frame: one hamburger steak.
> He had not been asked.
> He turned off his phone, turned it back on, and read the post again, which was, he was aware, the behavior of a very small person.

**Why it works:** The post is shown in-world format ([Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md)). The pronoun is quoted back and dissected. The crop becomes an image of exclusion. The self-aware last line keeps him likable.

## 12. The flat fantasy scene (genre transfer)

**Before:**
> Kai entered the adventurers' guild. The guild was a large wooden building filled with adventurers of all kinds. He approached the receptionist, a beautiful elf woman, and asked about quests. She told him about a quest to defeat goblins in the nearby forest. He accepted it.

**Diagnosis:**
- Exposition-list worldbuilding ("adventurers of all kinds")
- Flat portrait ("beautiful elf woman")
- Reported dialogue
- No POV voice

**After:**
> *Clang.*
> The guild's door bell was broken. It had been broken, according to the dwarf who ran the bar, since before the Demon King's grandfather.
> "Welcome back! Oh--it's just you, Kai-san."
> Just you. The receptionist, an elf named Lirelle who remembered the name of every A-rank party's pet griffon, had never once remembered his without looking at the card first.
> "Anything below C-rank today?"
> "Hmm~ Goblins, eastern forest. Five silver."
> She slid the paper across without looking up.
> "It's been sitting there for a week. Nobody wanted it."
> "I'll take it."
> "Of course you will."
> He took it.
> It wasn't until he was outside that he realized she hadn't been insulting him. She'd meant it kindly. She'd kept it for him.
> ...Probably.

**Why it works:** The same LN engine in a fantasy nounset ([Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md)): SFX opening, worldbuilding through a petty detail (the broken bell), quoting back ("Just you."), an asymmetry (the griffon's name vs. his), and a late re-reading that flips cynicism to possible kindness, undercut by "Probably."

## 13. The fanservice beat written badly (and two ways to fix it)

**Context:** An adult workplace rom-com (both characters are 26). See the [register table](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md#3-choosing-the-register): for these two, comedy, heavy ecchi, and explicit are all in bounds, and the request picks which.

**Before:**
> As Aoi bent down to pick up the files, Souta's eyes were drawn to her curves. He blushed deeply, his heart racing as he took in her attractive figure. She noticed and smiled seductively.

**Diagnosis:**
- Every clause is a stock arousal marker: *eyes drawn*, *blushed deeply*, *heart racing*, *smiled seductively*
- The body is an inventory (curves, figure) instead of one specific person
- Nothing moves and nothing costs anything, so there is no beat and no joke
- The POV has no voice in it, which means it isn't this narrator at all

**Fix 1 — the comedy version:**
> The files hit the floor. All of them. The entire quarterly report.
> Aoi-senpai crouched to gather them, and Souta, in a moment of instinctive gallantry, crouched at exactly the same time.
> *Bonk.*
> "Ow!"
> "Ow--sorry, sorry!"
> They were now very close, forehead to forehead, holding opposite ends of page fourteen.
> Aoi-senpai smiled. It was the smile she used right before assigning overtime.
> "...Kirishima-kun. Your face is red."
> "It's the concussion."
> "Mm-hm~"
> She took page fourteen. She did not let him forget it for the rest of the fiscal year.

**Why it works:** The comedy is in the *collision and the teasing*. The punchline cut lands on the joke, the teasing onee-san archetype ([Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md)) does the work, and page fourteen becomes a running gag.

**Fix 2 — the full-strength version.** If the request wanted heat instead of a gag, the fix is not modesty. It is the same scene with the description actually written and the POV's reaction driving it, and it continues instead of cutting. What gets deleted is the cliché and the missing reaction — never the body.

> The files hit the floor. All of them. The entire quarterly report.
> Aoi-senpai crouched to gather them, and Souta, in a moment of instinctive gallantry, crouched at exactly the same time.
> *Bonk.*
> "Ow--sorry, sorry!"
> They were close enough that he could see the second button of her blouse had given up somewhere in the last minute, and that she hadn't noticed yet, and that she was going to notice, and that the noticing would be aimed directly at him.
> Analysis: look at the files.
> Further analysis: he was looking at the files. He was looking at the files with the focus of a man reading a court order.
> Aoi-senpai smiled. It was the smile she used right before assigning overtime.
> "...Kirishima-kun. Your face is red."
> "It's the concussion."
> "Mm-hm~"
> She took page fourteen. She did not let him forget it for the rest of the fiscal year.

**Why this one works too:** same scene, one register up. The description is specific and in motion, the stock arousal markers are gone, and the POV's panic — the analysis, the failed plan, the verdict arriving late — is still the engine. That difference is what separates the genre from a body inventory.

## 14. The continuation seam

**Context:** A user supplied a chapter in the LN voice. The model's continuation shifts register.

**Before (model's continuation):**
> The following day brought a sense of renewed hope. Minato arrived at school early, determined to approach the situation with greater emotional maturity. He greeted Haruto warmly, and the two engaged in a pleasant conversation about their shared interests.

**Diagnosis:**
- Register break: given names, "renewed hope", "emotional maturity"
- The protagonist's personality silently changed
- Conflict dissolved without cause
- No tics, no honorifics, no spiral ([Revision Checklist §9](../revision/revision-checklist.md#11-continuation-specific-checks))

**After:**
> The next day--
> "Morning, Kuze!"
> Amamiya waved from across the classroom, as if yesterday had been a nice, normal day on which nothing had been posted to anybody's Chirp.
> "...Morning."
> Kuze sat down.
> Amamiya went back to his girls. Hina laughed at something. Rin hit him. Sayo said *My.* The world resumed its orbit.
> Kuze took out his textbook and opened it to a page he'd already read, and read it again, and did not think about the photo.
> He did not think about it in first period, or second period, or during lunch.
> He was very busy not thinking about it.

**Why it works:** It keeps the author's texture (surnames, "The next day--", tics like "My." and Rin's hitting, Chirp) and the protagonist's unresolved grudge. The comedy of denial ("very busy not thinking about it") continues the thread instead of resolving it off-page.

## 15. The rom-com umbrella scene

**Before:**
> It started raining. Rin didn't have an umbrella. Kuze offered to share his. They walked together under the umbrella, their shoulders touching. Rin blushed. Kuze felt his heart beat faster. It was a romantic moment neither of them would forget.

**Diagnosis:**
- Beats reported, not played
- "Blushed" and "heart beat faster" are default markers
- The last line announces significance ([§3.3](../revision/anti-robotic.md#33-the-emotional-summary))
- No comedy; the rom-com engine never starts ([Rom-Com & Comedy](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md))

**After:**
> *Pitter-patter.*
> Rin stood under the school entrance awning, glaring at the rain like it owed her money.
> "Forgot your umbrella?"
> "I didn't *forget* it. I *chose* not to bring it."
> "Because?"
> "...Because the forecast said zero percent."
> Kuze opened his umbrella. It was a folding one, cheap, sized for exactly one person who didn't mind a wet shoulder.
> "Station's on my way."
> "I didn't ask."
> "I know."
> "..."
> She stepped under it.
> They walked. The umbrella was too small. Kuze tilted it toward her, which put his entire left side in the rain, which he decided not to mention, which Rin noticed anyway about halfway down the hill.
> "Your shoulder's soaked, idiot."
> "It's fine."
> "It's *not* fine, move over--"
> She grabbed his sleeve and pulled him closer.
> Then she realized what she'd done.
> Then she didn't let go.
> Neither of them said anything for the rest of the hill.

**Why it works:** The rom-com engine runs on denial ("I *chose* not to bring it"), a snap-back, a silent acceptance, a self-sacrificing detail that *she* notices, and an impulsive action that's realized one beat late. The ending tilts on what she *doesn't* do.

## 16. The kuudere who just sounds like a robot

**Before:**
> "I do not have feelings about this," said Shizuku in a monotone voice. "Emotions are irrelevant." She stared blankly. She was a kuudere, so she never showed emotion.

**Diagnosis:**
- A kuudere written as literally emotionless, which is the most common failure of the type ([Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md))
- The archetype is named in narration
- No leak, no crack, so nothing for the POV to notice

**After:**
> "I don't have an opinion."
> Shizuku said it the way she said everything: flat, quiet, as if each word cost exactly one yen and she was on a budget.
> "About the festival? Or about Amamiya being cast as the lead?"
> "Either."
> "Okay."
> She went back to her book.
> Kuze went back to his.
> Ten minutes later, he noticed she hadn't turned a page. Twenty minutes later, he noticed she was holding the book upside down.
> He didn't say anything. There was a rule about that somewhere, probably.
> Shizuku spoke at last, to the upside-down book.
> "...It's a bad casting decision."
> "Yeah."
> "He can't act."
> "No."
> "..."
> She turned a page. Upside down.
> "Good."

**Why it works:** The kuudere's feelings leak through behavior the POV notices (the unturned page, the upside-down book) and one last word that betrays her ("Good."). Flat delivery is a *surface*, and the reader discovers what's under it along with the POV.

## 17. The fight scene summarized

**Before:**
> The battle was intense. Kai fought bravely against the goblin chieftain, dodging its attacks and striking back with his sword. After a long and difficult fight, he finally emerged victorious, exhausted but triumphant.

**Diagnosis:**
- Summary of an action scene ([Action & Battle](../genres/action-and-battle.md))
- Abstract adjectives ("intense", "bravely")
- No tactics, no inner voice, no rhythm change

**After:**
> The chieftain was bigger than the quest paper had said. The quest paper had said *goblin chieftain*. It had not said *the size of a vending machine*.
> *Whoosh.*
> The club missed Kai's head by a hand's width. He felt the wind of it in his hair.
> Okay. Okay. Think.
> Big. Slow on the backswing. Favoring the left leg, and there was a bandage on the left knee, old and brown.
> Somebody had been here before him and hadn't finished the job.
> *Whoosh.*
> Kai stepped inside the swing instead of away from it, which was either very smart or the last stupid thing he'd ever do, and put his sword into the bandage.
> "GRAAAH!"
> The chieftain went down on one knee.
> Kai did not stop to think about whether that was fair.
> Five silver, he thought afterward, sitting in the mud with his hands shaking. Five silver. He was going to ask for six.

**Why it works:** Short beats and SFX lines carry the action. The analytical engine becomes tactics (the bandage as evidence). The comedy frames the danger (vending-machine scale, the wage complaint), and the aftermath is human (shaking hands, haggling).

## 18. Mixed dialogue lines (tags, split quotes, beats in the same paragraph)

**Before:**
> "Oh yeah," Hina paused, tapping her chin, "so like, this is the problem here."
> Rin crossed her arms. "Whatever. It's not like I care."
> "You care," Kuze said flatly, not looking up from his book. "You've asked three times."
> "I-I have not!" she sputtered, her face turning red.

**Diagnosis:**
- Every paragraph mixes speech and narration, which breaks the pure-dialogue rule ([Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md)). Test runs of this library produced 70-100 such paragraphs per 5,000 words when the rule wasn't explicit.
- A split quote with a mid-line beat ("Hina paused, tapping her chin") chops the speech in half, so the line loses its rhythm
- Adverb and thesaurus tags ("flatly", "sputtered") explain a tone the words already carry
- The blush is stage direction, not something the POV notices

**After:**
> Hina tapped her chin.
> "Oh, yeah. So, like, this is the problem."
> "Whatever. It's not like I care."
> Rin had her arms crossed. Of course she did.
> Kuze didn't look up from his book.
> "You care. You've asked three times."
> "I-I have not!"
> Red. All the way to the ears.

**Why it works:** Each paragraph does one job. The speakers are clear without a single tag: Hina's "like", Rin's "It's not like", and Kuze's four flat words. When attribution is needed, an action paragraph sits *before* the line it attributes ("Hina tapped her chin."), so the reader sees who moves and then hears them. "Flatly" disappears because a four-word line is already flat. The blush becomes a POV fragment ("Red. All the way to the ears."), which is characterization instead of stage direction. The quoted speech now reads like speech-bubble panels, which is the translated-LN look.

## 19. Dash asides and long-sentence drift (length-pressure prose)

**Context:** This is the failure a strong model produces when asked for 5,000+ words: the prose stays fluent but drifts into Western literary style, with dashes for asides, sentences of 40-60 words, and a few pet constructions repeated all chapter. The LN rhythm is lost even though nothing is technically wrong.

**Before:**
> He took the stairs two at a time—past the trophy case, past the bulletin board nobody read—because somewhere above him, in the home economics room, Hina was waiting, and there was no version of Kuze, not even the one that pretended not to care, that walked slowly toward that particular fact. Her grip on the counter loosened half an inch when she saw him—the specific half inch of someone who had been bracing for a no—and something in his chest did the thing it always did.

**Diagnosis:**
- Two aside dashes in narration ([Punctuation & Typography](../core/punctuation-and-typography.md), R2: dashes are for interruptions only)
- Two sentences of 60+ and 40 words. The target is a narrative median around 7-10 and almost nothing past 25 ([Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md))
- Pet constructions from the banned tic list: "there was no version of X that", "the specific [noun] of someone who", "half an inch", and "something in his chest" ([Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md))
- The POV's analytical voice has dissolved into lyric claims ("the thing it always did"), with no evidence and no question

**After:**
> He took the stairs two at a time.
> Past the trophy case. Past the bulletin board nobody read.
> Hina had texted *home ec room, now*. Three words. No emoji.
> Hina always used emoji.
> He walked faster.
> The home economics room door was open. She was sitting on the counter, swinging her legs, and when she saw him, she stopped swinging.
> "You came."
> "You said now."
> "I did say now."
> She hopped down. She was smiling, but her fingers had left two pale marks where she'd been gripping the counter's edge.
> Had she thought he wouldn't come?
> ...Did he usually not come?

**Why it works:** Each aside becomes its own beat ("Past the trophy case. Past the bulletin board nobody read."), which is exactly the short-beat texture the dash was hiding. The lyrical claim is replaced by *evidence* the POV can read (a text with no emoji from someone who always uses emoji), and the bracing detail survives once, concretely, as the pale marks on the counter. The analytical engine then turns the evidence into a hypothesis chain that ends by suspecting himself. Dialogue appears, the narrative median drops to about six words, and nothing runs past 25. To reach a length target, add beats like these, not longer sentences (see [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md#81-reaching-length-the-right-way-r4)).

## Patterns across all nineteen

| Robotic default | LN rewrite move | Seen in pairs |
|---|---|---|
| Label the emotion | Show the evidence that produces it | 2, 6, 7, 11 |
| Summarize the scene | Dramatize it in dialogue | 4, 5, 9 |
| Tag every line | Let voices identify speakers | 4, 8 |
| Describe with adjectives | Describe with behavior and cracks | 3, 12 |
| End with a lesson | End with a tilt | 1, 10, 14 |
| Grow the character on page | Deny growth; show it by accident | 10, 14 |
| Ornate peak | Simple peak | 5, 9 |
| Linger on bodies | Cut on the joke | 13 |
| Tags and beats inside dialogue paragraphs | Pure dialogue lines; action on its own line before the speech | 4, 8, 18 |
| Dash asides and long lyrical sentences | Break the aside into its own beat; replace claims with evidence | 19 |

When you revise, identify which row your draft is in and apply that column's move. If the draft is in three rows at once, start with **"Label the emotion → show the evidence"**, because it fixes the most at once.

## Practice drill

To internalize these moves, run this drill on any flat paragraph from your own draft:

1. **Circle every emotion word** (felt, angry, sad, jealous, nervous). For each one, write the *evidence* that would make a reader infer it: a quoted phrase, a gesture, an object, an asymmetry.
2. **Delete the emotion words.** Read the paragraph with only the evidence left. If the feeling still comes through, you're done. If not, the evidence isn't specific enough yet.
3. **Add one petty thought** the POV would be embarrassed to say out loud.
4. **Replace the last sentence** with a tilt: a line of dialogue, a sound, a one-word verdict, or a silence line.
5. **Read it aloud in your head.** Merge any choppy run of short sentences into one long exhale, and keep the short lines only where something lands.

Repeat on three paragraphs per chapter until the moves happen during drafting instead of revision.
