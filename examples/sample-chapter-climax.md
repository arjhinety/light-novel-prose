# Sample Climax Chapter: "Do You Like Justice? 4"

> **Purpose:** A complete, original climax chapter from late in the first arc. It shows the voice at maximum pressure: the class turns on Rin, the POV fails by choosing "rationality", then breaks in an outburst that takes all the malice onto himself, and finally a confidant's monologue-and-silence scene brings him down.
> **Load when:** You are writing an arc climax, a bullying peak, an outburst, or a breakdown scene, or you need to see how the prose simplifies and repeats at emotional peaks.

**Where this sits in the arc (for the reader of this file; not part of the prose):** It is late May, some thirty chapters after Chapter 2. Kuze and Rin's lunches on the roof have become a routine, and they once went to a café to buy a proper tamagoyaki pan. An anonymous Chirp account posted photos of them together, captioned to suggest Rin was *keeping a spare* while chasing Amamiya. Reika Hoshino, who has always resented Rin, turned the class against her. Rin told Kuze on the phone that it was his fault for being seen with her. He hung up, and he has ignored her calls since, telling himself that staying out of it is the rational choice. Amamiya has said nothing.

**House rules this chapter demonstrates:** every paragraph that contains speech contains only the speech (attribution comes from voice, or from a narration paragraph of its own); dashes appear only where speech is cut off; the outburst runs long in dialogue while the narration stays short.

Chapter 31: Do You Like Justice? 4

The sticky notes were back.

Kuze saw them from the doorway. A crust of yellow squares covered the top of Rin's desk like scales. Each one was written in a different hand, so no single person could be blamed for it.

*Two-timer.*

*Spare parts collector (lol)*

*Kendo gorilla in heat*

*Haruto-kun deserves better*

*How much for lunch? (lol)*

The last one had a little drawing of a lunch box next to it.

He looked at it for exactly one second. Then he walked to his own desk and sat down.

*Not my problem.*

He'd been saying it for four days. He'd said it so many times that it had worn smooth, like a stone in a river. It fit perfectly in his hand now. He didn't even have to think about it anymore.

*She said it herself. It's my fault for being seen with her. So the rational thing is to not be seen with her. Stay out of it. Let it burn out. Reika gets bored. Everyone gets bored. It's June in two weeks and nobody will remember.*

*Not my problem.*

Around the room, the class was pretending not to look at Rin's desk. They did it with great concentration. It was the concentration of people looking at nothing else.

Hina stood next to the desk with her hands clenched at her sides. Her mouth was pressed into a line. She looked like she wanted to rip the notes off and couldn't make her arms move.

"Reika-chan... this isn't funny anymore. Can we just... stop?"

"Stop what, Hina-chan~? I didn't write any of them~"

"You know what I mean!"

"Hmm~ Not really~"

"..."

Hina's arms stayed at her sides.

Kujou sat by the window with her book open, turning pages. Momo had her phone out. Momo always had her phone out.

Amamiya sat at his desk with his head down and his eyes on his hands.

He hadn't looked up since Kuze came in. He hadn't looked up, Kuze was fairly sure, since Monday.

*Not my problem.*

The sediment was very thick now. It had stopped settling days ago. There was simply no more room for it to fall.

1

Rin came in at 8:24, one minute before the bell.

She'd started doing that on Tuesday. She arrived at the last possible moment and left at the first. It cut her time in the room by nearly twenty minutes a day. Kuze had noticed. He had done the math automatically, the way he did everything. Then he had hated himself for doing it, and for doing nothing with the answer.

She stopped in the doorway.

She looked at her desk.

For a second, nothing moved on her face. The gray eyes stayed flat and level. Her chin stayed up, her back straight. Every line of her was the girl who had walked through hallways while the crowd parted around her.

Then her fingers tightened on the strap of her shinai bag. The knuckles went white, and Kuze saw what was underneath.

Reika Hoshino rose from her seat in the second row like someone taking a stage.

"Oh~! Tachibana-san's here~!"

She was pretty in a way that took effort, glossy and laminated. Her brown hair was curled at the ends every morning. Her lashes had cost money. Her skirt stopped exactly one centimeter past what the discipline committee would tolerate. She wasn't the most popular girl in 2-3. She was something more dangerous. She was the one who decided what the popular opinion was.

"Morning, Tachibana-san~ Did you have a good weekend? Oh, wait, I know you did. It was *all over* Chirp~"

"..."

Rin walked to her desk without answering. She began peeling the sticky notes off one by one. She folded each in half before dropping it into her bag, as if she were tidying.

"Aw, don't take them off~ Everyone worked so hard on those~"

One of the girls at Reika's elbow leaned in, giggling.

"Seriously~ I wrote the lunch box one. It's cute, right?"

"*Totally* cute~"

"Did you see her face? She's pretending she can't read~"

"She can read. She just can't *handle* it~"

"Hey, Tachibana-san~ Which one's your favorite? We can make more~"

"We have *so* many sticky notes~"

"..."

Rin kept peeling.

Kuze kept not looking.

*Rationally,* he thought. The word was a handrail in the dark. *Rationally, if I say anything, it proves the post was right. If I stand up for her, I'm the spare defending his position. Rationally, the best thing I can do for her is nothing.*

*Rationally.*

Reika drifted over to Rin's desk. She took the last sticky note before Rin could reach it. Then she read it out in a bright announcer's voice.

"'*Haruto-kun deserves better.*' Hmm~ I think that's true, don't you, Tachibana-san? Everyone thinks it's true."

She turned, holding up the note like a winning lottery ticket.

"Amamiya-kun! Don't you think you deserve better?"

Every head in the room turned toward him.

Amamiya's shoulders jerked. He looked up for the first time. His face was pale and caught, like an animal on a night road. His eyes went to Reika, then to Rin. Then away. Down. Back to his hands.

"...I don't really want to get into it."

"..."

Something in Rin's face moved, and then stopped moving.

*Amamiya.*

The thought came up out of Kuze's chest before he could catch it. It came high and desperate, like a hand shooting up in class.

*Amamiya. You walked onto a dojo floor for her once. In your socks. You didn't even know what a point was. You just knew it wasn't fair.*

*This isn't fair. Don't you know that? You know that.*

*So get up. Get up and walk onto the floor.*

*I can't. I'm not you. That's the whole point of you. That's the one thing you have that I don't. So use it. Please.*

*Please.*

Amamiya didn't get up.

He sat with his head bowed and his hands folded on the desk. He looked like he was praying, or waiting for something to be over.

And Kuze, four rows behind him, sat exactly the same way.

2

It went on through homeroom, which Ogata-sensei cut short for a staff meeting. It went on through first period in whispers and passed notes. There was a snort of laughter whenever Rin was called on to read aloud. It went on through the ten-minute break, when Reika's group gathered at the back of the room.

"--always acting like she's too good to talk to us--"

"--hates guys, she says, but look at her--"

"--one on the roof and one in the club room, like, how greedy can you *be*--"

"--and Haruto-kun's too nice to say anything, that's the worst part--"

By the break after second period, the room had changed. Kuze could feel it. It was the way a room changes before a storm. The pressure drops, and everyone's ears feel strange.

The people who'd said nothing on Monday were laughing on Friday. They weren't laughing loudly. They weren't laughing because anything was funny. They laughed because laughing was the safest place to stand.

Then Reika turned around, and her eyes landed on him.

"Oh~ Kuze-kun."

"..."

She came down the aisle between the desks, slow, her skirt swinging. Her group trailed behind her in formation.

"Kuze-*kun*~ You've been so quiet this week. You're the star of the show, you know? You're in all the photos~"

"..."

She perched on the edge of the desk in front of his.

"Come on, don't be shy. Everyone wants to hear your side. Right, everyone?"

"Yeah~"

"Totally."

"Tell us, Kuze~"

Most of the room said nothing. Their silence agreed.

Reika tapped a finger against her chin, as though laying out a theory in class.

"So here's what I think. I think you're a nice, quiet guy who got used. Tachibana-san couldn't get Amamiya-kun, so she found someone lonely who'd eat her little lunches and make her feel wanted. That's all you were. A backup. Right?"

"..."

"It's okay! It's not your fault! Nobody's mad at *you!* Just say it. Say she used you. Say she's just a pretty face with nothing inside. Everybody already knows it's true, so you might as well say it, and then it's over. Easy~"

"..."

*It would be over.*

That was the terrible part. She was right about that. If he said it, the whole weight of the room would lift off him like a hand. He would be the victim again. Poor quiet Kuze. He could go back to his desk and his black coffee and his ledger. In two weeks, nobody would remember.

*Rationally.*

He looked past Reika's shoulder at Rin.

She was sitting very still. She wasn't looking at him. She was looking at the corner of her desk, where one sticky note had left a faint yellow rectangle behind. Her hands were in her lap, holding each other.

Reika began to clap, slowly.

"Come on, Kuze-kun~ Ad-mit it~"

One of her group picked it up. *Clap. Clap.*

"Ad-mit it~"

Then another. Then a boy at the back, grinning, glad it wasn't him.

"""Ad-mit it~"""

It spread the way these things spread. It didn't pass from person to person. It passed from fear to fear.

"""Ad-mit it~"""

*She used you.*

"""Ad-mit it~"""

*She's only a pretty face.*

"""Ad-mit it~"""

*She's got nothing inside.*

"""Ad-mit it~"""

*Nobody could actually like her.*

"""Ad-mit it~"""

Students from 2-4 had gathered in the doorway to watch. Someone was filming.

"""AD-MIT IT~ AD-MIT IT~"""

Kuze thought of an omelette the color of a traffic cone at dusk.

He thought of *thanks, for eating it*, so quiet the wind nearly took it.

He thought of a handshake, hard and calloused. *Deal.*

"..."

Somewhere at the very bottom of him, something cracked. It was under a month of silt. It was under every grain he had so carefully let fall.

It wasn't loud. It was the small, dry sound of ice on a river in March. The first fracture. The one that tells you the whole surface is about to go.

3

Kuze's voice came out flat and quiet.

"...You're right."

The clapping faltered and then died. The room leaned in.

Reika's face lit up.

"See? *See?* I knew it! Go on, Kuze-kun, tell everyone--"

His chair scraped back, loud in the sudden quiet. He was standing.

"She's rude. She's rude, and she's proud, and she hits people when she's embarrassed. She can't cook. She doesn't know the difference between a teaspoon and a tablespoon. She's the most difficult person I have ever met."

Rin's head came up. She was looking at him now. Her eyes were very wide, and there was nothing in them at all.

"You hear that, Tachibana-san? Even *he*--"

"So what?"

"...Huh?"

"I said, so what?"

He turned to look at her. He was aware, distantly, that his face was doing something he hadn't told it to do. His mouth had gone wide and strange at the corners. It wasn't a smile. It was what happened to a face when everything behind it came loose at once.

"She's rude. So what, Hoshino? What did she do to *you?*"

"W-What did she-- she's been flaunting herself around Amamiya-kun and--"

"And what? Did it hurt you? Did she take something from you? Tell me what she took."

"That's not the--"

"*Tell me what she took.*"

Reika's mouth opened. Nothing came out of it.

"You keep accounts, right? Everyone in this room does. Who owes who. Who's winning. Who deserves better."

He laughed. It came out ugly.

"I keep accounts too. I'm *really* good at it. Want to hear mine?"

He looked at the girl on Reika's left.

"Tuesday. Second period. You put gum in her shoe locker. I saw you do it."

The girl went white.

He turned.

"Wednesday. You tripped her on the stairs by the science wing. Then you said *oops* in a voice so everybody would know you didn't mean oops."

He turned again, to the boy who had been clapping at the back.

"You. You'd never said a word about her until Thursday. Then you wrote *kendo gorilla* on a sticky note and laughed about it at lunch for twenty minutes. Did she ever do anything to you? Ever? Or did you just feel which way the wind was blowing and want to be sure you were standing downwind?"

"I-- I didn't--"

"You did. I counted."

The words kept coming. They came out the way the sediment had gone in, one grain at a time. Except now there were too many to count. They weren't settling anymore. They were pouring.

"Monday, eight sticky notes. Tuesday, eleven. Wednesday, fourteen, and one of them was in pen, so it left a mark on the desk, and whoever wrote it was probably proud of that. Thursday, the thing with the milk. Today, Hoshino reads them out loud like a *game show*."

He was breathing hard now.

"I know every single one of you who did something. I know most of you who laughed. And I know *all* of you who sat there and didn't do anything. Because I was one of you. I was the *worst* one."

"..."

The room didn't breathe.

"I'm not even mad at you for doing it. You're scared. I get scared too. I'm mad that you think it's *justice*. You think if enough of you agree, it stops being cruel and starts being fair. You think if you clap in rhythm, it counts as a *verdict*."

He spun on Reika.

"That's what you're selling, right, Hoshino? Justice? Let's do justice, then."

Her phone lay on the desk beside her, still unlocked, still glowing. The group chat was open on the screen. The one where all the planning had happened.

He snatched it up.

"Hey! Give that--"

"Your messages. Let's post them. Everyone should see what you really think, right? That's fair. That's *justice.* If it's good enough for Rin, it's good enough for you."

"N-No, stop, *stop--*"

He held the phone up high, out of reach. His thumb was on the share button.

"Why? Everyone already knows what you're like. So you might as well say it, and then it's *over*. Easy~"

"*Kuze, stop!*"

A hand caught his wrist.

Amamiya's grip was hard and shaking. He'd crossed the room in the time it took Kuze to say *easy*. Now he stood between Kuze and Reika. His face was white. His jaw was set.

"That's enough. You're going too far. Give it back."

Kuze looked down at the hand on his wrist.

Then he looked up at Amamiya's face. He looked at it for a long time.

"*Now* you move."

"...What?"

Kuze's voice had gone very quiet. Somehow, that was worse than the shouting.

"Four days. Four days of sticky notes. Four days of her coming in one minute before the bell so she doesn't have to be in the room. You sat there with your head down for all of it. *I don't really want to get into it.* And the second Hoshino might get hurt, you're across the room like you've been shot out of a cannon."

"That's-- it's not the same, you can't just--"

"Why not?"

"Because-- because it's *wrong*, Kuze! You can't do that to a girl, it's--"

"And what were they doing to Rin? What was *that?*"

"..."

Amamiya's grip loosened.

"You walked onto the floor in your socks once. She told me. You didn't even know the rules. You just knew it wasn't fair."

His voice cracked. He let it.

"Where were your socks this week, Amamiya?"

Amamiya's hand fell away completely.

He looked at Rin. Rin was looking at him. Whatever he saw in her face made him flinch as though he'd been hit.

Kuze dropped the phone on the desk. Reika snatched it up and clutched it to her chest. She was shaking. Her eyes were streaming, mascara running in two thin gray lines.

He found he didn't feel anything about that at all. He supposed that meant something bad about him. He'd think about it later.

He turned to the class. To all of it. The forty faces, the doorway crowd, the phone still filming from 2-4.

"Listen, because I'm only going to say it once. Tachibana didn't use anyone. *I* followed *her*. I went to the roof every day because I wanted to. I ate her awful lunches because I wanted to. She was practicing for someone else the whole time, and she told me so, and I kept going anyway, because I'm pathetic and I had nothing better to do. If you need someone to be disgusting, fine. It's me. It was always me."

It wasn't true. It was very nearly true, and those were the best lies.

"So leave her alone. You want someone to write notes about? Write them about me. I don't care. I've never cared what any of you thought, and you all know it. That's the one thing about me everyone in this room agrees on."

The silence was total.

Then he walked out.

He passed Rin's desk on the way. She was half out of her chair, one hand reaching. Her mouth was already shaping his name.

He didn't stop. He didn't look at her. He barely moved his lips.

"*Stay.*"

One word. Too quiet for anyone else to hear.

Stay here, in the room. Stay where the story is that I was the pathetic one and you were the girl I followed. Don't come after me. If you come after me, all of it was for nothing.

Rin froze.

Her hand dropped.

She had heard him. He knew it from the way her shoulders came down. And he knew she understood. She had always understood him faster than anyone had any right to. It had taken her two days and one omelette.

The crowd in the doorway split to let him through. It split the way crowds used to split for her.

Down the corridor, a teacher's voice rose. Footsteps followed it.

"Kuze-kun! Kuze, wait--"

Then, lower, Ogata-sensei. Her voice was sharp in a way he'd never heard from her.

"Leave him. Later."

He was already on the stairs.

4

Behind the pool building there was a strip of cracked concrete. There was a drainage grate and a dead vending machine that nobody had come to take away. There was a view of absolutely nothing. It was the loneliest place at Seiryou. Kuze had found it in his first week of first year and had been coming back to it ever since.

He sat with his back against the warm cinderblock wall. His knees were pulled up and his arms were around them.

"..."

The chlorine smell drifted over the fence. Water slapped somewhere on the other side, rhythmic. A first-year swimming laps during free period.

*What did I just do?*

He'd been rational for four days. Four whole days, carefully and correctly. He'd thrown all of it away in four minutes.

He had made an enemy of Reika and her entire group. He had made an enemy of everyone who'd clapped, and that was half the class. He had grabbed a girl's phone and made her cry in front of forty people and a camera. He had told Haruto Amamiya, in front of everyone, that he was a coward.

He had made himself the villain of the story. By tomorrow it would be all over Chirp, and it would stay there.

He had gained nothing. There was no rational benefit anywhere in it. By every measure he knew, it was the worst trade he had ever made.

"..."

*No.*

That wasn't it.

He knew it wasn't it. He'd known the moment the ice cracked.

He hadn't done it for any reason he could put in the ledger. He'd done it because Rin had been sitting with her hands holding each other in her lap. She had been looking at a yellow rectangle on her desk. And he hadn't been able to stand it for one more second.

That was all. That was the whole thing.

And he'd wasted four days pretending there was a better reason not to.

He hated it.

He hated that he'd called it rational, when it was only cowardice in a clean shirt. He hated hanging up on her. He hated letting the phone ring out every night. He hated lying awake doing the math on what she'd say if he picked up. He hated waiting for Amamiya to do it, so that he wouldn't have to. He hated sitting there with his head down, exactly like him, while he judged him for it. He hated the ledger. He hated keeping score of people as though they were a debt.

He hated that the only way he'd found to help her was to make everyone hate him instead.

He hated that he couldn't think of a better way even now.

He hated himself. All of it. Every grain.

"...Damn it."

It came out small.

Creak.

The gate in the pool fence swung open.

"Yo. Mina."

Daichi stood in the gap in his swim-club jacket. His hair was plastered flat and dripping. A towel hung around his neck, and he was barefoot on the hot concrete. A puddle was spreading around his feet.

"...You're wet."

"I was in the pool. That's usually how it goes."

He padded over and sat down next to Kuze against the wall. Close, shoulder to shoulder. For a while he didn't say anything else. Water ran off his hair and dripped from his chin onto the concrete between them.

"How did you know I'd be here?"

"Mina. Twelve years. You always go somewhere nobody is, when things suck. But you always pick somewhere *I* can find you. Behind the pool. Come on. You might as well have left a map."

"..."

"Also, the swim club group chat."

"...Of course."

"We're like water."

"That's still terrible."

"Two points?"

"...One."

"Harsh."

Daichi grinned at the fence. Then the grin went away.

"So. You did a thing."

"..."

"Mina."

"...I made half the class hate me. I grabbed a girl's phone. I made her cry in front of a camera. I called Amamiya a coward in front of everyone."

"Yeah?"

"There's no benefit. I ran the numbers. There's nothing on the other side. It's the worst trade I've ever made."

"Huh. Okay."

"That's it? Okay?"

"You want me to argue with the numbers? I'm in the swim club, Mina. I can count to fifty and then I turn around."

"..."

"Kinda heard it from the pool. Well, not heard. Mitsuki from 2-3 is in the club, and she came down during break and told everybody. She was crying a little. Not in a bad way, I think. She said you were scary."

He paused.

"She also said you were right."

"..."

Kuze put his forehead down on his knees.

Daichi let him. He just sat there, dripping, and the pool slapped on the other side of the fence.

"Hey, Mina."

"..."

"You like her, right?"

"..."

"Or you don't. I don't know. Maybe you don't even know."

He pulled the towel from around his neck and dropped it over Kuze's head, like a curtain.

"Doesn't matter. You cared about her. That's enough of a reason. You don't need a better one."

"..."

"You're always looking for the better one. You've been doing it since we were kids. When you wouldn't get in the pool, you didn't say you were scared. You said the water was *statistically unsanitary*. You were five. You didn't even know what statistically *meant.*"

"..."

"It's always like that with you. You feel something, and you get embarrassed about feeling it, so you build a whole explanation around it. You build a house over it so nobody can see. It's rational. It's even. It's not my problem. And then you live in the house and forget what's under it."

"..."

"But it's still under there, Mina. It always was. That's what came out today. That wasn't somebody else. That was you. The real one. The guy under the house."

"..."

"And yeah, he's kind of scary. Mitsuki's not wrong."

Daichi laughed a little.

"But he stood up for somebody when nobody else would. That's not nothing. That's not a mistake in the math. That *is* the math. That's the part that counts."

"..."

"You don't have to keep score of everything. You know that, right? Some stuff you just do. Not because it's even. Because it's *her*. Or because it's you. Or because it was wrong and you were there."

"..."

"I'm not saying stop being smart. Be smart. You're smart. It's annoying. But the ledger is supposed to be a tool, Mina. You're not supposed to be living inside it."

"..."

"That's all. I'm done. I'm cold. I'm wet. This concrete is hot and I'm cold anyway. How is that possible?"

"..."

Under the towel, something was happening to Kuze's breathing.

It had started somewhere around *the guy under the house*. It came up from the bottom of him, from the place where the silt had been lying for years. It was a pressure pushing upward with nowhere to go. His throat locked around it. His shoulders pulled in.

"Guh..."

He couldn't breathe right. He didn't know how to do this. He hadn't done it since... he couldn't remember. Since before the ledger. Since before he'd decided that everything had to balance.

"...Damn it."

It came out wet.

"Damn it... *damn it...*"

Daichi didn't say anything. He didn't look over. He didn't touch him. He just sat there, shoulder to shoulder, dripping onto the concrete. Being there. The way he'd always been there, on the other side of the water, for twelve years.

Kuze cried.

He didn't know what for. For Rin, maybe, her hands holding each other in her lap. For Amamiya, maybe. He had turned out to be only a boy after all, afraid of the same things as everyone else. For the four days he couldn't take back. For the omelette, of all stupid things, and the way she'd watched him eat it.

For himself, maybe. For the kid at the edge of the pool who wouldn't get in. The one who had never once admitted that he wanted to.

"...Damn it..."

The towel smelled like chlorine. The pool slapped against its walls. Across the field, a bell rang for third period, and neither of them moved.

5

The staff room, afterward, was very quiet and very long.

Ogata-sensei sat at her desk with her ponytail coming loose. She did not say *teehee* even once. The vice principal stood beside her. Reika sat red-eyed, furious and frightened. The two girls flanking her had very suddenly remembered they were only standing near her. Rin sat apart, hands in her lap, not looking at anyone. And there was Kuze, with Daichi's towel still around his neck, because he'd forgotten to give it back.

The vice principal cleared his throat for the third time.

"Taking another student's property. Threatening to publish private messages. Shouting at an entire class. Kuze-kun, do you understand how serious this is?"

"Yes."

"Then explain it to me. Why?"

"..."

"Kuze-kun. I'm asking you a question."

"Because nobody else was going to. That's all. I don't have a better reason."

"That's not a reason, it's--"

"It's the only one I've got, sir."

"And you, Hoshino-san. You say none of this began with you?"

"It didn't! It was just a joke that got out of hand! Everyone was doing it, not just me! And *he* grabbed my phone!"

"A joke. Four days of it?"

"Sensei, you weren't *there*, you don't know what she's like! She looks down on all of us! She says she hates guys and then she's all over Amamiya-kun, and then she's all over *him*--"

"Hoshino-san. I asked about the notes."

"The notes were *everybody*!"

"..."

Rin didn't look up. Her hands stayed in her lap.

"Tachibana-san. Is there anything you want to say?"

"...No."

"Nothing at all?"

"...I didn't do anything. That's all."

"..."

The door opened without a knock.

Sayo Kujou stepped in with a folder under her arm. Her posture belonged in a tea ceremony, not a staff room.

"Excuse me. I thought I might be of assistance."

"Kujou-san? This is a private--"

"Of course. I'll only take a moment."

She laid the folder open on the vice principal's desk. Inside were screenshots, printed and dated, and a timeline written in her small, perfect hand. Four days of it. The sticky notes. The gum in the shoe locker. The stairs by the science wing. The milk. The group chat was in there too. Kuze couldn't imagine how she'd gotten it.

"I believe this covers everything. Kuze-kun's behavior was of course inappropriate, and I'm sure he regrets taking Hoshino-san's phone."

She turned a page with one finger.

"But I think, in context, the school may wish to consider which of these incidents it would prefer to see on Chirp. Someone was filming, after all."

She smiled.

The vice principal looked at the folder for a long time.

"...Hoshino-san. I think you owe Tachibana-san something."

"But--"

"Now, please."

Reika's eyes went to the floor. Her voice was stiff and furious and very small.

"...I'm sorry. Tachibana-san."

"..."

"Sorry."

"We're sorry."

The two girls beside her were faster, eager to be finished.

Kuze got a warning, a long lecture about *proper channels*, and a note in his file. Rin got nothing, because Rin had done nothing. Ogata-sensei pointed this out three separate times, each time more sharply.

"Tachibana did nothing. I want that written down. *Nothing.*"

"Yes, Ogata-sensei, it's noted."

"Write it down anyway."

When they were finally released, the corridor outside was empty. School had ended an hour ago.

Rin walked out first.

She stopped in the corridor with her back to him. She didn't turn around.

"...You said you were pathetic. In front of everyone."

"I was."

"You said you followed me."

"I did."

"You *didn't*. *I* made you come to the roof. *I* sat on your desk. You didn't follow anybody. You lied."

"..."

"You said my lunches were awful."

"They were."

"*Not all of them!*"

She spun around. Her eyes were red and furious and wet. Her mouth was shaking.

"The karaage was good! You *said* the karaage was good! On the first day! You said I didn't rush it!"

"...I did say that."

"So you *lied* in there!"

"...Yeah. A little."

"Idiot."

She crossed the corridor in three steps. He braced for the hit.

It didn't come.

She grabbed the front of his blazer in both fists instead. She pressed her forehead against his chest, hard, as though she were trying to push through him and out the other side.

"*Idiot.* You idiot. You *stupid*--"

"..."

"You didn't answer my calls. Four days. I said something awful to you, and I wanted to take it back, and you didn't *answer*."

"..."

"And then you just-- in front of *everyone*--"

She didn't finish. Her shoulders were shaking.

Kuze stood very still with his hands at his sides. He didn't know what to do with them. There was no entry in the ledger for this. There had never been an entry for this.

After a long moment, he lifted one hand. He put it on top of her head, carefully. It was the way you'd touch something you were afraid of breaking.

She didn't hit him.

"...Sorry. For not answering."

"..."

"I'll answer next time."

"...There better not *be* a next time."

"There won't be."

"And stop saying you're pathetic. You're not allowed to say that. Only I'm allowed to say that."

"That's not how--"

"It's how it works *now*. And don't ever say my lunches are awful in front of other people again. Say it to me. Only to me. On the roof. Where it's allowed."

"...Okay."

"And you're eating tomorrow. Whatever I make. All of it."

"Even if it's--"

"*All* of it."

"...Deal."

"Tch."

It was muffled, against his blazer. It was the gentlest *tch* he had ever heard.

6

The next morning, at 8:14 exactly, Kuze walked into Class 2-3.

The room went quiet. He'd expected that. He walked through it to his desk the way you'd walk through cold water. One step, and then the next.

He could feel how the room had rearranged itself overnight. Reika's desk was empty; she'd called in sick. Her two friends sat with their heads close together, whispering. They stopped the instant he passed. The boy from the back who had clapped was suddenly fascinated by his pencil case.

By the window, someone whispered not quite quietly enough.

"That's him."

"Shh! He'll hear you!"

"He *counts* things, you know. He counted everything."

"..."

Gon materialized beside his desk the moment he sat down, glasses flashing.

"Kuze-dono. I have heard the tale. The whole school has heard the tale. They are calling it *the Ledger Incident*, verily."

"Please don't."

"I merely report. There are three versions. In one, you threw a desk. In another, you threw Hoshino-dono. In the third, you merely spoke, and the class wept."

"Which one's winning?"

"The third. It is the least plausible, and therefore the most popular."

"..."

"For the record, I believe the third."

He retreated before Kuze could answer. It was, Kuze realized, the kindest thing Gon had ever said to him.

Hina caught his eye from beside Amamiya's desk. She didn't wave. She didn't shout his name across the room. She only pressed her lips together and gave him one small, fierce nod. It was the kind you give someone across a finish line.

At the break, she stopped by his desk for exactly as long as it took to say it.

"Kuze-kun. For Rin-chan. Thank you."

"I didn't do it for--"

"I know. Still. Thank you."

She was gone again before he could find the rest of the sentence.

Amamiya didn't look up at all.

Kuze found that he didn't mind any of it as much as he'd expected to. The looks were heavy, but they were only looks. He had spent a year being invisible. It turned out being visible for the wrong reasons weighed about the same.

There was a lunch box on his desk.

It was wrapped in navy-blue cloth and tied with a bow, and the bow was perfect. Tucked into the knot was a single folded sticky note. Yellow. The same kind as all the others.

He unfolded it.

*Tablespoon = 3 teaspoons. I checked. This one's yours. It's not practice. Don't get the wrong idea.*

*-R*

"..."

He looked up.

Across the room, Rin sat at her desk with her chin on her fist. She was glaring at the blackboard as if it had personally insulted her family. The tips of her ears were bright red.

She didn't look at him once all morning. She was so careful about it that everyone noticed.

At lunch, on the roof, he untied the bow and opened the lid.

The tamagoyaki was the pale, even gold of early sunlight. It was rolled in neat, tight layers and cut into four perfect slices.

He ate one.

It was just sweet enough.

The roof door banged open behind him.

"Don't just *eat* it. Say something."

"You said you weren't coming up today."

"I didn't say that. I said nothing. You assumed."

"..."

"So? How is it?"

"It's good."

"*Just* good?"

"It's just sweet enough. The layers are even. You didn't rush it. You used the tablespoon."

"...Obviously I used the tablespoon. I wrote it on the note, didn't I? Are you blind?"

"I'm not blind."

"Then eat the rest. All of it. That was the deal."

She sat down beside him, not close, not far. Exactly the width of a lunch box away.

"...Hey. Kuze."

"Hm?"

"Yesterday. Thanks."

"..."

"Don't make it weird. Eat."

He ate the second slice.

It was also just sweet enough.

7

That night, in a room with a view of the whole city, Sayo Kujou sat at a desk made of dark wood. She uncapped a fountain pen.

On the paper in front of her was a diagram. She had been adding to it since April.

It showed names, circled in black ink. *Haruto. Rin. Hina. Momo. Reika.* Arrows ran between them, some solid and some dotted. Along each one were notes in her small, perfect handwriting. *Admires. Resents. Afraid of. Using.* One arrow ran from *Momo* to a small box labeled *anon account (Chirp)*. That arrow was solid.

She had deleted the account herself this afternoon, from Momo's own phone. Momo had watched with a face the color of paper. It had taken a single conversation in an empty music room. Kujou had found it more tedious than satisfying.

She looked at the diagram for a long time.

Then she drew a new arrow.

It began at *Rin*. It ended at a name that had been sitting alone in the upper corner of the page since the day she'd watched a boy count the cans at a vending machine. That one was circled in pencil rather than ink.

*Kuze.*

She paused, the pen above the paper. Then she drew a second arrow, very faint and dotted, from *Kuze* back to *Rin*. Beside it she wrote a single question mark.

"How interesting."

She capped the pen and looked out the window at the lights of the city.

"Haruto hasn't noticed yet, has he."

Her reflection smiled back at her from the dark glass.

It wasn't the smile of a lawful wife.

"...Let's see how long it takes him."
