# Sample Climax Chapter: "Do You Like Justice? 4"

> **Purpose:** A complete, original climax chapter from late in the first arc. It shows the voice at maximum pressure: the class turns on Rin, the POV fails by choosing "rationality", then breaks in an outburst that takes all the malice onto himself, and finally a confidant's monologue-and-silence scene brings him down.
> **Load when:** You are writing an arc climax, a bullying peak, an outburst, or a breakdown scene, or you need to see how the prose simplifies and repeats at emotional peaks.
> **Related:** [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md), [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [Sample Chapter 1](sample-chapter-01.md), [Sample Chapter 2](sample-chapter-02.md)

**Where this sits in the arc (for the reader of this file; not part of the prose):** It is late May, some thirty chapters after Chapter 2. Kuze and Rin's lunches on the roof have become a routine, and they once went to a café to buy a proper tamagoyaki pan. An anonymous Chirp account posted photos of them together, captioned to suggest Rin was "keeping a spare" while chasing Amamiya. Reika Hoshino, who has always resented Rin, turned the class against her. Rin told Kuze on the phone that it was his fault for being seen with her. He hung up, and he has ignored her calls since, telling himself that staying out of it is the rational choice. Amamiya has said nothing.

Chapter 31: Do You Like Justice? 4

The sticky notes were back.

Kuze saw them from the doorway: a crust of yellow squares covering the top of Rin's desk like scales, each one written in a different hand so no single person could be blamed for it.

*Two-timer.*

*Spare parts collector (lol)*

*Kendo gorilla in heat*

*Haruto-kun deserves better*

*How much for lunch? (lol)*

The last one had a little drawing of a lunch box next to it.

He looked at it for exactly one second. Then he walked to his own desk and sat down.

*Not my problem.*

He'd been saying it for four days. He'd said it so many times that it had worn smooth, like a stone in a river. It fit perfectly in his hand now. He didn't even have to think about it anymore.

*She said it herself. It's my fault for being seen with her. So the rational thing is to not be seen with her. The rational thing is to stay out of it, let it burn out, and wait. Reika gets bored. Everyone gets bored. It's June in two weeks and nobody will remember.*

*Not my problem.*

Around the room, the class was pretending not to look at Rin's desk with the concentrated effort of people who were looking at nothing else.

Hina was standing next to it with her hands clenched at her sides and her mouth pressed into a line, as though she wanted to rip the notes off and couldn't make her arms move. Kujou sat by the window with her book open, turning pages. Momo had her phone out. Momo always had her phone out.

Amamiya sat at his desk with his head down and his eyes on his hands.

He hadn't looked up since Kuze came in. He hadn't looked up, Kuze was fairly sure, since Monday.

*Not my problem.*

The sediment was very thick now. It had stopped settling days ago. There was simply no more room for it to fall.

1

Rin came in at 8:24, one minute before the bell.

She'd started doing that on Tuesday: arriving at the last possible moment, leaving at the first. It cut the time she had to spend in the room by nearly twenty minutes a day. Kuze had noticed. He had done the math automatically, the way he did everything, and then hated himself for having done it and for doing nothing with the answer.

She stopped in the doorway.

She looked at her desk.

For a second, nothing moved on her face. The gray eyes stayed flat and level, her chin up, her back straight, every line of her the girl who had walked through hallways while the crowd parted around her.

Then her fingers tightened on the strap of her shinai bag until the knuckles went white, and Kuze saw what was underneath.

"Oh~! Tachibana-san's here~!"

Reika Hoshino rose from her seat in the second row like someone taking a stage.

She was pretty in a way that took effort, glossy and laminated: brown hair curled at the ends every morning, lashes that had cost money, and a skirt shortened exactly one centimeter past what the discipline committee would tolerate. She wasn't the most popular girl in 2-3. She was something more dangerous. She was the one who decided what the popular opinion was.

"Morning, Tachibana-san~ Did you have a good weekend? Oh, wait, I know you did. It was *all over* Chirp~"

"..."

Rin walked to her desk. She didn't answer. She began peeling the sticky notes off one by one, folding each in half before dropping it into her bag, as if she were tidying.

"Aw, don't take them off~ Everyone worked so hard on those~"

"Seriously~ I wrote the lunch box one," said one of the girls at Reika's elbow. "It's cute, right?"

"*Totally* cute~"

"..."

Rin kept peeling.

Kuze kept not looking.

*Rationally,* he thought, and the word was like a handrail in the dark. *Rationally, if I say anything, it proves the post was right. Rationally, if I stand up for her, I'm the "spare" defending his position. Rationally, the best thing I can do for her is nothing.*

*Rationally.*

Reika drifted over to Rin's desk, took the last sticky note before Rin could reach it, and read it out loud in a bright announcer's voice.

"'*Haruto-kun deserves better.*' Hmm~ I think that's true, don't you, Tachibana-san? Everyone thinks it's true."

She turned, holding up the note like a winning lottery ticket.

"Amamiya-kun! Don't you think you deserve better?"

Every head in the room turned toward him.

Amamiya's shoulders jerked. He looked up for the first time, and his face was pale and caught, like an animal on a night road. His eyes went to Reika, then to Rin, and then away, down, back to his hands.

"...I don't really want to get into it," he said quietly.

"..."

Something in Rin's face moved and then stopped moving.

*Amamiya.*

The thought came up out of Kuze's chest before he could catch it, high and desperate, like a hand shooting up in class.

*Amamiya. You walked onto a dojo floor for her once. In your socks. You didn't even know what a point was. You just knew it wasn't fair.*

*This isn't fair. Don't you know that? You know that.*

*So get up. Get up and walk onto the floor.*

*I can't. I'm not you. That's the whole point of you. That's the one thing you have that I don't. So use it. Please.*

*Please.*

Amamiya didn't get up.

He sat with his head bowed and his hands folded on the desk, as though he were praying, or waiting for something to be over.

And Kuze, four rows behind him, sat exactly the same way.

2

It went on through homeroom, which Ogata-sensei cut short for a staff meeting. It went on through first period, in whispers and passed notes, a snort of laughter whenever Rin was called on to read aloud. It went on through the ten-minute break, when Reika's group gathered at the back of the room and talked about Rin in voices pitched precisely loud enough to carry.

"--always acting like she's too good to talk to us--"

"--hates guys, she says, but look at her--"

"--one on the roof and one in the club room, like, how greedy can you *be*--"

By the break after second period, the room had changed. Kuze could feel it. It was the way a room changes before a storm, when the pressure drops and everyone's ears feel strange.

The people who'd said nothing on Monday were laughing on Friday. They weren't laughing loudly, and they weren't laughing because anything was funny. They laughed because laughing was the safest place to stand.

Then Reika turned around, and her eyes landed on him.

"Oh~ Kuze-kun."

"..."

"Kuze-*kun*~" She came down the aisle between the desks, slow, her skirt swinging, her group trailing behind her in formation. "You've been so quiet this week. You're the star of the show, you know? You're in all the photos~"

"..."

"Come on, don't be shy." She perched on the edge of the desk in front of his. "Everyone wants to hear your side. Right, everyone?"

A few voices said *yeah* and *totally*. Most said nothing, and their silence agreed.

"So here's what I think," Reika said, as though laying out a theory in class. "I think you're a nice, quiet guy who got used. Tachibana-san couldn't get Amamiya-kun, so she found someone lonely who'd eat her little lunches and make her feel wanted. That's all you were. A backup. Right?"

"..."

"It's okay! It's not your fault! Nobody's mad at *you!*" Reika leaned in, sweet as syrup. "Just say it. Say she used you. Say she's just a pretty face with nothing inside. Everybody already knows it's true, so you might as well say it and then it's over. Easy~"

"..."

*It would be over.*

That was the terrible part. She was right about that. If he said it, the whole weight of the room would lift off him like a hand. He would be the victim again, poor quiet Kuze, and he could go back to his desk and his black coffee and his ledger. In two weeks, nobody would remember.

*Rationally.*

He looked past Reika's shoulder at Rin.

She was sitting very still. She wasn't looking at him. She was looking at the corner of her desk, where one sticky note had left a faint yellow rectangle behind, and her hands were in her lap, holding each other.

"Come on, Kuze-kun~" Reika began to clap, slowly. "Ad-mit it~"

One of her group picked it up. *Clap. Clap.*

"Ad-mit it~"

Then another. Then a boy at the back, grinning, glad it wasn't him.

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

It spread the way these things spread. It didn't pass from person to person so much as from fear to fear.

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

*She used you.*

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

*She's only a pretty face.*

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

*She's got nothing inside.*

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

*Nobody could actually like her.*

"""Ad-mit it~ Ad-mit it~"""

Students from 2-4 had gathered in the doorway to watch. Someone was filming.

"""AD-MIT IT~ AD-MIT IT~"""

Kuze thought of an omelette the color of a traffic cone at dusk.

He thought of *thanks, for eating it*, so quiet the wind nearly took it.

He thought of a handshake, hard and calloused. *Deal.*

"..."

And somewhere at the very bottom of him, under a month of silt, under every grain of it he had so carefully let fall, something cracked.

It wasn't loud. It was the small, dry sound of ice on a river in March, the first fracture that tells you the whole surface is about to go.

3

"...You're right," Kuze said.

The clapping faltered and then died. The room leaned in.

Reika's face lit up.

"See? *See?* I knew it! Go on, Kuze-kun, tell everyone--"

"She's rude." He stood up. His chair scraped back loud in the sudden quiet. "She's rude, and she's proud, and she hits people when she's embarrassed. She can't cook. She doesn't know the difference between a teaspoon and a tablespoon. She's the most difficult person I have ever met."

Rin's head came up. She was looking at him now. Her eyes were very wide, and there was nothing in them at all.

"You hear that, Tachibana-san?" Reika sang. "Even *he*--"

"So what?"

"...Huh?"

"I said, so what?"

He turned to look at her. He was aware, distantly, that his face was doing something he hadn't told it to do. His mouth had gone wide and strange at the corners. It wasn't a smile, not exactly. It was what happened to a face when everything behind it came loose at once.

"She's rude. So what, Hoshino? What did she do to *you?*"

"W-What did she-- she's been flaunting herself around Amamiya-kun and--"

"And what? Did it hurt you? Did she take something from you? Tell me what she took."

"That's not the--"

"*Tell me what she took.*"

Reika's mouth opened and nothing came out of it.

"You keep accounts, right?" Kuze said. "Everyone in this room does. Who owes who. Who's winning. Who deserves better." He laughed. It came out ugly. "I keep accounts too. I'm *really* good at it. Want to hear mine?"

He looked at the girl on Reika's left.

"Tuesday. Second period. You put gum in her shoe locker. I saw you do it."

The girl went white.

He turned.

"Wednesday. You tripped her on the stairs by the science wing, then said 'oops' in a voice so everybody would know you didn't mean oops."

He turned again, to the boy who had been clapping at the back.

"You. You'd never said a word about her until Thursday. Then you wrote 'kendo gorilla' on a sticky note and laughed about it at lunch for twenty minutes. Did she ever do anything to you? Ever? Or did you just feel how the wind was blowing and want to be sure you were standing downwind of it?"

"I-- I didn't--"

"You did. I counted."

The words kept coming. They came out the way the sediment had gone in, one grain at a time, except now there were too many to count. They weren't settling anymore. They were pouring.

"Monday, eight sticky notes. Tuesday, eleven. Wednesday, fourteen, and one of them was in pen, so it left a mark on the desk, and whoever wrote it was probably proud of that. Thursday, the thing with the milk. Today, Hoshino reads them out loud like a *game show*." He was breathing hard now. "I know every single one of you who did something. I know most of you who laughed. And I know *all* of you who sat there and didn't do anything, because I was one of you. I was the *worst* one."

"..."

The room didn't breathe.

"I'm not even mad at you for doing it," Kuze said. "You're scared. I get scared too. I'm mad that you think it's *justice*. You think if enough of you agree, it stops being cruel and starts being fair. You think if you clap in rhythm, it counts as a *verdict*." He spun on Reika. "That's what you're selling, right, Hoshino? Justice? Let's do justice, then."

He snatched her phone off the desk where she'd set it down, still unlocked and still glowing. The chat was open on the screen, the one with her group, the one where all the planning had happened.

"Hey! Give that--"

"Your messages. Let's post them. Everyone should see what you really think, right? That's fair. That's *justice.* If it's good enough for Rin, it's good enough for you."

"N-No, stop, *stop--*"

"Why? Everyone already knows what you're like. So you might as well say it, and then it's *over*." He held the phone up high, out of reach. His thumb was on the share button. "Easy~"

"*Kuze, stop!*"

A hand caught his wrist.

Amamiya's grip was hard and shaking. He'd crossed the room in the time it took Kuze to say *easy*, and now he stood between Kuze and Reika with his face white and his jaw set.

"That's enough. You're going too far. Give it back."

Kuze looked down at the hand on his wrist.

Then he looked up at Amamiya's face. He looked at it for a long time.

"*Now* you move."

"...What?"

"Four days." Kuze's voice had gone very quiet. Somehow, that was worse than the shouting. "Four days of sticky notes. Four days of her coming in one minute before the bell so she doesn't have to be in the room. You sat there with your head down for all of it. *'I don't really want to get into it.'* And the second Hoshino might get hurt, you're across the room like you've been shot out of a cannon."

"That's-- it's not the same, you can't just--"

"Why not?"

"Because-- because it's *wrong*, Kuze! You can't do that to a girl, it's--"

"And what were they doing to Rin? What was *that?*"

"..."

Amamiya's grip loosened.

"You walked onto the floor in your socks once," Kuze said. "She told me. You didn't even know the rules. You just knew it wasn't fair." His voice cracked, and he let it. "Where were your socks this week, Amamiya?"

Amamiya's hand fell away completely.

He looked at Rin. Rin was looking at him, and whatever he saw in her face made him flinch as though he'd been hit.

Kuze dropped the phone on the desk. Reika snatched it up and clutched it to her chest, shaking, her eyes streaming, mascara running in two thin gray lines.

He found he didn't feel anything about that at all. He supposed that meant something bad about him. He'd think about it later.

He turned to the class. To all of it: the forty faces, the doorway crowd, the phone still filming from 2-4.

"Listen, because I'm only going to say it once. Tachibana didn't use anyone. *I* followed *her*. I went to the roof every day because I wanted to. I ate her awful lunches because I wanted to. She was practicing for someone else the whole time, and she told me so, and I kept going anyway, because I'm pathetic and I had nothing better to do. If you need someone to be disgusting, fine. It's me. It was always me."

It wasn't true. It was very nearly true, and those were the best lies.

"So leave her alone. You want someone to write notes about? Write them about me. I don't care. I've never cared what any of you thought, and you all know it. That's the one thing about me everyone in this room agrees on."

The silence was total.

Then he walked out.

He passed Rin's desk on the way. She was half out of her chair, one hand reaching, her mouth already shaping his name.

He didn't stop. He didn't look at her. He barely moved his lips.

"*Stay.*"

One word. Too quiet for anyone else to hear.

Stay here, in the room. Stay where the story is that I was the pathetic one and you were the girl I followed. Don't come after me, because if you come after me, all of it was for nothing.

Rin froze.

Her hand dropped.

She had heard him. He knew she had, from the way her shoulders came down. And he knew she understood, because she had always understood him faster than anyone had any right to. It had taken her two days and one omelette.

The crowd in the doorway split to let him through the way crowds used to split for her.

"Kuze-kun! Kuze, wait--"

That was a teacher's voice from down the corridor, and footsteps. Then, lower, Ogata-sensei's voice, sharp in a way he'd never heard from her:

"Leave him. Later."

He was already on the stairs.

4

Behind the pool building there was a strip of cracked concrete, a drainage grate, a dead vending machine that nobody had come to take away, and a view of absolutely nothing. It was the loneliest place at Seiryou. Kuze had found it in his first week of first year and had been coming back to it ever since.

He sat with his back against the warm cinderblock wall and his knees pulled up and his arms around them.

"..."

The chlorine smell drifted over the fence. Water slapped somewhere on the other side, rhythmic, a first-year swimming laps during free period.

*What did I just do?*

He'd been rational for four days. Four whole days of being rational, carefully and correctly, and he'd thrown all of it away in four minutes. He had made an enemy of Reika and her entire group. He had made an enemy of everyone who'd clapped, and that was half the class. He had grabbed a girl's phone and made her cry in front of forty people and a camera. He had told Haruto Amamiya, in front of everyone, that he was a coward.

He had made himself the villain of the story. By tomorrow it would be all over Chirp, and it would stay there.

He had gained nothing. There was no rational benefit anywhere in it. By every measure he knew, it was the worst trade he had ever made.

"..."

*No.*

That wasn't it.

He knew it wasn't it. He'd known the moment the ice cracked.

He hadn't done it for any reason he could put in the ledger. He'd done it because Rin had been sitting with her hands holding each other in her lap, looking at a yellow rectangle on her desk, and he hadn't been able to stand it for one more second.

That was all. That was the whole thing.

And he'd wasted four days pretending there was a better reason not to.

He hated it.

He hated that he'd called it rational, when it was only cowardice in a clean shirt. He hated hanging up on her. He hated letting the phone ring out every night and then lying awake doing the math on what she'd say if he picked up. He hated waiting for Amamiya to do it, so that he wouldn't have to. He hated sitting there with his head down exactly like him while he judged him for it. He hated the ledger. He hated keeping score of people as though they were a debt.

He hated that the only way he'd found to help her was to make everyone hate him instead.

He hated that he couldn't think of a better way even now.

He hated himself. All of it. Every grain.

"...Damn it."

It came out small.

Creak.

The gate in the pool fence swung open.

"Yo. Mina."

Daichi stood in the gap in his swim-club jacket, his hair plastered flat and dripping, a towel around his neck, barefoot on the hot concrete. There was a puddle spreading around his feet.

"...You're wet."

"I was in the pool. That's usually how it goes."

He padded over and sat down next to Kuze against the wall, close, shoulder to shoulder, and didn't say anything else for a while. Water ran off his hair and dripped from his chin onto the concrete between them.

"How did you know I'd be here?"

"Mina." Daichi snorted. "Twelve years. You always go somewhere nobody is, when things suck. But you always pick somewhere *I* can find you. Behind the pool. Come on. You might as well have left a map."

"..."

"Also, the swim club group chat."

"...Of course."

"We're like water."

"That's still terrible."

"Two points?"

"...One."

"Harsh." Daichi grinned at the fence. Then the grin went away, and he said, in a completely different voice, "So. You did a thing."

"..."

"Kinda heard it from the pool. Well, not heard. Mitsuki from 2-3 is in the club, and she came down during break and told everybody. She was crying a little. Not in a bad way, I think. She said you were scary." He paused. "She also said you were right."

"..."

Kuze put his forehead down on his knees.

Daichi let him. He didn't say anything for a while. He just sat there, dripping, and the pool slapped on the other side of the fence.

"Hey, Mina."

"..."

"You like her, right?"

"..."

"Or you don't. I don't know. Maybe you don't even know." Daichi pulled the towel from around his neck and dropped it over Kuze's head, like a curtain. "Doesn't matter. You cared about her. That's enough of a reason. You don't need a better one."

"..."

"You're always looking for the better one. You've been doing it since we were kids. When you wouldn't get in the pool, you didn't say you were scared. You said the water was 'statistically unsanitary.' You were five. You didn't even know what 'statistically' *meant.*"

"..."

"It's always like that with you. You feel something, and you get embarrassed about feeling it, so you build a whole explanation around it. You build a house over it so nobody can see. 'It's rational.' 'It's even.' 'It's not my problem.' And then you live in the house and forget what's under it."

"..."

"But it's still under there, Mina. It always was. That's what came out today. That wasn't somebody else. That was you. The real one. The guy under the house."

"..."

"And yeah, he's kind of scary. Mitsuki's not wrong." Daichi laughed a little. "But he stood up for somebody when nobody else would. That's not nothing. That's not a mistake in the math. That *is* the math. That's the part that counts."

"..."

"You don't have to keep score of everything. You know that, right? Some stuff you just do. Not because it's even. Because it's *her*. Or because it's you. Or because it was wrong and you were there."

"..."

"I'm not saying stop being smart. Be smart. You're smart. It's annoying. But the ledger is supposed to be a tool, Mina. You're not supposed to be living inside it."

"..."

"That's all. I'm done. I'm cold. I'm wet. This concrete is hot and I'm cold anyway. How is that possible?"

"..."

Under the towel, something was happening to Kuze's breathing.

It had started somewhere around *the guy under the house*. It came up from the bottom of him, from the place where the silt had been lying for years, a pressure pushing upward that had nowhere to go. His throat locked around it. His shoulders pulled in.

"Guh..."

He couldn't breathe right. He didn't know how to do this. He hadn't done it since... he couldn't remember. Since before the ledger. Since before he'd decided that everything had to balance.

"...Damn it."

It came out wet.

"Damn it... *damn it...*"

Daichi didn't say anything. He didn't look over. He didn't touch him. He just sat there, shoulder to shoulder, dripping onto the concrete. Being there. The way he'd always been there, on the other side of the water, for twelve years.

Kuze cried.

He didn't know what for. For Rin, maybe, her hands holding each other in her lap. For Amamiya, maybe, who had turned out to be only a boy after all, afraid of the same things as everyone else. For the four days he couldn't take back. For the omelette, of all stupid things, and the way she'd watched him eat it.

For himself, maybe. For the kid at the edge of the pool who wouldn't get in, and had never once admitted that he wanted to.

"...Damn it..."

The towel smelled like chlorine. The pool slapped against its walls. Somewhere across the field, a bell rang for third period, and neither of them moved.

5

The staff room, afterward, was very quiet and very long.

There was Ogata-sensei at her desk, her ponytail coming loose, not saying *teehee* even once. There was the vice principal, who said a great deal. There was Reika, red-eyed and furious and frightened, flanked by two girls from her group, who had very suddenly remembered they were only standing near her. There was Rin, sitting apart, hands in her lap, not looking at anyone. And there was Kuze, with Daichi's towel still around his neck because he'd forgotten to give it back.

And there was Sayo Kujou.

She'd come in without being called, with a folder under her arm, and asked very politely whether she might be of assistance. Before anyone could answer, she laid out on the vice principal's desk a set of screenshots with a precise timeline of four days.

It covered the sticky notes, and the gum in the shoe locker. It covered the stairs by the science wing and the milk. It covered the group chat, whose messages were in it too, though Kuze couldn't imagine how she'd gotten them.

"I believe this covers everything," she said. "Kuze-kun's behavior was of course inappropriate, and I'm sure he regrets taking Hoshino-san's phone. But I think, in context, the school may wish to consider which of these incidents it would prefer to see on Chirp. Someone was filming, after all."

She smiled.

The vice principal looked at the folder for a long time.

In the end, Reika apologized to Rin. It was stiff and furious, with her eyes on the floor, but it was an apology, in front of witnesses, and it was written down. Her two friends apologized too, faster, eager to be finished. Kuze got a warning, a long lecture about "channels", and a note in his file. Rin got nothing, because Rin had done nothing, which Ogata-sensei pointed out three separate times with increasing sharpness.

When they were finally released, the corridor outside was empty. School had ended an hour ago.

Rin walked out first.

She stopped in the corridor, her back to him, and didn't turn around.

"...You said you were pathetic," she said to the wall. "In front of everyone."

"I was."

"You said you followed me."

"I did."

"You *didn't*. *I* made you come to the roof. *I* sat on your desk. You didn't follow anybody. You lied."

"..."

"You said my lunches were awful."

"They were."

"*Not all of them!*"

She spun around. Her eyes were red and furious and wet, and her mouth was shaking.

"The karaage was good! You *said* the karaage was good! On the first day! You said I didn't rush it!"

"...I did say that."

"So you *lied* in there!"

"...Yeah. A little."

"Idiot."

She crossed the corridor in three steps, and he braced for the hit.

It didn't come.

She grabbed the front of his blazer in both fists instead and pressed her forehead against his chest, hard, as though she were trying to push through him and out the other side.

"*Idiot.* You idiot. You *stupid*--"

"..."

"You didn't answer my calls. Four days. I said something awful to you, and I wanted to take it back, and you didn't *answer*."

"..."

"And then you just-- in front of *everyone*--"

She didn't finish. Her shoulders were shaking.

Kuze stood very still with his hands at his sides. He didn't know what to do with them. There was no entry in the ledger for this. There had never been an entry for this.

After a long moment, he lifted one hand and put it on top of her head, carefully, the way you'd put your hand on something you were afraid of breaking.

She didn't hit him.

"...Sorry," he said. "For not answering."

"..."

"I'll answer next time."

"...There better not *be* a next time."

"There won't be."

"Tch."

It was muffled, against his blazer. It was the gentlest *tch* he had ever heard.

6

The next morning, at 8:14 exactly, Kuze walked into Class 2-3.

The room went quiet. He'd expected that. He walked through it to his desk the way you'd walk through cold water, one step and then the next.

He could feel how the room had rearranged itself overnight. Reika's desk was empty; she'd called in sick. Her two friends sat with their heads close together, whispering, and they stopped the instant he passed. The boy from the back who had clapped was suddenly fascinated by his pencil case. Somebody by the window said *that's him* not quite quietly enough.

Hina caught his eye from beside Amamiya's desk. She didn't wave. She didn't shout *Kuze-kun!* across the room. She only pressed her lips together and gave him one small, fierce nod, the kind you give someone across a finish line.

Amamiya didn't look up at all.

Kuze found that he didn't mind any of it as much as he'd expected to. The looks were heavy, but they were only looks. He had spent a year being invisible, and it turned out being visible for the wrong reasons weighed about the same.

There was a lunch box on his desk.

It was wrapped in navy-blue cloth, tied with a bow, and the bow was perfect. Tucked into the knot was a single folded sticky note, yellow, the same kind as all the others.

He unfolded it.

*Tablespoon = 3 teaspoons. I checked. This one's yours. It's not practice. Don't get the wrong idea.*

*-R*

"..."

He looked up.

Across the room, Rin sat at her desk with her chin on her fist, glaring at the blackboard as if it had personally insulted her family. The tips of her ears were bright red.

She didn't look at him once all morning. She was so careful about it that everyone noticed.

At lunch, on the roof, he untied the bow and opened the lid.

The tamagoyaki was the pale, even gold of early sunlight. It was rolled in neat, tight layers, and cut into four perfect slices.

He ate one.

It was just sweet enough.

7

That night, in a room with a view of the whole city, Sayo Kujou sat at a desk made of dark wood and uncapped a fountain pen.

On the paper in front of her was a diagram. She had been adding to it since April.

It showed names, circled in black ink: *Haruto. Rin. Hina. Momo. Reika.* There were arrows between them, some solid and some dotted, notes in her small, perfect handwriting along each one. *Admires. Resents. Afraid of. Using.* One arrow ran from *Momo* to a small box labeled *anon account (Chirp)*, and the arrow was solid.

She had deleted the account herself this afternoon, from Momo's own phone, while Momo watched with a face the color of paper. It had taken a single conversation in an empty music room. Kujou had found it more tedious than satisfying.

She looked at the diagram for a long time.

Then she drew a new arrow.

It began at *Rin* and ended at a name that had been sitting alone in the upper corner of the page, circled in pencil rather than ink, since the day she'd watched a boy count the cans at a vending machine.

*Kuze.*

She paused, the pen above the paper. Then she drew a second arrow, very faint and dotted, from *Kuze* back to *Rin*, and beside it she wrote a single question mark.

"How interesting," she murmured.

She capped the pen and looked out the window at the lights of the city.

"Haruto hasn't noticed yet, has he."

Her reflection smiled back at her from the dark glass.

It wasn't the smile of a lawful wife.

"...Let's see how long it takes him."
