# Anti-Robotic Prose

> **Purpose:** Find and remove the verbal habits that make generated fiction read as machine-written, and replace them with the specific, petty, human texture of a translated light novel.
> **Load when:** Always, during revision of any draft, and before drafting if you have produced flat or "AI-sounding" prose in this session.
> **Related:** [Revision Checklist](revision-checklist.md), [MTL vs. Natural](mtl-vs-natural.md), [Before & After](../examples/before-after.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Style Bible](../core/style-bible.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)

---

## 1. Why "robotic" happens

A language model writing fiction drifts toward the **statistical middle** of all prose it has seen. The middle has five properties, and each one kills the light-novel voice:

| Default drift | What it produces | Why it kills the LN voice |
|---|---|---|
| **Smoothing** | Every sentence is complete, balanced, medium length | The LN voice lives on uneven beats: a two-word line, then a winding one |
| **Abstraction** | "A complex mix of emotions" instead of the hamburger he ordered | The POV narrator's charm is petty, concrete observation |
| **Explanation** | The narrator restates what dialogue just showed | LN readers enjoy inferring; being told twice is condescending |
| **Symmetry** | Tricolons, "not X but Y", mirrored paragraphs | Real overthinking is lopsided: three suspicions and one half-formed doubt |
| **Resolution** | Scenes end with a lesson, a hug, or a summary | Sections here end on a tilt: a reveal, a silence, a sour verdict |

Robotic prose is not bad grammar. It is **prose with no one behind it**. The fix is always the same in spirit: put Minato Kuze, a specific seventeen-year-old with specific grudges, back behind every sentence.

The rest of this file is the concrete hit list, with detection and a fix for each item.

## 2. The banned lexicon

These words and phrases are the loudest tells. Treat the **Banned** column as zero-tolerance in narration. Dialogue may use a few if a *character* would really say them, but they will almost never be right.

### 2.1 AI-ism vocabulary

| Banned | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| delve, delved | Nobody in a classroom delves | "dug into", "went through", or just say what he read |
| tapestry, mosaic, kaleidoscope (of emotions/voices) | Decorative abstraction | Name two actual voices in the room |
| testament to | Essay diction | Cut; show the evidence itself |
| a symphony of, a cacophony of | Pretentious for a teen POV | "Everyone was talking at once." |
| palpable (tension) | Default intensifier | Show one body reacting: "Hina stopped chewing." |
| electric, charged (air/atmosphere) | Cliché | A sound, a stop, a silence line `"..."` |
| unspoken (understanding/agreement) | Tells a subtext instead of making one | Let a wink or a look do it, then have Kuze misread it |
| visceral | Critic word | Physical detail: "His stomach turned over." |
| intricate, nuanced, multifaceted | Review-speak | Specific contradiction: "Kind to cats. Cruel to Momo." |
| resonate, resonated | Therapy/essay | "stuck", "stayed with him", "wouldn't leave" |
| navigate (a situation/feelings) | Corporate | "get through", "deal with", "survive lunch" |
| foster, cultivate (a bond) | HR brochure | "They started eating lunch together. That was all." |
| profound, profoundly | Grandiose | Cut, or make it smaller and truer |
| ethereal, otherworldly (beauty) | Purple | Concrete: "The kind of pretty that made boys suddenly remember homework." |
| myriad, plethora | Thesaurus | "a lot of", a number, or a list of two |
| embark (on a journey) | Brochure | "started", "went" |
| realm, (in the realm of) | Fantasy-ad copy (fine only for literal realms in fantasy) | Cut |
| beacon (of hope) | Cliché | Name what the person actually did |
| enigma, enigmatic | Lazy mystery | Show the behavior that doesn't add up |
| a dance (of glances, words) | Cliché metaphor | Describe who looked away first |
| sentinel, stood sentinel | Purple | "stood there", "leaned against the door" |
| juxtaposition | Critic word | Just place the two things side by side |
| undeniable, undeniably | Empty intensifier | Cut |
| inexplicably | Lazy (the POV explains everything!) | Have Kuze try to explain it and fail |
| seemingly | Hedge | Commit, or turn it into a question: "Was she actually...?" |

### 2.2 Stock-phrase AI tells (narration)

Each of these appears so often in generated fiction that one instance marks the passage.

| Banned phrase | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "let out a breath he didn't know he was holding" | "He exhaled." / "Haa..." as its own line |
| "a smile tugged at the corners of her lips" | "Hina grinned." / "Her mouth twitched." |
| "the ghost of a smile" | "a faint, wry smile" (the LN register allows *wry*), or no smile |
| "something shifted (between them / in his chest)" | Name the shift: "She'd called him 'Kuze.' Not 'you.'" |
| "the weight of (her words / the moment)" | Cut; show him not answering |
| "hung in the air" | `"..."` or "Nobody said anything." |
| "for what felt like an eternity" | "For a few seconds." (under-claim; it's funnier) |
| "time seemed to stop / slow down" | A sound-effect line: *Tick.* Or cut |
| "in that moment" | Cut; it's always "that moment" |
| "little did he know" | Cut; use a cut-away scene to show what he doesn't know |
| "the silence was deafening" | `"..."` |
| "every fiber of his being" | Cut |
| "a wave of (relief/dread) washed over him" | "Relief. Stupid, cheap relief." |
| "his heart hammered / pounded against his ribs" | Rare; prefer "His chest tightened." once per scene max |
| "her eyes sparkled with mischief" | "Momo's eyes did the thing they did before she lied." |
| "a mix of X and Y" (emotions) | Pick the one that's embarrassing to admit |
| "couldn't help but (smile/notice)" | Ration: once per 10,000 words. Otherwise "He smiled." |
| "his voice barely above a whisper" | A sound in its own narration line: *It came out smaller than the air conditioner.* (Never a tag like "she said quietly.") |
| "eyes widened in surprise" | "Eh?" as a line of dialogue |
| "a comfortable silence settled" | Cut, or make it uncomfortable, which is funnier |
| "he found himself (doing)" | "He was (doing)." |
| "it was as if" (more than once per section) | Commit to the comparison or drop it |
| "something about her (made him...)" | Name the something. The POV is an analyst. |
| "a pang of (guilt/jealousy)" | "The sediment stirred." (use the character's leitmotif) |
| "their eyes met, and..." (followed by meaning) | "Their eyes met. She looked away first." |
| "only to find" | Plain sequence: "He opened the door. Empty." |

### 2.3 Stock-phrase AI tells (dialogue)

Teenagers in this genre speak in fragments, jokes, stammers, and deflections. These lines sound like a customer-service agent wrote them:

| Banned in teen dialogue | What a character would actually say |
|---|---|
| "I appreciate you telling me that." | "...Okay." / "Why are you telling me this?" |
| "That means a lot to me." | "S-Shut up. ...Thanks." |
| "I'm here for you." | "You're an idiot, Mina. Drink your coffee." |
| "Let's unpack that." | "Wait, back up. She said *what*?" |
| "I understand how you feel." | "Yeah. That sucks." |
| "It's okay to feel that way." | "You're allowed to be pissed off, you know." |
| "We should talk about this." | "Got a sec? Roof. Now." |
| "I hear you." | Never. |
| "That's valid." | Never (unless a character is being mocked for talking like a counselor). |
| "Thank you for being honest with me." | `"..."` then a nod |
| "What do you mean by that?" (flat) | "Huh?" / "Hah!?" / "Meaning?" |
| "I didn't mean to hurt you." | "I... that came out wrong. Forget it." |

### 2.4 Purple anime-fanfic clichés

These are not AI-specific, but models reproduce them from fan fiction. They clash with the plain, lightly colloquial LN register.

| Banned | Replace with |
|---|---|
| orbs (for eyes) | eyes |
| cerulean, azure, sapphire (eyes) | blue; better, a behavior ("eyes that never quite looked at you") |
| emerald / amethyst / crimson orbs | green / violet / red eyes, once, at introduction |
| raven locks, ebony tresses | long black hair |
| porcelain skin | pale; or skip skin entirely |
| a crimson blush spread like wildfire | "Her face went red all the way to her ears." |
| the bluenette / the blonde / the silver-haired girl (as a name substitute) | Her surname. Always. |
| the older teen / the younger girl (as a name substitute) | Surname or pronoun |
| smirked (every other line) | Ration: once per scene per character, and give it to the characters who'd smirk (Sayo, Momo) |
| chuckled darkly | "Hehehe..." as dialogue, from the one character who laughs like that |
| her voice like tinkling bells | Cut |
| his heart skipped a beat | Once per book, at the moment it matters |

### 2.5 Model-specific tics (found in test runs)

Test runs of this library found that different models carry different signature tics. Each is harmless once. Repeated, it becomes a fingerprint the reader learns to see, and after the third one they stop seeing the characters. **Search for every row. Each has a hard cap.**

| Tic | Example | Cap | Replace with |
|---|---|---|---|
| "the [adjective] [noun] of someone who..." | "with the brisk efficiency of someone triaging a waiting room"; "the specific quiet urgency of someone who had decided..." | **1 per chapter** | A concrete action: *She sorted the side dishes into rows without looking up.* |
| "the specific / the particular [noun]" | "the particular pitch she used when..."; "the specific half inch of a person deciding..." | 1 per chapter | Drop *specific* and *particular*; name the thing plainly |
| "something in [her] [shoulders/chest/face] [eased/loosened/gave/came down]" | "Something in her shoulders eased, half an inch." | 1 per chapter | A visible, countable action: *She let go of the box.* |
| Precise micro-measurements of feeling | "half an inch", "a full second too long", "exactly two degrees warmer" | 1 per chapter | Cut, or use one plain beat: *She looked a moment too long.* |
| "which was true, and which was also..." | "...which was true, and which was also the only compliment that landed." | 0 | Two short sentences: *It was true. It was also the only compliment that ever landed with her.* |
| Mock-legal self-narration | "he would like the record to show"; "for the record" in narration | 0 in narration | Let the POV's deadpan show in a short verdict line |
| "X and Y weren't mutually exclusive" | Used as a callback three times in one chapter | 1 per chapter | Say it once. A callback has to change its meaning, or it's just a tic |
| Echo-verb sentences | "he felt the thrill he felt every time" | 0 | "The same thrill as always." |
| "for once" / "for once in her life" | Several per chapter | 1 per chapter | Cut; the scene already shows it's unusual |
| "which, this being X, ..." | "which, this being Hina, she basically had" | 1 per chapter | A standalone verdict line: *This was Hina. Of course she had.* |
| "the way [someone] does [something]" as a simile engine | "the way audiences watch a man test a parachute"; "the way court clerks read verdicts" | 1 per scene | Keep one per scene, and only when it's funnier than the plain version |
| **Stacked novelty similes** | "looked like a science fair that had been told no" / "held their positions like suspects" / "like a kettle deciding against it" (three within a page) | **1 per scene** | Keep the single best one. Turn the rest into plain action |
| Paragraph-ending wisdom | "Those weren't mutually exclusive either, in his experience." | 0 | End on a tilt (§3.12) |

**Why models do this:** each construction *sounds* observant and literary, so it gets rewarded in isolation. But the LN voice is observant through **content** (the seat position, the crossed-out seven on the price tag), not through **constructions** that announce observation. When you catch one of these, ask what concrete thing the POV actually saw, and write that.

## 3. Structural tells

Word lists catch the loud tells. The quiet ones are **shapes**. Each shape below includes a detection test and a fix.

### 3.1 The tricolon reflex

**Shape:** Everything comes in threes. "She was kind, bright, and unguarded." "He was tired, confused, and alone." Paragraphs end on three-beat lists.

**Detect:** Scan for `X, Y, and Z` endings. More than one per 400 words is a tic.

**Fix:** Break the symmetry. Use two items, or four with the last one petty, or one item stated twice.

> ✗ She was bright, friendly, and beautiful.
>
> ✓ She was bright. She was friendly to everyone, including people she had no reason to be friendly to, which Kuze counted as a flaw.

### 3.2 The "not X, but Y" antithesis

**Shape:** "It wasn't anger. It was something quieter." "Not a threat, but a promise." Models love the pivot because it sounds profound.

**Detect:** Search `It wasn't`, `not ... but`, `wasn't X. It was Y`.

**Fix:** The source voice does use this, but as **quoting-back analysis** of someone's exact words ("Not 'they made up.' *I got them* to make up."). That use is allowed because it dissects dialogue. Cut every antithesis that merely dramatizes the narrator's own feelings.

### 3.3 The emotional summary

**Shape:** After a scene, the narrator tells us what it meant: "He felt a strange mixture of relief and disappointment, as if something important had ended."

**Detect:** Last paragraph of a section contains `felt`, `emotions`, `feelings`, `as if something`.

**Fix:** End on a tilt ([Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md)): a line of dialogue, a sound, an action, a one-word verdict. If the feeling really must be named, name it in the smallest, most embarrassing words: "He was jealous. Obviously."

### 3.4 The explained subtext

**Shape:** Dialogue shows it, and then the narrator explains it.
> ✗ "I-It's not like I made extra or anything!" Rin shoved the bento at him. **She had clearly made it for him but was too embarrassed to admit it.**

**Detect:** A narration sentence immediately after dialogue that starts with `She clearly`, `It was obvious`, `In other words`, `What she meant was`.

**Fix:** Cut the bold sentence. If the POV must comment, make the comment *wrong*, *petty*, or *sideways*, which adds character instead of repeating information:
> "I-It's not like I made extra or anything!"
>
> Rin shoved the bento at him.
>
> The rolled omelet was cut into neat, even slices. Nobody made neat, even slices for leftovers.

### 3.5 Everyone is articulate

**Shape:** All characters speak in complete, grammatical, well-organized sentences. Their feelings come pre-analyzed.

**Detect:** Read only the dialogue. If you can't tell who is speaking without tags, the voices have collapsed.

**Fix:** Apply [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md). Give fragments to the genki girl, stammers to the tsundere, formal cutting sentences to the ojou, and two words to the protagonist. Let someone misunderstand. Let someone answer the wrong question.

### 3.6 The dialogue that answers everything

**Shape:** Every question gets a direct, complete answer. Conversations proceed like a Q&A.

**Fix:** Real conversations deflect. Characters answer with a question, change the subject (Hina does this constantly), pretend not to hear, or reply `"..."`. At least one question per scene should go unanswered.

### 3.7 The balanced hedge

**Shape:** "Maybe he was right, or maybe he was wrong. Perhaps it didn't matter." The narrator refuses to commit.

**Fix:** The LN narrator *does* hedge, but as an **obsessive spiral** with specific hypotheses, not as vague both-sidesing. Compare:
> ✗ Perhaps Amamiya meant well. Perhaps he didn't. It was hard to say.
>
> ✓ Did Amamiya offer because he meant it? Or because Hina was watching? Or, worst of all, because he'd honestly never noticed that Kuze had been standing there the whole time?

### 3.8 The weather-as-mood opening

**Shape:** "The sky was a dull gray, mirroring Kuze's mood."

**Fix:** Open with a sound, a line of dialogue, or a time stamp ([Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md)). If weather appears, let it be flat and literal, with the irony left unstated: "It was cloudy. It had been cloudy all week."

### 3.9 The sensory checklist

**Shape:** Sight, sound, smell, touch, taste, in sequence, in the first paragraph of a scene.

**Fix:** LN description is portrait-first and scenery-light ([Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)). One concrete detail per new location is plenty: "The family restaurant smelled like the drink bar's melon soda."

### 3.10 Stage-direction overload

**Shape:** Every line of dialogue gets a nod, a smile, a sigh, a glance, a shrug.

**Detect:** Count `nodded`, `smiled`, `sighed`, `shrugged`, `glanced`. More than one per four lines of dialogue is too many.

**Fix:** Keep action paragraphs for **signature gestures** (Rin slams her hands on the desk; Sayo raises one finger; Hina crosses her forearms into an X) and for beats where the gesture changes the meaning. Delete the rest. Every surviving gesture goes in **its own paragraph**, never in the same paragraph as the quote (§3.16).

### 3.11 Adverb stacking

**Shape:** "She said softly, gently, almost tenderly."

**Fix:** One adverb at most, and only for volume or speed. Prefer a verb or a punctuation mark: `~` for drawl, `--` for cut-off, `...` for trailing.

### 3.12 The moral ending

**Shape:** A chapter ends with the protagonist learning something: "Maybe, he thought, people weren't so bad after all."

**Fix:** Growth in this genre is **slow and denied**. The protagonist notices a change and immediately rationalizes it away. Even at the confidant scene, he answers the thesis speech with silence and tears, not a lesson.

### 3.13 The em-dash cascade

**Shape:** Em dashes used for asides, emphasis, and rhythm everywhere.

**Detect:** Count dashes (`--` or `—`) that sit *outside* quotation marks. Test runs of this library produced 47 dashes in a single chapter, most of them narrative asides: *She'd left it there -- plain box, string undone -- and...*

**Fix:** In this house style, the dash means **speech cut off or interrupted** (inside quotes), and in narration **only a thought that breaks off** (*Why would Kujou, of all people--*). It is never used for asides, appositives, emphasis, or dramatic pauses in narration. For asides, use a period and a new sentence, a comma, or a new one-line paragraph. Target: almost zero dashes in narration. Scene time stamps ("The next day--") are the one conventional exception if your story uses them; see [Punctuation & Typography](../core/punctuation-and-typography.md).

> ✗ Her bento was plain -- rice balls, nothing else -- and she'd pushed it behind everyone else's.
>
> ✓ Her bento was plain. Rice balls, nothing else.
>
> She'd pushed it behind everyone else's.

### 3.14 The colon reveal and "Here's the thing"

**Shape:** "There was only one explanation: he was jealous." "And honestly? He didn't care." "Here's the thing about Amamiya."

**Fix:** These are blog-post rhythms. Replace them with the LN's question-then-verdict:
> Why did it bother him so much?
>
> ...Jealousy. Obviously.

### 3.15 Name overuse and name avoidance

**Shape:** Either every sentence repeats "Kuze" (a machine-translation habit) or the text avoids names with epithets ("the boy", "the brunette").

**Fix:** Surname at the start of each paragraph where the subject changes, and pronouns inside the paragraph. Never use epithets as name substitutes. See [MTL vs. Natural](mtl-vs-natural.md#33-over-literal-subject-repetition).

### 3.16 Tagged and beat-wrapped dialogue

**Shape:** Speech shares a paragraph with narration: `"It's fine," she said.` / `"Well," she paused, "maybe."` / `Rin crossed her arms. "Whatever."` / `"Okay." Something in her shoulders eased.` This is the default of Western fiction, and it is the pattern the user of this library explicitly rejected.

**Detect:** Every paragraph that contains a quotation mark *and* any word outside the quotation marks. Test runs produced 69 and 100 such paragraphs in single 4-6k-word chapters. The target is **zero**. The optional script [`tools/voice_metrics.py`](../tools/voice_metrics.py) counts them as "mixed paragraphs".

**Fix:** Split every one. The quote stands alone. The action goes in the paragraph before or after it. Tags ("said", "asked", "whispered") are deleted outright, and if the speaker becomes unclear, sharpen the line's voice or add an action paragraph. Full rule and examples: [Dialogue Mechanics §2](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md#2-the-pure-dialogue-line-hard-rule).

> ✗ "It's just rice," Rin said, fast. "It's really nothing special."
>
> ✓ "It's just rice. It's really nothing special."
>
> It came out too fast, the words tripping over each other.

### 3.17 Length padding

**Shape:** A draft is short of its word target, so the writer inflates sentences: longer reflection, restated feelings, more similes, more *which* clauses. Median sentence length climbs from 7 to 15 words, and dialogue drops toward 30%.

**Detect:** The draft hits the word count, but the narrative median is over 10 words, or more than 5% of narrative sentences run to 25 words or more.

**Fix:** Length comes from **beats and scenes**, never from longer sentences. Add a new arrival, a second escalation, a quiet two-person moment, a cut-away, or a callback. See [Revision Checklist](revision-checklist.md) (the length procedure) and [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md).

## 4. Therapy-speak and modern-internet register

Models trained heavily on self-help and social media give teenagers in 2000s-style Japanese high schools the vocabulary of a counseling session. It destroys the setting.

**Banned in narration and in most dialogue:** boundaries, valid, validate, process (emotions), hold space, healing journey, trauma (as casual word), toxic, gaslighting, red flag, self-care, vulnerable/vulnerability, "show up for", "do the work", "safe space", "emotional labor", "triggered" (except mockingly), "main character energy", "it's giving", "slay", "rent-free", "unalive", "the ick".

**Why:** The genre's emotional grammar is *indirect*. People show care by buying the wrong canned coffee, by lying about having extra bento, by standing next to someone behind the gym shed without looking at him. A character who names feelings in clinical vocabulary removes all the subtext the reader came for.

**The exception:** A character whose tic is sounding like a self-help book, used for comedy, and noticed by the POV: "Momo said 'energy' again. That was the fourth time today."

**Fix pattern:** Translate every clinical phrase into (a) an action, (b) a deflection, or (c) a line that says the opposite of what's meant.

> ✗ "I think you need to set boundaries with Amamiya. His behavior isn't healthy for you."
>
> ✓ "Stop letting Amamiya drag you around, Mina. You look like a dog on a leash. A really grumpy dog."

## 5. Over-explaining: the single biggest tell

Over-explaining deserves its own section because it is the most common failure and the hardest to see in your own draft.

### 5.1 The four forms

1. **Restating dialogue.** A narration line that says what the dialogue said.
2. **Labeling emotion after showing it.** "Her hands trembled. She was nervous."
3. **Explaining the joke.** "It was a joke, of course, but it stung."
4. **Pre-explaining the scene.** "What happened next would change everything."

### 5.2 The difference from the LN analytical monologue

The LN voice *is* explanatory. Kuze analyzes everything. So what's the difference?

| Over-explaining (robotic) | Analyzing (LN voice) |
|---|---|
| Explains what the reader already saw | Notices what the reader *missed*: a word choice, a seat position, who got offered the drink |
| Is neutral and correct | Is suspicious, biased, and possibly wrong |
| Settles the question | Opens more questions |
| Could be written by any narrator | Could only be written by Kuze |

**Test:** Delete the explanatory sentence. If the reader loses nothing, it was over-explaining. If the reader loses *Kuze's twisted reading*, it was the voice. Keep it.

### 5.3 Worked example

> ✗ "You came with Hina?" Amamiya asked. He seemed surprised that Kuze had arrived with Hina, and Kuze could tell he was a little jealous. Kuze felt uncomfortable.

> ✓ "You came with Hina?"
>
> Not *What are you doing here?* Not *Done with class duty?*
>
> *You came with Hina?*
>
> Kuze turned the phrasing over. It was a small thing. It was always a small thing, with Amamiya.

The second version never states "jealous". It makes the reader suspect it, through Kuze, who might be projecting.

## 6. Detection protocol (run on every draft)

Do this mechanically, before the stylistic read.

1. **Lexicon sweep.** Search the draft for every term in §2.1-§2.4. Delete or replace every hit in narration. Allow at most one hit total in dialogue per chapter, and only if a character's voice justifies it.
2. **Shape sweep.** For each §3 shape, run its detection test. Record counts.
3. **Thresholds (per 1,000 words of prose):**

| Signal | Max per 1,000 words |
|---|---|
| `nodded` + `smiled` + `sighed` + `shrugged` | 3 |
| Tricolon (`X, Y, and Z`) in narration | 2 |
| "It wasn't X. It was Y." (non-quoting) | 0 |
| `felt` + `feeling(s)` + `emotion(s)` | 4 |
| Sentences over 30 words | 2 |
| Paragraphs over 6 sentences | 1 |
| `couldn't help but` | 0.1 (i.e., once per 10k) |
| Similes (`like a`, `as if`, `the way X does`) | 3, and no more than 1 novelty simile per scene |
| Mixed dialogue paragraphs (quote + narration in one paragraph) | **0** |
| Dashes in narration (outside quotes) | ~0 (only a thought breaking off) |
| Narrative sentences of 25+ words | ≤5% of narrative sentences |
| §2.5 model tics (each row) | per the §2.5 caps |

4. **Last-line audit.** Read only the last line of each numbered section. Each must be a tilt (reveal, arrival, reframe, silence, verdict). Any that summarizes gets rewritten.
5. **Dialogue-only read.** Read only the dialogue. Can you identify each speaker? Is at least one question dodged per scene?
6. **Explanation audit.** For every narration sentence that follows dialogue, apply the §5.2 deletion test.

## 7. The humanizing moves

Removing tells is half the job. The other half is **adding the things only a person would write**. Use at least four of these per chapter.

1. **Pettiness.** Kuze notices that Amamiya ordered the strawberry parfait *after* Rin ordered chocolate, "so they could share". He resents a parfait.
2. **Wrong assumptions.** The POV confidently deduces something that later turns out to be false. Plant it plainly and don't flag it.
3. **Specific trivia.** Brand-parody names, the price of the drink bar (280 yen), the exact seat by the window, the fifth-period ethics teacher's habit of saying "Well, well."
4. **Bodily banality.** His coffee is too hot. His collar is still creased from where Rin grabbed it. He's hungry during a serious conversation.
5. **Unfinished thoughts.** "If she liked Amamiya that much, then why--" and the thought stops because he doesn't want to finish it.
6. **Jokes that fail in-world.** Daichi's pun gets graded "Two points."
7. **Self-contradiction.** He says he doesn't care, then checks her Chirp account twice.
8. **Non-sequitur honesty.** In the middle of a tense scene: "The hamburger was good, which annoyed him."
9. **Callbacks.** A detail from four chapters ago returns with new weight.
10. **Under-reaction.** Rin's dramatic declaration gets "I'll try." The gap between the energy levels is the comedy.
11. **Misplaced priorities.** He worries about what to order while the girl across from him is having a crisis, and then feels guilty about it.
12. **Private rules.** Kuze's ledger of debts: "He owed Hina one canned coffee. He'd pay it tomorrow, so it wouldn't turn into anything."

## 8. Allowed repetition vs. robotic repetition

The source voice repeats itself on purpose, and a model's anti-repetition training may "fix" exactly the thing that makes it work. Know the difference.

| Allowed (voice) | Robotic (fix it) |
|---|---|
| **Leitmotif**: "the sediment" returns across chapters, growing | The same simile reused because the model forgot it used it |
| **Anaphoric spiral** at an emotional peak ("He hated... He hated... He hated...") | Three paragraphs in a row that open with "Kuze" |
| **Quoting back** a phrase to dissect it | Restating dialogue in narration |
| **Running gags** (Hina's "no polite language!") | The same gesture (nod, smile) on every line |
| **Chants** in triple quotes at a bullying climax | Repeated sentence frames ("She was X. She was Y. She was Z.") in calm scenes |

Rule of thumb: **repetition is voice when it escalates, and a tic when it's flat.**

## 9. Worked full-paragraph repair

**Robotic draft:**
> The classroom was filled with the warm glow of the afternoon sun, casting long shadows across the desks. Kuze sat quietly, watching as Amamiya laughed with the girls. A complex mix of envy and curiosity stirred within him. He couldn't help but wonder what made Amamiya so special. It wasn't just his looks; it was something deeper, an effortless charm that drew people in like moths to a flame. Kuze let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. Perhaps, he thought, he would never understand.

**Tells:** sensory opener with light and shadows (§3.8, §3.9); "a complex mix of" (§2.2); "couldn't help but" (§2.2); "It wasn't just... it was something deeper" (§3.2); moths-to-a-flame cliché; the breath (§2.2); a moral-ish ending (§3.12); no dialogue, no specificity, no pettiness.

**Repaired:**
> After school. Class 2-3.
>
> "Haruto, you forgot your notebook again!"
>
> "Whoa, whoa, I didn't forget it. I *entrusted* it to you, Hina."
>
> "That's the same thing~!"
>
> Four girls, one boy, and one desk that wasn't even Amamiya's, which he was sitting on anyway.
>
> Kuze wiped the blackboard and watched them in the reflection of the window.
>
> Why him?
>
> His grades were average. His jokes were average; *entrusted* had gotten a laugh it did not deserve. He wasn't tall. He wasn't rich. He was, as far as Kuze could tell, a perfectly ordinary second-year who happened to live at the center of a small, loud solar system.
>
> So what was the difference?
>
> The eraser squeaked.
>
> ...Maybe the difference was that Amamiya sat on other people's desks, and Kuze wiped other people's blackboards.
>
> He decided not to think about that one too hard.

**Why it works:** It opens with a time stamp and dialogue. The analysis is concrete (*entrusted* got a laugh it didn't deserve). The rhythm runs from short to long to short. There's a sound-effect line. The ending is a petty, self-deprecating verdict that he immediately flinches from, which is a tilt, not a lesson.

## 11. Genre-specific tells

Each genre has its own generated-fiction clichés. The genre files carry full checklists ([Genre Index](../genres/genre-index.md)). These are the most common tells:

| Genre | Robotic tell | Human fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fantasy / isekai | "An ancient power stirred within him." | Give it a stupid name the narrator mocks: "a skill called [Moderate Luck], which sounded like an insult" |
| Fantasy / isekai | Status windows full of numbers nobody reads | Show one number, and the narrator's reaction to it |
| Action | "He moved with blinding speed." | Beat-by-beat: "Step. Feint. The shinai snapped against his wrist. *Ow.*" |
| Rom-com | "Their hands brushed, sending sparks through them." | "Their fingers touched on the same eraser. Both let go. The eraser fell. Nobody picked it up for five seconds." |
| Harem | Every heroine described as "beautiful" | Each heroine gets one specific, *unflattering-but-endearing* detail |
| Drama | "Tears streamed down her face as she sobbed uncontrollably." | "Rin didn't cry. She just kept wiping the same spot on her desk." |
| Mystery | "Something didn't add up." | List exactly what doesn't add up, like a ledger |
| Villainess | "She felt the weight of her noble lineage." | "Three hundred years of ancestors, and not one of them had left instructions for this." |
| Romance | "Their love was unlike anything either had known." | A specific, shared, slightly embarrassing habit |

## 12. One-line summary

**Robotic prose explains, balances, summarizes, and decorates. LN prose notices, suspects, stammers, and cuts off.** When in doubt, ask: *what would Kuze notice here that nobody else would, and what petty thing would he think about it?* Write that.
