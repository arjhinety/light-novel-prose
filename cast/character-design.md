# Character Design

> **Purpose:** A step-by-step method for turning a role into a specific, memorable, human character: visual signature, verbal tic, contradiction, secret, arc, and place in the relationship web.
> **Load when:** You are creating any named character, or an existing one feels generic, interchangeable, or like a trope with no person inside.
> **Related:** [Archetypes](archetypes.md), [Dere Types](dere-types.md), [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md), [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md), [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md), [Character Sheet template](../templates/character-sheet.md)

---

## 1. The six layers

Build every named character in this order. Each layer depends on the one before it.

| # | Layer | Question | Why it comes here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Role** | What does this person do to the story? | Function first. See [Archetypes](archetypes.md). |
| 2 | **Stereotype** | What familiar shape does the reader recognize? | LN readers read in shorthand, so give them the handle. See [Dere Types](dere-types.md) and [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md). |
| 3 | **Visual signature** | What do we see in two seconds? | The portrait on first appearance. |
| 4 | **Voice** | What do we hear? | A tic, an address pattern, a rhythm. |
| 5 | **Contradiction** | Where does the stereotype crack? | This is what makes her a person. |
| 6 | **Secret and arc** | What is she hiding, and how does she change? | This is what makes her a story. |

A character with only layers 1-4 is a mascot. **Layers 5 and 6 are mandatory for anyone who speaks more than ten lines.**

## 2. Layer 3: Visual signature

The genre is anime-visual. Readers "see" characters in character-design-sheet terms, so give them exactly that.

### 2.1 The portrait formula (first appearance)
Write two to four sentences, in this order:
1. **Hair**: color, length, and *one quirk*.
2. **Eyes / face**: shape and one habitual expression.
3. **Build / bearing**: one physical impression.
4. **Reputation**: what the school says about her.
5. **The crack**: one contrast that complicates the image.

> Her silver-ash hair was cut in a short bob that stopped just above her jaw, and her sharp gray eyes seemed to be permanently deciding whether you were worth the effort. She was small, but she held a bamboo sword like it weighed nothing, and half the boys in the second year were rumored to be scared of her. The other half wanted her to hit them. When she grinned, though, one canine showed, and for a second she looked about twelve.

For a full treatment, see [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md).

### 2.2 Signature gestures (pick two or three, never more)
A gesture is **attribution the reader learns**. It goes in its own narration paragraph right before the character speaks, never in the same paragraph as the quote (see [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), pure dialogue lines). After three uses, the gesture alone identifies who is about to speak.

| Character | Gestures |
|---|---|
| Rin Tachibana | Slams both hands on desks; hits Amamiya's shoulder when flustered; flashes a canine |
| Sayo Kujou | Taps a pen against her lips; a slow, one-sided smile; tucks hair behind her ear before cutting remarks |
| Hina Matsuri | Makes an "X" with her forearms; trots rather than walks; raises one finger to her lips |
| Momo Shirasagi | Holds her phone up at chest height; puffs her cheeks; tilts her head with big eyes |
| Kuze | Hand on chin while walking; counts passing cars; turns an empty can over in his hands |
| Amamiya | Claps hands to "settle" things; pats heads; scratches the back of his neck |
| Daichi | Tosses canned drinks without warning; high-fives; sits beside rather than across |

**Rule:** gestures are **exclusive**. If Rin slams desks, nobody else slams desks. When two characters share a gesture, the reader loses a tag.

### 2.3 Color coding
Anime casts are color-coded, and the LN reader expects it. Give each heroine a **distinct hair color**, and consider giving each an object or color motif (Rin's bamboo sword, Hina's scrunchie, Sayo's red pen). Motifs make callbacks easy.

### 2.4 Failure modes
- **The shopping list.** "She had long black hair, blue eyes, a slim figure, pale skin, red lips..." A list with no crack and no attitude is a character creator menu. Always end on the crack.
- **Re-portraiting.** Once the portrait is done, never repeat it in full. Later, use a *single* feature as an emotional signal ("Her gray eyes went flat.").
- **Male gaze drift.** The POV notices attractiveness; that is the genre. With adult characters the gaze can go where it likes — bodies, clothes, weight, skin — as long as the *character* is doing the looking and paying for it. With a school cast, keep appreciation to faces, hair, bearing, and reputation. Either way the portrait ends on the crack. See [Ecchi & Fanservice](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md) for the content rule.

## 3. Layer 4: Voice

### 3.1 The three voice elements
1. **Tic**: a word or sound only she uses ("My." / "Fueee~" / "verily" / "Seriously~").
2. **Address pattern**: what she calls each person. This is plot, so log it. See [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md).
3. **Rhythm**: long formal sentences, short barks, sentences that trail off, or sentences that never end.

| Character | Tic | Calls Kuze | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rin | "Huh!?", "Shut--up!" | "you", later "Kuze" | Short barks, exclamations |
| Sayo | "My.", "Indeed." | "Kuze-kun" | Complete, precise, formal |
| Hina | "Yup!", nicknames | "Kuze-kun", scolds his politeness | Bubbly, run-on, tildes |
| Momo | "Fueee~", "Umyu" | "Kuze-kun~" (to others: flat) | Cutesy, then suddenly clipped |
| Daichi | Bad jokes, "Mina" | "Mina" | Loose, teasing, and serious in long paragraphs |
| Reika | "Seriously~", "Totally~" | "Kuze", "the loser" | Drawling mockery |

### 3.2 The two-voice test
Read five quoted lines aloud with the narration between them covered. If you can't tell who is speaking, one character's voice isn't done. For a deeper bank of lines per archetype, see [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).

## 4. Layer 5: Contradiction

The contradiction is **the crack in the stereotype**. It is the single most important layer for avoiding the "robotic" feel, because tropes in their pure state *are* robotic. They behave predictably.

### 4.1 Types of contradiction

| Type | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface vs. core** | Acts X, is Y | Rin acts fearless and is fragile, so one cruel sentence can sink her for a week |
| **Belief vs. behavior** | Says X, does not-X | Rin says she hates boys, and clings to Amamiya |
| **Strength as weakness** | Her best trait hurts her | Hina's lack of walls means she has no private self to retreat to |
| **Weakness as strength** | Her worst trait saves someone | Rin's loyalty (read as stubbornness) is why she won't apologize to a bully for being seen with Kuze |
| **Public vs. private** | One face at school, another alone | Momo is sweet to Amamiya and throws away the ice cream she queued an hour to photograph |

### 4.2 How to reveal a contradiction
- **Never announce it** ("Rin was actually very fragile inside."). That's the robotic version.
- **Show it in a one-on-one scene** away from the group, ideally in transit (a station platform, a walk home, a train) where the social stage is gone.
- **Let the Observer misread it first.** Kuze sees Rin's silence on the platform and assumes she's sulking. Three lines later she says, "I don't really have friends who are girls, you know," and his whole read collapses.
- **Echo it later** in a group scene where only the Observer (and the reader) knows what it means.

### 4.3 Sample reveal
> The train was late. Rin kicked at a crack in the platform tiles.
> "You're weird, you know."
> "You've mentioned."
> "No, I mean... you're the first guy who didn't look at me like that. Like the other guys do."
> She kicked the tile again.
> "And girls don't really talk to me. At all. So."
> "..."
> So, what? Kuze waited for the rest.
> It didn't come. She just stood there with her hands jammed in her blazer pockets, and her ears had gone red. She had said the most honest thing he'd ever heard her say, and she was waiting to be laughed at.
> The girl who announced she was *plenty popular, thank you very much* didn't have a single friend who was a girl.
> "...The train's here."

## 5. Layer 6: Secret and arc

### 5.1 The secret
Every major character hides one thing. It doesn't have to be dramatic, but it has to *change how earlier scenes read* once it's revealed.

| Character | Secret | What it re-reads |
|---|---|---|
| Kuze | He gets off the train a stop early so no one sees which station he really uses | Every "see you" at the station |
| Rin | She has no friends who are girls | Her "I'm popular" bragging and her violence |
| Sayo | She's been mapping everyone's affections, including toward Kuze | Every piece of advice she gave him |
| Hina | Her openness is a habit from being the "easy" kid in a strict family | Her scolding him for politeness |
| Momo | Her feed is curated, and her life is not | Every "Fueee~" |
| Amamiya | (Kept ambiguous until the finale) | Everything |
| Daichi | He'd noticed Kuze's isolation for years and waited to be asked | The shed scene |

### 5.2 Planting and paying off
1. **Plant** as a small odd detail, with no emphasis (Kuze "got off at the next station").
2. **Water** it once or twice with an odd reaction (someone asks which station, and he changes the subject).
3. **Pay off** with a single flat reveal line, usually as a section ending. See [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md).

### 5.3 The arc
Every major character's arc runs through one **belief** that the story tests:

> **Starts believing** → **Event that challenges it** → **Doubles down** → **Cost of doubling down** → **Breaks** → **New, messier belief**

Kuze: "Rational is safe" → Rin is bullied → "It's not my problem, it's rational to wait" → he watches her break → the outburst → "I acted on feeling, and I'm not sure that was wrong."

Arcs don't have to be positive. A heroine can double down and stay in orbit. That is also an ending. See [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md).

## 6. Public face vs. private face

The genre is built on the difference between who someone is **at school** and who they are **alone**. Design both deliberately.

| Character | Public face (classroom) | Private face (alone / with Kuze / online) |
|---|---|---|
| Rin | Loud, violent, "I'm popular" | Friendless, easily wounded, loyal to a fault |
| Sayo | Elegant, logical, the "lawful wife" | Calculating, amused, lonely in a way she won't name |
| Hina | Everyone's friend | Afraid of being disliked by anyone |
| Momo | Clumsy, cute, "Fueee~" | Flat voice, contempt, validation-hungry |
| Kuze | Terse, cold, "the go-home club guy" | Overthinking, guilty, wanting |
| Amamiya | The sun | ??? (the series' question) |

**Staging tools** for showing the private face:
- **Transit scenes**: walks home, trains, and station platforms.
- **Rooms**: a character alone in her bedroom (for cut-aways).
- **Screens**: social media posts and late-night messages. See [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md).
- **Duty scenes**: class duty and cleaning duty pair two people with nothing to do but talk. See [School Life](../setting/school-life.md).

## 7. The relationship web

### 7.1 Draw it
Before the first chapter, draw (literally, in the story bible) who feels what about whom. Use arrows with labels:

```
Rin ──(loves, loud)──▶ Amamiya
Hina ──(loves, quiet)──▶ Amamiya
Momo ──(wants to be seen with)──▶ Amamiya
Sayo ──("lawful wife", performative?)──▶ Amamiya
Sayo ──(observing, ???)──▶ Kuze
Kuze ──(resents / envies)──▶ Amamiya
Kuze ──(respects, from a distance)──▶ Sayo
Reika ──(despises)──▶ Rin
Daichi ──(protects)──▶ Kuze
Amamiya ──(???)──▶ everyone
```

This mirrors the Mastermind's own diagram, and you can literally show Sayo drawing it in a cut-away. See [Archetypes](archetypes.md#7-the-mastermind).

### 7.2 Rules for the web
- **Every arrow is a potential scene.** If an arrow never gets a scene, delete it or schedule it.
- **Asymmetry drives plot.** A→B strong while B→A is weak is a story. Symmetrical arrows are resolutions.
- **Keep at least one "???" arrow** unresolved for the whole series.
- **Arrows change.** Update the web after each arc. The change in arrows *is* the arc summary.

### 7.3 Triangles to seed
- **The Observer / Foil / heroine triangle**: the core engine.
- **Heroine / heroine / Foil**: the rivalry within the orbit.
- **Heroine / Bully Queen / crowd**: the social triangle.
- **Observer / Confidant / heroine**: the Confidant sees the Observer's feelings before he does.

## 8. Names

### 8.1 Choosing names
- **Surnames do the work in narration**, so make them distinct in sound and first letter (Kuze, Amamiya, Tachibana, Kujou, Matsuri, Shirasagi, Enomoto, Hoshino, Ogata, Iwashita). Avoid two K-surnames among heroines. (Kuze and Kujou is a deliberate near-rhyme between the Observer and the Mastermind. Only do this on purpose.)
- **Given names** are used by intimates, so they should *feel* different from the surname: soft given names for hard-seeming characters (Rin = "dignified/cold" and short, fitting her) or ironic ones.
- **Kanji meaning** is a legitimate LN device. A character can explain her name's characters, and another can use that to needle her. The source does exactly this with a girl's name. Invent your own kanji breakdowns, and keep them plausible.

### 8.2 Nicknames
Nicknames reveal the *speaker* more than the named: Hina calls Rin "Rinrin", Momo calls Amamiya "Haru-kun~", and Daichi calls Kuze "Mina". Log them all in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md).

## 9. Designing minor characters fast

For characters with fewer than ten lines, use the **three-beat mini-design**:
1. **One visual** ("a girl with a braid who always sat by the window").
2. **One behavior** ("she volunteered for everything and finished nothing").
3. **One moment of surprise** (she's the first to talk to Kuze after the incident).

Example: *Kaede Morimoto, a film-team girl with a messy braid, less prejudiced than the rest. She is the first classmate to decide Kuze "isn't actually crazy, just really righteous" and nearly volunteers for the heroine role before someone else takes it.* One scene, one shift in public opinion, done.

## 10. Testing a character

Run these tests on every major character before and during drafting:

| Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| **Swap test** | Swapping her lines with another heroine's is obviously wrong |
| **Silhouette test** | One gesture or tic identifies her without a name |
| **Want test** | She wants something that has nothing to do with romance |
| **Crack test** | There's a scene where she acts against her stereotype |
| **Misread test** | The Observer misjudges her at least once |
| **Secret test** | Her reveal changes how an earlier scene reads |
| **Offstage test** | You know what she did last weekend |
| **Cruelty test** | If she's cruel, she has a reason; if she's kind, it costs her something |

## 11. Common design failures and fixes

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "All the girls sound the same" | Voice layer skipped | Give each a tic, an address pattern, and a rhythm (section 3) |
| "She's just a tsundere" | No contradiction | Add a belief-vs-behavior or strength-as-weakness crack (section 4) |
| "Why would anyone like him?" (the Foil) | One-sided evidence | Give him an unambiguously kind act with no audience, or reveal an audience later |
| "The villain is cartoonish" | No grievance | Give her a real wound the target caused (see [Archetypes](archetypes.md#6-the-social-antagonist-the-bully-queen)) |
| "The protagonist is insufferable" | No self-suspicion | Every judgment gets an "Or was that just...?" (see [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md)) |
| "The twist came from nowhere" | Secret unplanted | Plant, water, pay off (section 5.2) |
| "The cast is too big" | Too many roles | Merge roles and use the chorus (see [Archetypes](archetypes.md#10-the-chorus-classmates-crowds-fans)) |

## 12. Designing boys (not just heroines)

LN casts skew toward heroines, and male side characters often end up as blank props, especially in harem stories. In this voice, the Observer's relationships with boys matter as much as his relationships with girls, because the core question ("why *him*?") is a question about another boy.

- **Give the Foil a male friend**, and let the Observer wonder why he has only one. The source makes this a real clue: a popular boy with almost no male friends is a boy the other boys don't trust.
- **Give the Observer a male world** that isn't romantic: the Confidant, the otaku friend, a rival. Their scenes run on banter and deadpan scoring, not blushing.
- **Fan boys are chorus, not characters,** but one of them can be promoted: the boy who "just wanted her to scold him" turns out to be the only one who stands up later.
- **Male contradiction works the same way.** Daichi's surface is jokes and his core is years of quiet watching. Gon's surface is archaic grandiosity and his core is a bottomless loyalty to his two actual friends.

A useful test: could the Observer have a whole scene with this boy with no girl present and no girl mentioned? If not, the boy is a prop for the romance plot, not a character.

Use the same six layers. Visual signatures for boys should rely more on bearing and habit (hunched shoulders, a lazy stride, a hand always in a pocket) than on hair color, though anime casts still color-code boys too.

## 13. Evolving a character mid-series

Characters in serialized web novels change over hundreds of pages, and readers notice drift. Change must be **visible, motivated, and marked**.

1. **Mark the change with an address shift.** Rin moving from "you" to "Kuze" is her arc in one word. See [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md).
2. **Retire a tic, or bend it.** When Hina stops scolding Kuze for politeness, it's because he stopped being polite. Point the change out once, through another character or the narrator.
3. **Mirror an old scene.** Put the character back in a location from an earlier arc (the same family restaurant, the same menu) and let her behave differently. See [Series Planning](../structure/series-planning.md).
4. **Keep the core.** Rin can become kinder, but she should never become *soft-spoken*. The stereotype is the handle readers hold; bend it, don't swap it.
5. **Update the sheet.** After every arc, revise the [Character Sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md) and the relationship web (section 7) so later chapters don't regress her.

**Drift to avoid:** a character who becomes nicer because the author grew fond of her, with no event causing it. If you can't name the scene that changed her, she hasn't changed. She's broken.

## 14. Worked example: building Momo from zero

1. **Role:** The Mask, who reveals the gap between an online self and a real self.
2. **Stereotype:** Airhead/clumsy ("dojikko"), with a two-faced undertone. See [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md).
3. **Visual:** Pale pink hair in loose twin tails, round eyes that she widens on purpose, a phone always at chest height. The crack: her eyes are the only part of her face that doesn't smile.
4. **Voice:** "Fueee~", "Umyu~", "Haru-kun~" to Amamiya. To other girls, she's flat and clipped: "What."
5. **Contradiction:** Public vs. private. She is adorable around the Foil and contemptuous around the girls.
6. **Secret / arc:** Her feed is a performance built on a lonely home life. Arc: exposed by Sayo, she doubles down and turns the class against Sayo, and then *one* post near the finale is unfiltered.
7. **Web:** Momo → Amamiya ("wants to be seen with"), Momo → Sayo (hostile), Momo → Kuze (doesn't see him at all, which is exactly why he can see her).
8. **First appearance (the cold open, collision scene):**
> "Fu... fueee! I'm gonna be late!"
> A girl with toast in her mouth came flying around the corner, and Amamiya caught her by the elbows as if he'd been standing there waiting.
> "Momo? Why're you in uniform? It's Saturday."
> "It's *Saturday!?*"
> She covered her mouth with both hands, cheeks flaming, the picture of a girl who had just discovered what day it was.
> Behind Amamiya's back, where he couldn't see it, she smiled a small smile that had nothing clumsy about it at all.

Notice what this example does *not* do: it never tells the reader Momo is two-faced. It shows a public performance in dialogue, then gives one private expression that only the narrator's camera catches. The reader does the rest, and will remember that smile the next time she says "Fueee~".
