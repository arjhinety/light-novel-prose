# Character Tropes: Onee-san, Kouhai, Childhood Friend, Gyaru, Chuuni, and the Rest

> **Purpose:** Catalog the non-dere character stereotypes of light novels and show how to write each one as a person with a contradiction, a voice, and an arc, filtered through the cynical LN narrator.
> **Load when:** You are designing or writing a character defined by a social role or visual trope (onee-san, kouhai, childhood friend, ojou-sama, gyaru, chuunibyou, class rep, transfer student, delinquent, rival, and so on), or when such a character feels like a cardboard cut-out.
> **Related:** [Dere Types](dere-types.md), [Archetypes](archetypes.md), [Character Design](character-design.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md), [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md), [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md), [School Life](../setting/school-life.md), [Harem](../genres/harem.md), [Rom-com & Comedy](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md), [Ecchi & Fanservice](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md), [Character Sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md)

## 1. Tropes vs. dere types vs. roles

Three layers stack on every LN character. Keep them separate in your head:

| Layer | Answers | Examples | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | What does she *do* in the plot? | Harem lead, confidant, bully queen, mastermind | [Archetypes](archetypes.md) |
| **Trope** | What *social slot* does she occupy? | Onee-san, kouhai, childhood friend, gyaru, ojou-sama | This file |
| **Dere type** | How does she *hide or show* affection? | Tsundere, kuudere, yandere | [Dere Types](dere-types.md) |

A good character combines one from each layer, plus **one detail that contradicts the trope**:

- Sayo Kujou = mastermind (role) + ojou-sama (trope) + kuudere mask over sadodere (dere) + *she secretly reads cheap shoujo manga to learn how normal girls talk* (contradiction).
- Daichi Enomoto = confidant (role) + childhood friend (trope) + deredere (dere, male) + *he's the most popular boy in the humanities class and never mentions it* (contradiction).

Tropes are **reader shorthand**. The moment you say "gyaru", the reader pictures her. Use that to skip exposition, then spend your words on the contradiction. The trope gets her in the door; the contradiction makes her stay.

### 1.1 Universal trope rules

1. **Introduce with the trope and complicate within three scenes.** See [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md) on "the crack" in every portrait.
2. **Never let the narrator name the trope straight.** He can name it *ironically* ("the gyaru, apparently, because apparently that was a thing people still were"), but only once.
3. **The trope should create friction with the POV.** A loner POV and a gyaru clash on noise level; a cynic and a chuuni clash on sincerity. Friction is scenes.
4. **Tropes carry honorific implications.** A kouhai says "senpai"; a childhood friend uses the bare given name or a nickname; an ojou-sama says "-san" to everyone. See [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md).
5. **Fanservice-adjacent tropes** (onee-san, gyaru, the "teasing" types) run at full strength with adult characters: attitude, voice, and bodies. With anyone under 18 they stay non-sexual — comedy, attitude, and social energy. See [Ecchi & Fanservice](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md) for the content rule.

## 2. Onee-san

### 2.1 What she is

The **onee-san** ("older sister" type, not necessarily a relative) is an **older woman who is confident, teasing, and a little bit in control of every conversation**. She is amused by younger people's awkwardness and plays with it, often while being genuinely kind underneath.

**Age rule for this skill:** the onee-san is an **adult**: a university student living next door, a café part-timer in her twenties, a young teacher, a cousin home from Tokyo. When the POV is a high schooler, her teasing toward him is social and verbal — no sexual framing, with the comedy coming from her adult ease against his teenage stiffness. When the POV is also an adult (a workplace or university story), the teasing can be physical and the romance explicit. See [Ecchi & Fanservice](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md) and [Romance & Melodrama](../genres/romance-and-melodrama.md).

### 2.2 Core psychology

She enjoys teasing because she has **already survived the awkwardness** the younger characters are drowning in, and she finds it nostalgic, endearing, and funny. The interesting onee-san has a **gap of her own**: she's a mess about her own life (failing her thesis, broke, dumped last month) and teases others because it's easier than looking at herself.

### 2.3 Speech and body

- Drawn-out, amused syllables: "Ara ara~", "Hmm~?", "Ufufu." Use "Ara ara" **once at most** per appearance. It's a spice, not a staple.
- Nicknames that shrink the other person: "Kuze-*kun*" with audible italics, "shounen", "kiddo".
- Questions she already knows the answer to: "Oh? And who's the girl you were walking with~?"
- Relaxed posture: leaning on a railing, a canned drink in hand, sitting on the stairs of the apartment complex, sunglasses pushed up into her hair.
- She ruffles hair. She pokes foreheads. She steals a sip of your drink. **Nothing more physical than that with a minor POV.**

### 2.4 How the cynical POV reads her

Kuze can't read her, and it drives him mad. She sees through him *instantly*, which is his move, so he is on the wrong end of his own trick. His monologue about her is a list of failed counterattacks. Eventually he catches her in her own mess (the thesis, the ex, the unopened letter from her parents), and the dynamic becomes two people who see through each other and are quietly grateful for it.

### 2.5 Sample lines (an invented character, Kaede Mitarai, 21, university student next door)

1. "Ara, Kuze-kun. Coming home at eight? Was it a *date*~?"
2. "You made that face again. The one where you think you're being subtle."
3. "Shounen, when a girl gives you homemade chocolate and says it's 'obligation', you should check whether she made it herself."
4. "Want the rest of my convenience-store oden? No? You're growing, eat."
5. "I'm not teasing you. I'm *mentoring* you. It's different. It's more fun for me."
6. (her gap) "...My thesis advisor said 'this isn't research, it's a diary.' Don't laugh. Okay, you can laugh a little."

### 2.6 Mini-scene

> 3
>
> Mitarai was sitting on the apartment stairs again, a can of highball in one hand and her phone in the other, as though the stairs were her living room and everyone else was just passing through it.
> "Welcome home~"
> "It's not your home."
> "Every stair is my home."
> She tilted the can at him.
> "You're late. And you've got the face."
> "What face."
> "The face where somebody said something nice to you and you've been trying to find the trick in it for two hours."
> Kuze stopped on the third step.
> "..."
> "Ooh. Bullseye."
> She grinned.
> "Was it the ponytail girl? The loud one?"
> "It's none of your business."
> "Everything's my business. I'm unemployed in spirit."
> She patted the stair beside her.
> "Sit. Tell onee-san about the trick you couldn't find."
> He didn't sit. He also didn't go upstairs. He stood there for a while, which Mitarai seemed to count as a victory, and she hummed and scrolled her phone and didn't say anything else until he did.

### 2.7 Arc

Teasing mentor, then her crack revealed, then an exchange in which the POV helps *her* for once, then she moves away or graduates (the onee-san often exits the story, and her absence is its own lesson).

### 2.8 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sexualized teasing toward an underage POV | Out of bounds. Keep it verbal, social, and sisterly. |
| "Ara ara" every line | Once, if at all. Build her voice from specific observations. |
| All-knowing oracle with no life | Give her a mess: thesis, rent, a breakup, a family fight |
| Exists only to explain the heroine's feelings | She should misread things sometimes too |

## 3. Onee-chan (actual big sister)

### 3.1 What she is

A **real older sister**: bossy, protective, embarrassing, and occasionally the only person in the house who notices when the POV is falling apart. In comedy she's a tyrant who steals pudding; in drama she's the person who knocks on the door at 1 a.m.

**This skill treats siblings as family only, never as romantic options.**

### 3.2 Speech and body

- Commands without explanation: "Take out the trash." "Give me your pudding."
- Uses the POV's given name or a childish nickname ("Mina-chan", which he hates).
- Barges in without knocking. Lies on his bed reading his manga. Eats his food.
- When serious: sits on the floor with her back to his door and talks to the wood.

### 3.3 POV read

The narrator's cynicism **stops at the front door**. He's defenseless against her because she knew him at age six. That makes her an excellent pressure valve for the analytical monologue: she cuts through it with one blunt sentence.

### 3.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Nagisa Kuze, 22)

1. "Mina-chan~ You bought pudding. You bought pudding and didn't tell me. That's betrayal."
2. "Your face is doing the thing. Did a girl happen?"
3. "Mom's working late. I made curry. It's bad. Eat it anyway."
4. (at the door, not opening it) "I'm not going to ask. I'm just going to sit here for a bit. My back hurts. It's a good door."

### 3.5 Failure modes

A pure comic tyrant with no care, or an info-dump device. Let her be petty *and* the one who notices.

## 4. Kouhai

### 4.1 What she is

A **junior** (a first-year to a second-year POV) who attaches herself to him, calls him "senpai", and brings energy, admiration, mischief, or all three. Two strong sub-types:

- **Teasing kouhai:** mischievous and provocative, pokes at the senpai's dignity. ("Senpai, you're so lonely it's actually impressive~")
- **Earnest kouhai:** admires him, often for something he doesn't remember doing. ("You helped me at the entrance ceremony! You don't remember? You gave me your safety pin!")

### 4.2 Core psychology

The kouhai sees the POV **from below and from outside**. She doesn't know his reputation in his own class, or doesn't care. She met a different version of him. That's powerful: she's evidence that the POV isn't who he thinks he is.

### 4.3 Speech and body

- "Senpai" constantly, in many tones: sing-song, whining, reverent, accusatory.
- Keigo that slips: polite forms mixed with casual jabs. ("Please don't be so gloomy, senpai. It's contagious.")
- Appearing at his classroom door (a big deal socially, since first-years rarely visit second-year floors; see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md)).
- Tugging his sleeve. Walking one step behind, then half a step ahead.

### 4.4 POV read

Kuze's first theory: *She wants something. Notes. An introduction to Amamiya.* When it turns out she wants *him* around, he has no model for it. The reversal "you're the one person here who doesn't want something from Amamiya" is a great arc tilt.

### 4.5 Sample lines (an invented character, Koharu Akiyama, first-year)

1. "Senpaaai~ You're eating lunch alone on the stairs again? Tragic. I'll sit here. Out of charity."
2. "You held the door for me in April. You held it for eleven seconds while I dropped everything. Nobody else stopped."
3. "Senpai's face when he's surprised is my favorite face. Do it again."
4. "Is Amamiya-senpai really that great? He called me 'Koyuki' twice."
5. "I'm not following you. We just have the same route. And the same convenience store. And the same bench."
6. (serious) "Senpai, you always look like you're apologizing for being in the room. Stop it. I like the room better with you in it."

### 4.6 Mini-scene

> 2
>
> The stairwell to the roof was supposed to be empty. That was its entire value.
> "Found you~"
> Akiyama's head appeared around the landing, then the rest of her, clutching a melon soda like a trophy.
> "How."
> "Senpai, you have exactly three hiding spots. The library, the vending machines by the gym, and here. I'm a detective."
> She sat two steps below him and looked up.
> "Also I asked the janitor."
> "..."
> "You're making the face. The 'why is this happening to me' face."
> "Why is this happening to me."
> She had the straw in her mouth, and she said it simply.
> "Because you gave me a safety pin. At the entrance ceremony. My skirt button broke and I was about to cry in front of three hundred people, and some gloomy second-year just handed me a safety pin and walked away without saying anything."
> Kuze didn't remember that. He tried, and found nothing at all.
> "That wasn't anything."
> "It was to me."
> She slurped the last of her soda.
> "So now I'm your kouhai. Those are the rules."
> There were a lot of rules lately, Kuze thought. And he hadn't agreed to any of them.

### 4.7 Failure modes

Teasing that's just rude with no warmth underneath. Admiration with no reason (give her the safety pin). The kouhai as a pure mascot: give her own problems, such as a club she's failing at or a classmate who excludes her.

## 5. Senpai

### 5.1 What she is

An **upperclassman** (a third-year to the POV) who is admired, mysterious, a mentor, or a lazy club president. The senpai often sits at the center of a club and represents **the future the POV is afraid of**: graduation, choices, leaving.

Common flavors: the lazy genius club president, the kind-but-distant senpai everyone admires, and the senpai with a secret (illness, a move abroad, failing entrance exams).

### 5.2 Speech and body

- Addresses the POV by surname with no honorific, or "Kuze-kun", in a teacher's tone.
- Speaks in pronouncements, questions, riddles. Uses the club room like her territory.
- Napping on desks, reading with her feet up, making tea at 4 p.m. sharp.

### 5.3 POV read

The narrator respects her and resents being seen through. His monologue treats her statements as puzzles. Her graduation is a built-in emotional climax at the end of the school year (March; see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md)).

### 5.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Yuzuriha Kanzaki, third-year, Literature Club president)

1. "Kuze-kun. Tea. Two sugars. It's your turn. It's been your turn since April."
2. "You read people like books. That's fine. But books don't change when you close them. People do."
3. "I'll be gone in March. Someone needs to water the pothos. I've decided it's you."
4. (the secret) "Exams went badly. Don't tell the others. I'd like them to keep thinking I'm clever for a few more months."

### 5.5 Failure modes

Wise-oracle syndrome. Let her be wrong, lazy, petty, and scared of the future too.

## 6. Childhood Friend (Osananajimi)

### 6.1 What she (or he) is

The person who has known the POV **since before he became who he is now**. The childhood friend is the trope with the **heaviest emotional ammunition** and the most notorious fate in rom-coms: "the childhood friend never wins." The best writers play with that doom.

In the shared cast, the childhood friend is **Daichi Enomoto (male)**, used as the **confidant** rather than as a love interest. This is a strong, underused choice. The childhood friend's power is the thesis speech at the low point ([Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md)).

### 6.2 Core psychology

The childhood friend knows the **old version** of the POV and measures the current one against it. That knowledge is both intimacy and threat: they can say "you weren't always like this" and it lands like no one else's line can.

For a **female** childhood friend in a romance: her tragedy is **proximity mistaken for permanence**. She assumed there would always be time. The new girl, the transfer student, has none of that assumption and moves faster.

### 6.3 Speech and body

- Bare given name or childhood nickname ("Mina"), and the POV uses hers.
- Shorthand: half-sentences only they understand. ("The thing with the frogs." "Don't.")
- Easy physical familiarity: sitting on his bed, stealing his fries, flicking his forehead.
- Knowing his hiding spots, his coffee order, what he does when he's upset.

### 6.4 POV read

The narrator's defenses **don't work** on the childhood friend, and he knows it. His monologue about Daichi is short and unguarded: *Daichi always found him. It was annoying. It was the least annoying thing in his life.*

### 6.5 Sample lines

Daichi (male confidant):
1. "Yo, Mina. Canned coffee. Black. Don't say thanks, it's weird."
2. "You hide out alone when things suck, but you hate being alone. Tricky guy."
3. "How long have we been friends? Right. So don't lie to me with that face."
4. (thesis) "You're not a calculator, Mina. You're allowed to want stuff. That's not a bug."

Female childhood friend (an invented character, Aoi Morishita):
1. "You still put ketchup on your tamagoyaki? Disgusting. Give me one."
2. "I've known you since you cried at a goldfish scooping stall. You can't be cool with me."
3. "It's fine. It's always been fine. We have time, right? ...Right?"
4. (too late) "I thought if I stayed next to you long enough, you'd just... notice. That was stupid, huh."

### 6.6 Mini-scene (the female variant's quiet doom)

> 7
>
> Morishita was waiting at the station like she had for ten years, same pillar, same side, earphones in, one sock slightly higher than the other.
> "You're late."
> "Train was late."
> "Train was on time. You were late."
> She pulled one earphone out and held it toward him out of habit. He took it out of habit.
> A song he'd heard a hundred times. She'd played it for him in middle school and never stopped.
> "Hey, Mina."
> "Hm."
> "The transfer student. She walked home with you yesterday, right?"
> "She asked for directions."
> "To your house?"
> "..."
> Morishita laughed. It was the laugh she used when she'd lost a game she'd pretended not to care about.
> "It's fine. We have time."
> The song ended. The next one started. Neither of them moved to change it.

### 6.7 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Loser childhood friend" with no agency | Give her a move she makes, even if too late |
| Only defined by history | She has a present: club, friends, ambitions |
| Male confidant as pure plot device | Daichi has his own arc, his own silence, his own loneliness |
| Shared memories dumped in exposition | Reveal them through shorthand and callbacks |

## 7. Ojou-sama

### 7.1 What she is

A **rich young lady**: elegant, formal, often haughty, sometimes naïve about ordinary life. In the shared cast, **Sayo Kujou** is the ojou and the mastermind, a sophisticated variant.

Sub-types: the **haughty ojou** (the "ohoho" laugh, which should be rare and ideally ironic), the **sheltered ojou** (never been to a convenience store), and the **cold ojou** (logic, composure, contempt for inefficiency; Sayo).

### 7.2 Core psychology

She lives inside **a script written by her family**: marry well, never embarrass the name, never want what isn't proper. Her haughtiness is the script talking. Her interesting moments are when she goes **off-script**, eating a cheap cup of ramen with delight, or wanting something unapproved.

For Sayo specifically: she's learned that the script can be *used*. If everyone sees an elegant, harmless lady, no one sees her moving pieces.

### 7.3 Speech and body

- Formal, complete sentences. "Indeed." "My." "How vulgar." "I suppose I shall permit it."
- Calls everyone by surname with "-san", including friends. Dropping it is a *massive* event.
- Posture: straight-backed, hands folded, walking as if on a runway. Tea held correctly even when it's a juice box.
- Cutting honesty delivered politely: "Your logic is charming. It's also wrong."

### 7.4 POV read

Kuze admires her because she once helped him indirectly, the way the source novel's narrator respects a sharp-tongued lady from afar. That admiration **blinds** him to her manipulation. His slow realization that the most "honest" person in the cast is the biggest liar is a series-level payoff (see [Series Planning](../structure/series-planning.md)).

### 7.5 Sample lines (Sayo)

1. "My. Accepting chocolate from another girl? Haruto, you have some nerve. As your lawful wife, I'm obliged to discipline you."
2. "Kuze-kun. You looked at me as if I were a puzzle. I'm flattered. Most people look at me as if I were a painting."
3. "A family restaurant? How quaint. ...The drink bar is unlimited? Unlimited? Show me."
4. "Emotion is a poor strategist. It's an excellent weapon, though."
5. (alone, to herself, with a pen) "This girl likes Haruto. And this one. And this one. And this boy..." (she draws an arrow, and smiles)

### 7.6 Failure modes

"Ohohoho" as the entire personality. Richness as the entire contradiction. Give her a real interest (competitive shogi, a pet tortoise, cheap shoujo manga) and a specific pressure from home.

## 8. Genki Girl

### 8.1 What she is

**High-energy, cheerful, and loud.** She runs everywhere, ropes people into things, and treats the world as a club activity. Hina Matsuri is genki plus deredere.

### 8.2 Core psychology

Energy can be **pure temperament** or a **performance of okay-ness**. The best genki girls have both: she genuinely loves people, *and* she's never allowed herself to be sad in public, because she decided at age ten that someone in the family had to be the cheerful one.

### 8.3 Speech and body

- Exclamations, invented rules, nicknames, tildes: "Rule three! No walking alone!"
- Speaking in plurals and invitations: "Let's", "everyone", "together".
- Waving with her whole arm. Jumping. The ponytail as a mood meter (see [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)).
- The **tell**: when she's actually upset, she gets *quieter*, but only by a notch. Most people miss it. The analytical POV doesn't.

### 8.4 POV read

Suspicion first (*nobody is this happy*), then irritation, then the day he notices the notch. That moment ("You're fine-but-not-fine today") is often the first time anyone has noticed, and it bonds them.

### 8.5 Sample lines (Hina)

1. "Kuze-kun! Morning! Today's plan: we're all going to be happy! Mandatory!"
2. "Rin-chan, smile! No? Okay, I'll smile for both of us!"
3. "Haruto-kun's the sun, so I figure I'm, like... a streetlight? Still useful!"
4. (notch quieter) "I'm good! I'm... good."

### 8.6 Failure modes

Noise without an inside. A genki girl who is only a mood-lifter for the protagonist. Make her sadness *hers*, not his lesson.

## 9. Gyaru

### 9.1 What she is

A **fashion-forward, confident, socially fluent girl** with dyed hair, decorated nails, loose-worn uniform, slang-heavy speech, and a phone always in hand. Modern LNs love the "**gyaru who is kind to otaku**" trope: she is outwardly intimidating and inwardly the least judgmental person in the class.

**Rule for this skill:** the gyaru's appeal is **social confidence and warmth** first. With school-age characters the description stays on fashion — hair color, accessories, nail art, how she wears the uniform — and on her face and attitude. An adult gyaru gets the full treatment, body included, at whatever heat the register calls for; see the [content rule](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md#1-the-content-line).

### 9.2 Core psychology

She chose her look as **self-authorship**: she decided who she'd be instead of being assigned it. That makes her naturally allergic to judgment of others, since she's been judged plenty ("easy", "dumb", "loud") by teachers and parents. Beneath the confidence: grades that are bad because of a part-time job supporting her family, or a talent (drawing, math, sewing) she hides because it's "not gyaru".

### 9.3 Speech and body

- Slang, abbreviations, intensifiers: "Legit", "no cap", "that's so cringe", "super", "like, for real". Adapt to contemporary English carefully and don't overload it.
- Nicknames for everyone within five minutes: "Kuzecchi", "Kuze-P".
- Direct, unfiltered compliments: "Your handwriting's actually so pretty, that's unfair."
- Sitting on desks. Phone held sideways for photos. Leaning over to see what you're reading.

### 9.4 POV read

Kuze's cynicism predicts contempt. He expects her to mock his book, his lunch, his existence. Instead she asks what the book is about and *listens*. His monologue short-circuits. *He'd prepared a whole defense for an attack that didn't come.* That anticlimax is comedy and tenderness at once.

### 9.5 Sample lines (an invented character, Ruka Shinjo)

1. "Kuzecchi, what're you reading? ...Wait, the one with the lighthouse? I cried at that! Legit cried!"
2. "Why is everyone scared of me? I'm literally so nice. I'm nice like ninety percent of the time."
3. "Your ears go red when you're embarrassed. Cute. Don't hide them."
4. "Grades? Ugh. I work at the karaoke place four nights a week, so. Priorities."
5. "People who laugh at other people's hobbies are the actual lamest. Like, the lamest."
6. (hidden talent) "...Don't tell anyone I did the math homework for the whole group chat. It'd ruin my brand."

### 9.6 Mini-scene

> 4
>
> Shinjo sat on the desk in front of his, which was not her desk, and not her row, and not really how desks were meant to be used.
> Her nails were pale green today, each one with a tiny white star.
> "Kuzecchi."
> Kuze kept his eyes on his book. He had prepared for this. She would ask what he was reading, laugh, show her friends, and the group chat would have a new joke by lunch.
> "What're you reading?"
> There it was.
> He held up the cover without a word. Let her laugh.
> Shinjo leaned in, squinted, and went very still.
> "Wait. *Wait.* Is that the one where the lighthouse keeper writes letters to his dead brother?"
> "..."
> "I cried so hard at that. On the train. A salaryman gave me a tissue."
> She pressed a hand to her chest.
> "Volume two, the part with the storm? Legit destroyed me."
> Kuze lowered the book an inch.
> He had prepared a whole defense for an attack that hadn't come, and now he was just a boy holding a book, with nowhere to put any of it.
> "...The storm part was good."
> "*Right?*"

### 9.7 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sexualized description of a school-age gyaru | Out of bounds: fashion, attitude, face, and voice only |
| Slang soup, unreadable | 1-2 slang markers per line, max |
| "Secretly nerdy" as her only depth | Add pressure: work, family, a friend she's losing |
| She fixes the loner POV | She has her own arc; he gives back |

## 10. Chuunibyou

### 10.1 What she (or he) is

"Eighth-grader syndrome": someone who **believes, or performs belief, that they have secret powers, a dark destiny, or a hidden identity**, complete with an eyepatch, a bandaged arm, invented incantations, and a dramatic alias.

### 10.2 Core psychology

Chuunibyou is **a fantasy built to escape something**: a boring life, a painful loss, loneliness. The comedy is the performance; the heart is what it protects. The most famous emotional move is the reveal that the "sealed power" is a way of not accepting that someone has died or left.

### 10.3 Speech and body

- Grandiose self-titles: "I am the Wielder of the Azure Eclipse."
- Invented vocabulary, capitalized: "the Organization", "the Contract", "my Dark Flame".
- Poses (one hand over the eye, a coat flared). Refusing to drop character in public.
- **The drop:** when she breaks character, she sounds very young and very normal. Use the drop sparingly and precisely.

### 10.4 POV read

Kuze finds it cringe-inducing at first, then notices the **consistency**: she never breaks, even when mocked. *Commitment like that costs something. What was she paying for?* The deadpan-cynic and chuuni pairing is a classic tsukkomi/boke engine (see [Rom-com & Comedy](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md)).

### 10.5 Sample lines (an invented character, Kurumi Hoshizora, "the Twilight Oracle")

1. "Mortal. You have entered the domain of the Twilight Oracle. Your lunch is forfeit."
2. "My right eye sees the threads of fate. Your thread is... beige."
3. "Kuze Minato. The Organization has taken notice of you. Try to act natural. ...That was natural? Hm."
4. "Do not touch the bandage. It seals the Dark Flame. Also I scraped it on a vending machine."
5. (the drop) "...My dad said he'd come back when the stars aligned. I checked. They align a lot, actually. He still hasn't."

### 10.6 Failure modes

Mocking her without the heart. Curing her by making her "normal". The best endings let her keep the fantasy *and* face reality at the same time.

## 11. Meganekko / Class Representative

### 11.1 What she is

The **glasses-wearing, responsible, rule-keeping girl**: the class rep (iinchou), the disciplinary committee member, the one who collects homework and scolds boys for running.

### 11.2 Core psychology

Rules are her **safety structure**. If everyone follows them, nothing unexpected hurts anyone. Beneath the strictness: fear of chaos (a chaotic home), a need to be useful to be valued, and exhaustion from being "the responsible one" everyone dumps work on.

### 11.3 Speech and body

- Formal-polite, corrective: "Kuze-kun. The homework was due at 8:30. It's 8:34."
- Pushing her glasses up (**limit to once per scene**; this is the trope's most overused gesture).
- A clipboard, a stack of printouts, a pen tucked behind her ear.
- When flustered: glasses fog, or slip, or she takes them off and becomes briefly, comically helpless.

### 11.4 POV read

Kuze and the class rep share a love of logic, which makes them natural allies and natural rivals. He notices that everyone dumps work on her, which is exactly the asymmetry his monologue feeds on. *Six people had "forgotten" their cleaning duty. Six. And she'd done all of it without a word.*

### 11.5 Sample lines (an invented character, Chitose Iida)

1. "Running in the hallway is prohibited. Yes, Amamiya-kun, even if Tachibana-san is chasing you."
2. "I'm not strict. I'm consistent. It only feels strict because nobody else is."
3. "Could you... possibly... carry half? Only half. I can do half."
4. (glasses off) "I can't see anything. I'm going to trust that you're Kuze-kun. Please don't be a coat rack."
5. (cracking) "Why is it always me? Why do I always have to be the one who remembers?"

### 11.6 Failure modes

Glasses-push every paragraph. Strictness played only as a joke. Let the rules be a response to *something*.

---

## 12. Shy Bookworm / Literary Girl (Bungaku Shoujo)

### 12.1 What she is

A **quiet reader**, often in the library or literature club, who lives more in books than among people. She overlaps heavily with the **dandere** ([Dere Types](dere-types.md#5-dandere)). The trope adds a specific world: books as her language.

### 12.2 Distinctive craft

- She **quotes or references books** to say what she can't say directly. The narrator decodes the reference, and the reader gets a double message.
- Her place (the library, the club room) is her sanctuary; entering it is a relationship beat.
- Recommending a book to someone is her version of a confession.

### 12.3 Sample lines (an invented character, Fumika Sato)

1. "...You're reading that in the wrong order. Book three first. Trust me."
2. "In the novel, she says 'the moon is beautiful.' Do you... know what that means?"
3. "I lend books to people I want to talk to. I've never lent one before."
4. "Real people don't have chapter breaks. It's hard to know when to stop talking."

### 12.4 Failure modes

Literary quotes as filler (keep them original or clearly invented in-world titles). "Quiet girl blossoms when a boy notices her" as the entire arc; let the books give her opinions, arguments, and pride.

## 13. Tomboy

### 13.1 What she is

A girl who is **sporty, blunt, and "one of the guys"**: short hair, a track jacket, eats three rice balls at lunch, punches shoulders, and gets called "bro" by the boys.

### 13.2 Core psychology

The tomboy's arc is traditionally "realizing she's a girl", which is a stale framing. The richer version: she's **completely comfortable being who she is**, and what scares her is that *other people* will stop treating her as a friend if feelings enter the picture. Her fear is losing the friendship, not her femininity.

### 13.3 Speech and body

- Casual, blunt, boy-coded slang: "Dude." "Let's go get ramen." "You're so slow."
- Physical ease: headlocks, sitting cross-legged on desks, stealing food.
- Flustered in unfamiliar situations (a festival yukata that her mother forced on her, being called "cute"), but **not ashamed of her usual self**.

### 13.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Natsuki Oda, track and field)

1. "Kuze! Race you to the station. Loser buys drinks. Go!"
2. "I'm not *mad*. I just didn't expect you to say I looked nice. That's cheating."
3. "If this gets weird, can we still get ramen? Promise me we can still get ramen."

### 13.5 Failure modes

Making her change her style to "win" the guy. Treating her athletic body as an object that belongs to the reader rather than to her. Keep her comfort, and let the romance adapt to *her*.

## 14. Imouto (Little Sister)

### 14.1 What she is

The POV's **younger sister**: cheeky, needy, sharp-tongued, often the one who roasts him at dinner.

**Rule for this skill:** the imouto is **family, not a romance option**. The "little-sister romance" subgenre is excluded. Her role is warmth, comedy, and occasional brutal honesty.

### 14.2 Speech and body

- "Onii-chan" (in affection or sarcasm), "Nii-san" (formal or annoyed), or his name when angry.
- Brutal assessments: "You smell like loneliness and canned coffee."
- Stealing the TV remote, demanding he buy things, borrowing his phone charger forever.
- When he's hurt: awkward, clumsy kindness (leaves pudding outside his door).

### 14.3 POV read

The imouto is the one person whose contempt the narrator **accepts as fair**, which makes her a truth-teller. Her rare worry for him is a strong emotional beat.

### 14.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Sora Kuze, middle-schooler)

1. "Onii-chan, a girl texted you. A *girl*. Are you in a cult? Is it a cult?"
2. "Mom says eat. I say you look like a ghost. We're both right."
3. "I put pudding by your door. It's not for you. It's for the door."
4. "You were weird tonight. Not normal-weird. Bad-weird. ...You can tell me. Or not. I'll be in my room."

## 15. Delinquent with a Heart of Gold (Yankee)

### 15.1 What they are

A **scary-looking** student (bleached hair, rolled-up sleeves, a glare, rumored fights) who is secretly gentle: feeds stray cats, helps old women, gets good grades in home economics.

### 15.2 Core psychology

The delinquent was **misjudged early** (a face, a family, an incident) and decided that if everyone was going to see a monster, they'd at least be left alone. It's reputation as armor, and a cousin of the cynical POV's own defenses.

### 15.3 Speech and body

- Rough speech: "Hah?", "Tch", "What're you looking at?"
- Crouching (the classic yankee squat) by the vending machines or behind the gym.
- The tell: caught doing something kind and getting furious *about being seen*, the same lag as the tsundere.

### 15.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Ryuji Kamata)

1. "Hah? You got a problem?"
2. "The cat's not mine. It just lives here. I just bring it food. Every day. Shut up."
3. "People see my face and decide. So I let 'em."
4. "...You're the first guy who didn't flinch. Weird."

### 15.5 POV read

Kuze is one of the few who **doesn't** judge on appearance. He judges on behavior, and the behavior doesn't match the rumors. The delinquent notices that difference and becomes an unlikely ally.

### 15.6 Mini-scene

> 5
>
> Behind the gym, where the school's ventilation fans hummed and nobody came on purpose, Kamata was crouched in the dirt with his back to the path.
> Kuze had come for the silence. He stopped when he saw the bleached hair.
> Everyone knew about Kamata. He'd broken a third-year's nose in April. Or a window. Or both, depending on who told it.
> "Hah?"
> Kamata turned his head. The glare was as advertised.
> "What're you looking at?"
> Kuze looked. There was a paper plate on the ground in front of Kamata's sneakers, and on the plate were little chunks of grilled fish, carefully deboned, and eating the fish was a very small, very orange cat.
> "..."
> Kamata didn't look up.
> "It's not mine."
> "I didn't say anything."
> "It just lives here. I just bring it food."
> A pause.
> "Every day. Shut up."
> The cat finished the fish and began, with enormous dignity, to wash its face.
> Kuze considered the rumor about the nose. He considered the deboned fish. The two facts refused to sit in the same person, and so one of them was probably wrong, and he had a feeling he knew which.
> He sat down on the step, a polite distance away.
> "What's its name?"
> Kamata stared at him for a long time, as if checking for a trap.
> It took him a long time to answer.
> "...Sergeant. Don't laugh."
> Kuze didn't laugh. Sergeant climbed into Kamata's lap, and Kamata let it, glaring at the fans the whole time, as if daring them to say anything about it.

The scene works because the narrator's **evidence-weighing** (rumor against deboned fish) does the characterization. Nobody explains that Kamata is kind. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md).

## 16. Student Council President

### 16.1 What she is

The **most capable person in school**: elected, respected, feared, organized. Often a kamidere or kuudere ([Dere Types](dere-types.md)).

### 16.2 Craft notes

- Her power is **institutional**: she can summon the POV to the council room, assign him tasks, and pull strings for the cultural festival (see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md) for council functions and election arcs).
- The council room scene is a set piece: the POV summoned, a long table, her at the end with fingers steepled, and something disarmingly petty she wants.
- Her crack: the loneliness of being the one who decides; being liked for her role, not herself.

### 16.3 Sample lines (Tenka Ichinose)

1. "Kuze Minato. You've been summoned. Sit. Tea? Good. I need you to judge a curry contest."
2. "Everyone calls me 'President'. I've forgotten what my name sounds like in someone's mouth."
3. "I have a plan with nine contingencies. None of them cover you. I find that irritating."

## 17. Mysterious Transfer Student

### 17.1 What she is

A **new arrival** who disrupts the established order: beautiful or strange, with an unclear past, and an unexpected connection to the POV (she knew him as a child, she chose the seat next to his, she knows something about him).

### 17.2 Craft notes

- Her entrance is a set piece: homeroom, the teacher writing her name on the board, the class murmuring, and her eyes finding the POV. **Do one twist on it**: she chooses the worst seat, or she ignores the harem lead completely, or she says something that makes no sense until chapter 30.
- She is a **structural tool**: she arrives to shake up a stuck dynamic (see [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md)).
- Her secret must pay off. Seed it with 2-3 concrete clues.

### 17.3 Sample lines (an invented character, Yuki Kirishima)

1. (on the first day, to Kuze) "It's you. You still don't remember, do you?"
2. "I asked for this seat. The one next to the boy who looks like he wants to disappear."
3. "Amamiya-kun? Who is that? Oh. The loud one."

### 17.4 POV read

Paranoia, obviously. *Nobody picks the seat next to him on purpose.* His investigation of her is a natural mystery thread.

## 18. Otaku Friend

### 18.1 What he (or she) is

The POV's **nerdy friend**: speaks in anime references or archaic speech, is passionate about hobbies, is socially awkward, and is fiercely loyal. In the shared cast, this is **Gon Iwashita** ("verily", "-dono").

### 18.2 Craft notes

- **Comic voice with dignity.** He's funny because of his speech patterns and enthusiasm, never because he's pathetic.
- He's often the **first person to stand with the POV** when the class turns on him. That loyalty, from the "lowest" person in the hierarchy, is quietly powerful.
- Pair with a straight-man foil (another friend who says "What's with 'verily'?").

### 18.3 Sample lines (Gon)

1. "Kuze-dono! The new season of *Magical Tax Accountant Rina* airs tonight. Verily, we must watch in real time."
2. "Such a predicament. Our film lacks a heroine. Perhaps... I could don a wig."
3. "They mock you? Then they mock me also. It is the code, verily."
4. (serious, no archaic speech) "Hey. For real. You okay?"

The **drop out of the tic** into plain speech is the most powerful thing this character can do. Use it once or twice per story.

## 19. Oblivious Harem Lead

### 19.1 What he is

The boy **every girl likes**, who seems not to notice. In the shared cast, **Haruto Amamiya**. His full *story role* is in [Archetypes](archetypes.md). This section covers his *trope surface* as seen by a cynical POV.

### 19.2 Surface markers

- "Whoa, whoa", "Got it, got it", "Can't just ignore someone in trouble, right?"
- Headpats. Dramatic rescues. Speeches about friendship.
- The "Huh? Did you say something?" (the classic selective deafness). **Use it once in a whole story, and have the narrator notice it.**
- Surrounded; never alone with one girl for long.

### 19.3 The cynical re-read (the source novel's great move)

The POV refuses to accept obliviousness at face value. He treats it as a **strategy**: *He isn't dense. He can't afford to notice. The moment he chooses, the whole arrangement collapses.* Whether that reading is correct should stay ambiguous for a long time. The harem lead might be sincere, or manipulative, or sincere *and* self-serving without knowing it. That ambiguity is the most interesting thing about him.

### 19.4 Sample lines (Haruto)

1. "Whoa, whoa, calm down, Rin! Kuze, sorry, she's always like this."
2. "Let's all go eat together! Right? Right, Kuze?"
3. "I just can't leave someone in trouble alone, you know?"
4. (a crack) "...Do you think I don't know? Every day I'm scared someone's going to make me choose."

See [Harem](../genres/harem.md) for managing his likability.

## 20. Rival

### 20.1 What they are

A character who **competes with the POV**: for a girl, for rank in exams, for a club position, for the truth. In the source's genre, the rival is often the harem lead himself, seen through the POV's resentment.

### 20.2 Craft notes

- A rival must be **genuinely good at something the POV wants**. Otherwise there's no tension.
- The best rival relationships include **grudging respect**: a moment when each sees the other clearly.
- Rival types: the **mirror** (same flaws, different choices), the **opposite** (Kuze's logic vs. Amamiya's sincerity), the **former friend**.

### 20.3 Sample lines (an invented character, Sousei Takamura, the exam rival)

1. "Second place again, Kuze. Comfortable down there?"
2. "You write like you're scared someone will read it. That's why you lose."
3. "...Your answer on question six was better than mine. Don't make me say it twice."

## 21. Best Friend's Girl / The Unattainable One

### 21.1 What she is

The girl the POV **can't** pursue, because she likes (or dates) someone he's loyal to, or because pursuing her would break something. The school idol type often lands here: Mizushiro-type "top beauty" who likes the harem lead.

### 21.2 Craft notes

- She generates **clean longing**. The POV's feelings are real and never acted on. The monologue is where the romance happens.
- Her kindness to the POV is **sincere and hurts more for it**.
- The strongest version: she knows, and chooses not to acknowledge it, out of kindness. One scene where both of them know and neither says it.

### 21.3 Sample lines (Hina, in this role)

1. "You're my friend, Kuze-kun. One of my best ones. That's... that's a lot, okay?"
2. "Don't look at me like that. It makes me want to apologize and I don't know what for."

## 22. Male-side tropes (quick reference)

For otome, reverse harem, BL, or a female POV:

| Trope | Gist | Voice marker | Contradiction to add |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prince type (ouji)** | Perfect, polite, admired | "Are you alright, princess?" | Exhausted by performing perfection |
| **Bad boy** | Rude, dangerous, alluring | "Tch. Get lost." | Takes care of younger siblings |
| **Gentle senpai** | Soft-spoken upperclassman | "Take your time." | Is hiding a deadline (a move abroad, illness) |
| **Genki kouhai (male)** | Puppy energy, "Senpai!!" | Exclamation spam | Deeply insecure about being "just cute" |
| **Stoic swordsman / kuudere boy** | Silent, capable | "..." / "Hn." | Loves sweets or small animals |
| **Glasses strategist** | Calm, calculating | "As expected." | Terrible at feelings, great at plans |
| **Childhood-friend boy** | Always there | Nickname, shorthand | Assumed there'd be time |
| **Playboy** | Flirts with everyone | "Hey, cutie~" | Has never actually been chosen by anyone |
| **Older mentor (onii-san)** | Adult, teasing, kind | "Kid." | A mess about his own life |

The same rule applies to boys as to girls: the trope is the door, the contradiction is the room.

## 23. Assembling a cast from tropes

### 23.1 Contrast rules

A cast works when its tropes create **contrast in energy, speech, and social slot**. Check your heroine group against these axes:

| Axis | Low end | High end |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Dandere, bookworm | Genki, gyaru |
| Formality | Gyaru, tomboy | Ojou, class rep |
| Honesty | Performed bakadere, mastermind ojou | Deredere, kuudere |
| Social power | Otaku, shy transfer | Council president, bully queen |
| Age/position | Kouhai | Senpai, onee-san |

If two heroines sit in the same spot on three or more axes, merge them or push one away. The source novel's five-girl entourage works because each occupies a clearly different slot: sporty-genki, elegant-sharp, idol-kind, fierce-beautiful, and airhead-performer.

### 23.2 Trope collision scenes

The best scenes put **two tropes with opposite values** in a small space:

- **Ojou and gyaru** at a family restaurant drink bar: formality against ease, and the ojou discovering melon soda.
- **Class rep and delinquent** on cleaning duty: rules against reputation, and they discover they're both the one who does all the work.
- **Kouhai and childhood friend** meet at his door: past against present, polite smiles, a war fought entirely in honorifics.
- **Chuuni and kuudere**: the chuuni's drama bounces off total deadpan. "I am the Oracle." "I see. I'm the library committee."

See [Scene Playbook](../scenes/scene-playbook.md) for full recipes.

### 23.3 Voice differentiation test

Take one line of intent, *"I want to walk home with you,"* and write it for each trope in your cast. If you can't tell them apart from the quoted words alone, go back to [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).

- Onee-san: "Walking me home, shounen? How gentlemanly~ Carry my convenience-store bag."
- Kouhai: "Senpai, our routes overlap for exactly six hundred meters. I measured. Coincidence!"
- Childhood friend: "Same pillar, same time. Like always. ...Right?"
- Ojou: "I shall permit you to walk beside me. My driver is... indisposed. Deliberately."
- Genki: "Walk-home party! Party of two! Let's go!"
- Gyaru: "Kuzecchi, walk me to the station, there's a sketchy guy at the crossing and you look scary enough."
- Chuuni: "The Oracle requires an escort through the Twilight Streets. You've been chosen. Don't argue with fate."
- Class rep: "Walking together is efficient. We have the same homework. I'm not-- it's efficient."
- Tomboy: "Race you. Loser walks the winner home. ...Wait, that's the same thing either way."
- Delinquent: "Tch. It's dark. Just walk. Don't talk."

## 24. Checklist: before a trope character appears

- [ ] I can name her role, trope, and dere type separately.
- [ ] She has one concrete contradiction that isn't just "secretly nice".
- [ ] Her first description uses the trope as shorthand and adds a crack within three scenes.
- [ ] Her signature gesture (glasses push, ponytail swing, "ara ara") appears at most once per scene.
- [ ] Her honorifics and nicknames are recorded in the story bible and consistent.
- [ ] The POV has a first theory about her that is plausible and incomplete.
- [ ] If she's an adult, her teasing and her body can carry whatever heat the register calls for. If she's under 18, nothing about her is framed sexually at all.
- [ ] Siblings are family, never romance.
- [ ] She could not be swapped with another cast member without the reader noticing.
- [ ] She wants something in the scene that has nothing to do with the POV.

Then run the scene through [Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md), paying special attention to catchphrase frequency and repeated gestures.
