# Archetypes: Story Roles

> **Purpose:** A catalog of the *story roles* a light-novel cast needs, covering who does what in the plot and in the POV character's head, and how each role feeds the voice.
> **Load when:** You are assembling a cast for a new story, or a scene feels flat because a character has no clear job.
> **Related:** [Character Design](character-design.md), [Dere Types](dere-types.md), [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md), [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Story Bible template](../templates/story-bible.md)

---

## 1. Roles vs. personalities

A character in this genre has two layers, and mixing them up is the most common casting mistake.

- **Role** is *what the character does to the story*: the Observer watches, the Harem Lead attracts, the Bully Queen isolates, the Confidant reveals, and the Mastermind moves pieces. A role is defined by function.
- **Personality stereotype** is *how the character behaves while doing it*: tsundere, kuudere, onee-san, gyaru, chuunibyou. A stereotype is defined by surface behavior and speech.

The same role can wear any personality. A **Mastermind** can be an ojou-sama (Sayo Kujou in the default cast), a genki girl whose cheer is a mask, or a kuudere who never raises her voice. A **Confidant** can be a joking swim-club boy (Daichi Enomoto) or a teasing onee-san cousin. When you cast, **pick the role first and dress it second.** A cast of five "tsunderes" is a costume party. A cast of five roles, each with a different personality, is a story.

This file covers roles. For personality stereotypes, see [Dere Types](dere-types.md) and [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md). The quick map is in [section 12](#12-personality-overlay-quick-map).

### 1.1 The minimum viable cast

The source novel runs a hundred chapters on roughly this skeleton:

| Slot | Role | Default-cast example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Observer POV (cynical loner) | Minato Kuze |
| 2 | Foil / "protagonist-type" classmate | Haruto Amamiya |
| 3-6 | Orbit heroines (each a distinct role in the web) | Rin, Sayo, Hina, Momo |
| 7 | Confidant | Daichi Enomoto |
| 8 | Social antagonist | Reika Hoshino |
| 9 | Adult with authority and blind spots | Ogata-sensei |
| 10 | Comic-relief friend(s) | Gon Iwashita |

Ten named characters are enough for a full series. Adding more before arc 3 dilutes every one of them. Unnamed classmates ("a girl near the window", "the boys by the lockers") act as a **chorus** and do most of the crowd work.

## 2. The Observer (the POV loner)

The engine of the voice. Everything in [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md) assumes this role exists.

### 2.1 Function
- **Sees what the story's "main character" misses.** The Observer stands at the edge of the rom-com and reads it like a crime scene.
- **Converts social micro-events into analysis.** Who got offered a drink? Why "you came *with* her" instead of "what are you doing here"?
- **Distrusts goodwill**, including his own.
- **Stays uninvolved** until the story forces him in. The arc is the cost of that uninvolvement.

### 2.2 Variants

| Variant | Core belief | Flaw that drives the arc | Speech |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Rationalist** (default: Kuze) | "Everything can be explained by motive and incentive." | Uses logic to avoid feeling, and stays silent while someone suffers because "it's rational" | Terse, precise, deadpan |
| **The Bitter Veteran** | "I've been burned once. Never again." | A past betrayal (a middle-school confession gone public) makes him pre-emptively reject people | Sardonic, self-deprecating |
| **The Invisible Man** | "No one notices me, so I notice everyone." | Mistakes invisibility for safety, and is shocked to learn he mattered to someone | Quiet, polite, and "-san" with everyone |
| **The Self-Appointed Critic** | "Everyone's a hypocrite; I'm just honest about it." | Hypocrisy of his own that he can't see | Cutting, lecture-prone |
| **The Fixer in Denial** | "I don't care." (He cares.) | Helps in secret and sabotages the credit | Gruff, dismissive, with kind actions |

### 2.3 How the Observer serves the voice
- **Terse outside, verbose inside.** A 70-word analysis followed by a two-word reply is the signature ratio. See [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).
- **Surname in narration.** "Kuze", never "Minato", which keeps the camera cool and distant.
- **A private leitmotif** for his bad feelings. Kuze's is "the sediment". It returns at every low point and overflows at the climax.
- **A mental ledger.** Kuze tracks social debts ("He owed Hina one bento, and Hina owed him nothing, which was worse").

### 2.4 Failure modes
- **The edgelord.** He is cynical with no self-doubt, which makes him unbearable. Every accusation he makes must bounce back at least once: "Or was that just envy talking?"
- **The mouthpiece.** The Observer becomes the author delivering essays on society. Keep the analysis tied to *this* drink, *this* seat, *this* caption.
- **The secret saint.** Revealing on page 20 that he is actually kind drains the tension. Let the reader suspect it for ten chapters before anyone in the story does.
- **Never wrong.** He must misread at least one person badly per arc. In the default arc, Kuze misjudges Rin as shallow and Sayo as an ally.

### 2.5 Sample
> Amamiya had offered to buy drinks for both of them. The moment Hina left for the club room, the offer vanished along with her.
> Kuze turned the empty can over in his hands.
> It was reasonable. They weren't friends, they'd barely spoken, and a stranger buying a stranger a drink would be strange. He knew all that.
> And yet the offer had lasted exactly as long as the girl had.
> Was it the drink he cared about, or who was watching when he offered it?
> ...Or was Kuze just annoyed that nobody bought *him* drinks?
> The sediment shifted, somewhere under his ribs, and settled again.

## 3. The Foil (the "protagonist-type" classmate)

In a normal rom-com he would be the hero. Here he is the **mirror** the Observer can't stop staring into.

### 3.1 Function
- Embodies everything the Observer lacks: ease, popularity, the ability to say what he wants out loud.
- Surrounds himself with heroines and forms a "little world" that the Observer literally steps back from.
- Makes **righteous speeches** ("Can't just ignore someone in trouble, right?") that the Observer reads as performance.
- Becomes **the question** of the series: *Why is* he *the one everyone likes?*

### 3.2 The key design rule: keep him ambiguous
Haruto Amamiya must support **two readings at all times**:
1. **The generous reading:** he's a genuinely kind, slightly dense boy, and the Observer is jealous.
2. **The cynical reading:** he's a self-serving harem-keeper who performs kindness when girls are watching.

Every scene with the Foil should give evidence for both. He fixes a stranger's bicycle chain (kind), but only while two girls watch (suspicious). He mediates a fight (kind), then posts about how *he* made them make up (suspicious). The reader should flip between readings several times per arc. **Never resolve it fully before the final arc.** The ambiguity is fuel.

### 3.3 Variants

| Variant | Generous read | Cynical read |
|---|---|---|
| **The Oblivious Sun** (default: Amamiya) | Kind, dense, can't see affection | Pretends not to see so he never has to choose |
| **The Nice Guy Hero** | Protects everyone | Needs to be needed; picks fights to rescue girls from |
| **The Charismatic Leader** | Natural class president type | Uses the class as an audience |
| **The Wounded Prince** | Carries a tragic past that explains everything | Uses the tragedy to keep girls orbiting |

### 3.4 Speech and behavior
"Whoa, whoa." "Got it, got it." Head pats. Shoulder slaps. He makes decisions for groups ("Settled! Let's all go!") and talks over the Observer without noticing. See [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md) for his line bank.

### 3.5 Failure modes
- **Strawman villain.** If he's obviously a creep, the Observer's suspicion is merely correct and therefore boring. The suspicion must *cost* the Observer something, such as making him look petty to the reader.
- **No inner life.** Give the Foil one cut-away scene per arc where the reader sees him alone. He might stare at his phone after a heroine leaves, or sigh. What he's feeling stays unreadable.

### 3.6 Sample
> "Let's all go get food, the three of us! Settled!"
> Amamiya clapped his hands. Settled, apparently. Nobody had asked Kuze.
> Rin was already standing.
> "If Haruto says so, I guess I don't *mind*..."
> Kuze looked at the boy grinning between them. He looked like the protagonist of every rom-com Kuze had ever skipped. He had a warm smile and a warmer voice, and he took it for granted that everyone wanted to be included in whatever he decided.
> Maybe that was all it was: a nice guy being nice.
> Kuze picked up his bag. He'd believe it when Amamiya invited him somewhere without a girl in the room.

## 4. The Orbit Heroines

In the harem structure, several girls circle the Foil. In this voice they're seen **from outside** by the Observer, which is what makes them interesting. Each must hold a **different role in the web**, not just a different hair color.

### 4.1 Web roles

| Web role | Function | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **The First Contact** | The heroine who pulls the Observer into the plot, often through a fight or an errand | Rin Tachibana (the trash-duty argument) |
| **The Kind Idol** | Treats the Observer like a person when no one else does, and becomes his "why does *she* like *him*?" | Hina Matsuri |
| **The Strategist** | Sees more than anyone, including the Observer, and moves the pieces | Sayo Kujou |
| **The Mask** | A sweet public face over something colder, revealed through social media | Momo Shirasagi |
| **The Loyal One** | Loves the Foil loudly and without calculation, and gets hurt | (Hina or Rin, depending on the arc) |

### 4.2 The heroine's two faces
Every heroine has:
1. **A face for the Foil**: blushes, clinginess, "lawful wife" jokes.
2. **A face for the Observer**: what she's like when the Foil isn't in the room.

The drama lives in the gap. Rin is violent and loud around Amamiya, but alone with Kuze on a station platform she's fragile and admits she has no girl friends. Hina has no walls with anyone, and that turns out to be its own kind of loneliness. See [Character Design](character-design.md#6-public-face-vs-private-face).

### 4.3 The arc a heroine walks
1. **Seen as a stereotype** by the Observer, through his cynical lens.
2. **A crack appears** in a one-on-one scene.
3. **The Observer revises** his judgment and hates that he has to.
4. **Social cost**: she's punished by the class or by the Foil's orbit for her connection to the Observer.
5. **A choice**: does she stay in orbit or step out?

### 4.4 Failure modes
- **The interchangeable squad.** If you can swap two heroines' lines without anyone noticing, one of them has no job. Fix it by giving them different *roles* in the web, not just different tics.
- **The trophy.** A heroine who exists only to be won. Give her a want that has nothing to do with any boy: a kendo tournament, a sister, a grade.
- **The punching bag.** It's fine to let the class torment a heroine. The narrative must not *agree* with the class.

## 5. The Confidant

One friend who sees straight through the Observer. The source uses a childhood friend who finds the protagonist behind the storage shed and talks him down.

### 5.1 Function
- **Grounds the Observer's humanity** early. Just the existence of a friend who calls him "Mina" tells the reader he's not a monster.
- **Delivers the thesis** at the low point. The Confidant names the Observer's flaw out loud, gently, while the Observer can only answer `"..."`.
- **Is socially valuable.** Often popular in another class, his friendship with the Observer shocks classmates and slowly rehabilitates the Observer's reputation.

### 5.2 Design rules
- **Different class or club.** Keeping him offstage most of the time preserves the Observer's isolation and makes his entrances count.
- **Comedy first, sincerity later.** Bad jokes and deadpan scoring ("Lame. Two points.") set up the contrast for the serious speech.
- **He knows where to find the Observer.** "How long have we been friends?" The knowing is the love.
- **He doesn't fix the plot.** He fixes the Observer. The Observer still has to walk back into the classroom himself.

### 5.3 The thesis speech: shape
1. He sits beside the Observer without looking at him, maybe with a canned coffee.
2. He opens with a joke.
3. He says it plainly: "You wanted to help her, right?"
4. He lays it out in long, gentle paragraphs, each answered by `"..."`.
5. He names the curse, whether that's "rational" or "not caring", as the Observer's own word turned against him.
6. The Observer breaks.
7. The Confidant stays and doesn't comment on the tears.

See [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md) and [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md) for full technique.

### 5.4 Failure modes
- **The therapist.** If he uses words like "valid", "boundaries", or "process your feelings", cut them. He talks like a teenage boy who happens to be right. See [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md).
- **Always available.** If he appears every chapter, his speech has no weight.

## 6. The Social Antagonist (the Bully Queen)

The source's sharpest insight is that **the real danger isn't the harem, it's everyone else**. The Bully Queen is the embodiment of the crowd.

### 6.1 Function
- **Rules the class from the shadows.** The Foil and his heroines are the visible top of the hierarchy, while the Bully Queen controls opinion.
- **Weaponizes a sense of justice.** Her target "deserves it": arrogant, flirty, two-timing. This mirrors the Foil's performed righteousness from the opposite side.
- **Rallies a chorus.** A clique of two or three echoes ("Totally~", "Seriously~"), and they speak in unison with `""Totally~~""`.
- **Forces the Observer's hand.** Her campaign is the thing he can't rationalize away forever.

### 6.2 Escalation ladder
1. Loud gossip meant to be overheard.
2. A photo or post spreads (see [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md)).
3. Papers taped to a desk, removable before the teacher arrives. She is careful.
4. Public mockery: patting a head too hard, splashing water, drawing on a face.
5. **The chant**, with the whole class pulled in: `"""Say it, say it, say it~"""`.
6. The Observer's outburst.
7. The staff-room resolution and an apology that settles nothing.

### 6.3 Design rules
- **Give her a real grievance.** Reika Hoshino actually has been mocked by the target. That's what makes her dangerous and the story honest.
- **Make her logic sound.** When a heroine defends the victim, the Bully Queen should win the argument in the moment. "Then you're saying it's fine that she looks down on all of us?"
- **Let her break.** When the Observer turns her own methods on her, she cries, and the Observer says crying won't get her off either. The reader should feel the ugliness of that victory.

### 6.4 Failure modes
- **Cartoon evil** with no grievance. That removes the moral complexity the whole arc depends on.
- **Redeemed too fast.** One apology in the staff room doesn't fix her. Let her sulk in the background for a full arc.

## 7. The Mastermind

Someone is moving pieces, and the Observer suspects it too late.

### 7.1 Function
- **Explains coincidences.** Who leaked the photo? Who told the Foil where to be? Who switched teams at exactly the right moment?
- **Provides the series' long thread** across arcs. See [Series Planning](../structure/series-planning.md).
- **Appears as an ally.** The Mastermind is usually the character who helps the Observer the most. She mediates in the staff room and gives harsh, true advice.

### 7.2 How to seed
- **Cut-aways.** A short scene in her room: names on paper, arrows between them, a quiet laugh. "Things are getting interesting."
- **Contradictions the Observer notices and dismisses.** She condemns a girl for posting shamelessly, then lectures him with the same intensity. Something doesn't add up, and he talks himself out of the doubt: "She called me a friend. Doubting her would be shameful."
- **Script edits.** She volunteers for a role and "subtly adjusts" the content. Only she knows what it means.
- **Unanswered questions** at the end of each arc point at her without naming her.

### 7.3 Design rules
- **Her goal must be comprehensible** when revealed: protecting someone, testing someone, or loneliness wearing a strategist's clothes.
- **Her help must be real.** A mastermind whose help was fake all along cheapens every scene she was in. The unsettling version is that her help was real *and* calculated.
- **Don't reveal her in her own POV.** Cut-aways show *what* she does, never *why*.

### 7.4 Sample cut-away
> In her room, Sayo Kujou uncapped a red pen.
> She wrote five names in a neat column, and then a sixth, a little apart from the rest.
> An arrow from Rin to Amamiya. Another from Hina. A third from Momo, drawn with a small question mark.
> The sixth name had no arrows yet.
> She tapped the pen against her lips.
> "My. What will you do next, Kuze-kun?"

## 8. The Adult (the Teacher)

### 8.1 Function
- **Authority with blind spots.** The homeroom teacher can end a conflict with one word, but only when she sees it.
- **Comic relief.** Ogata-sensei is casual ("Teehee"), gets scolded by other teachers for her language, and uses students as errand runners.
- **The quiet save.** At the crisis, she's the one who stops another teacher from chasing the Observer: "Later." One word shows she understood.

### 8.2 Variants
- **The Big Sister Teacher** (default): young, casual, and knows the Foil personally.
- **The Burnt-Out Veteran**: sees bullying, sighs, and does paperwork.
- **The By-the-Book Disciplinarian**: an obstacle, rigid, and occasionally right.
- **The Club Advisor**: rarely appears and says one wise thing per arc.

### 8.3 Failure modes
- **Deus ex sensei.** Adults who solve student problems drain the stakes. The teacher resolves the *procedure* (the staff-room meeting) but never the *feeling*.

## 9. Comic-Relief Friends

### 9.1 Function
- Give the Observer a **non-romantic social life** once he starts to change.
- Provide **banter rhythm**: two friends bicker while the Observer smiles faintly.
- Serve as **allies of convenience** in group projects such as the festival film or the study group.

### 9.2 Types
| Type | Tic | Default |
|---|---|---|
| **The Otaku Knight** | Archaic speech, "verily", "-dono" | Gon Iwashita |
| **The Loud Normie** | Complains, cross-dressing threats, "We're screwed!" | (invent: Mitsuki Ono) |
| **The Girl-Crazy Sidekick** | Greets the harem, gets roasted, is unfazed | Amamiya's only male friend |

### 9.3 Rule
**Comic friends never carry emotional climaxes.** They react to them. Their job at the climax is to be absent, or to be shocked in the background.

## 10. The Chorus (classmates, crowds, fans)

Unnamed voices doing collective work.

- **Fan boys** fawn over heroines ("I want her to scold me...!") and envy the Foil ("Why only him...!"). This establishes the heroines' status without narration.
- **Gossiping girls** carry public opinion. Their shift from "Kuze's crazy" to "Kuze stood up to bullying, kinda cool" is how you show reputation changing.
- **Onlookers** pile into the doorway during a confrontation. The Observer turns on them too.

**Technique:** render the chorus as untagged overlapping lines, then one narrative sentence that tells us what the Observer makes of it. See [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md).

## 11. Secondary roles worth having

| Role | Function | When to introduce |
|---|---|---|
| **The Rival Observer** | Another loner who sees what Kuze sees and draws the opposite conclusion | Arc 2-3 |
| **The Kouhai Who Admires Him** | Shows the Observer from outside, and misreads his coldness as coolness | Arc 3+ |
| **The Ex / Old Wound** | A middle-school incident that explains his distrust | Mentioned early, appears mid-series |
| **The Family Member** | A sister or parent who shows his home self (for example, a little sister who calls him hopeless) | Anytime; keep it light |
| **The Heroine Who Chooses Him** | A girl outside the Foil's orbit who likes the Observer for exactly the things he hates in himself | After the first climax |
| **The Transfer Student** | An outsider who disrupts the hierarchy | The start of an arc |

## 13. Introduction order

The order in which roles enter the page shapes how the reader reads everything. The source novel's opening is a model: it introduces the Foil and his orbit *through* the Observer's first suspicious observation, before the Observer has even been named in full.

| Chapter range | Enters | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Ch. 1, first page | The Foil plus one heroine, seen by the Observer | Establishes the question ("why him?") immediately |
| Ch. 1-2 | The other orbit heroines, one per scene, each with a portrait | Readers can absorb only one portrait per scene |
| Ch. 1-3 | The Adult, in a comic errand scene | Gives the Observer a reason to cross into the orbit's space |
| Ch. 3-5 | The First Contact heroine picks a fight | The engine starts |
| Ch. 5-8 | The Confidant, mentioned first and seen later | Tells the reader the Observer isn't beyond saving |
| Ch. 6-10 | The Mastermind's first odd moment (a soft laugh, "perhaps...") | Seed early and pay off late |
| Ch. 15-30 | The Social Antagonist steps out of the chorus | She's been in the background all along, and the reader should realize it |

**Rule of one:** introduce at most one *new* named character per numbered section. Two portraits in one section blur together.

**Rule of the chorus-to-name promotion:** the most satisfying antagonists were unnamed background voices first. Reika Hoshino can be "a girl laughing by the window" in chapter 4 and receive her name and portrait in chapter 20. The reader then re-reads every earlier laugh.

## 14. Roles across genres

The roles survive genre transplant. Only the costume changes. When you move the voice to another genre (see [Genre Index](../genres/genre-index.md)), recast like this:

| Role | School drama | Fantasy academy | Isekai | Villainess / otome | Workplace |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Observer | Go-home club loner | Scholarship commoner who reads the nobles | Reincarnated office worker with an adult's cynicism | The villainess herself, reading "the script" | Mid-level employee watching office politics |
| Foil | Harem-lead classmate | The prince or hero candidate | The "real" summoned hero | The otome game's heroine | The golden-boy new hire |
| Bully Queen | Class social ruler | Noble faction leader | Guild gossip or rival party | The salon's queen bee | The office gossip network |
| Confidant | Childhood friend | Roommate or familiar | The first companion | The loyal maid | The friend from another department |
| Mastermind | The ojou in the orbit | Headmaster's daughter | The goddess who sent you | The "helpful" second prince | HR, or the boss's assistant |
| Adult | Homeroom teacher | Professor | Guildmaster | Duke father | Section chief |

For genre-specific adjustments, see [Harem](../genres/harem.md), [Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md), [Isekai & Reincarnation](../genres/isekai-and-reincarnation.md), and [Villainess & Otome](../genres/villainess-and-otome.md).

## 15. Role conflicts and merges

Sometimes one character must play two roles. That works when the roles are **in tension**, because the tension becomes the character:

- **Confidant + Mastermind:** the friend who helps you is also steering you. This is the most unsettling merge in the genre, so use it once per series at most.
- **First Contact + Loyal One:** Rin, whose fight with the Observer becomes loyalty. It's natural and the most common.
- **Foil + Observer (late series):** the Foil gets a POV section and turns out to be analyzing *everyone* too. It's powerful as a late twist.
- **Bully Queen + Kind Idol:** avoid it. The reader can't hold both, and the result reads as a continuity error rather than depth.

When two roles merge, **keep both functions visible on the page** in the same arc. Otherwise the merge reads as a retcon.

## 16. Casting checklist

Before drafting chapter 1, confirm:

- [ ] There is one Observer POV with a named core belief and a named flaw.
- [ ] The Foil supports both a generous and a cynical reading.
- [ ] Each heroine holds a distinct web role, a distinct stereotype, and a want unrelated to romance.
- [ ] A Confidant exists and is kept mostly offstage.
- [ ] The Social Antagonist has a real grievance.
- [ ] The Mastermind's help is real, and her first cut-away is planned.
- [ ] The teacher can resolve procedure but not feeling.
- [ ] Every named character's address forms are logged in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md) (see [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md)).
- [ ] Every character has a filled [Character Sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md).

For turning a role into a person, continue to [Character Design](character-design.md).
