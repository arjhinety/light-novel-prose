# Dere Types: Writing Tsundere, Yandere, Kuudere, and the Rest as People

> **Purpose:** Explain each "dere" archetype in enough depth that the writer can produce a heroine (or hero) who reads as a real person with a real contradiction, not a costume of catchphrases.
> **Load when:** You are designing, introducing, or writing dialogue for a character built on a dere type, or when a dere character feels flat, repetitive, or like a gimmick.
> **Related:** [Archetypes](archetypes.md), [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md), [Character Design](character-design.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md), [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Tone & Comedy](../core/tone-and-comedy.md), [Harem](../genres/harem.md), [Rom-com & Comedy](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md), [Character Sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md)

## 1. What a "dere" type actually is

"Dere" comes from *deredere* (デレデレ), the sound of someone going soft, lovestruck, and melting. Each dere label names **the shape of the gap between how a character presents and how she feels**. The first half of the word is the mask and the second half is the melt:

| Label | Mask (outside) | Melt (inside) | The gap is... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tsundere | *tsun-tsun*: prickly, cold, hostile | Affection | **Denial** |
| Yandere | Sweet, devoted | *yanderu*: sick, obsessive | **Escalation** |
| Kuudere | *kuuru*: cool, flat, composed | Affection | **Suppression** |
| Dandere | *danmari*: silent, withdrawn | Affection and chatter | **Shyness** |
| Deredere | Sweet | Sweet | **No gap**, which is the point |
| Himedere | *hime*: princess, demanding | Affection | **Entitlement as armor** |
| Bakadere | *baka*: clumsy, airheaded | Affection | **Chaos as charm** |
| Kamidere | *kami*: god-complex, arrogant | Affection | **Grandiosity** |
| Undere | *un*: "yes, yes", agreeing | Affection | **Compliance** |
| Sadodere | Sadistic, teasing, cruel-fun | Affection | **Control** |

The single most important rule in this file:

> **A dere type is a *defense mechanism with a cause*, not a personality.** Every tsun, every coldness, every obsession is protecting something. If you do not know what she is protecting, you are writing a vending machine that dispenses "B-Baka!"

In the LN voice, dere types are filtered through a **cynical POV narrator** (see [Narration & POV](../core/narration-and-pov.md)). This changes everything. The reader does not get the heroine's inner monologue. They get Kuze's *reading* of her: suspicious, analytical, frequently wrong. The dere gap becomes a puzzle the narrator keeps misdiagnosing. That is the engine. The tsundere hits Amamiya, and Kuze thinks, *Childish. She hits him because she can't look at him.* The narrator is right about the mechanism, wrong about how it will turn out, and blind to the day she starts hitting *him*.

### 1.1 The three questions for any dere character

Before writing a single line, answer:

1. **What does the mask protect?** (Pride? A past rejection? A family that punished softness? Fear that her feelings are too big?)
2. **Who is the mask for?** (Everyone? Only the boy she likes? Only the other girls?)
3. **What happens when the mask fails?** (She runs. She cries. She laughs too loudly. She goes very still.)

Put the answers in her [Character Sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md). They determine every scene.

### 1.2 How dere types serve the LN voice

- **They generate readable asymmetries.** The analytical monologue ([Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md)) feeds on gaps between words and behavior. Dere characters are gap machines.
- **They give comedy a timing structure.** Mask, crack, and re-mask is a three-beat gag. See [Tone & Comedy](../core/tone-and-comedy.md).
- **They give drama a payoff.** The first time the mask fails completely, with no gag and no re-mask, is one of the strongest emotional beats the genre has. Save it.

## 2. Tsundere

### 2.1 Core psychology

The tsundere is **someone whose pride cannot survive being seen wanting something**. Affection feels like losing. So every soft impulse gets converted into its opposite at the border between feeling and action: she wants to thank him, so she insults him; she wants to sit next to him, so she complains he took her seat.

There are two sub-shapes:

- **Harsh tsundere (tsun-heavy):** hostile by default, soft in rare flashes. Often violent in comedy registers (the slap, the kendo-stick bop). Rin Tachibana is this.
- **Soft tsundere (dere-heavy):** mostly sweet, and switches to tsun only when caught being sweet. "I-It's not like I made extra for you!"

**The cause** should be concrete. Rin's: her older brother was the family's "gifted one", and every time Rin wanted something openly (praise, a new bamboo sword, her father at a tournament) she was told not to be greedy. She learned that wanting things out loud gets you laughed at. So she wants things *sideways*.

### 2.2 How the split really works

Beginners write the tsundere as "mean, then blushes". The real mechanism is **a lag**:

1. The soft impulse arrives first. (She notices his bag is heavy.)
2. The action comes out soft. (She grabs one strap to help.)
3. She *notices herself being soft*. **This is the key beat.**
4. She overcorrects into hostility. ("Y-You're walking too slow! It's annoying!")

So the tsun is not the first reaction. It is the **second**, a reaction to her own reaction. Write that lag and the character becomes human. The narrator can even catch it:

> Tachibana's hand was already on the strap before her face caught up with it. Kuze watched the realization arrive, a full second late, and turn her ears red.
> "Y-You're slow. It's annoying."
> "You're holding my bag."
> "I'm holding it *so you'll hurry up*."

### 2.3 Speech patterns

- Stutter on the *first word of the denial* ("I-It's", "W-Who", "D-Don't"), not randomly.
- Inverted thanks: "Don't get the wrong idea", "It's not for you", "I just happened to".
- Insults as nicknames: "you idiot", "dummy", or just "you".
- Questions she answers herself before you can: "You want to walk together? Fine. Not like I care."
- **She never says "I like you" until the payoff**, and when she does, she says it plainly, without a stutter. The absence of the stutter is the signal.

### 2.4 Body language

Arms crossed. Face turned *away* while eyes slide *back*. Blush that reaches the ears (see [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)). A physical outlet for the overcorrection, such as a shoulder punch, a stomp, or a door slammed a little too hard. For Rin, the canine flash when she grins genuinely and forgets to stop herself.

### 2.5 How the cynical POV reads her

Kuze dissects the tsundere accurately and coldly at first: *She hits him because she can't talk to him face-to-face. Childish.* This is the source novel's move exactly, where the narrator decodes the heroine's violence as embarrassment. The fun is that his accuracy makes him **overconfident**. He can read her hostility toward Amamiya perfectly and cannot read her hostility toward *him*, because he assumes no one would bother being tsundere about him. The reader sees it chapters before he does.

### 2.6 Sample lines (Rin)

1. "Don't talk to me. ...Wait, where are you going? I didn't say *leave*."
2. "Haruto, you idiot! Your tie's crooked! Honestly, how do you even survive?"
3. "It's leftover. Leftover! Take it before I throw it out."
4. "Who said I was waiting? I was standing here. People stand places."
5. "Tch. You're weirdly okay at this. Don't let it go to your head."
6. "If you tell anyone I cried, I'll hit you with a shinai. Two shinai."
7. "...Thanks. There. I said it. Happy?"
8. (payoff, no stutter) "I like you. I've liked you since the thing with the umbrella. You're so slow it's insulting."

### 2.7 Mini-scene

> 3
>
> The bento was in a plain navy cloth, knotted badly, like someone who had never wrapped anything for anyone.
> Tachibana set it on his desk without breaking stride and kept walking.
> "..."
> Kuze looked at the bento. Then at the back of her head, already three desks away.
> "Tachibana."
> "What."
> "You left something."
> "I didn't leave anything. Mom made too much. It's leftover."
> Leftover. Her mother, apparently, had made too much and then tied it in a knot that had clearly taken three attempts.
> "Your mom's knot is terrible."
> Tachibana spun around.
> "W-What's wrong with my knot!?"
> The class went quiet. Somewhere near the window, Hina made a small sound like a kettle.
> Tachibana's face went through several colors in order and settled on red.
> "...Just eat it, you idiot."
> She sat down hard enough to scrape her chair.
> Kuze untied the knot. It took him three attempts.

### 2.8 Arc

1. **Hostile default.** Hostility is aimed at the POV or at the love interest.
2. **Leak.** Private kindness, discovered by accident.
3. **Denial escalation.** The more she is caught, the louder the tsun.
4. **The failure of the mask.** Something real happens (bullying, a loss, a fight) and she cannot overcorrect. She just cries, or just asks.
5. **Honest sentence.** One clean line with no stutter.
6. **Settled tsun.** She stays prickly, but now the prickliness is a shared joke rather than a wall. **Never delete the tsun entirely.** Readers love her for it.

### 2.9 Clichés to avoid and how to fix them

| Cliché | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "B-Baka!" every chapter | A catchphrase becomes noise | Give her a *personal* insult vocabulary tied to her life (kendo terms, "slow", "dummy") |
| Violence without consequence | Reads as abuse, not comedy | Keep it small and slapstick, and have her *feel bad after* in one beat |
| Tsun toward everyone equally | No gap means no romance | Make the tsun **selective**: she is civil to strangers and awful to people she cares about |
| Instant softening after one nice act | No earned payoff | Minimum: several leaks before the mask fails |
| Stutter on every line | Unreadable | Stutter only when caught |

### 2.10 Making her human

Give her **one area where she is not tsun at all**, where she is simply competent and calm, like kendo, a younger sibling, or cooking. Seeing her without the defense shows the reader what the defense is protecting.

## 3. Yandere

### 3.1 Core psychology

The yandere is **love that has lost its brakes**. Sweet on the surface, possessive underneath, escalating toward controlling or frightening behavior when the object of her love is threatened, whether by rivals, by distance, or by the possibility of being unneeded.

The psychological core is **terror of abandonment**, expressed as control. She is not "evil". She has concluded, somewhere, that love you do not hold tightly will leave, and so she holds tighter and tighter.

**Tone rule for this skill:** the source register is teen drama. Yandere menace here is **psychological**: surveillance, isolation, rumor-spreading, sabotage, and the smile that does not reach the eyes. It is not gore. Violence, if any, stays implied and off-page. And the cynical POV **treats it as frightening, not romantic**. Do not write the yandere as an aspirational love story. Write her as a person the narrator is right to be afraid of, whom the reader can also pity.

### 3.2 Sub-types

- **Obsessive-sweet:** devoted to one person, sabotaging rivals. The classic.
- **Self-destructive (menhera-leaning):** turns the fear inward: "If you leave, I don't know what I'll do." Handle with care, and never make self-harm a punchline.
- **Hidden yandere:** reads as a deredere or bakadere until the reveal. This works beautifully in a mystery-leaning story (see [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md)).
- **Protective yandere:** directs aggression outward at anyone who hurts her person. This is sometimes played for comedy, and it is dangerous comedy.

### 3.3 How the escalation works

A yandere must **escalate on-page in visible steps**, each small enough to be deniable at the time:

1. Knowing things she shouldn't (his schedule, his lunch order).
2. Appearing where he is, "by coincidence".
3. Commenting on other girls in a sweet voice with a sharp edge.
4. Something happening to a rival (a lost notebook, a rumor, a photo posted).
5. Confronting the love interest with "evidence" of his betrayal.
6. The mask slipping fully: flat eyes, calm voice, the terrible line.

The narrator notices each step and **explains each away**. That is where the horror lives: in his rationalizations. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md) on self-suspicion running in the wrong direction.

### 3.4 Speech patterns

- Sweetness with **precision**: exact times, exact quantities. "You talked to Tachibana-san for four minutes today."
- Questions that aren't questions: "You'll walk home with me, right? *Right?*"
- The switch to **flat declaratives**: all tildes and exclamation points vanish. The typography goes quiet. (See [Punctuation & Typography](../core/punctuation-and-typography.md), where dropping `~` mid-scene is a tonal alarm.)
- Using the loved one's given name with no honorific *before* earning it.
- Repetition as pressure: "It's fine. It's fine. It's fine, isn't it?"

### 3.5 Body language

A smile held a beat too long. Standing slightly too close. Stillness where you'd expect fidgeting. Hands clasped in front of her, too neat. Eyes that stay on him while the rest of her face performs for everyone else. **The single best tell is gaze without blinking in a scene where everyone else is moving.**

### 3.6 How the cynical POV reads her

This is where the cynical narrator is useful. Kuze distrusts sweetness by reflex, so he notices. But because he also distrusts *himself*, he assumes he is being paranoid. *She's just friendly. You're reading malice into everything again, as usual.* The reader watches his self-doubt disarm him. When he finally names it, the line should be short and cold: *She wasn't friendly. She was keeping count.*

### 3.7 Sample lines (an invented character, Tsumugi Ayase, first-year)

1. "Kuze-senpai! What a coincidence~ I was just passing the station you use. Every day."
2. "You bought canned coffee today. Black. You usually buy the one with the blue label. Did something happen?"
3. "Tachibana-senpai is so pretty, isn't she~? It'd be sad if something happened to her kendo gear before the tournament."
4. "I'm not angry. Why would I be angry? I'm smiling, look."
5. "You don't have to reply to her messages. I can do it for you. I already know what you'd say."
6. (flat) "You said you'd walk me home. You said it on the fourteenth. At 4:12."
7. "If you're busy, I'll wait. I'm good at waiting. I've been waiting since April."
8. (after the reveal, almost gentle) "Everyone leaves eventually. I just wanted to be the one who didn't."

### 3.8 Mini-scene

> 5
>
> Kuze's shoe locker was open when he got there.
> Not broken. Open. As if someone had simply had the key.
> Inside, on top of his indoor shoes, was a canned coffee. Black. Still cold. The blue-label brand he'd switched away from last week.
> "..."
> He looked down the hallway. Empty, except for the late sun and the smell of floor wax.
> Coincidence. Somebody's joke. Enomoto, probably, with his usual bad sense of humor.
> Except Enomoto didn't know he'd switched brands. Nobody knew. He hadn't told anyone because it wasn't the kind of thing a person tells anyone.
> A folded note was taped to the can.
>
> *You looked tired today, senpai. This is the one you like. The other one isn't good for you. ♡*
>
> The heart was drawn carefully. Traced over twice.
> Kuze read the note a second time. Then a third, searching for the part where it became normal.
> It didn't.
> He closed the locker, and for the first time since April, he locked it.

Note how the menace is carried entirely by **specificity** and by the narrator's failing attempts to rationalize. No knife is needed.

### 3.9 Arc options

- **Tragic:** she is not saved; she is removed from the story (a transfer, hospitalization handled with dignity), and the narrator is left with guilt.
- **Redemptive:** someone breaks the abandonment logic by staying *without being controlled*: "I'm not going anywhere. And you still can't open my locker." Slow, uncertain, and never instant.
- **Mystery reveal:** she was the one leaking photos all along. Pairs with [Series Planning](../structure/series-planning.md) and the mastermind thread.

### 3.10 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Yandere as sexy danger fantasy | The POV treats her as a threat and as a person in pain, never as a prize |
| Gore for shock | Keep violence off-page and implied. Dread lives in detail, not blood. |
| Instant reveal in chapter 1 | Escalate in deniable steps |
| She's only her obsession | Give her a hobby, a friend, a mother who works nights, a reason |
| The problem is solved by a speech | Abandonment fear doesn't vanish; show relapse and slow work |
| Self-harm as a punchline | Never. If it appears, it is handled seriously and briefly. |

## 4. Kuudere

### 4.1 Core psychology

The kuudere is **composed on the outside and warm underneath, with the warmth expressed in facts rather than feelings**. Unlike the tsundere, she isn't denying her affection; she simply doesn't *perform* emotion. Her care shows up as actions: she remembers your allergy, she brings the right notes, she stands in the right place.

Causes vary: a household where emotions were considered noise, a gifted child treated as a tool, an introvert who processes everything internally, or simply a person whose feelings run deep and slow. **Kuudere is not the absence of feeling. It is feeling with low surface tension.**

### 4.2 The split

- Mask: flat voice, economical words, unreadable face.
- Melt: **micro-signals**. A pause before answering. The tiniest smile. A sentence one word longer than usual. Using your name.

The kuudere's dere arrives in **increments so small that only an attentive narrator catches them**, which makes her a perfect partner for the analytical POV. Kuze notices asymmetries for a living. He will notice that she said "good morning" and not just "morning".

### 4.3 Speech patterns

- Short, declarative, and complete sentences. No stutters and no tildes.
- Literal answers to rhetorical questions. ("Isn't it hot?" "It's twenty-nine degrees. Yes.")
- Deadpan honesty that lands as comedy: "I would like to hold your hand. It's cold. That is the only reason. Also, I like you."
- When moved, she gets *more* precise, not less.
- "I see." "Understood." "Is that so." This is her punctuation.

### 4.4 Body language

Stillness. Direct eye contact that doesn't waver. Tilting her head a few degrees. Standing slightly closer than necessary *without comment*. A single blink held longer. Her hands tell the truth: they grip her skirt, fiddle with a pen, or stay very still around him.

### 4.5 How the cynical POV reads her

The kuudere is the one heroine Kuze **respects on sight**, because she seems to have no pretense to see through. That is her trap: he assumes she has no hidden layer, because he can't find the seam. When he finally does find it (a crack in her voice, one wrong breath), it lands like a revelation. *She wasn't calm. She was holding very still so nothing would spill.*

Sayo Kujou can wear kuudere as a *mask*. She is composed, logical, and unreadable, and underneath is not warmth but calculation. This makes a lovely twist: the reader has learned "kuudere means hidden warmth" and gets hidden *strategy* instead. Use it knowingly.

### 4.6 Sample lines (an invented character, Shizuku Minase, library committee)

1. "You're late. Four minutes. It's fine. I wasn't counting."
2. "I brought two umbrellas. I had a feeling. That's all."
3. "Your handwriting is bad, but your notes are correct. I prefer correct."
4. "I don't dislike this." (her highest praise)
5. "Kuze-kun. You're sad. You don't have to say why. Sit."
6. "I will be honest, because lying is inefficient. I was jealous."
7. "...That was a joke. I'm told I should say so."
8. (confession) "I have thought about it carefully for three months. My conclusion has not changed. I like you. Please respond when you're ready. I can wait."

### 4.7 Mini-scene

> 2
>
> The library was empty except for the sound of the heater and the scratch of Minase's pen.
> "You didn't eat lunch."
> Kuze looked up. She hadn't.
> "How do you know that?"
> "You didn't go to the cafeteria, you didn't have a bag with you, and you've read the same page for eleven minutes."
> She kept writing.
> "Hunger lowers reading speed."
> "..."
> She reached into her bag without looking and set a melon pan on the desk between them. It was still in its wrapper, and it had clearly been bought that morning, before she could possibly have known.
> "I bought two by mistake."
> "You don't make mistakes."
> The pen stopped.
> For a moment, a very short one, Minase Shizuku did not know what to say. Kuze saw it. The half-second of blank space where her next line should have been.
> "...Then it wasn't a mistake. Eat."
> The pen started again. Her ears, he noticed, were a slightly different color than before.

### 4.8 Arc

1. Composed default, respected but distant.
2. Precise acts of care that the POV misreads as efficiency.
3. The first **visible crack**: a hesitation, an unplanned sentence.
4. She articulates her feeling *clearly and logically*, often before the POV is ready.
5. She stays composed, but now she allows herself small visible warmth *around him only*.

### 4.9 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Robot girl, with no inner life | Give her hands, micro-pauses, and one irrational fondness (cats, sweets, tokusatsu) |
| Monotone making every scene dead | Pair her with a loud character; her deadpan becomes the punchline |
| The crack happens too often | Rare cracks. Three or four per arc, max. |
| Emotionless equated with smart | Give her a blind spot: social cues, jokes, her own feelings |
| Written as an emotionally stunted "ideal girlfriend" | Let her want things and demand things |

## 5. Dandere

### 5.1 Core psychology

The dandere is **quiet because speaking feels dangerous**, not because she's cold. It is social anxiety, fear of being judged, a stammer she's ashamed of, or a history of being laughed at. Around people she trusts, she becomes chatty, funny, even bold. The gap is **who she's with**.

This is the most *relatable* dere type for loner readers. The source novel's genre is built on social isolation, and danderes are its mirror: someone as lonely as the narrator, but lonely in a softer key.

### 5.2 The split

- Mask: silence, one-word answers, hiding behind hair, a book, or a friend.
- Melt: rapid enthusiastic talking about her interest, laughing at her own jokes, forgetting to be afraid, and then **noticing she forgot** and going quiet again (the same lag as the tsundere, pointed at fear instead of pride).

### 5.3 Speech patterns

- Trailing ellipses and whispered volume: "U-Um... that's... mine..."
- Apologizing constantly: "Sorry. Sorry, I'm in the way."
- When comfortable: long, run-on, detailed speech about her passion, with no stutter at all.
- Written notes or texts are more fluent than her speech. Use [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md): she writes paragraphs in chat and says four words in person.

### 5.4 Body language

Shoulders up. Eyes down or behind bangs. Holding an object like a shield (a sketchbook, a book, a bag strap). Walking at the edge of hallways. Startling at sudden voices. When comfortable: leaning *in*, hand movements, eye contact that surprises even her.

### 5.5 How the cynical POV reads her

Kuze **recognizes himself** in her, and this makes him protective and uncomfortable. His monologue about her tends to be gentler than about anyone else, and that gentleness is a tell the reader catches. Danger: he may decide what she needs without asking, the same meddling he despises in Amamiya. That irony is a strong arc engine (see [Archetypes](archetypes.md) on the harem lead's "imposed justice").

### 5.6 Sample lines (an invented character, Nanami Hotta, art club)

1. "...Mm."
2. "S-Sorry, is this seat... no, it's fine, I'll stand, sorry."
3. "You... you read *that*? Nobody reads that. I mean, it's good. It's so good. The second volume has this part where the lighthouse keeper--sorry. I'm talking too much."
4. "I can't... in front of people. But I can here."
5. (in chat) "sorry for being weird today!! I wanted to say thank you for the umbrella but my mouth didn't work so I'm saying it here, thank you, really, I'll return it tomorrow (probably I'll just put it in your shoe locker because I can't talk)"
6. "Kuze-kun is... easy to be quiet with."
7. "I practiced this. In the mirror. So please don't interrupt. ...I like you. That's all. Okay, I'm leaving."

### 5.7 Mini-scene

> 4
>
> Hotta had been drawing the same corner of the courtyard for twenty minutes.
> Kuze sat on the other end of the bench, reading, which was the agreement. Neither of them had said so, but that was the agreement.
> "...Kuze-kun."
> "Hm."
> "That tree. The shadow's wrong. In my drawing. I can't--"
> She stopped. Her shoulders came up toward her ears, like she'd said something too loud.
> Kuze looked at the drawing. Then at the tree.
> "The sun moved."
> "..."
> "You've been drawing for twenty minutes. The shadow is correct for twenty minutes ago."
> Hotta blinked. Looked at the tree. Looked at the drawing.
> Then she laughed. Not a polite laugh. A real one, surprised out of her, and she covered her mouth too late.
> "S-Sorry. That's so stupid. I'm so stupid."
> "You're not stupid. The sun is inconsiderate."
> She laughed again, into her sleeve this time, and Kuze went back to his book and pretended not to feel pleased.

### 5.8 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shyness as cuteness only | Show what it costs her (lost friendships, a presentation she dreads) |
| The POV "fixes" her | She changes on her own terms; he only makes space |
| She has no voice at all | Give her fluency somewhere (text, art, a hobby) |
| Stammer as comedy | Never mock a speech difficulty; the comedy comes from situations, not her disability |

## 6. Deredere

### 6.1 Core psychology

The deredere is **openly, relentlessly affectionate**. There is no mask, and that is the challenge: without a gap, how do you make her interesting?

Answer: **the gap is in the world, not in her.** She is sincere, and the cynical narrator *cannot believe* sincerity exists. The drama is in *his* refusal to take her at face value. Hina Matsuri is this type: no walls, cheerful, and she likes people openly.

The deeper deredere also has **a wound she doesn't let affect her kindness**. She was lonely once, moved schools a lot, or had a sick sibling. Her openness is a *choice*, which makes it brave rather than naïve.

### 6.2 Speech patterns

- Exclamations, nicknames, tildes, and "Yup!", but grounded in specific warmth. She compliments *details*: "You always hold the door. I noticed!"
- Says "I like you" (as a friend and as more) freely, which makes it hard for others to tell which one she means.
- Rarely lies. When she does, she is terrible at it.

### 6.3 Body language

Leaning in, bouncing, grabbing sleeves, waving across the hallway with her whole arm, a ponytail that swings as a mood indicator.

### 6.4 How the cynical POV reads her

With suspicion. *Nobody is this nice for free.* He hunts for her angle. There isn't one, and his failure to find it is the arc. When he finally accepts her kindness at face value, that is *his* character growth, not hers.

The twist option: make her kindness real, but let her have **one selfish thing she wants** (to be *someone's* first choice, just once). That one desire makes her three-dimensional.

### 6.5 Sample lines (Hina)

1. "Kuze-kun! Morning! You look sleepy~ Did you stay up reading again?"
2. "No polite language! We're in the same year! That's a rule now. I made it."
3. "I like your coffee face. The serious one. It's cute."
4. "Rin-chan's grumpy today, so I'm giving her extra smiles. It's a strategy!"
5. "I'm okay! Really! ...I'm just, um, okay in a slightly sad way today."
6. "Everybody likes Haruto-kun. So sometimes I wonder if anyone would notice if I didn't."
7. "Thank you for noticing. Nobody notices when I'm fine-but-not-fine."
8. "Hey, Kuze-kun. Can I be selfish? Just once? Walk home with me. Only me."

### 6.6 Mini-scene

> 1
>
> "Kuze-kun!"
> A hand clapped onto his shoulder, followed by the rest of Matsuri Hina.
> "You're walking alone again! That's against the rules!"
> "What rules."
> "Mine! Rule three: no walking alone when I can see you."
> She fell into step beside him, ponytail swinging.
> "Rule four is no polite language, but you already know that one."
> Kuze kept walking. He had spent two weeks trying to figure out what she wanted.
> Notes, maybe. Or an introduction to someone. Or she'd lost a bet.
> "Why do you do this?"
> "Do what?"
> "Talk to me."
> Hina tilted her head, genuinely puzzled, as if he'd asked why she breathed.
> "Because I want to?"
> "..."
> That was it. That was the entire answer. No angle. No catch.
> Kuze found he had no category to put it in, and so he said nothing, and Hina hummed a song from a commercial the whole way to the station.

### 6.7 Failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sunshine with no shadow | One wound, one want, one bad day |
| She exists to heal the protagonist | She has her own arc and her own desire |
| Every line is exclamation-heavy | Vary; her quiet lines hit hardest *because* they are rare |

## 7. Himedere

### 7.1 Core psychology

The himedere ("princess") **demands to be treated like royalty** and is affectionate toward whoever indulges her or, more interestingly, whoever *refuses* to. The entitlement is armor: if she demands care, she never has to risk *asking* for it. Often the cause is a wealthy but emotionally absent family, where objects came easily and attention never did.

### 7.2 Speech and body

- Imperatives: "Carry this." "Open that." "You may accompany me."
- Self-reference in the third person, or ornate first person, in comedy registers.
- Genuine confusion when told no.
- An upright posture, a chin lifted, and a hand extended *expecting* someone to take it.

### 7.3 How the cynical POV reads her

Kuze's reflex is to refuse, because being ordered around offends his ledger of social debts. That refusal is precisely what fascinates her. *No one has ever said no to me. What are you?* The dynamic is comedic until the moment she drops the princess act and simply says, quietly, "Could you... stay? Please?" and the "please" breaks something in both of them.

### 7.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Elena Kisaragi, transfer student)

1. "You. Commoner. Carry my bag to the station."
2. "No? ...No? Is that a word people say to me?"
3. "I have decided you are my retainer. Congratulations."
4. "A princess does not *wait*. A princess merely arrives late on purpose."
5. "The cafeteria bread is disgusting. Buy me another one."
6. (quietly) "My father has not attended a single event. Not one. So... you'll come. That's an order. ...Please."

### 7.5 Mini-scene beats

- She orders; he declines.
- She orders more loudly; he declines more politely.
- She is baffled, then intrigued, then keeps finding reasons to be where he is.
- The reveal: an empty house, a birthday dinner with staff only.
- She asks, with no order in it. He goes.

### 7.6 Failure modes

Pure brat with no reason: give her the empty house. Class snobbery as her only joke: let her be genuinely good at something and generous in a clumsy way (she tips absurdly, buys the whole class ice cream, and doesn't understand why that's weird).

## 8. Bakadere

### 8.1 Core psychology

The bakadere is **clumsy, airheaded, and chaotic, and affectionate through the chaos**. She trips, forgets, misunderstands, and loves loudly. At her best, the "idiocy" is a different kind of intelligence: emotional honesty, a willingness to look foolish, and a lack of fear about being uncool.

**Important for this skill:** the shared cast has Momo Shirasagi, who *performs* bakadere ("Fueee~", "Umyu") as a *mask* over a calculating, social-media-driven personality. This is a deliberate subversion: the dere type becomes a costume. The narrator's cynicism is **right** about Momo, and wrong about a genuine bakadere. Use that contrast.

### 8.2 Genuine vs. performed bakadere

| Signal | Genuine | Performed (Momo) |
|---|---|---|
| Clumsiness | Happens when no one is watching too | Happens only in front of the target |
| Catchphrases | Inconsistent, natural | Branded, repeated, post-worthy |
| After a mistake | Laughs at herself, apologizes to the person affected | Checks who saw |
| Toward other girls | Same energy | Flat, sharp, or dismissive |
| Online | Posts blurry photos of lunch | Curated angles, the "cute" filter on someone else's pain |

Kuze's investigation of which one he's looking at is a whole arc. See [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md) for how posts expose the performance.

### 8.3 Sample lines

Genuine (an invented character, Pochi Nakamura):
1. "I brought an umbrella! ...It's a folding one. It's also broken. I brought a broken umbrella."
2. "Wait, is today Tuesday? Then where did Monday go?"
3. "I'm bad at studying but I'm good at believing in you! That counts!"

Performed (Momo):
1. "Fueee~ Haruto-kun, I dropped my pencil case again~"
2. "Umyu... I'm so clumsy, somebody take care of me~"
3. (flat, to Tachibana, when Amamiya is gone) "You're in my seat."

### 8.4 Mini-scene (performed, caught)

> 6
>
> "Fueee~!"
> Shirasagi's pencil case hit the floor right at Amamiya's feet. Pens scattered in a neat fan, like they'd rehearsed.
> "Whoa, whoa, Momo, you okay?"
> "Umyu... I'm so clumsy..."
> Amamiya crouched, gathering pens. Shirasagi crouched too, close enough that their shoulders touched.
> Kuze, three desks back, watched the pens.
> They'd fallen *toward* Amamiya. Not away from the desk, which was the direction gravity usually preferred. Toward.
> Coincidence, maybe. Pencil cases bounce.
> But Shirasagi's eyes, while her mouth said *umyu*, went first to Amamiya's face. Then, for one quick flick, to the girls watching from the window.
> Checking the audience.
> Kuze looked away before she could check him too.

### 8.5 Failure modes

Real clumsiness written as stupidity: give her competence elsewhere. Performed bakadere revealed too early: seed three incongruities before the narrator names it.

## 9. Kamidere

### 9.1 Core psychology

The kamidere has a **god complex**: arrogant, grandiose, sure she is superior, and affectionate in a way that treats love as a favor she is granting. Beneath it is usually **fear of being ordinary**: a gifted kid who was only ever loved for being extraordinary, and who is terrified that without the pedestal she is nothing.

This type overlaps with **chuunibyou** (see [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md#10-chuunibyou)) in comedy registers and with the **student council president** in serious ones.

### 9.2 Speech and body

- Declarations: "I have permitted you to sit beside me."
- Treating her own feelings as decrees: "You will like me. I have decided."
- Laughing *at* things. Hands on hips. Looking down, sometimes literally from a staircase or a desk.
- When the mask fails: very small, very quiet, asking whether she's still "enough".

### 9.3 Cynical POV read

Kuze finds her unbearable and hilarious. His monologue skewers every proclamation. Eventually he sees the fear: *She announced things because announced things couldn't be refused.* The pairing works because his flat deadpan deflates her, and she *likes* being deflated; it is the only honest attention she gets.

### 9.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Tenka Ichinose, student council president)

1. "Kneel. Metaphorically. I'm not a barbarian."
2. "I've reviewed your midterm scores. Adequate. You may continue existing near me."
3. "Affection is something I bestow. You should be honored."
4. "Why are you laughing? Stop laughing. ...Why is it making me want to laugh?"
5. (mask off) "If I weren't first, who would I even be?"

### 9.5 Failure modes

Arrogance without comedy or pain: she needs both. Instantly humbled: the pedestal should crack slowly, and she should keep a bit of grandeur afterward (it's fun).

## 10. Undere

### 10.1 Core psychology

The undere says **"yes" to everything the loved one says**. She agrees, goes along, and mirrors. It reads as sweetness but is often **a lack of a self**: she doesn't know what she wants, or she's afraid that disagreeing will make her unlovable.

This is a *great* type for a story about sincerity and hypocrisy, the source novel's thematic heart. The narrator sees her agreeing with Amamiya about everything, and sees Amamiya enjoying it, and reads it as another piece of evidence against him: *He likes her because she never pushes back.*

### 10.2 Speech and body

- "Mm, mm!" "Yeah, totally!" "Whatever you think!"
- Echoing the last phrase: "It's hot today." "It's so hot today!"
- Nodding before the sentence ends.
- Looking at the loved one to check her own reaction.

### 10.3 Arc

The arc is **learning to say no**, the first disagreement. Even a tiny one ("Actually... I don't like strawberry. I never did.") is a huge moment. The narrator might be the one who asks her what *she* wants, and be shocked she has never been asked.

### 10.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Aoi Sakamaki)

1. "Mm! Haruto's right, as usual!"
2. "I'll have what you're having!"
3. "I don't mind. I never mind."
4. (to Kuze, confused) "What do *I* want? ...Nobody's asked me that."
5. (the turn) "No. I don't want to. I-- I don't want to go. Is that... allowed?"

### 10.5 Mini-scene beats

A family restaurant. Everyone orders; she orders "the same as Haruto". Kuze, with no reason to care, asks the waiter to wait and asks her what she actually wants to eat. Long silence. She orders the omurice. Her hands shake. Nobody else noticed anything happened. The narrator's verdict: *The first thing she'd chosen for herself all year, and it was eggs.*

### 10.6 Failure modes

Undere as an ideal doormat: never frame her compliance as romantic. The POV "rescuing" her into agreeing with *him* instead just swaps masters. Watch that irony.

## 11. Sadodere

### 11.1 Core psychology

The sadodere **shows affection through teasing, mild cruelty, and enjoying your discomfort**. She pokes because reactions are proof of connection. Beneath it is usually **control as safety**: if she is the one making you flustered, she's never the one who's flustered.

**Tone rule:** in school settings, sadodere teasing is **verbal and social**, not sexual. She teases your haircut, your grades, your terrible poker face. The adult onee-san variant is covered in [Character Tropes](character-tropes.md#2-onee-san), and even there this skill keeps it non-explicit (see [Ecchi & Fanservice](../genres/ecchi-and-fanservice.md) for the hard rules).

### 11.2 Speech and body

- Teasing questions: "Oh? Are you blushing, Kuze-kun? How interesting."
- Faux-sweetness with a knife in it: "I'll help you study. Every mistake, you owe me a juice."
- Delighted laughter at others' embarrassment. Half-lidded eyes. A slow smile. Resting her chin on her hand to watch you squirm.
- **The dere flip:** when *she* gets flustered, she has no defense at all. She's never practiced being on the receiving end.

### 11.3 Cynical POV read

Kuze refuses to react, which frustrates her completely. His deadpan is her kryptonite. The comedy engine is her escalating attempts to crack him and his flat "I see" in return. The payoff is the one time he teases *back* and she goes crimson and speechless.

Sayo Kujou has sadodere tendencies toward Amamiya ("You have some nerve. I'll have to discipline you"). Use this carefully, because in her case the teasing is also a tool of control over the harem, which the reader should eventually see.

### 11.4 Sample lines (an invented character, Nagisa Hiiragi, senpai)

1. "You look like a kicked puppy. Do it again, I didn't get a picture."
2. "Wrong answer. That's three juices you owe me. I'm keeping a spreadsheet."
3. "Oh? You're not reacting? How rude. I worked hard on that."
4. "Ufufu. I love it when you get serious."
5. (flipped, after he calls her cute) "W-- that's-- you can't just-- *I* do the teasing! That's the arrangement!"

### 11.5 Failure modes

Cruelty that actually hurts without acknowledgment: if she crosses a line, *show* her realize it. Teasing with sexual edges in a school setting: not in this skill. Keep it verbal and social.

## 12. Rarer and hybrid types (quick reference)

Use these as flavors layered onto the main types.

| Type | Gist | Mask/Melt | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mayadere** | Antagonist/enemy who switches sides out of love | Hostile, then loyal | Fantasy, action, rival arcs (see [Action & Battle](../genres/action-and-battle.md)) |
| **Hinedere** | Cold, cynical, arrogant, but soft underneath | Contempt, then warmth | A *female* version of the Kuze POV; a great mirror character |
| **Kekkondere** | Obsessed with marriage from the first meeting | Proposal, proposal, proposal | Pure comedy; pair with a straight-man POV |
| **Bodere** | Affection expressed through physical violence | Punches, then blush | Treat as a *tsundere excess* and keep the violence cartoonish and rare |
| **Nyandere** | Cat-like: affectionate on her own terms | Aloof, then suddenly clingy | Slice-of-life, a senpai who naps in the club room |
| **Dorodere** | Sweet outside, bitter or dark inside, not yandere-violent | Smile, then private venom | Two-faced characters like Momo |
| **Oujidere** (male himedere) | Prince-like, demands, adores | Arrogant, then devoted | Otome and villainess genres (see [Villainess & Otome](../genres/villainess-and-otome.md)) |

**Hybrids** are more realistic than pure types. People are layered:
- **Tsun-kuu:** composed, then irritated when flustered (colder than a tsundere, spikier than a kuudere).
- **Dan-yan:** a quiet girl whose silence hides obsessive attention. Terrifying and sad.
- **Dere-sado:** sunshine who enjoys teasing. Hina can go here when comfortable.
- **Kuu-kami:** composed *and* grandiose, like a council president who never raises her voice.

Rule of thumb: **one primary type, one secondary flavor, one trait that contradicts both.**

## 13. Male dere types

Dere labels apply to boys and men too, especially in otome, villainess, reverse harem ([Harem](../genres/harem.md)), and BL/romance stories. Shifts to account for:

- **Male tsundere:** hostility often reads as *rivalry* instead of embarrassment ("Don't get in my way." / leaves a sports drink by your bag).
- **Male kuudere:** the stoic prince, very common in otome. Micro-signals are the same.
- **Male yandere:** handle the menace with extra seriousness. Real-world controlling behavior by men toward women is a genuine danger, so never frame it as romantic devotion. Treat it as horror or tragedy.
- **Male dandere:** the quiet library boy. Pairs beautifully with a genki heroine POV.
- **Male deredere:** the golden retriever. The cynical POV finds him baffling, just like Hina.

The Kuze POV himself is a **hinedere**, cynical and arrogant in thought, with warmth leaking out in action (he takes the class duty so Hachiya-type girls can go to their club). Knowing this helps you keep his voice consistent. See [Narration & POV](../core/narration-and-pov.md).

## 14. How to show dere types through the narrator (not labels)

**Never write the label in the prose.** Characters in the story don't say "she's a tsundere" (unless the POV is meta-aware and doing it as a joke, and even then, once).

Instead, let the analytical monologue *discover* the pattern:

> *She insulted Amamiya every time he did something nice for her. Every single time. The nicer the thing, the worse the insult. It was almost a formula.*
> *An exchange rate. One kindness, one "idiot."*

That's a tsundere, described as evidence. The reader enjoys recognizing the type; the narrator enjoys solving it.

### 14.1 The dere reading table (what Kuze notices)

| Type | Tell Kuze notices | His first (wrong) theory | The truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tsundere | Insults scale with kindness received | She's just mean | She's embarrassed |
| Yandere | Knows things she shouldn't | Coincidence / his paranoia | Surveillance |
| Kuudere | Precise acts of care | She's just efficient | Care in her native language |
| Dandere | Talks freely only in one place | She's antisocial | She's afraid |
| Deredere | Kind to everyone, no angle | There must be an angle | There isn't |
| Himedere | Demands everything | She's spoiled | She's never been cared for without asking |
| Bakadere (performed) | Clumsiness only in front of targets | She's clumsy | It's a performance |
| Kamidere | Declares everything | She's arrogant | She's afraid of being ordinary |
| Undere | Always agrees | She's easygoing | She has no permission to want |
| Sadodere | Teases constantly | She enjoys his pain | She's hiding her own nerves |

The "first wrong theory" column is your **mid-arc material**. The "truth" column is your **payoff**.

## 15. Dialogue calibration: one situation, ten types

To check that your dere types are distinct, run them all through the same prompt. Situation: *the POV forgot his umbrella, and it's pouring.*

- **Tsundere (Rin):** "Ugh, you're pathetic. Here. Get under. Don't touch my shoulder. ...You're getting wet, idiot, come *closer*."
- **Yandere (Tsumugi):** "I brought two. I checked the forecast for your station, not mine. Yours said rain at five."
- **Kuudere (Shizuku):** "Umbrella. Take the handle. You're taller. That's the only reason."
- **Dandere (Nanami):** (holds out the umbrella without a word, then runs into the rain herself)
- **Deredere (Hina):** "Umbrella share! Umbrella share! Come on, it's fun, we'll walk slow!"
- **Himedere (Elena):** "You may hold my umbrella over me. And yourself, I suppose, if you insist."
- **Bakadere (Pochi):** "I have one! ...It's inside out. It's always inside out. Can we still use it?"
- **Kamidere (Tenka):** "The heavens themselves defer to my umbrella. Walk beside me, and be sheltered."
- **Undere (Aoi):** "Oh, do you want to share? Okay! Or not! Whatever you want!"
- **Sadodere (Nagisa):** "Hmm~ I *could* share. What will you give me? ...Kidding. Mostly. Get in."

If any two lines could be swapped without the reader noticing, the types aren't differentiated enough. Push their **specific vocabulary, rhythm, and wants** apart. See [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).

## 16. Arc-level rules for dere characters

1. **The mask costs something.** Show at least one scene where her defense *hurts her*: she misses a chance, loses a friend, or is misunderstood.
2. **The mask fails at the climax, not before.** Small cracks along the way, full failure once. See [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md).
3. **After the failure, the type doesn't vanish; it softens.** The tsundere still says "idiot", but now she's smiling. The kuudere still says "I see", but she's holding your sleeve.
4. **Other characters react to the type.** The class has opinions (see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md) on social hierarchy). The bully queen targets the tsundere because her harshness makes her an "acceptable" target, exactly the source novel's bullying logic.
5. **The POV's read evolves.** The narrator's monologue about her in chapter 5 and in chapter 50 should be recognizably different: colder then, more precise and more tender later. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md) on escalation over an arc.

## 17. Checklist: before writing a dere character's scene

- [ ] I know what her mask is protecting (a specific cause, not "she's shy").
- [ ] I know who the mask is for in this scene.
- [ ] Her dere signal is visible to the POV in at least one concrete detail (ears, pause, knot, a precise number).
- [ ] The POV's interpretation is plausible *and* possibly wrong.
- [ ] Her catchphrase appears at most once (or zero times) in the scene.
- [ ] Her stutters happen only when she is caught.
- [ ] If yandere or sadodere: menace or teasing is psychological or verbal, never sexual in a school setting, and never glamorized.
- [ ] She wants something in this scene besides the love interest.
- [ ] If another heroine is present, their lines could not be swapped.
- [ ] The scene ends on a tilt caused by *her* (see [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md)).

Run the scene through [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md) afterward. Dere characters are where AI prose most often collapses into catchphrase loops and "her cheeks flushed a deep crimson" three times per page.
