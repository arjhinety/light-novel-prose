# Social Media & Texts

> **Purpose:** How to write in-world social media posts, chat messages, and phone calls in the LN voice, and how the POV character *reads* them, which is where most of the value is.
> **Load when:** A scene involves a phone: a post, a reply thread, a group chat, a DM, a call, a leaked photo, or a character scrolling alone at night.
> **Related:** [Punctuation & Typography](../core/punctuation-and-typography.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Character Design](../cast/character-design.md#6-public-face-vs-private-face), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [School Life](school-life.md), [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md)

---

## 1. Why phones matter in this genre

In the source novel, the protagonist's most devastating realizations happen **alone in his room, scrolling**. A classmate's cheerful post about "getting them to make up" poisons him more than the event itself did. A heroine's photo of a crying baby, captioned "so cute lol", makes him decide she is frightening.

Phones do four jobs no other device does as well:

1. **The public face made permanent.** A post is how someone *wants* to be seen. Put it next to what the Observer saw in person, and the gap is the story. See [Character Design](../cast/character-design.md#6-public-face-vs-private-face).
2. **Close reading material.** The Observer can quote a post word by word, which feeds the [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md) engine perfectly ("Not 'they made up.' '*I got* them to make up.'").
3. **Plot delivery.** Leaked photos, screenshots, and group-chat gossip let the social antagonist move without appearing on stage.
4. **Private channels.** A late-night message or a call is the only place some characters drop their mask.

---

## 2. The in-world platforms

**Never use real brand names.** LN convention parodies them. The default cast uses:

| In-world name | Parody of | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Chirp** | Short public posts with photos and replies | Public face, gossip, leaks |
| **Lime** (or "the class Lime group") | Messaging app with read receipts and stickers | DMs, group chats, "read" pressure |
| **PicPic** | Photo-first feed | Curated lives, influencer heroines |
| **NicoTube** | Video platform | Idol and streamer stories, viral clips |

Mention a platform's features **the first time only**, in one sentence, the way the source does: *"Chirp. A social app where people posted their daily thoughts, with users numbering in the billions."* After that, just use the name.

## 3. Formatting

### 3.1 A single post

Username on its own line, then the post text, then an attachment marker in parentheses.

```
Haruto
Went out to eat with Rin and Kuze today. Looked like those two weren't getting along, but I got them to make up.
(Tap to view photo)
```

**Rules:**
- **The username is the display name**, exactly as the character would set it. Display names reveal character: Amamiya uses plain `Haruto`, Momo uses `momo♡shirasagi`, Hina uses `Hinacchi!!`, Sayo uses `S. Kujou`, Rin uses `rin_t` and never posts, and Reika uses `reika★`.
- **Post text is written in the character's typing voice**, which is *not* their speaking voice. Online voice is lazier and performative: lowercase, "lol", "www", emoji-free (see 3.5), and trailing tildes.
- **The attachment line** is always `(Tap to view photo)` or `(Tap to view video)`. The Observer tapping it is a beat of its own.

### 3.2 Reply threads

Indent the replies by four spaces, with each reply as username then text:

```
Haruto
Went out to eat with Rin and Kuze today. Looked like those two weren't getting along, but I got them to make up.
(Tap to view photo)

    Hinacchi!!
    Haru-kun's such a good guy~!!

    momo♡shirasagi
    fueee you went without me?? lol

    Haruto
    @momo♡shirasagi next time, promise lol
```

Keep threads to **3-6 replies**. The Observer reads them, so the reader doesn't need twenty.

### 3.3 Chat messages (Lime)

Use a lighter format for private messages, with the name, a colon, and the text, one line each. Add `(Read)` markers only when read receipts matter to the scene.

```
Rin: you there
Rin: hey
Rin: I know you're reading this
(Read)
```

For **group chats**, add the group name as a header line:

```
[2-3 Culture Festival Group]
Reika: so who's gonna tell Tachibana she's not welcome lol
Reika: jk jk
Mika: lolol
Aoi: lol
(Read by 31)
```

`(Read by 31)` with no replies is one of the scariest lines in the genre. Thirty-one people saw it, and no one said anything.

### 3.4 Phone calls

Calls are written as **normal dialogue**, with the call mechanics as their own short lines:

> Ring, ring. Click.
> "...Hello?"
> Her voice sounded like it had been scraped hollow.
> "It's me."
> "..."

Use onomatopoeia for the connect (`Click.`) and hang-up (`Beep.` or `Tsu-, tsu-, tsu-` for the dial tone after a hang-up, which is a very LN detail). For an **ignored call**, write the phone, not the ring:

> His phone lit up on the desk. *Rin.*
> He looked at it.
> "..."
> He turned it face down.

### 3.5 Emoji and stickers

**Render emoji as words or not at all.** A long run of pictographs breaks the prose texture and dates badly. Use one of these instead:
- The text marker: `(heart)` or `(crying face sticker)`. Stickers are fine as parenthetical descriptions.
- Kaomoji in moderation for cutesy characters: `(≧▽≦)`, `(´・ω・`)`. These are *very* in-genre. Limit them to one per character who would use them, and never in narration.
- `♡` and `★` in display names or a cutesy character's posts are acceptable decoration.

### 3.6 Laughing conventions

| Marker | Who uses it | Signals |
|---|---|---|
| `lol` | Most teens | Default casual |
| `lolol` | Excited or mocking | Pile-on energy |
| `www` | Otaku-adjacent, very online | Japanese internet flavor. Use sparingly, since English readers may need it established. |
| `(lol)` in parentheses | Mocking labels | The source uses this for bullying notes: "Dumped by Rin (lol)" |
| no laugh at all | Serious or cold characters | A Sayo post with no "lol" reads like a verdict |

## 4. How the Observer reads a post (the core technique)

The post itself is a prop. **The reading is the scene.** Follow this sequence:

### 4.1 The setup: alone, at night, a little ashamed
> Kuze lay on his bed with the light off and the phone held above his face, scrolling through posts he had no reason to be scrolling through.

Establish that he knows it's unhealthy. The self-disgust is part of the voice.

### 4.2 The discovery
He finds the post and reacts with a silent line before any analysis:

> ```
> Haruto
> Went out to eat with Rin and Kuze today. Looked like those two weren't getting along, but I got them to make up.
> (Tap to view photo)
> ```
> "..."

### 4.3 The tap
Tapping the attachment is its own beat, and the photo is described **precisely and flatly**:

> His thumb tapped the photo before he'd decided to.
> A table at the family restaurant. Carbonara and napolitan on one side, their forks crossed where they'd been sharing. On the other side, a lonely hamburger steak and a cup of black coffee.
> He hadn't known he was in the picture. He wasn't, really. Only his dinner was.

### 4.4 The quote-back
The Observer repeats the exact phrasing and dissects the grammar:

> *I got them to make up.*
> Not *they made up*. Not *we all hung out*. *I* got them to.
> Nobody had asked him to. Kuze hadn't wanted to go. Rin hadn't wanted to go. And somewhere between the parfaits and this post, the evening had become a good deed with Amamiya's name on it.

### 4.5 The replies
He reads the replies and guesses who's who. The replies usually confirm the public-face reading:

> Two replies. `Hinacchi!!` called him a good guy. `momo♡shirasagi` wanted to know why she hadn't been invited.
> Nobody asked if the other two had actually made up.

### 4.6 The spiral and the self-check
> Hypocrite.
> The word surfaced on its own, and Kuze let it sit there.
> And then, because he was who he was, he turned it over. What kind of person lay in the dark reading a classmate's posts until his stomach hurt? Who was the hypocrite, really, the guy who posted, or the guy who couldn't stop reading?

### 4.7 The exit
End with a physical action, not a summary:

> He tossed the phone onto the far end of the bed. It bounced once and lay there, screen still glowing.
> The sediment in his chest settled a little thicker than before.

## 5. Reading *curated* feeds (the Mask technique)

For a character whose feed is a performance (Momo, in the default cast), the Observer scrolls through **several** posts to establish the pattern, then hits one that breaks the pattern and changes everything.

```
momo♡shirasagi
had ice cream today!! so pretty~ lol
(Tap to view photo)

momo♡shirasagi
ate with haru-kun and he dropped his food lolol
(Tap to view photo)

momo♡shirasagi
saw my cousin's puppy today and he walked into the glass door and yelped sooo loud lol too cute
(Tap to view video)
```

> Kuze's thumb stopped.
> He watched the video once. A puppy, maybe a few months old, stumbling back from a sliding door with its ears flat, crying. Off-screen, someone laughing. The camera didn't move toward the dog. It moved closer to get a better angle.
> "..."
> He set the phone down, then picked it up and watched again, as though a second viewing might make it something else.
> It didn't.

**Technique notes:**
- **Pattern, then break.** The first two posts are harmless. The third lands because of them.
- **Describe what the camera does**, not what the character felt. "The camera moved closer" is the whole indictment.
- **The time gap.** The Observer can reflect on the delay between event and post: someone saw this, filmed it, chose a caption, and pressed send. That chain of choices is what makes it chilling. See [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md) for building dread with this.
- **Keep it proportionate.** A puppy yelping and a baby crying are the right scale for school drama. Don't escalate to gore or cruelty that breaks the genre.

## 6. Leaks and viral spread

Social media is the Bully Queen's weapon. See [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md#6-the-social-antagonist-the-bully-queen).

### 6.1 Leak sequence
1. **The photo exists.** Someone took it: Kuze and Rin together outside a station, at a café, on a bench.
2. **The confrontation.** The antagonist shows it *privately* first, on her phone screen, held up: "This is you, right?"
3. **The demand.** She wants a specific confession ("Say you're dating her."). The Observer realizes the confession matters more to her than the photo, and wonders why.
4. **The spread.** The next morning the air in the classroom is different. Show it through behavior (whispers stopping as he enters, a phone turned face-down on a desk) before anyone shows him the post.
5. **The artifact.** Only then does the reader see the post or group chat.

### 6.2 Rendering spread without showing every post
Use **fragments** in quick succession:

> By second period it had forty replies.
> By lunch, someone had cropped Kuze out of it.
> By the end of the day, it wasn't a photo anymore. It was a story, and everyone had their own version.

### 6.3 Unknown accounts
An anonymous account (`user_8829140`, `2-3 anon`) that seems to know too much is a mystery hook. Who is it? Someone in the class? A first-year with connections? Let the Observer loop on it without an answer. See [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md).

## 7. Private messages: where masks come off

### 7.1 The late-night message
Characters say things in text at 1 a.m. they'd never say in the classroom. Keep them **short and unpunctuated**, with gaps implied between lines:

```
Rin: are you awake
Rin: forget it
Rin: actually no
Rin: thanks. for today
Rin: dont reply to this
```

> Kuze read it three times.
> *Don't reply to this.* It was probably the most Rin sentence ever typed.
> He didn't reply. He also didn't sleep.

### 7.2 The typing indicator
`Rin is typing...` appearing and disappearing is a full emotional beat:

> *Rin is typing...*
> It vanished.
> *Rin is typing...*
> It vanished again.
> Whatever she'd been trying to say, she'd deleted it twice.

### 7.3 Messages the Observer doesn't send
Write a drafted message in the narration, then its deletion. This shows his interior without a speech:

> He typed: *Are you okay.*
> Deleted it.
> *What happened today.*
> Deleted it.
> *I'm sorry.*
> His thumb hovered over the send button for a long time. Then he deleted that too and put the phone face down.

## 8. Characters' online voices

Each major character needs an **online voice distinct from their spoken voice**. Log it in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md).

| Character | Display name | Online voice | Contrast with speech |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kuze | (none, read-only) | Doesn't post | Watches, never speaks, just like in class |
| Amamiya | `Haruto` | Upbeat and self-crediting, "lol", group photos | Same sunny tone, but the *framing* centers him |
| Rin | `rin_t` | Almost never posts; DMs have no punctuation or capitals | Loud in person, whispered online |
| Sayo | `S. Kujou` | Rare, complete sentences, no "lol", sometimes a quote | Identical precision, which is unsettling |
| Hina | `Hinacchi!!` | Exclamation marks, nicknames, tags everyone | The same, and it's sincere |
| Momo | `momo♡shirasagi` | Lowercase, "fueee", "lol", constant photos | Cuter online, colder in person with girls |
| Daichi | `DAICHI_swim` | Memes, bad puns, practice times | Same jokes, and he never mentions feelings |
| Reika | `reika★` | Vague-posting ("some people really think they're better than everyone lol") | Mocking in person, and deniable online |

**Vague-posting** (a post clearly about someone without naming them) is the social antagonist's signature move. The Observer knows who it's about, and so does everyone. That deniability is the weapon.

## 9. Phones as staging devices

- **Face-down phone:** refusal, secrecy, or shame.
- **Phone lighting up across a room:** a hook ("Her screen lit up. *Haruto.* She didn't look at it.").
- **Photographing instead of helping:** a quick moral read on a character.
- **Screenshot:** proof and threat. "I screenshotted it, obviously."
- **Muting the class group:** the Observer's opt-out, his "go-home club" of chat.
- **Deleted post:** someone saw it before it vanished. Who?
- **Dead battery / phone off:** cutting himself off. The source's protagonist powers down his phone after a fight and stares at the ceiling.

---

## 10. Pacing and frequency

- **One phone scene per 2-4 chapters** in a school drama is plenty. More than that, and the story happens on screens instead of in rooms.
- **Put phone scenes at section ends** or in the evening after school. They're natural night-time beats, and the post makes a great [hook](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md): the section ends on `(Tap to view photo)` and the next section opens on what he saw.
- **Never let a phone scene resolve a conflict.** Posts escalate, and in-person scenes resolve. An apology by text is always "not enough" in this genre.

## 11. Genre variants

| Genre | Phone equivalent |
|---|---|
| Fantasy / isekai | Communication crystals, guild notice boards, gossip at the tavern, a noble house's letters. The same close-reading technique applies to letters and notices. See [Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md). |
| Villainess / otome | Letters, invitations, and the seating chart at a ball. A misspelled name on an invitation is a whole scene. See [Villainess & Otome](../genres/villainess-and-otome.md). |
| Idol / streamer | Comment sections, live-chat scrolls, and trending tags. Render the chat as fast, short fragments. See [Sports, Idol & Club](../genres/sports-idol-and-club.md). |
| Workplace | Company chat, email CCs, and who was left off the meeting invite |

The underlying technique never changes: **present the artifact, then have the POV read it closer than anyone intended it to be read.**

## 12. Common failures

| Failure | Why it's robotic | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The post states the character's feelings plainly ("I'm so sad Kuze ignored me") | Real posts perform; they rarely confess | Make the post cheerful or vague, and let the Observer find the sadness underneath |
| The narration summarizes the thread ("Many people replied supportively") | It throws away the close-reading material | Show two or three actual replies in voice |
| The Observer reacts with a named emotion ("He felt betrayed") | It explains instead of showing | Use `"..."`, a quote-back, and a physical action |
| Everyone types identically | There's no online voice | Use the table in section 8 |
| Phone scenes every chapter | The story migrates onto screens | Space them out (section 10) |
| Real app names | Dates the text and breaks the LN convention | Use a parody name (section 2) |

---

## 13. Checklist

- [ ] Platform names are parodies, and features are explained once, briefly.
- [ ] Posts use the character's online voice, which differs from their speech.
- [ ] Display names reveal personality.
- [ ] The formatting matches section 3 (username line, text, attachment marker, indented replies).
- [ ] Emoji are rendered as words or omitted, and kaomoji are rare.
- [ ] The Observer's reading follows setup, discovery, `"..."`, tap, quote-back, spiral, self-check, and physical exit.
- [ ] At least one exact phrase from the post is quoted back and dissected.
- [ ] No conflict is resolved by text.
- [ ] Phone scenes are spaced out and placed at section ends or at night.
