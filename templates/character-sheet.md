# Template: Character Sheet

> **Purpose:** A per-character reference covering look, voice, psychology, relationships, and arc, detailed enough to keep a character consistent across a hundred chapters and distinct from everyone else in the cast.
> **Load when:** Creating any named character who will appear in more than one scene, and before writing a scene centered on a character you haven't written in a while.
> **Related:** [Story Bible](story-bible.md), [Chapter Plan](chapter-plan.md), [Character Design](../cast/character-design.md), [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md), [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md), [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md), [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md)

---

## 1. How to use this sheet

- **One sheet per named character** with recurring scenes. Walk-ons don't need one. Give them a single line in the [Story Bible](story-bible.md) cast table.
- **Start with the archetype, then break it.** Pick the base type from [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md) or [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md), then fill in the *Contradiction* and *Secret* fields. Those two fields are what turn a type into a person.
- **Write sample lines before you write scenes.** If you can't produce five lines only this character would say, the voice isn't ready. See [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).
- **Keep the portrait to what the POV would notice.** The narrator is a teenager in close third. Describe what *they* see, in the order they'd see it (see [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)).
- **Update the "State at chapter N" block** whenever the character changes. Arcs drift out of sync when nobody writes down where a character currently stands.

## 3. Filled example: Rin Tachibana

```
CHARACTER SHEET

NAME:                  Tachibana Rin (narration and most speech: "Tachibana"; the POV's thoughts drift to "Rin" late in Arc 1)
Nickname(s):           None. Hina tries "Rin-rin", and gets hit.
Year / class / club:   2nd year, Class 2-3, kendo club (the club's strongest second-year)
Role:                  Main heroine of Arc 1
Base type:             Tsundere, the "defensive" subtype rather than "violent comic"
                       (see dere-types: the tsun is armor, not a personality)

— LOOK —
Hair:                  Silver-ash bob, cut sharply at the jaw. Wind moves it across her cheek, and she doesn't brush it away when upset.
Eyes / face:           Gray, flat, measuring; "checking whether you're within striking distance." Flashes one canine when she grins, which is rare.
Build / bearing:       Straight back always, arms crossed. Walks through hallways while crowds part.
Signature gestures:    1) clicks her tongue ("Tch."), 2) hits Amamiya (never hard enough to hurt)
Signature prop:        Shinai bag over one shoulder; later, the navy-blue lunch cloth.
Portrait (as Kuze first sees her in class):
  "Her silver-ash hair was cut in a short, sharp bob that stopped exactly at her jaw,
   and her gray eyes had the flat, measuring quality of someone checking whether you
   were within striking distance."

— VOICE —
Register:              Blunt, casual, clipped. Commands rather than requests.
Average line length:   3-8 words; longer only when flustered (then run-on and loud).
Tic(s):                "Tch."; "Don't get the wrong idea."
Interjections:         "Hyah!" (startled), "Tch.", "Idiot."
Calls the POV:         "you" -> "Kuze" (Arc 1 end), and signs notes "-R".
POV calls her:         "Tachibana" (speech) / "Rin" (once, in the climax)
Topics:                Kendo, fairness, food, and not owing anyone.
Five sample lines:
  1. "You're late." "It's eight-fourteen." "You're late for *me*."
  2. "It's not like I made it for you. It's leftovers. Just leftovers."
  3. "Bring *two* spoons. So I can see the difference. It's efficient!"
  4. "The guys look at me like I'm a poster. It's gross."
  5. "There better not *be* a next time." (said into his blazer)
Line she would NEVER say:
  "Kuze-kun, I made this for you with all my heart~"

— INSIDE —
Public face:           Scary, proud, hates boys, a kendo monster.
Private face:          Careful, lonely, terrible at cooking and determined to fix it; practices lines to a fire extinguisher.
Contradiction:         "Hates guys" but is devoted to one; "doesn't care" but keeps her own ledger so she never owes anyone.
Want:                  To give Haruto a lunch good enough to say what she can't.
Need:                  Someone who sees her, not the poster and not the kendo record.
Fear:                  Being looked at and not seen. Crying in front of someone who hurt her.
Secret:                No girl friends. Her mother is in Osaka until summer, and she eats dinner alone.
Wound:                 In middle school, a senpai hit her after the call in practice while everyone watched.
                       Haruto walked onto the floor in his socks and said "That wasn't a point."

— RELATIONSHIPS —
-> Kuze:               Blackmail partner -> taste tester -> the one person who understands her fastest. Arc 1 ends unnamed but real.
-> Haruto:             Gratitude that she has called love for years. Starts to crack when he says "I don't really want to get into it."
-> Hina:               Hina tries; Rin doesn't know how to let her.
-> Kujou:              Wary. Can't read her. Is right to be wary.
-> Momo:               Thinks Momo is harmless. Wrong.
-> Reika:              Mutual contempt since first year.
Jealous of:            Hina's ease with people.
Would protect:         Anyone being hit "after the call."

— ARC (Arc 1) —
Starts as:             Hiding behind the tsun; practicing on a fire extinguisher.
Turning point:         The class turns on her. Kuze stays silent for four days, then burns himself down for her.
Ends as:               Makes a lunch "not for practice"; lets her head rest against someone.
Name escalation:       Signs "-R" on the note, her first self-given name to him.

— USE IN SCENES —
Best scene types:      Rooftop lunch, dojo, walking home, the staff-room aftermath.
Comedy role:           Tsukkomi with a shinai. Becomes boke when flustered (spoon logic).
Reactions:
  embarrassment -> red to the ears, then hits someone or shouts "Shut up!"
  praise        -> suppresses a smile "pushing back from underneath"; "Tch. Of course it's good."
  anger         -> goes quiet and precise; white knuckles on the bag strap
  being ignored -> calls repeatedly, then goes silent; later: "You didn't *answer*."

— STATE AT CHAPTER 31 —
Current feelings:      Furious at Kuze for lying to protect her; something she won't name.
Current address:       "Idiot" (spoken), "-R" (written).
Unresolved thread:     What she feels for Haruto now that he didn't walk onto the floor.
```

## 4. Common mistakes this sheet prevents

1. **Two characters share a tic.** If Rin says "Tch." then nobody else says "Tch." Check the tic fields across all sheets before drafting.
2. **The archetype is the whole person.** A tsundere with no *Contradiction* or *Secret* is a vending machine that dispenses "B-baka!" Fill both fields before writing her first scene.
3. **The portrait is written from the author's eye, not the POV's.** A cynical boy notices the shinai bag and the way crowds part. He doesn't notice "lustrous tresses".
4. **Voice drift.** Rin at chapter 30 talks like everyone else because nobody reread her sample lines. Reread the five sample lines before any scene she's in.
5. **Growth erases the character.** When Rin softens, she still says "Tch." It just changes meaning (the "gentlest *tch*" in the climax). Growth changes what the tic means. It doesn't delete the tic.
6. **Wound as vague trauma.** "She had a hard past" gives the writer nothing. "A senpai hit her after the call, and a boy walked onto the floor in his socks" gives you a callback that can pay off twenty-nine chapters later.
7. **The rival or antagonist has no sheet.** Bullies and masterminds need the *Inside* block more than anyone. Reika Hoshino's "insecure, rules by majority" line is what keeps her scenes from being cartoon cruelty.

## 5. Quick sheet for secondary characters

For recurring side characters (a club senpai, a classmate who appears every few chapters), fill only these lines:

```
NAME / role:
Visual signature (1 item):
Tic (1):
What they call the POV:
One contradiction:
Function in the story: (information source / comic relief / pressure / mirror of the POV)
```

Six lines are enough to stop them from turning into interchangeable "a classmate said" filler, and short enough that you'll actually fill them in.
