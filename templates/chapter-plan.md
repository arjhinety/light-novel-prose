# Template: Chapter Plan

> **Purpose:** A one-page plan to fill before drafting each chapter, so every chapter has a situation, a turn, a hook, and sections that each end on a tilt.
> **Load when:** Before drafting any chapter or long scene, and when a finished draft feels shapeless and you need to diagnose it.
> **Related:** [Story Bible](story-bible.md), [Character Sheet](character-sheet.md), [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md), [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md), [Scene Playbook](../scenes/scene-playbook.md), [Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md)

---

## 1. How to use this template

- Fill it in **before** drafting. Aim for 10-15 minutes of thinking, not an essay. A plan longer than a third of the chapter is over-planned.
- The plan is a promise, not a cage. If the draft finds a better turn, take it, and then **update the plan** so revision checks the chapter you actually wrote.
- After drafting, reuse the plan as a checklist: does every section actually end on the tilt you planned?
- Each field below has a note explaining why it exists. Delete the notes in your working copy.

## 2. Blank template

```
CHAPTER PLAN

Chapter #:            Title:
Arc / beat:           (which of the arc beats this chapter serves; see arc-design)
Calendar:             (month, day of week, school event)
POV anchor:           (whose head; list any cut-away POVs)
Target length:        (words)   Sections: (count, sized from the target: 5,000 words = 6-8)
Temperament:          (POV temperament: cynical / sincere / genki / anxious / schemer)
Canon sheet:          (fan fiction only: done? which characters?)

1. SITUATION (one line)
   The concrete, small thing that happens this chapter.

2. TURN (one line)
   The moment the POV's reading of the situation changes.

3. HOOK (one line)
   The final beat. What makes the reader click "next"?

4. EMOTIONAL LINE
   POV starts feeling:            -> ends feeling:
   Leitmotif state:  (settles / stirs / thickens / cracks / pours)

5. SECTION MAP
   § opening (unnumbered): opener type, beat, tilt at end
   § 1:  location / who / beat / TILT
   § 2:  location / who / beat / TILT
   § 3:  ...
   § cut-away (optional): whose POV / what the reader learns that the POV doesn't

6. MONOLOGUE TARGETS
   The 1-3 small asymmetries the POV will dissect this chapter
   (exact words someone says, who got what, who was left out).

7. DIALOGUE SET PIECES
   - banter exchange (who vs. who, snap-back topic)
   - group chatter moment (who is in it; where the POV physically stands)
   - silent "..." beats planned (where and why)

8. PLANTS & PAYOFFS
   Planting:    (callback seeds for later)
   Paying off:  (callbacks from earlier chapters)

9. ADDRESS CHECK
   Any honorific / name changes this chapter? (log in the story bible)

10. GENRE DIALS
   Comedy : drama ratio for this chapter:
   Genre checklist to run after drafting:

11. DON'T FORGET
   Continuity facts, running jokes, banned phrases for this book.

12. VOICE GATES (check after drafting)
   Dialogue share 40-55% / narrative median <= 10 words / <= 5% sentences of 25+ words /
   0 mixed dialogue paragraphs / 0 dash asides in narration / no tic repeated 3+ times
   Short on length? Add a beat from chapter-and-section §8.1, never longer sentences.
```

### Field notes

- **Situation** should be small and concrete: a class duty, an errand, a lunch, a post. Light novels live on small situations with large inner reactions. "The festival begins" is not a situation. "Kuze has to carry the club's banner up four floors with Hina" is.
- **Turn** is the chapter's reason to exist. If you cannot write it in one line, the chapter is filler. The turn usually lands in the second-to-last section, so the last section can react and hook.
- **Hook** should be one of the types in [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md): an arrival, a reveal, a reframe, a silence, or a cut-away tease. A summary is never a hook.
- **Emotional line.** One adjective in, one adjective out, and they must differ. Track the leitmotif's state so escalation stays gradual across the arc.
- **Section map.** Give each section a location, a cast, and a *tilt*, the small shift at its end. See [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md) for section lengths (600-1,200 words) and opener types.
- **Monologue targets.** The analytical monologue needs raw material. Plan the specific phrases and asymmetries in advance so the POV's thinking reacts to something on the page instead of floating (see [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md)).
- **Dialogue set pieces.** Aim for 40-55% dialogue. Planning at least one banter exchange and one group scene guarantees it. Every line is written as pure dialogue, with narration on its own lines (see [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md)).
- **Sections from length.** Size the section count from the target before drafting (see [Chapter & Section §8.1](../structure/chapter-and-section.md#81-reaching-length-the-right-way-r4)). A 5,000-word chapter planned as four sections will either fall short or get padded with long sentences.
- **Voice gates.** The measurable checks from the [Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md). Estimate by hand, or run the optional [voice metrics tool](../tools/voice_metrics.py) if you can execute code.
- **Plants and payoffs.** Every chapter should plant at least one thing and, from Chapter 3 on, pay off at least one.
- **Address check.** Name changes are plot. Plan them and never let them happen by accident.

## 3. Filled example: Sample Chapter 2

This is the plan that produced [Sample Chapter 2](../examples/sample-chapter-02.md).

```
CHAPTER PLAN

Chapter #: 2         Title: Do You Like the Go-Home Club? 2
Arc / beat:          Beat 2 -> 3 ("Pulled in" -> "Small bond")
Calendar:            April, day 10 of the school year, Thursday
POV anchor:          Kuze. Cut-away: Momo (final section)
Target length:       6,000 words   Sections: opener + 8

1. SITUATION
   Rin summons Kuze to the roof and makes him taste-test the lunch she's practicing for Amamiya.

2. TURN
   Rin tells the dojo story. Amamiya once really did stand up for her, and Kuze's theory cracks.

3. HOOK
   Momo has photos of the roof lunch, saved as a draft on an anonymous gossip account.

4. EMOTIONAL LINE
   Kuze starts: wary, suspicious  -> ends: unsettled, oddly warm
   Leitmotif: stirs, then settles "into a shape he didn't recognize"

5. SECTION MAP
   § opener: Amamiya explains Rin to Kuze ("that's just how she is").
             TILT: Kuze reads it as ownership.
   § 1: hallway / Daichi / intro plus the roof "confess or fight" warning.
             TILT: "Because she's in love with Amamiya." (said too flat)
   § 2: roof / Rin / the omelette tasting, spoon comedy.
             TILT: "Thanks. For eating it."
   § 3: roof / Rin / no friends, the dojo story.
             TILT: the theory cracks. "I see."
   § 4: roof / Rin / the deal, "you look like a cashier", her own ledger.
             TILT: the bell; she flees; "he didn't feel as bad as he'd expected."
   § 5: west stairs / Kujou / she saw him count the cans.
             TILT: "Everyone except you."
   § 6: classroom, then shoe lockers / Amamiya's karaoke invitation, "be nice to her."
             TILT: Kuze blurts "I eat her omelettes."
   § 7: riverbank / Hina repays the coffee debt; night LINE with Daichi.
             TILT: "the first entry that had closed itself"; "you know where the pool is."
   § 8: CUT-AWAY / Momo / the anonymous account, saved draft.
             HOOK.

6. MONOLOGUE TARGETS
   - "That's just how she is." (the pronoun, the fence-owner apology)
   - The karaoke invitation's timing ("Why now?")
   - Kujou's "Everyone except you."

7. DIALOGUE SET PIECES
   - Banter: Daichi's joke scoring ("Two points").
   - Banter: the teaspoon/tablespoon snap-back with Rin.
   - Silent beats: tasting the omelette ("..." x3), after the dojo story, at the lockers.

8. PLANTS & PAYOFFS
   Planting: dojo socks; spoon lesson; "you know where the pool is"; Momo's anon account; Kujou's diagram (hinted).
   Paying off: Ch. 1 coffee "tastes like sadness"; Hina's "I owe you one".

9. ADDRESS CHECK
   Hina: reminds Kuze about polite speech ("Yeah. What?" "Better!"). No changes.

10. GENRE DIALS
   Comedy 60 : Drama 40. Run the school-drama checklist plus the rom-com checklist.

11. DON'T FORGET
   Rin's mother is in Osaka until summer. Kuze makes his own lunch. 8:14 arrival.
```

## 4. Diagnosing a failed draft with the plan

If a draft feels flat, check it against the plan in this order:

1. **No turn?** The chapter is a sequence of events. Find the moment where the POV's understanding *could* change, and make it change.
2. **Turn too early?** If the turn lands in section 2, the rest of the chapter is aftermath. Move it later, or add a second, smaller turn.
3. **Sections end on summaries?** Cut the final paragraph of each section. The tilt is usually the line *before* it.
4. **Monologue floats?** It isn't anchored to a planned target. Replace general musing with a dissection of a specific phrase from this chapter.
5. **Dialogue share under 40%?** Convert reported speech ("She told him she'd forgotten") into lines.
6. **Hook is soft?** Swap it for a cut-away that shows the reader something the POV doesn't know.

## 5. Plan variants by chapter type

Not every chapter uses the full shape. Adjust the plan like this:

| Chapter type | What changes in the plan |
|---|---|
| **Comedy / "breather" chapter** | The *turn* can be tiny: a running joke escalates, or a character reveals one private detail. The *hook* can be warm instead of ominous ("It was the first one he'd had all year."). Plan at least three banter set pieces and cap the monologue at one target. |
| **Setup chapter (arc beat 1-2)** | Plant three or more items. Introduce at most two new named characters, each with a full portrait. The turn is usually an invitation or a summons. |
| **Pressure chapter (arc beats 4-5)** | The emotional line only goes down. Every section tilt should tighten something. Plan where the POV *chooses* not to act. Readers must see the choice. |
| **Climax chapter** | The leitmotif reaches "cracks" or "pours". Plan the repetition device (a chant, an anaphoric spiral) and the silent `"..."` duet. Put the turn in the middle and the release in the second half. End on a quiet image, then a cut-away. See the [Climax Chapter](../examples/sample-chapter-climax.md). |
| **Aftermath chapter** | Two or three short sections. Answer one question and open two. Show what changed in the room (who looks, who doesn't). |
| **Cut-away-heavy chapter** | List every POV. Give each non-anchor section one purpose, *what the reader learns that the POV doesn't*, and keep it under 600 words. |

If the user asks for "just a scene", keep fields 1-4 and 6-7 and skip the rest. Even a single scene needs a situation, a turn, and a last line that tilts.
