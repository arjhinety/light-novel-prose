# Scene Playbook: 24 School-Life Scene Recipes

> **Purpose:** Give the writer ready-to-run recipes for the scenes a light novel is built from, each with setup, beats, POV angle, pitfalls, and an original sample snippet in the voice.
> **Load when:** You are drafting or planning a scene that matches a common LN situation (class duty, family restaurant, rooftop lunch, rain, festival, confession, and so on), or you need ideas for what a chapter's scene could be.
> **Related:** [School Life](../setting/school-life.md), [Emotional Climaxes](emotional-climaxes.md), [Chapter & Section](../structure/chapter-and-section.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md), [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Genre Index](../genres/genre-index.md), [Romcom & Comedy](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md)

---

## 1. How to use a recipe

Each recipe has five parts:

1. **Setup:** the institutional reason the scene exists. In this genre, the school system forces the meeting, and the POV rarely *chooses* it. See [School Life §5](../setting/school-life.md).
2. **Beats:** the minimum sequence of moments. Five to eight beats fill one numbered section (600–1,200 words).
3. **POV angle:** what the cynical narrator notices and misreads. This is the part that makes the scene *this voice* rather than generic rom-com.
4. **Pitfalls:** how this scene usually goes wrong in AI drafts.
5. **Snippet:** an original sample in the voice, using the default cast (Minato Kuze as POV).

**Universal scene rules** (these apply to every recipe):
- **Enter late, leave early.** Start on a sound, a line of dialogue, or the POV already mid-thought. End on a tilt, not a summary. See [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md).
- **One small asymmetry per scene.** Every scene should contain one detail the POV can chew on: a word choice, a seat, a gesture withheld. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md).
- **About half dialogue.** If a scene is all narration, it's a reflection section. Label it that way in your plan and keep it short.
- **The POV acts less than he thinks.** A two-word reply after a hundred words of analysis is the house rhythm.
- **Escalate within the scene.** The situation at the end should be slightly worse, stranger, or warmer than at the start.

The recipes are grouped: **daily-life scenes** (§2), **outings** (§3), **event scenes** (§4), **relationship turning points** (§5), and **solitary scenes** (§6). §7 is a mix-and-match table.

## 2. Daily-life scenes

### Recipe 1: Class duty after school
**Setup:** Two students share class duty by attendance number. One wants to leave for club.
**Beats:**
1. The empty classroom, with the board still covered in chalk.
2. The partner apologizes and tries to leave, or stays unwillingly.
3. Work in silence, one task each.
4. Someone from the harem appears at the door, and the partner's face changes.
5. The POV offers the "efficient" solution ("I'll finish, go"), which is really a way of not watching.
6. Alone, he notices the small asymmetry (who was offered what, who was thanked).
7. The walk to the staff room with the class diary, where a teacher hands him an errand as the hook.

**POV angle:** Kuze sees the duty as a ledger. Who owes whom? He frames his own kindness as "efficiency" so he never has to call it kindness.
**Pitfalls:** don't make the partner cruel; she's just normal, and that's what stings. Don't let him say what he's thinking.
**Snippet:**
> "Kuze! Sorry sorry sorry, can I go? Club's starting and senpai's gonna kill me~"
> Hina already had her bag on her shoulder. The apology was a formality, and they both knew it.
> "Go."
> "You're a lifesaver! I owe you one, okay? Seriously!"
> She was gone before the "okay" finished echoing.
> Kuze picked up the eraser. The board was still full of the math teacher's cramped equations, row after row of them, the way the man wrote when he'd lost track of time.
> I owe you one.
> He'd heard that phrase from her three times since April. He'd never once heard it paid back. Not that he'd asked. Asking would make it a debt, and debts made people avoid you.
> He wiped the board clean in long strokes, from left to right, the way you're supposed to.

### Recipe 2: The cleaning-group trash run
**Setup:** Cleaning duty. The trash has to go to the collection area behind the building. Two people, a long walk, no audience.
**Beats:**
1. Someone drafts the POV ("You. You're coming.").
2. The walk: a stilted exchange.
3. At the collection point, other students (fans, gossips) react to seeing the two together.
4. The heroine says something harsh about those onlookers.
5. The POV answers with an unexpected line (a fact, a kanji breakdown, a quiet challenge).
6. She's thrown. It's the first crack in her image.
7. The walk back is silent, but a different kind of silent.

**POV angle:** the heroine's popularity seen *from beside her*. He clocks how the onlookers look at her and how she looks back.
**Pitfalls:** don't turn her harshness into instant softness. The crack is small.
**Snippet:**
> "Ugh. Boys are disgusting." Tachibana dumped the bag into the bin without looking at the three first-years whispering by the fence. "They think I can't hear them."
> "You can hear them."
> "Obviously."
> "Then they're not whispering for their own sake."
> She stopped. "...What's that supposed to mean?"
> Kuze shrugged and turned back toward the building. He didn't really know what he meant. It had sounded truer out loud than it had in his head, which was usually a bad sign.
> Behind him, footsteps, then a hard slap between his shoulder blades.
> "Don't say weird stuff and walk off!"

### Recipe 3: The seat change lottery
**Setup:** End-of-term homeroom. Slips of paper in a box. New seats.
**Beats:**
1. The class buzzes with hopes (the window seat, next to a crush).
2. The POV draws, and the result is good or bad.
3. The reveal of who's next to him.
4. Reactions ripple (envy, pity, laughter).
5. The first exchange between the new neighbors: hostile, awkward, or unexpectedly easy.
6. The tilt: someone *else's* reaction the POV wasn't supposed to see.

**POV angle:** fate as bureaucracy. He reads the room's reaction to *his* seat as a census of how the class ranks him.
**Snippet:**
> The moment Kujou sat down beside him, the temperature of the room changed. Not warmer. Sharper.
> "My. Of all people."
> "Same to you."
> "I'll be counting on you when I fall asleep in classical literature, Kuze-kun. You'll wake me before the teacher notices."
> "I won't."
> "You will." She smiled with half her mouth. "You're the type who can't stand a thing left undone."
> He opened his mouth to deny it and found nothing there.
> Three rows away, Amamiya was looking at them. When Kuze met his eyes, he laughed and turned back to Hina, too quickly.

### Recipe 4: Rooftop lunch
**Setup:** The rooftop is locked. Someone has a key or knows the trick with the broken latch. It's a private place.
**Beats:**
1. The POV's routine of lunch alone, with the wind and the fence.
2. The door creaks. Someone else knows about this place.
3. The territorial exchange ("This is my spot." "It's not *yours*.").
4. Eating at a distance, then the distance shrinks.
5. Bento comparison or a food trade (tamagoyaki for a sausage).
6. One honest line said to the sky, not to the other person.
7. The bell, and the rule is set: "Don't tell anyone about this place."

**POV angle:** his solitude is a fortress, and he notices every brick she removes.
**Pitfalls:** don't make the rooftop a crowded public spot. Its power is secrecy.
**Snippet:**
> Creak.
> Kuze didn't turn around. Only one other person had ever figured out that the latch didn't catch if you lifted the door while turning the knob.
> "You again," Tachibana said.
> "It's my spot."
> "It's a roof. Nobody owns a roof."
> She sat on the far side of the ventilation duct, exactly as far from him as the duct allowed, and unwrapped a bento that was ninety percent rice.
> They ate. The wind flipped the corner of her napkin over and over.
> "...Your tamagoyaki looks good," she said, to the sky.
> "It's sweet. You'd hate it."
> "How would you know what I'd hate?"
> He held the box out without a word. She took one, chewed, and scowled like it had insulted her family.
> "...It's good," she muttered. "Don't look at me."

### Recipe 5: The errand for a teacher
**Setup:** A teacher (Ogata-sensei) catches the POV in the staff room and hands him something to deliver: a CD, a handout, a key. The delivery drags him into a space he doesn't belong in.
**Beats:**
1. The staff room ("Excuse me") and the teacher's casual tone.
2. Another teacher scolds her for being too casual. Comic beat.
3. The item and the target (a clubroom, a heroine).
4. The journey, where he spots someone lurking or hiding on the way.
5. Arrival at the "home base" of another group. The outsider enters.
6. The group's reaction to him tells him his rank.
7. He delivers and leaves, and the one odd detail follows him out.

**POV angle:** he's a courier through other people's worlds. The errand is an excuse to observe.
**Snippet:**
> "Kuze! Perfect timing, my favorite go-home clubber."
> "I'm not—"
> "Take this to the Literature and Broadcast Club, would you? It's Matsuri's script. She forgot it on my desk again. Teehee."
> "Ogata-sensei," said the vice principal from two desks over, not looking up.
> "Sorry, sorry~" Ogata-sensei stuck out her tongue and pushed a folder into Kuze's hands. "You're a good kid. Don't get lost."
> It was four floors and a building away. He'd planned to be on the 4:12 train.
> He went anyway. He always went. That was the trouble with being the kind of person teachers thought of as convenient.

### Recipe 6: The morning commute
**Setup:** The same train, the same walk from the station. Routine is the stage for interruptions.
**Beats:**
1. The POV alone, hand on his chin, thinking.
2. Someone calls from behind and catches up.
3. A light exchange ("Morning!", "Morning.").
4. A third person arrives, usually the harem lead, and the two form "their own little world".
5. The POV physically falls back a step, then another.
6. The group gathers and he's now walking behind a crowd.
7. A verdict line as he walks alone again.

**POV angle:** the step back. Narrate the literal distance growing. That's the emotional content.
**Snippet:**
> "Hina, morning! And... Kuze? Morning."
> "Morning, Haruto!"
> "...Morning."
> There it was again, the small upward tilt at the end of his name, as if Amamiya were checking the label on a jar. Two months in the same class, and Kuze was still a question mark.
> "Nice day today, huh?"
> "Right? It makes me want to skip and go to the beach~"
> The two of them fell into step, shoulder to shoulder, and the sidewalk wasn't wide enough for three. Kuze slowed. Then he slowed some more.
> By the time they reached the gate, he was ten meters back, and neither of them had looked around.

### Recipe 7: Overheard gossip (the desk-sleeper)
**Setup:** Break time. The POV pretends to sleep on his desk. The class talks around him as if he were furniture.
**Beats:**
1. He puts his head down. The sound design of the classroom: chairs scraping, a phone chime.
2. A cluster nearby gossips about someone.
3. A name he cares about comes up.
4. Details escalate. The story being told is wrong, and he knows it's wrong.
5. His urge to correct them, and his decision not to.
6. Someone notices him ("Is Kuze asleep?" "Who cares.").
7. He keeps his eyes closed, and the tilt comes: a new piece of information he didn't expect.

**POV angle:** invisibility as a superpower and as a wound.
**Snippet:**
> "Did you see Tachibana walking home with someone yesterday?"
> "No way. Tachibana? She hates boys."
> "That's what she *says*~"
> Kuze kept his forehead on his arm. The desk smelled faintly of pencil shavings.
> "Who was it?"
> "Couldn't see. Some guy. Not Amamiya-kun, though."
> "Seriously? Then who?"
> He could have lifted his head. He could have said it was me, and it was because we're on the same cleaning group and she missed her train. That's all. He didn't. Explanations only made people look closer.
> "Is Kuze asleep?"
> "Who cares? He's always asleep."

## 3. Outings

### Recipe 8: The three-person family restaurant
**Setup:** The harem lead "mediates" a conflict by dragging the POV and a heroine to a family restaurant, then spends the meal flirting with her.
**Beats:**
1. The waiter asks how many. Someone says "two" before correcting to "three".
2. Seating: the two sit side by side and the POV sits alone across from them.
3. Ordering. *The menu is strategy.* They order shareable dishes, and he orders something unshareable on purpose.
4. The meal: they share and he eats alone. He watches.
5. The heroine needles him. He answers flat and she explodes. The lead soothes her with a head pat.
6. The forced reconciliation ("Say sorry to each other!").
7. The walk home: the POV counts cars.
8. Night: a social media post about the meal (see Recipe 23).

**POV angle:** he decodes the lead's *real* reason for inviting him. He's a chaperone, an alibi.
**Pitfalls:** don't let him voice the theory. It stays inside him.
**Snippet:**
> "Carbonara and the napolitan," Amamiya said. "And we'll split two parfaits, right, Rin?"
> "D-Don't just decide that!" Tachibana's ears had gone red. "...Strawberry. I want strawberry."
> Amamiya looked across the table. "Kuze?"
> "Hamburg steak. Rice. Black coffee."
> Nothing on that list could be passed across a table. He'd chosen it for exactly that reason. If he'd ordered pasta, one of them might have felt obliged to offer him a bite, and then everyone would have had to pretend it wasn't awkward.
> It was simple. He was the reason this was a group meal instead of a date, and the least he could do was stay out of the frame.

### Recipe 9: The study group
**Setup:** Exams in a week. Someone is failing. The POV is quietly smart.
**Beats:**
1. The request, which is either begging or disguised as an order.
2. The location: the library (forced whispers) or a family restaurant (the drink bar).
3. The tutoring. She's worse than expected, and he's better at explaining than expected.
4. A break, where personal questions slip in between math problems.
5. She's surprised by his patience, or he's surprised by her effort.
6. The next exam result as a later payoff.

**POV angle:** competence as intimacy. He can't give compliments, but he can give a clear explanation, and that's his love language.
**Snippet:**
> "I don't get it."
> "Which part?"
> "The part where it's math."
> Hina slumped onto the table so hard her ponytail bounced. The drink bar melon soda fizzed beside her open workbook.
> Kuze turned the page toward himself and drew a line under the question. "Forget the formula. Tell me what it's asking in normal words."
> "...How fast the train is?"
> "Right. So what do we know?"
> She blinked at him. Then she sat up, slowly, and started to read the question again, properly this time, her lips moving.
> He didn't say *good*. He just waited. It seemed to be enough.

### Recipe 10: Karaoke with the group
**Setup:** A class or club outing. The POV was invited as an afterthought and went because refusing takes more energy.
**Beats:**
1. A dim room, tambourines, the touch-panel song selector.
2. The top-caste members sing hits and everyone cheers.
3. The panel comes to him. He passes. Pressure.
4. He sings (badly, or surprisingly well), or someone saves him.
5. A song choice from a heroine that feels aimed at someone.
6. The drink-bar corridor: a quiet, honest exchange outside the room.

**POV angle:** the song lyrics as unspoken messages he refuses to decode, then decodes anyway.
**Snippet:**
> Out in the corridor, the noise from twelve rooms blurred into one dull roar. Kuze pressed the button for iced coffee and watched it fill.
> "You didn't sing."
> Kujou stood by the soft-serve machine, holding an empty cup like a prop.
> "I'm not the singing type."
> "I know. I wanted to hear what you'd pick." She set her cup under the nozzle. "People choose songs the way they choose words. Carelessly, and then they're stuck with them."
> "What did you pick?"
> "Something with a lot of high notes. So no one would listen to the lyrics."

### Recipe 11: The accidental weekend encounter
**Setup:** A day off. The POV is somewhere mundane (a bookstore, a konbini, a supermarket) and runs into a classmate out of uniform.
**Beats:**
1. The POV in his natural habitat.
2. The spot: a heroine, or a teacher, in casual clothes. It's disorienting.
3. Mutual "What are you doing here?"
4. The private-self reveal (a hobby, a younger sibling in tow, a part-time job).
5. An unspoken deal: don't tell anyone.
6. They part, and a detail lingers.

**POV angle:** uniforms are masks. Out of uniform, people are strangers wearing a familiar face.
**Snippet:**
> The girl at the register wore a green convenience store apron and an expression of pure horror.
> "...Welcome," Tachibana said, through her teeth.
> Kuze set down a can of coffee and a rice ball.
> "One hundred and ninety-eight yen." Beep. Beep. "You didn't see anything."
> "I'm buying a rice ball."
> "You didn't see anything," she repeated, pushing the receipt at him. "Also, the tuna mayo's better. That one's gross."
> He took the receipt. "Noted."
> At the door, he heard her mutter something that might have been "thank you for your patronage" and might have been a threat.

## 4. Event scenes

### Recipe 12: Long homeroom decides the festival
**Setup:** Long homeroom. The class rep stands at the board. The festival theme must be chosen.
**Beats:**
1. The rep asks for ideas. Silence.
2. The top-caste group proposes something (a maid café, a play).
3. Instant, performative agreement.
4. A lone objection or an alternative, possibly from the POV.
5. A vote tallied on the blackboard. The social map is made visible.
6. Roles are assigned, and the leftovers go to the unpopular.
7. The POV ends up on the team no one wanted.

**POV angle:** democracy as theater. He counts who looked around before raising their hand.
**Snippet:**
> "Okay, so, maid café: nineteen votes. Haunted house: eleven. Indie film..." The class rep squinted at the board. "One."
> Nobody turned around. They didn't need to. Everybody knew exactly whose hand had gone up.
> "So we'll do the maid café," said Hoshino from the middle of the room, not asking.
> "Well, the vote is—"
> "Nineteen, right? Majority rules~"
> The class rep looked at the board, at Hoshino, and at the chalk in her own hand. Then she drew a circle around *maid café* and put the chalk down very gently, as if it might break.

### Recipe 13: Festival prep after hours
**Setup:** The week before the festival. Staying late with permission. Cardboard, paint, and the smell of markers.
**Beats:**
1. The transformed classroom, with desks pushed to the walls.
2. Everyone busy, or pretending to be.
3. A casting or task dispute.
4. Two people end up working side by side on a menial task (painting a sign, cutting fabric).
5. Paint on a cheek, a shared snack, dusk through the window.
6. An interruption: the harem lead arrives to reclaim a heroine.
7. The POV says one thing he'd never have said in April.

**POV angle:** the festival as a temporary society. The usual rules loosen, and he notices who takes advantage.
**Snippet:**
> "You've got paint on your face."
> "Where?"
> Hina pointed at her own cheek. Kuze wiped his and made it worse.
> "Other side. No, the *other*—ugh, come here." She reached over with her sleeve and scrubbed at his jaw like she was cleaning a window. "There. Now you look like a person."
> "What did I look like before?"
> "A sad raccoon."
> Outside, the sky over the sports ground had gone orange, then purple. Somewhere downstairs the brass band was rehearsing the same four bars for the twentieth time.
> It was, Kuze thought, a very stupid moment to feel comfortable.

### Recipe 14: The cultural festival day
**Setup:** The festival itself: visitors, noise, the class stall, a shift schedule.
**Beats:**
1. A montage of noise (barkers, music, smells of yakisoba).
2. The POV's shift at the class stall. He's out of place.
3. An unexpected visitor (a sibling, a middle school acquaintance, a parent).
4. Free time: someone asks him to walk around the festival "since you're free anyway."
5. A stall game or a haunted house, with physical closeness through fright.
6. A glimpse of something he shouldn't see (a heroine alone, the mastermind scheming).
7. The approach of the after-party.

**POV angle:** a crowd scene through a loner's eyes. Everyone is performing happiness; he watches the seams.
**Snippet:**
> The haunted house was three classrooms of black trash bags and a first-year in a sheet.
> "It's not scary," Tachibana said, holding his sleeve so hard the seam creaked.
> "You're holding my sleeve."
> "It's dark. It's a navigation aid."
> Something wet and cold brushed his neck, a konjac jelly on a fishing line. Tachibana made a sound he would remember for the rest of his life.
> "That was you," she said immediately. "That noise. That was you."
> "Sure."
> "*Say* it was you."
> "It was me."
> She kept holding the sleeve all the way to the exit, and for ten steps after it, until she noticed.

### Recipe 15: The after-festival bonfire
**Setup:** The festival's end. A bonfire on the sports ground. A folk dance with rotating partners.
**Beats:**
1. Fire, dusk, music over the speakers.
2. The legend: whoever you're dancing with when the song ends...
3. The POV watches from the edge, the steps of the school building, or the fence.
4. Someone comes to get him, or doesn't.
5. The dance: partners rotate and the countdown matters.
6. The song ends. Who is in front of whom?
7. A line that's quieter than the music.

**POV angle:** the rotation as fate's lottery again, a mirror of the seat change.
**Pitfalls:** avoid the neat romantic resolution. The music ending one partner early is better.
**Snippet:**
> The partners changed every sixteen beats. Kuze counted them.
> Hina, laughing, stepping on his foot. Sixteen. A first-year he didn't know. Sixteen. Kujou, who danced like she'd been taught by a professional and looked at him like he was a puzzle she'd already solved. Sixteen.
> Tachibana was two places away.
> Sixteen.
> One place away.
> The music stopped.
> Across the ring, where the fire made everything gold, Tachibana was holding Amamiya's hands. She wasn't looking at Amamiya.
> "..."
> Then the applause started, and everyone let go.

### Recipe 16: The sports festival borrowing race
**Setup:** The borrowing race. Runners draw a card and must bring the item or person written on it to the finish line.
**Beats:**
1. The field, the tents, the class colors, and the announcer.
2. A heroine runs and draws a card. Her face changes.
3. She scans the tents, and the crowd follows her gaze.
4. She runs toward the POV's section. Everyone assumes she's going for the lead.
5. She grabs the POV, or someone else, and the choice is a public statement.
6. The finish: the announcer reads the card aloud.
7. Aftermath: the card's text is ambiguous ("someone reliable"), and the POV spends a section on that one word.

**POV angle:** public embarrassment plus private overanalysis.
**Snippet:**
> "And the card says..." The announcer's voice crackled over the speakers. "'Someone you owe a favor to'! Can we confirm that with the runner? ...Confirmed!"
> Scattered applause. A few whistles. Tachibana let go of his wrist like it was hot.
> "Don't read into it."
> "I'm not."
> "You're reading into it. I can *see* you reading into it."
> He wasn't, really. He was thinking about the phrase. *Owe a favor.* So she kept a ledger too.

### Recipe 17: The school trip night talk
**Setup:** The school trip inn. Lights out. Futons in rows. Teachers patrolling.
**Beats:**
1. The dark room and the breathing of classmates.
2. Someone starts it: "Hey... who do you like?"
3. One by one, others answer or dodge.
4. It comes to the POV. Silence.
5. Pressure, jokes, a pillow thrown.
6. A teacher knocks. Everyone freezes and plays dead.
7. After, a whisper from the next futon that is honest only because it's dark.

**POV angle:** darkness as permission. He can't see faces, so for once he can't read them, and he has to listen.
**Snippet:**
> "Kuze. You awake?"
> Gon's voice, from the next futon over. The others had finally gone quiet.
> "No."
> "Verily, a lie." A rustle. "You didn't answer before. When they asked."
> "Because I don't have an answer."
> "Hmm." A long pause. Somewhere down the hall, a teacher's slippers passed and faded. "Kuze-dono. Not having an answer and not wanting to say it are different things."
> "..."
> "Good night," Gon said, and was snoring inside a minute. Kuze lay awake and stared at the grain of the ceiling boards until it started to look like a map.

### Recipe 18: Posted exam rankings
**Setup:** Exam results, top 50 posted on the hallway board.
**Beats:**
1. A crowd in the hallway.
2. Reactions: joy, despair, jokes.
3. A surprising name high on the list (the POV, or a "dumb" heroine).
4. Someone reacts to the POV's rank publicly.
5. A rivalry forms, or a promise made in a study group is kept.
6. A tilt: whose name is *missing*?

**POV angle:** ranks as a social currency he pretends not to spend.
**Snippet:**
> 4th: Kujou Sayo.
> 7th: Kuze Minato.
> 31st: Matsuri Hina.
> "Thirty-first!" Hina grabbed his arm with both hands and shook it. "Kuze! Thirty-first! I was a hundred and something last time!"
> "One hundred and twelve."
> "Why do you *remember* that?"
> Because you told me. Twice. And then you cried a little at the drink bar and pretended it was the wasabi from the fried chicken, which didn't have wasabi.
> "No reason," he said.

## 5. Relationship turning points

### Recipe 19: The shared umbrella in the rain
**Setup:** Sudden rain after school. One umbrella. Two people at the shoe lockers.
**Beats:**
1. Rain sound, the entrance crowded with people waiting.
2. One of them has an umbrella and the other doesn't.
3. The offer, awkward and deflected, then accepted.
4. Walking under one umbrella: shoulders, the umbrella tilted.
5. A small reveal about the other person during the walk.
6. The POV notices his own shoulder is soaked because he tilted the umbrella toward her. He doesn't mention it.
7. At the station, she notices the wet shoulder. The tilt.

**POV angle:** chivalry he refuses to admit to. The narration can mention the wet shoulder only once, late.
**Snippet:**
> "It's fine. I'll run."
> "It's four hundred meters to the station."
> "I'm fast."
> "You'll be fast and wet."
> Tachibana glared at the rain like it had personally betrayed her. Then she ducked under his umbrella with a sound like "tch" and stood exactly one centimeter away from him, like she'd measured it.
> They walked. The rain drummed on the nylon.
> At the ticket gate she turned, finally, and saw his left side.
> "...You're soaked."
> "It's raining."
> "Only on *one* side." She stared at the sleeve. Then at him. Then, very fast, at the floor. "...Idiot."

### Recipe 20: The staff-room aftermath
**Setup:** After a big incident (a fight, bullying exposed, a public outburst), the people involved are called to the staff room.
**Beats:**
1. Chairs arranged in a half circle. The teacher's desk.
2. The accounts: everyone's version, and the POV notices who lies.
3. A mediator (a teacher or a composed classmate) states what happened.
4. A formal apology, bowed and stiff, and not quite sincere.
5. The resolution on paper, with the real questions unresolved.
6. The teacher holds the POV back for a private word.
7. He leaves with more questions than answers.

**POV angle:** institutional justice vs. real justice. He knows the apology is a performance, and he knows it's also the best anyone can get.
**Snippet:**
> "I'm sorry," Hoshino said to the carpet, bowing exactly as far as a person bows when a teacher is watching.
> "Tachibana?" said Ogata-sensei.
> "...I accept."
> And that was it. Two sentences, a signature on a form, and the thing that had eaten the last two weeks was officially over.
> When the others filed out, Ogata-sensei tapped the desk in front of Kuze. "Not you. Sit."
> He sat.
> She didn't say anything for a while. Then she said, not in her teehee voice at all: "That was a stupid, stupid thing you did, Kuze."
> "I know."
> "And I'm proud of you. Don't tell anyone I said that. I'll deny it."

### Recipe 21: The confession behind the gym
**Setup:** A letter in a shoe locker: "Behind the gym after school." Is it love, or a threat?
**Beats:**
1. The letter and the POV's forensic analysis (handwriting, paper, stationery).
2. The day drags. He tries not to care.
3. Behind the gym, the wind and the smell of the storage shed.
4. The person who shows up is not who he expected (or is).
5. The confession itself: short, trembling, direct.
6. His answer, delayed by a long silence.
7. The aftermath. Someone saw.

**POV angle:** he expects a trap because he's been conditioned to. The scene's emotion is the moment he realizes it isn't one.
**Pitfalls:** the confession must be *short*. Long confessions sound robotic. See [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md).
**Snippet:**
> The letter had been folded into a perfect triangle, and the handwriting sloped slightly to the left, like the person writing it had been leaning away from the page.
> *Behind the gym. After school. Please come.*
> *Please.* That was the word he kept returning to. Nobody who meant to hurt him would write *please*.
> Unless they knew that he'd think that.
> "..."
> He went anyway.
> The girl standing by the storage shed was a first-year he'd never spoken to. Her ribbon was red. Her hands were shaking.
> "Um— Kuze-senpai. I— You helped me. In April. With the— at the station, when my pass didn't work. You probably don't remember."
> He didn't.
> "I— I like you." It came out all at once, too loud. A crow took off from the gym roof.

### Recipe 22: The reconciliation walk
**Setup:** After a falling-out, two people end up walking the same way home. Neither apologizes first.
**Beats:**
1. Silence and the sound of footsteps.
2. An attempt at small talk that fails.
3. One of them stops walking.
4. A partial apology (in this genre, apologies are partial).
5. The other's partial reply.
6. They walk on, slightly closer.

**POV angle:** the gap between words and meaning. Neither says "sorry," but both *mean* it.
**Snippet:**
> "The thing I said," Tachibana started. Stopped. Started again. "In the classroom. About you being a coward."
> "You were right."
> "I *know* I was right. That's not the point." She kicked a pebble. It skittered into a gutter. "The point is I shouldn't have said it in front of everyone."
> "..."
> "So." She didn't look at him. "That part. I take back that part."
> "Only that part?"
> "Don't push it."
> They walked. Somewhere between the crosswalk and the station, the distance between their shoulders went from a meter to about half of one.

## 6. Solitary scenes

### Recipe 23: The night-time social media spiral
**Setup:** The POV at home, in bed, phone in hand. He finds a post about something he was part of.
**Beats:**
1. The room: ceiling, lamp, an ordinary evening.
2. Idle scrolling, then a familiar account.
3. The post: an original caption and "(Tap to view photo)." See [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md).
4. He taps. The photo includes him, or excludes him, or shows something private.
5. He close-reads one phrase in the caption.
6. Replies from the harem appear.
7. He throws the phone onto the bed. `"..."` The phrase echoes.

**POV angle:** the gap between the event and its broadcast version. The *editing* reveals the person.
**Snippet:**
> Haruto
> Mediated a little fight between Rin and a classmate today. Food fixes everything lol
> (Tap to view photo)
> He tapped.
> Two parfaits, a carbonara, a napolitan. And at the very edge of the frame, half cut off, a hamburg steak and a cup of black coffee.
> His coffee. His hands weren't in the shot. Nothing of him was, except what he'd ordered.
> *Mediated.*
> Kuze read the word three times. It was a nice word. It sounded like a service someone had provided.
> "..."
> He turned the phone face down on the blanket and stared at the ceiling until the ceiling started to feel like it was staring back.

### Recipe 24: The hideout and the confidant
**Setup:** The POV runs away after something terrible and hides where he always hides. His childhood friend finds him.
**Beats:**
1. The hiding place (behind the storage shed, an empty stairwell).
2. His self-recrimination, in a list-like spiral.
3. Footsteps. The friend arrives, casual, tossing a canned drink.
4. A joke, a laugh, a moment of normal.
5. "So, what happened?" He tells it. We skip the retelling with a scene cut.
6. The friend's long, gentle thesis speech, met by `"..."` after `"..."`.
7. The break. He cries, coughs, or laughs.
8. The friend stays. He says nothing, just stays.

**POV angle:** the only scene where the POV is *seen* by someone else. The narration's analysis drops away because someone else is doing it for him.
**Pitfalls:** don't let the friend's speech become therapy-speak. It should sound like a teenager who knows him. See [Emotional Climaxes §5](emotional-climaxes.md) and [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md).
**Snippet:**
> Crunch. Footsteps on gravel.
> "Yo, Mina. Catch."
> A can arced through the air. Kuze caught it, and immediately dropped it.
> "Hot!"
> "It's hot coffee. It's November. What did you expect?" Enomoto laughed, loud and uncomplicated, and sat down beside him in the dirt without asking. "Now it's sandy. Congrats."
> "How did you find me?"
> "Please. You always come here when things suck." He cracked his own can. "You act like you want to be alone. You never actually want to be alone. It's kind of your whole problem."

## 7. Mix-and-match table

Combine a recipe with a relationship stage and a tone to get a scene idea instantly.

| Recipe | Early arc (strangers) | Mid arc (bond forming) | Late arc (crisis / payoff) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class duty | First contact, mutual irritation | Staying longer than needed | Duty alone, and the empty seat beside him |
| Trash run | Sharp exchange | Walk-and-talk honesty | She takes the trash alone so she can cry |
| Seat change | Hostile neighbors | Passing notes | Separated by the next lottery |
| Rooftop | Territorial dispute | Shared lunch ritual | The rooftop is locked for good |
| Family restaurant | Chaperone humiliation | Study group | Returning to the same booth, a different person across the table |
| Rain | Refused umbrella | Shared umbrella | Standing in the rain on purpose |
| Festival prep | Assigned the leftover role | Paint on a cheek | Sabotage discovered |
| Bonfire | Watching from the fence | Pulled into the dance | The song ends one partner early |
| Night spiral | Resentment | Worry | A post that changes everything |
| Hideout | — | — | The confidant scene |

**Parallel scenes are powerful.** Reusing a recipe later in the series with the same place, the same menu, and a different relationship shows growth without a single line of explanation. See [Series Planning](../structure/series-planning.md).

## 8. Scene quality checklist

- [ ] The scene has an **institutional or practical reason** to exist (duty, errand, rain), and the POV didn't simply decide to seek someone out.
- [ ] It contains **one asymmetry** for the POV to analyze.
- [ ] Dialogue makes up **roughly half** of it, stacked and action-tagged.
- [ ] The POV's **spoken lines are shorter** than his thoughts.
- [ ] There is at least one **physical beat** that carries emotion (a step back, a sleeve held, a wet shoulder).
- [ ] The scene **escalates**: its end state differs from its start.
- [ ] It **ends on a tilt**, not a summary. See [Hooks & Cliffhangers](../structure/hooks-and-cliffhangers.md).
- [ ] School details are **correct**. See [School Life](../setting/school-life.md).
- [ ] No banned phrasing. Run [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md) and the [Revision Checklist](../revision/revision-checklist.md).
