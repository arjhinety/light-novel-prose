# Character Voices

> **Purpose:** How to make every character *sound* distinct on the page: tic design, archetype speech profiles with sample lines, and how voices bend under pressure.
> **Load when:** Creating a character, writing any dialogue-heavy scene, or when a draft's characters "all sound the same".
> **Related:** [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md), [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [Character Design](../cast/character-design.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](dialogue-mechanics.md), [Honorifics & Address](honorifics-and-address.md), [Emotional Dialogue](emotional-dialogue.md), [Diction & Japanese Terms](../core/diction-and-japanese-terms.md)

This file is about **speech itself**: word choice, sentence shape, interjections, and rhythm. For the psychology behind each type (why the tsundere lashes out, what the yandere fears), see [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md) and [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md). For each type's story role, see [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md).

## 1. The shuffle test

Take any group-chatter burst from your draft. Strip every name and action beat. Shuffle the lines. Can you put the names back?

If you can't, your characters don't have voices yet. They have *positions*. In a light novel, where 4-6 people often talk at once with no tags ([Dialogue Mechanics §4](dialogue-mechanics.md#4-group-chatter)), passing the shuffle test isn't optional. It's how the reader follows the scene.

A voice is built from **five dials**:

| Dial | Range | Example contrast |
|---|---|---|
| **Length** | Clipped fragments to run-on gushing | Kuze: "Done." vs. Hina: "Oh-my-gosh, wait, wait, wait, you're done already? That's so fast!" |
| **Register** | Formal to slangy | Sayo: "That would be inadvisable." vs. Rin: "Don't be dumb." |
| **Energy** | Flat to exclamatory | Kuze's periods vs. Hina's exclamation marks and tildes |
| **Directness** | Blunt to evasive | Sayo says exactly the cruelest true thing; Momo never says what she means |
| **Signature tokens** | Interjections, catchphrases, sentence-enders | "My.", "Fueee~", "Whoa, whoa", "verily", "Yup!" |

Set every dial for every named character in the [character sheet](../templates/character-sheet.md). Two characters may share one or two settings, but **never all five**.

## 2. Designing a verbal tic

### 2.1 What makes a good tic

- **One to three per character.** More becomes a parody. One strong one plus one subtle one is ideal.
- **Unshared.** If Hina says "Yup!", nobody else does. If a second character starts saying it, that's a *story event* (they're imitating her, or mocking her).
- **Tied to personality.** Sayo's "My." is a fan snapping open: superior, amused, and old-fashioned. Momo's "Fueee" is a performance of helplessness. The tic should be something the character *would* say, not a random sound.
- **Modulable.** The tic changes under pressure. That change is where acting happens (§4).

### 2.2 Types of tics

| Type | Examples | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Interjection | "My.", "Whoa, whoa", "Fueee", "Eh?", "Tch", "Haa..." | Instant identification in stacks |
| Sentence-ender | "...verily.", "...right?", "...you know~", "...got it?" | Rhythm; a Japanese sentence-final particle feel |
| Catchphrase | "Can't just leave someone in trouble, right?" | Lead characters; ironic when it's betrayed |
| Address habit | Nicknames everyone; calls one person "you" | Relationship signaling ([Honorifics](honorifics-and-address.md)) |
| Structural habit | Always asks two questions at once; always answers a question with a question | Subtle, high-craft |
| Self-reference | "Onee-san will handle it~", "This humble one..." | Onee-san, chuuni, and archaic types |
| Sound play | Elongated vowels ("Fiiine~"), stutters | Genki, tsundere, airhead |

### 2.3 Tics to avoid or handle with care

- **"Desu" or "nya" appended in English.** It reads as parody unless the story is a parody. A cat-girl can purr in a narrative beat without "nya" in every line.
- **Heavy dialect written phonetically.** Suggest Kansai-ben with vocabulary and rhythm ("Nah, that ain't it", "What's that, huh?"), not misspellings.
- **The same tic in every line.** A tic works best at the *start* of a character's turn in a scene and then again at a key moment. Not every sentence.

## 3. Archetype speech profiles

Each profile gives the markers and sample lines in the default cast's voice. The samples are templates for *rhythm*; don't reuse them verbatim across a story.

### 3.1 The cynical protagonist (Kuze)

**Markers:** Very short lines. Periods, not exclamation marks. Answers exactly the question asked, sometimes *too* exactly. Corrects the premise. Rarely uses names. Almost never asks questions out loud, because he asks them all in his head. Deadpan humor through understatement. Stiff politeness as a defense ("Is something the matter?").

**Sample lines:**
- "No."
- "I'll try."
- "Done."
- "That's not what I said. I said you might want to think about it."
- "Go to your club."
- "You've used three already."
- "I like it black. It's my choice."
- "Lame. Two points."
- "I'm in the go-home club. I'll finish up."
- "...Sure."

**Under pressure:** when he finally breaks, the terseness inverts into long, run-on, question-heavy accusations ("Who do you think you are? Say it! Tell me!"). The contrast is the whole payoff; see [Emotional Dialogue §2](emotional-dialogue.md#2-the-outburst).

**Don't:** give him witty one-liners every turn. He's deadpan, not a quip machine. His best lines are the ones that stop a conversation dead.

### 3.2 The harem lead (Amamiya)

**Markers:** Easygoing, friendly, and loud. Repeats phrases ("Got it, got it", "Whoa, whoa"). Justifies with shared values ("right?", "isn't it?"). Speaks in group-inclusive "we" and "everyone". Short righteous speeches with rhetorical tags. Flusters easily around physical contact ("H-Hey, not in public!"). Says girls' given names constantly.

**Sample lines:**
- "Whoa, whoa, calm down, Rin."
- "Got it, got it. I'll share, okay?"
- "Can't just leave someone in trouble, right?"
- "You don't want bad blood between classmates, right? Let's make up."
- "Everyone, let's all go together! It's more fun that way!"
- "Wait, me too!?"
- "...Kuze? Oh. Morning."
- "I want you there! It's not the same without everyone!"

**The craft move:** his lines are *reasonable on the surface* and slightly off underneath. The "right?" tag pressures agreement. The "everyone" includes everyone except Kuze. Write them so a reader could see him as a hero *and* as the POV sees him. Both readings must be available.

### 3.3 The tsundere (Rin)

**Markers:** Sharp, clipped commands. "You" as a name-substitute. Stuttered first syllables when flustered ("W-What!?"). Denials that confess ("It's not like I..."). Insults as affection ("idiot"). `!?` more than `?!`. Physical violence as a punctuation mark. When the dere side slips through, her sentences get *quieter and shorter*, not sweeter.

**Sample lines:**
- "Hey. You. Got a second?"
- "Huh? You don't even know my name? Unbelievable."
- "It's Tachibana. Remember it."
- "Shut--up!"
- "W-What!? I don't care about Haruto or anything!"
- "It's not like I remembered you drink black coffee. The machine just gave me the wrong one."
- "Don't click your tongue at me!"
- "...Thanks. Or whatever."
- "You better remember this after school!"
- (Dere slip, very small) "...Don't go."

**Voice note:** the tsundere's comedy is in **the speed of the denial**. The faster she denies, the more she means it. See [Dere Types: Tsundere](../cast/dere-types.md).

### 3.4 The ojou / elegant sharp-tongue (Sayo)

**Markers:** Complete, grammatical sentences. Formal vocabulary. "My." and "Indeed." as interjections. Precise and cutting logic. Never raises her voice until it matters. Honorifics on everyone, always. Uses rhetorical questions as blades. Theatrical self-claims ("his lawful wife"). Laughs as "Fufu" or "Hehe..." in a lower key.

**Sample lines:**
- "My. You have some nerve, accepting gifts from another girl."
- "Indeed."
- "Kuze-kun, if you intend to sulk, please do it somewhere I can't see."
- "That's inadvisable, Haruto-kun. You as well."
- "Don't drag Haruto-kun into this. Involving others because you can't win is beneath you."
- "Button it up. You look careless."
- "Does someone who calls herself his wife really love him, I wonder?"
- "Fufu. Things are getting interesting."
- "Regret doesn't change the past. Move."

**The mastermind layer:** her lines should hold up on a reread with a second meaning. "Things are getting interesting" is flirtatious the first time and sinister after the reveal.

### 3.5 The genki / no-walls girl (Hina)

**Markers:** Exclamation marks. Tildes. Nicknames for everyone ("Rinrin", "Sayocchi", "Momocchi", "Haru"). Run-on excitement. Repeats words for emphasis ("One! Just one!"). Physical in speech ("Let's go, let's go!"). "Yup!", "Totally!", "Hey there~". Scolds the POV for politeness. Transparent lies, delivered badly.

**Sample lines:**
- "Morning, Kuze-kun! You look like a gloomy raincloud!"
- "Ugh! No polite language! We're the same year!"
- "Hey there~"
- "Yup! Totally!"
- "Let's all talk together! All of us!"
- "I was just, um, checking the floor for, um, dangerous stuff! You know?"
- "Don't tell anyone! It's embarrassing! Yup!"
- "Wow, look at this! So cute~!"

**Voice note:** her *sad* voice is devastating because she almost never uses it. Keep her cheer consistent so the one flat line ("...Yeah. I know.") lands.

### 3.6 The two-faced girl (Momo)

**Markers:** Two registers. **Public:** "Fueee~", "Umyu", "Ehe~", tildes on everything, baby-talk, overlong vowels, name-plus-"-kun~" for the lead. **Private/flat:** short, dry, faintly contemptuous, with no tildes, no interjections, and bare honorifics. The switch between them is the character.

**Sample lines (public):**
- "Fueee, Haruto-kun, your tie's all crooked~ Let me fix it!"
- "Umyu... thank you, Haruto-kun~"
- "Ehe~ I got praised!"
- "Haruto-kun, Kujou-san's saying weird stuff again~ Help~"
- "So many cute ones! Which one would look best on Chirp?"

**Sample lines (private):**
- "Kuze-kun." (flat)
- "What."
- "It's my choice what I do."
- "Don't look at me like that. You're no better."

**Craft note:** write the flat register **without comment the first time**. Let the reader and the POV catch the contrast on their own. Explaining it ("Her voice suddenly turned cold") is weaker than letting the missing tilde do it.

### 3.7 The childhood-friend confidant (Daichi)

**Markers:** Relaxed, teasing, and slangy. Terrible jokes delivered with total commitment. Calls the POV by a private nickname ("Mina"). Laughs at his own jokes ("Hahaha!"). Can switch in one line to dead-serious sincerity, using long, gentle, "I think..." sentences. Asks questions he already knows the answer to.

**Sample lines (light):**
- "Yo, Mina!"
- "Got hit by alien slime. Heroic, right?"
- "Up from two points!? I'm improving!"
- "How long've we been friends, huh?"
- "You always hide when stuff sucks. But you don't actually wanna be alone. Tricky guy."

**Sample lines (serious):**
- "Mina... you're not as rational as you think you are."
- "It's okay to want something. You're allowed."
- "I think 'rational' turned into a leash somewhere along the way."

**Voice note:** his seriousness works because it uses the *same vocabulary* as his jokes, just slower. Don't make him suddenly eloquent; make him suddenly quiet.

### 3.8 The bully queen (Reika)

**Markers:** Performance for an audience. Tildes as mockery ("Hachiya-chan~"). Fillers like "Seriously~", "Totally~", "like". Rhetorical questions that demand agreement ("Right?"). Fake-sweet honorifics ("-chan" as an insult). Chants. Commands framed as jokes ("Say it~"). Addresses the room more than the target.

**Sample lines:**
- "Everyone, look who's here~!"
- "Seriously, what's her deal?"
- "Hey~ Tachibana-chan~ Still think you're cute~?"
- "Come on, Kuze-kun, just say it~ Say it, say it~"
- "Totally~ So gross~"
- "I mean, it's not like I'm *wrong*, right?"

**Under pressure:** she collapses into stammers and sobs when confronted directly, because her power was the crowd. Her collapse lines are short: "T-That's..." "N-No way..." "U... ugh..."

**Content note:** bullying dialogue should be ugly enough to be felt but not gratuitous. See [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md).

### 3.9 The cool young teacher (Ogata-sensei)

**Markers:** Too casual for her job ("Teehee, sorry!"). Gets scolded by senior teachers mid-scene. Repeats herself when she's being fake-casual ("I'm counting on you... counting on you, Kuze!"). Lazy commands. But in the one moment that matters, she says one short, perfect word.

**Sample lines:**
- "Oh, Kuze. You're alone?"
- "Teehee, sorry~"
- "Could you run this over to the club room for me? Pretty please?"
- "Tell them to tone down the lovey-dovey stuff at school, okay? Tell them!"
- "Later." (stopping another teacher from chasing Kuze)

### 3.10 The otaku friend (Gon)

**Markers:** Archaic, grandiose speech in modern situations. "Verily". "-dono". "Good folk". Inverted syntax ("Troubling, this is"). References to anime or games. Genuinely kind under the bit.

**Sample lines:**
- "Troubling indeed, verily."
- "Kuze-dono, the heroine situation is most dire."
- "Now, now, good folk, let us not quarrel."
- "What say you? None desire to see Mitsu-dono in a skirt, verily."

### 3.11 Additional type profiles (quick reference)

For deeper psychology and arcs, see [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md) and [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md).

**Kuudere** (cool, emotionless surface)
- Markers: monotone, minimal words, no exclamation marks, literal answers, and long pauses rendered as `"..."`. Warmth appears as unusually specific attention ("You changed shampoo.").
- Lines: "I see." / "Understood." / "...That's fine." / "You're bleeding. Left hand." / "I don't dislike it."

**Dandere** (shy, quiet)
- Markers: trailing ellipses, stutters, apologies, questions phrased as statements, and voice volume noted in beats ("barely above a whisper"). Opens up in long, fast sentences about a passion (books, birds).
- Lines: "U-Um... sorry..." / "I-It's fine, really..." / "...Is this seat... taken?" / (about her passion) "Oh, that's a first edition, the cover art's different because the publisher changed, and--sorry. Sorry. I talk too much."

**Yandere** (loving to the point of danger)
- Markers: sweetness that is too consistent, possessive pronouns ("my Kuze-kun"), repetition, questions that aren't questions, calm at the wrong moments, and specific knowledge she shouldn't have.
- Lines: "Kuze-kun, who was that girl? Just asking~" / "You'll eat the bento I made, right? Right?" / "I know your schedule. It's cute, how regular you are." / "It's fine. I'll make sure no one bothers you again."
- Craft: menace lives in **what's calm**. Never make her shout until the break.

**Deredere** (openly, sweetly affectionate)
- Markers: warm, direct, unashamed, complimenting, with no stutter. Her honesty embarrasses *others*.
- Lines: "I like you, Kuze-kun! As a person! And also probably the other way!" / "You look nice today."

**Himedere** (princess-type, wants to be treated as royalty)
- Markers: commands, "commoner", dramatic self-regard, and genuine loneliness underneath.
- Lines: "You may carry my bag. Consider it an honor." / "A princess doesn't wait in line."

**Onee-san** (teasing older woman)
- Markers: "Onee-san" as self-reference, drawn-out vowels, teasing questions, calling the boy "-kun" with a smirk, and total composure until genuinely flustered.
- Lines: "Oh my~ Is Kuze-kun blushing? How cute~" / "Leave it to Onee-san." / "You don't have to act tough around me, you know."

**Kouhai** (younger underclassman)
- Markers: "Senpai!" as a name and a greeting, energetic, cheeky, teasing the senpai's loneliness, and sometimes polite-casual speech.
- Lines: "Senpai! Eating alone again? How sad~" / "I'll keep you company. Out of pity, obviously." / "Senpai's so dense, seriously."

**Gyaru**
- Markers: heavy slang, "like", "totally", abbreviations, nicknames, "-cchi", casual touchiness, surprising kindness, and direct judgments.
- Lines: "Kuzecchi, you're like, super gloomy. Smile!" / "No way, that's lit." / "Whatever, I'll help. Not like I'm busy."

**Chuunibyou**
- Markers: grandiose invented titles, fake powers, hand-over-eye poses noted in action beats, capitalized Concepts, and a normal voice slipping out when surprised.
- Lines: "My Dark Right Hand yearns for... the vending machine." / "You dare approach the Sealed One?" / (normal slip) "Wait, seriously? You'll come?"

**Student council president**
- Markers: crisp, organized speech, numbered points, deadlines, "Kaichou" address, and dry humor.
- Lines: "Three points. One, you're late. Two, the form is wrong. Three, sit." / "I don't have time for your feelings. I have time for your signature."

**Delinquent with a heart of gold**
- Markers: rough "you" and "oi", clipped threats, and deep, embarrassed kindness toward animals, kids, and underdogs.
- Lines: "Oi. You. Move." / "...It's not like I was feeding the cat. It was just hungry."

**Imouto (little sister)**
- Markers: "Onii-chan" or "Nii-san", bossy or clingy, domestic topics, and brutal honesty about her brother's social life.
- Lines: "Onii-chan, you have zero friends. It's kind of impressive." / "Dinner's in ten. Wash your hands, loser."

## 4. Voices under pressure

A character's voice is only fully designed when you know how it **bends**. Map each character's baseline to three states:

| Character | Baseline | Angry | Flustered | Hurt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kuze | Terse, flat | Colder, shorter, surgical ("Go back to Amamiya. You're a nuisance.") | Over-precise, formal | Silence (`"..."`); if pushed, the outburst |
| Amamiya | Loud, friendly | Righteous speech, "right?!" | Stutters, "H-Hey!" | Looks away, says nothing (a failure the POV notes) |
| Rin | Clipped, commanding | Physical, shouting, `!?` | Stutter + denial | Quiet, short, and rare. "...I see." |
| Sayo | Formal, cutting | Quieter, more formal, and lethal | Rare. A single "...!" | Unseen by others; revealed only in cut-away |
| Hina | Bubbly | Pleading ("Everyone's awful!") | Flimsy excuses | Flat, no tildes, no nicknames |
| Momo | Sugary | Flat mask, "What." | Overdone "Fueee" | Mutters too quietly for anyone to hear |
| Daichi | Jokey | Rare; slow, disappointed | Laughs it off | Doesn't show it; changes the subject |

**The key principle:** the most powerful moment for any character is when their **tic disappears**. Hina without a tilde. Sayo raising her voice. Kuze talking for a full paragraph. Plan these moments ([Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md)), and don't spend them early.

## 5. Voices in other genres

The dials transfer; the vocabulary changes.

- **Fantasy/isekai:** the ojou becomes a noble lady ("Indeed" still fits perfectly). The genki girl becomes the adventurer party's cheerful mage. The cynical POV's terseness works well as a reincarnated narrator who is unimpressed by magic. See [Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md), [Isekai & Reincarnation](../genres/isekai-and-reincarnation.md).
- **Action:** add attack names and battle calls, and keep them sparse. See [Action & Battle](../genres/action-and-battle.md).
- **Workplace/adult romance:** the tics mature. The genki girl becomes the chatty coworker; the onee-san becomes the section chief. See [Romance & Melodrama](../genres/romance-and-melodrama.md).
- **Villainess:** the ojou register becomes the narrator's own voice. See [Villainess & Otome](../genres/villainess-and-otome.md).

## 6. Common failures

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyone talks like the narrator** | All lines are complete, grammatical, and reasonable | Apply the five dials; make at least three characters use fragments |
| **Tic overload** | "Fueee" in every line | Tic at entry plus one key moment per scene |
| **Archetype as costume** | The tsundere is only ever angry | Map the pressure table (§4); give each type a quiet mode |
| **Therapy-speak** | "I feel hurt when you ignore me." | Let them protect themselves ([Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md)) |
| **Same-sounding girls** | Two heroines both say "Ugh, jeez!" | Unshared tokens; different sentence lengths |
| **The POV quips too much** | Kuze has a zinger every line | Cut half; replace with "..." or "Sure." |
| **Western slang anachronism** | "Yeet", "no cap" in a Japanese high school | Use timeless teen slang: "seriously", "no way", "for real" |

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Every named character has five dials set in the character sheet.
- [ ] Every character has 1-3 unshared tics.
- [ ] The shuffle test passes on at least one group burst per chapter.
- [ ] The POV speaks the least and the shortest.
- [ ] Each character's pressure modes are known, and their "tic disappears" moment is planned.
- [ ] No phonetic dialect, no "desu" or "nya" unless the story is parody.
- [ ] Two-faced characters switch registers without narrator commentary the first time.

## 8. Worked exercise: one situation, eight voices

The same trigger, the POV walking in late with a bruised cheek, answered by each of the default cast. Use this as a calibration drill: if you can't write a line like each of these for your own cast, the voices aren't done.

> **Amamiya:** "Whoa, whoa, Kuze!? What happened to your face? You okay? Did someone-- hey, you can tell me, right?"

Concern, "whoa, whoa", and the pressuring "right?". It's reasonable, public, and slightly performative, since he says it loud enough for the girls to hear how caring he is.

> **Rin:** "Hah!? Who did that? ...Not that I care. I'm just asking. Tell me who."

Anger first, denial second, and the demand hiding the worry. The line contradicts itself, and that's the tsundere voice.

> **Sayo:** "My. A new accessory, Kuze-kun? It doesn't suit you. Sit still; I have a bandage."

Cool teasing, the formal suffix, and practical kindness delivered as an insult. The bandage matters more than the words.

> **Hina:** "Kuze-kun!? Oh my gosh, oh my gosh, does it hurt? Wait, don't answer, obviously it hurts! Let's go to the nurse! Let's go, let's go!"

Repetition, run-on energy, and physical urgency. She answers her own question.

> **Momo:** "Fueee, Kuze-kun, your face~ Scary~" (then, quietly, when no one's looking) "...Who'd you pick a fight with?"

The public register, then the flat private one. The second line is the real character.

> **Daichi:** "Mina. Tell me you at least won."

A joke, but pointed. He already knows it's serious; the joke is his way of giving Kuze room.

> **Reika:** "Ehh~ Kuze-kun got beat up? Seriously~ Kinda deserved, right~?"

Mockery addressed to the room, not to him, with the tilde as a weapon.

> **Ogata-sensei:** "Kuze. Nurse's office. Now." (then, when he's at the door) "...And come tell me who, later."

Casual authority, and one sentence of real care, placed where only he hears it.

> **Kuze himself:** "Door."

Asked what happened, he says the door hit him. One word, which is obviously a lie, and everyone lets it stand. That silence says more about his relationships than any of the eight replies.

## 9. Voice drift over a long series

Across 40, 80, or 100 chapters, voices change, and they should change **on purpose**:

- **Growth softens tics.** Rin's stutter grows rarer as she gets comfortable with Kuze. By the late arcs, it appears only when she's genuinely ambushed by feeling. The reader notices its absence as intimacy.
- **Growth sharpens the POV.** Kuze gradually says more out loud. His first multi-sentence sincere speech (not the outburst, a calm one) should come late and feel earned, like the moment he tells Amamiya "I want her on our team" to his face.
- **Masks crack gradually.** Momo's flat register leaks more often as the mastermind thread tightens: first to Kuze alone, then in front of Sayo, and finally in front of Amamiya.
- **Unintended drift is a bug.** If the genki girl gets quieter because the writer got tired, not because the story demanded it, fix it. Reread each character's last three scenes before writing them again.

Record every intended voice change in the [story bible](../templates/story-bible.md) with the chapter where it happens. That record is how a long series stays consistent across many sessions and many drafts.

## 10. Voices in narration-adjacent text

Character voice extends beyond quotation marks:

- **Chirp posts** carry the same tics: Momo's posts are all tildes and "lol", Sayo would never post (or would post one flawless sentence a month), and Amamiya's posts perform his own kindness ("I got them to make up"). See [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md).
- **Texts and DMs** compress the voice: Rin texts in all-lowercase fragments with no punctuation, Hina sends five messages in a row with emoji described in words, and Kuze replies "ok".
- **Notes and letters** (a letter in a shoe locker, a note passed in class) should sound like their writer speaking, with the handwriting described in one beat ("neat, slanted characters, like a textbook").
- **Free indirect thought from a non-POV character**, in a cut-away, adopts that character's voice in narration. Sayo's cut-away narration becomes cool and formal. Momo's becomes sweet on the surface and sharp at the edge. See [Narration & POV](../core/narration-and-pov.md).

## 11. A final test: read one line cold

Pick a random line of dialogue from your chapter and show it to yourself without context. Ask:

1. Who said this? (Should be obvious.)
2. To whom? (The address form or register should hint at it.)
3. What do they want? (Every line wants something: to win, to hide, to connect, to escape.)
4. What aren't they saying? (If the answer is "nothing", the line is probably flat.)

Lines that fail all four are filler. Cut or rewrite them.

## 12. Building a voice from scratch: a five-minute procedure

When the story needs a new character (a transfer student, a rival's sister, a shopkeeper who recurs), don't start from the archetype label. Start from the dials, in this order:

1. **Pick the want.** What does this person want from every conversation? To be admired, to be left alone, to control, to be useful, to be liked by one specific person. The want shapes directness: people who want control are blunt; people who want to be liked are evasive.
2. **Pick the length.** Talkers or clippers? Match it to the want: the lonely are often talkers, and the guarded are clippers.
3. **Pick the register.** Where did they learn to talk? A rich household (formal), a big noisy family (loud, interrupting), older siblings (slang ahead of their age), books (precise, slightly archaic), the internet (abbreviations, memes rendered in words).
4. **Pick one token and one habit.** One interjection or catchphrase plus one structural habit, such as answering questions with questions, or always adding a qualifier ("probably", "I guess").
5. **Pick the break.** Decide now what their voice sounds like when the token disappears. Write that line first. If you can write the break line, you understand the character.

Then write ten throwaway lines of them reacting to ordinary things (the weather, a test result, a vending machine eating their coin). If three of the ten could have been said by an existing character, adjust a dial and try again.

## 13. Voice and the POV's ear

Remember that in close third, every voice reaches the reader **through Kuze's ear**. That filter adds texture:

- He **notices** tics and sometimes names them in narration ("the usual 'Whoa, whoa'"), which reinforces them without the writer overusing them in dialogue.
- He **misreads** some voices. He hears Amamiya's "right?" as pressure every time, even when it's innocent. Later, the reader can be allowed to suspect the POV's ear was biased.
- He **quotes** lines back at himself days later, so a character's exact wording should be memorable enough to quote.
- He **can't hear** what he doesn't care about: background classmates blur into "someone said" until a voice matters to him. That's why the unnamed chorus in gossip scenes stays anonymous. It's his indifference, rendered as style.

**Checklist addendum:** before finalizing a chapter, reread only one character's lines in sequence, top to bottom, ignoring everything else. They should read like one coherent person having one continuous day. Repeat for each main character.
Any line that breaks that continuity without a story reason is voice drift. Fix it before moving on.
