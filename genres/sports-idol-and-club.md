# Sports, Idol & Club

> **Purpose:** Teach the writer how to write sports, club, band, and idol stories in LN prose: competition arcs, training, performances, and team dynamics, all filtered through a voice that notices people first and scores second.
> **Load when:** The story centers on a club (sports, culture, music), a competition or tournament, an idol or band's rise, or a chapter needs a match, a performance, or a training arc.
> **Related:** [Genre Index](genre-index.md), [Action & Battle](action-and-battle.md), [School Drama & Slice of Life](school-drama-and-slice-of-life.md), [Romance & Melodrama](romance-and-melodrama.md), [School Life](../setting/school-life.md), [Scene Playbook](../scenes/scene-playbook.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md), [Dialogue Mechanics](../dialogue/dialogue-mechanics.md), [Arc Design](../structure/arc-design.md), [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md)

## 1. What readers want

Club and competition stories run on **belonging**. The match, the concert, and the tournament are the occasion; the real question is *will these people become a team, and will the narrator let himself be part of it?*

That makes the genre a natural fit for the base voice. The source protagonist is proudly "go-home club": unaffiliated, watching club members with a twinge he won't name. A sports or club story can be **the story of a go-home-club kid joining something**. His cynicism is the obstacle; the team is the cure.

Readers want:
1. **A clear goal with a date**: nationals, the regional qualifier, the cultural festival stage, the idol showcase.
2. **An underdog** (a team one member short of disbanding, a band with no drummer, an idol unit nobody watches).
3. **Distinct teammates**, each with a weakness that the story turns into a strength.
4. **Competition that is legible** even to readers who don't know the sport.
5. **Big performances** where the prose gets physical, rhythmic, and loud.
6. **Loss.** At least one real loss. Sports stories without losses are brochures.

## 2. How the voice shifts

| Dial | Base voice | Sports / club / idol voice |
|---|---|---|
| POV | Cynical loner | The same, or a teammate. An outside observer POV (the manager, the fan) is a strong option |
| Comedy ratio | Frequent deadpan | High in practice, with team banter; near zero in the final seconds of a match |
| Rhythm | Short beats | Matches use the [exchange-lull engine](action-and-battle.md#3-the-exchange-lull-engine). Performances use long flowing sentences for music and short ones for the crowd |
| Dialogue | ~47% | Higher in practice (team banter), lower in matches (shouts, calls) |
| Description | Portraits | Add **bodies in motion**: sweat, breath, sore muscles, calluses. Venue gets 2-3 lines |
| Inner monologue | Social analysis | Social analysis + **technical analysis** (the opponent's serve, the tempo of the song). The two should merge |
| Earnest peaks | Rare, powerful | More frequent: the genre earns sincerity. Still: undercut before, never after |

## 3. Legibility for non-fans

The reader may not know volleyball rotations or how an idol showcase is scored. Rules:
- **One rule per scene, in action.** Explain a rule the first time it matters, through dialogue or a quick thought, never as a paragraph of rules up front.
- **Anchor every match on a scoreboard.** State the score at every lull. The reader should always know who's winning and by how much.
- **Use a translator character.** The narrator knows nothing about kendo; Tachibana explains it by yelling at him. The newcomer POV is a gift, since his questions are the reader's questions.
- **Reduce the sport to one duel inside it**: the ace vs. the blocker, the vocalist vs. her stage fright. A team game is readable when it's two people and a problem.

> "So what's a *kote*?"
>
> Tachibana didn't look up from wrapping her grip.
> "The wrist. You hit the wrist, you get a point. You hit it *wrong*, you get nothing and I laugh at you."
>
> "That seems like a lot of rules for hitting someone."
>
> "That's the whole *point*, idiot."

## 4. Sports stories

### 4.1 Team composition
Build the team as a **set of complementary flaws**, and give each member a trope and a twist. See [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md).

| Role | Trope | Twist that makes them human |
|---|---|---|
| The ace | Arrogant prodigy | Plays only for a sibling who quit |
| The captain | Serious senpai | Terrified the club dies on their watch |
| The newbie (often the POV) | Go-home club, reluctant | Has a transferable skill (patience, spatial reading, a musician's timing) |
| The comic relief | Loud first-year | Is the only one who notices when someone's hurt |
| The manager | Genki girl or quiet kuudere | Knows every stat, never played |
| The rival school | Mirror team | They want it for a better reason |

### 4.2 The match in prose
Use the exchange-lull engine from [Action & Battle](action-and-battle.md#3-the-exchange-lull-engine), adapted:
- **Exchange = rally/play/bout.** Short lines, sound effects (the squeak of shoes, the thwack of a ball), score calls.
- **Lull = timeout, set break, between points.** The narrator reads the opponent *and his teammates*, and something personal surfaces.
- **The final point** gets its own section, slowed to near-freeze: one detail per line, crowd noise dropping out, a single sound, and then the result on its own line.

### 4.3 Sports sample passage

> "Match point."
>
> The ref's whistle, then silence of a kind a gym only makes twice a year.
>
> Twenty-four to twenty-three. Kaiou serving.
>
> Kuze wiped his hands on his shorts. It didn't help. It hadn't helped for the last six points, but his hands kept doing it anyway, the way your tongue keeps going back to a loose tooth.
>
> Across the net, their server bounced the ball once. Twice. Three times. He'd done four bounces every other serve today. Three meant--
>
> "Short! It's short!"
>
> Enomoto was already moving. He'd known Kuze for ten years; he didn't ask why.
>
> The serve came in low and soft, barely clearing the tape, exactly where nobody had been standing a second ago and exactly where Enomoto now was. He got under it with both forearms. The ball went up, high, higher than it needed to, spinning lazily under the gym lights.
>
> Too high. Their setter was going to have to wait for it.
>
> Kaiou's blockers were already set, three of them, a wall of arms and height.
>
> "Kuze!"
>
> The setter wasn't setting to the ace.
>
> The ball was coming to *him*.
>
> ...Seriously?
>
> He jumped. He had no idea what he was doing. His form was bad and his approach was worse, and every muscle in his body was screaming that go-home-club kids did not do this.
>
> The wall of arms rose to meet him.
>
> He didn't spike. He couldn't have; they'd have eaten it. He just tapped it, a small, rude, cowardly little tip over the fingers of the middle blocker, the kind of shot his own team captain had called *a waste of a jump* in practice on Tuesday.
>
> It dropped.
>
> One bounce, on their side of the line.
>
> The whistle.
>
> Twenty-five, twenty-three.
>
> "..."
>
> Then the whole bench hit him at once, and the gym came back, all of it, all the noise in the world.

**What it demonstrates:** score anchors; the analytical narrator as a tactical asset (the bounce count); team trust in one line; the final point slowed to near-freeze; the newbie's unglamorous skill winning; a silent beat before the release.

## 5. Club stories (non-sports)

Literature clubs, broadcast clubs, film clubs, tea ceremony, astronomy, the "service club" that solves students' problems. The base source puts its harem in a broadcast club and its protagonist in the "go-home club"; club membership is a social map (see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md)).

### 5.1 What drives a club story
- **Survival**: the club needs five members by Golden Week or it's dissolved. This is the classic opener.
- **Project**: an indie film for the cultural festival, a club magazine, a radio drama, a star-watching night.
- **The club room as home**: a physical space with a kettle, a battered couch, a whiteboard of in-jokes. Describe it once, fully, and then let small details change over the series (a new mug, a photo pinned up) to show belonging.

### 5.2 The indie-film or festival-project arc
This is a strong mid-series arc for the base voice. It forces the loner to collaborate, cast, write, and ask people for things.
**Beats:** a project is assigned or chosen; nobody volunteers for the key role; the narrator has to *ask*, which is the hardest thing he does; an unlikely person steps up; filming comedy; a creative conflict; the mastermind edits something; the screening, where the audience's reaction is the verdict.

### 5.3 Club-room sample passage

> The Literature and Broadcast Club's room had been a storage closet until three years ago, and it had not entirely forgotten.
>
> There was a mop in one corner that nobody used and nobody threw out. There was a stack of cardboard boxes labeled *1998 SPORTS FESTIVAL - DO NOT OPEN* that somebody had, at some point, opened. There was a kettle, and a single outlet, and a strict rotation for who got to use the outlet: the kettle, then Shirasagi's phone charger, then whatever Amamiya was trying to plug in that week.
>
> Kuze had been in this room twice before. Both times he'd been delivering something. Both times he'd left in under a minute.
>
> Today he was sitting down.
>
> Matsuri was on the couch, and she informed him pleasantly.
> "You're sitting in Haruto's chair."
>
> "Should I move?"
>
> "Nope! He's late, so he forfeits it! That's the rule!"
>
> "Since when?"
>
> "Since just now! Yup!"
>
> Kujou, at the window, turned a page of her book without looking up.
> "The rules of this club are whatever Matsuri-san says they are when Amamiya-kun is late. It's been that way since April."
>
> "It's a *good* system."
>
> "...Is it."
>
> The kettle clicked off. Nobody moved to get it. Then, after a moment, all three of them looked at him: the newcomer, the one in the forfeited chair, the one nearest to the outlet.
>
> Kuze sighed and got up to make the tea.
>
> Somehow, it felt like being let in.

**What it demonstrates:** the club room as a character; in-jokes that exclude and then include; a small ritual (the tea) as a belonging beat; the house-voice deadpan ("...Is it.").

## 6. Training montages in prose

A montage compresses time. In prose it can easily turn into a dull list. Techniques:

### 6.1 The dated list
Each day gets one line, and the lines change subtly to show progress, with one line that breaks the pattern.
> Monday: fifty receives. Missed thirty-one.
> Tuesday: fifty receives. Missed twenty-six.
> Wednesday: fifty receives. Missed twenty-six. Enomoto said it was fine. It was not fine.
> Thursday: rain. The gym leaked. Fifty receives anyway. Missed nineteen.
> Friday: he didn't count.

The last line is the payoff: he stopped counting because he stopped needing to, or because he stopped caring about the ledger. Either reading works.

### 6.2 The object that changes
Track one object across the montage: blisters on a palm, a notebook of lyrics going from blank to crossed-out to full, the sole of a running shoe.

### 6.3 The refrain
One line of dialogue repeated at the end of each training day, with a different response each time:
> The captain didn't raise his voice.
> "Again."
> "..."
> "Again."
> "Yes."
> "Again."
> "Yes!"

### 6.4 Montage rules
- Maximum 400-600 words per montage.
- One comic beat, one setback, one tiny victory.
- End with a scene, not a summary: drop back into real time for the moment that matters.

## 7. Idol and band stories

### 7.1 What's different
- **The stage is a battlefield and a confession at once.** The performance is where characters say what they can't say in dialogue.
- **Public vs. private face** is the core theme. It matches the base voice's obsession with the gap between what people show and what they mean. Idol stories are full of Chirp-style posts (see [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md)): the cheerful selfie posted ten minutes after crying in the green room.
- **Fans are a chorus.** Stacked crowd lines, glow-stick colors, and the chant of a member's name.

### 7.2 Writing music in prose
Music can't be heard on a page. Write the **effect**, not the notes:
- **The body**: the drummer's count-in, feedback in the monitors, the bass in the sternum.
- **The crowd**: stillness, then a wave, and the light sticks changing color.
- **The performer's inner experience**: the moment the fear drops away, the lyric that suddenly means something personal.
- **Selective lyrics**: quote at most one or two original lines, and only when the words matter to the story.
- **Sentence shape mirrors the song.** Build long, flowing sentences for a rising chorus, then cut to a single line for the silence before the last note.

### 7.3 Idol sample passage

> The green room smelled like hairspray and convenience-store onigiri.
>
> Matsuri sat in front of the mirror with her ponytail half-tied, the ribbon hanging from her fingers, looking at herself as if she'd never met this person.
>
> Someone called from the door.
> "Hinacchi, five minutes!"
>
> "Yup!"
> Bright and instant and perfect. The door closed.
>
> Her smile stayed on her face for exactly one second longer. Then it went out, gently, the way a light goes out on a timer.
>
> She picked up her phone. Chirp, open to her own account. The last post was from this morning: a peace sign, a filter with little sparkles, *Showcase today!! Everyone please come!! ♪*.
>
> Twenty-three likes.
>
> The other unit, the one on before them, had posted at the same time. Four thousand.
>
> She put the phone face down on the counter.
>
> "..."
>
> Then she tied the ribbon, tight, a neat bow, and looked at the mirror again and did the smile once, twice, three times, until the girl in the glass stopped looking like a stranger.
>
> "Yup. Yup. Okay."
>
> Outside, the stage lights were coming up, and somewhere in the dark, in the fourth row, sitting stiffly with a borrowed light stick he had no idea how to switch on, was a boy from the go-home club who'd told her flatly that idols weren't his thing.
>
> She didn't know he was there yet.

**What it demonstrates:** public vs. private face in one small scene; a social-media number as the emotional blow; a signature tic ("Yup!") turned into self-soothing; an omniscient pull-back at the end for a romantic hook.

### 7.4 Band sample passage (the performance)

> Enomoto clicked the sticks four times, and the gym stopped being a gym.
>
> Kuze had never been on a stage. He'd barely been *near* one; the closest he'd come was carrying chairs for the cultural festival in first year. Now there were three hundred faces out there in the half-dark, and his fingers were on the strings of a bass he'd owned for six weeks, and the first note came out of him like it belonged to someone else.
>
> It was wrong. A half-step flat.
>
> Nobody noticed, because Tachibana came in with the guitar right on top of it, loud and furious and exactly on time, and the sound of it filled up every corner of the room, pushed the audience back in their chairs, and swept his bad note away as if it had never been played, as if she'd decided on his behalf that it didn't count.
>
> He looked at her. She didn't look back. She was glaring at the audience as if they owed her money.
>
> Then she stepped to the mic.
>
> For one bar, she didn't sing.
>
> The whole room waited.
>
> Kuze waited.
>
> And then she did, and it wasn't pretty, not the way the choir club was pretty. It was raw and a little too loud and cracked on the high note in the chorus, and it was the most honest thing he had ever heard come out of her mouth.
>
> In the front row, the first-years started shouting her name.

**What it demonstrates:** a count-in as the opening beat; the newcomer's error rescued by a teammate (trust); a long flowing sentence for the music; a one-bar silence as a single line; imperfection as the emotional truth.

## 8. Tropes: execution and failure

| Trope | Execute well | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **The club facing dissolution** | A specific deadline and a specific missing member type | Vague "not enough members" with no clock |
| **The genius who quit** | The reason for quitting is a wound the story must heal | Quit for no reason, returns for no reason |
| **The rival school** | They're sympathetic and want it for a good reason | Cartoon villains |
| **Training camp** | Summer camp comedy plus one night conversation that changes a relationship | Pure training, no feelings |
| **The injury** | Arrives at the worst moment, tests a relationship | Healed off-page with no cost |
| **The manager's crush** | Revealed through stats she tracks too carefully about one player | Declared in chapter 1 |
| **Idol's secret dating ban** | The ban creates real stakes and tension | Ignored once the romance starts |
| **The first concert flop** | The flop is specific (the sound failed, they froze) and the recovery is earned | "It went badly" in summary |
| **"Let's go to nationals!"** | The promise is personal (to someone who can't come) | A slogan |
| **The final match loss** | The team loses, and something more valuable is won and named | A loss that is actually a win |

## 9. Scene recipes

1. **The recruitment**: the club captain corners the narrator with a flyer; his excuses; her counters; he says "one week, trial only" and the reader knows he'll stay.
2. **The first practice**: he's bad at everything; one small moment of natural talent; a teammate notices.
3. **The locker-room/club-room talk**: after a loss, someone says what they've been holding back.
4. **The practice match against the rival**: the exchange-lull engine; lose, but learn the opponent's tell.
5. **Training camp night**: the futon room, whispers, a pillow fight, and one person awake on the veranda.
6. **The injury**: the sound, the silence, the teammate's face.
7. **The final match / performance**: score anchors, slow final point, release.
8. **The aftermath**: the bus ride home, the convenience-store celebration, the one who cries in the bathroom.

## 10. Chapter and arc shapes

- **Match chapter**: 1-2 lulls per section, with a score line at every section break. Cut chapters at a timeout or set break, never on a rally.
- **Club arc (10-20 chapters)**: recruitment, the first gathering, first failure, a new member (often a trope with a secret), a training arc, the practice match loss, internal conflict, reconciliation, the main event (split into 2-4 chapters), and the aftermath with the next goal.
- **Idol/band arc**: formation, first gig flop, a viral moment (good or bad), public/private conflict, a member's crisis, the showcase, and the new stage.

## 11. Naming and setting conventions

- Tournaments: "the Inter-High", "the Spring Koshien", "the prefectural qualifier". For fictional events, use a region plus the event ("the Kanto Regional Showcase").
- Clubs: "the Light Music Club", "the Literature and Broadcast Club", "the Go-Home Club" (joke).
- School rivals: a school name with a strong kanji feel (Kaiou, Hakuran, Shiranui).
- Idol units: an English phrase with odd punctuation (*Stella☆Pocket*, *Mint Parade!*). Bands: an in-joke name nobody can explain.
- Venue details: the gym's squeaky floor, the livehouse's sticky floor, the cultural festival's cardboard stage.

## 12. Genre-specific revision checklist

- [ ] Is the goal specific, with a date or deadline?
- [ ] Is there at least one real loss in the arc?
- [ ] Does each teammate have a flaw that becomes a strength?
- [ ] Is every rule explained only when it first matters, in dialogue or action?
- [ ] Is the score stated at every lull in a match?
- [ ] Is the final point or final note slowed down, one beat per line?
- [ ] Are montages under ~600 words, ending in a real-time scene?
- [ ] Does the performance write the *effect* of music rather than describing notes?
- [ ] Are public and private faces contrasted in idol stories?
- [ ] Is comedy strong in practice and near zero in the final moments?
- [ ] Have you cut sports clichés: "gave it their all", "left everything on the court", "the crowd went wild" (write *what* the crowd did), "a moment frozen in time"? See [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md).
- [ ] Does the narrator's belonging arc move forward at least one step per chapter?
