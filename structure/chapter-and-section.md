# Chapter & Section Anatomy

> **Purpose:** Define the physical shape of a light-novel chapter (title, opening, numbered micro-sections, scene skips, endings, and length) so every chapter reads like a serialized web-novel episode.
> **Load when:** Before drafting or restructuring any chapter, and whenever a draft "feels like an essay" instead of an episode.
> **Related:** [Arc Design](arc-design.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md), [Series Planning](series-planning.md), [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md), [Punctuation & Typography](../core/punctuation-and-typography.md), [Chapter Plan template](../templates/chapter-plan.md), [Sample Chapter 01](../examples/sample-chapter-01.md)

---

## 1. Why structure matters in this voice

A translated web novel is read **one episode at a time**, usually on a phone, often in a queue between other series. Its structure is built for that reader:

- The **chapter** is one sitting (roughly 2,500-4,500 words). It must have its own small shape: a situation, a turn, and a hook.
- The **numbered section** is one breath (roughly 600-1,200 words). The reader can stop at any numeral and feel they got something.
- The **one-line paragraph** is one heartbeat. It sits inside the section and is covered in [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md).

If you write a chapter as one undivided block, the voice loses its pulse. The short-beat narration of [Rhythm & Flow](../core/rhythm-and-flow.md) needs a matching short-beat architecture: the prose breathes in short sentences, and the chapter breathes in short sections.

The second reason is **serialization psychology**. Web-novel readers decide whether to continue within the first 200 words and whether to click "next" within the last 50. Everything in this file serves those two moments.

## 2. Chapter format (the canonical skeleton)

```
Chapter 12: Do You Like Family Restaurants? 3

<opening beat: a sound effect, a line of dialogue, or a time/place stamp>

...section 1 prose...

2

...section 2 prose...

3

...section 3 prose, ending on a hook...
```

Rules:

1. **The heading is one line.** Use `Chapter N: Title`. No subtitle, no epigraph, and no "Previously on".
2. **Section 1 has no numeral.** The chapter heading serves as its marker. Numerals start at `2`. (Some writers print `1` as well. Pick one convention per story and keep it.)
3. **A numeral sits alone on its line**, with a blank line above and below. Never use `Section 2`, `Part II`, or `## 2`, because that breaks the web-novel look.
4. **Scene skips inside a section** use a single blank line plus a time phrase, or an optional centered `* * *` (see §6).
5. **Nothing follows the final line.** No author's note inside the prose. If the user wants notes, they go after a `---` separator, outside the chapter.

## 3. Chapter titles

Titles in this genre are playful, serial, and signal the arc. Choose **one pattern for the whole series** and vary only inside it.

### 3.1 The question-form arc title (the house default)

A recurring question with a part number. The question names the arc's object, and the number counts the episodes.

- `Do You Like Family Restaurants? 1`, `... 2`, `... 3`
- `Do You Like Hangers-On? 4`
- `Do You Like Rumors? 2`
- `Do You Like Black Coffee? 1`

Why it works: the question is subtly the POV character's cross-examination of the world, which is the same interrogative engine as the [inner monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md). It promises a recurring format, and it is funny when the object is petty ("Do You Like Cleaning Duty?") and chilling when it isn't ("Do You Like Being Hated?").

### 3.2 Other acceptable patterns

| Pattern | Example | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive noun phrase | `The Girl Who Hid Behind the Wall` | Character-introduction chapters |
| Line of dialogue | `"You Don't Have to Be Polite With Me!"` | Chapters whose turn is a line |
| Deadpan statement | `Kuze Does Not Go to the Festival` | Comedy-heavy chapters |
| Event plus number | `Cultural Festival, Day One` | Event arcs |
| Prologue / Epilogue | `Prologue: Chapter Two Begins` | Arc boundaries |
| Interlude | `Interlude: Kujou Sayo's Diagram` | Omniscient cut-away chapters |

### 3.3 Title don'ts

- Don't spoil the turn: `Chapter 20: Amamiya Betrays Rin` kills the chapter.
- Don't use grand or literary titles ("Echoes of the Unspoken Heart"). They read as AI-generated fantasy and clash with the deadpan register. See [Anti-Robotic](../revision/anti-robotic.md).
- Don't mix patterns mid-arc.

## 4. Openings (vary them across consecutive chapters)

The first 1-3 lines decide whether the reader stays. Rotate through these five openings, and never use the same one three chapters in a row.

### 4.1 The sound effect

```
Ding-dong-dang-dong.

The bell rang out over Seiryou High, as cheerful and indifferent as always.
```

The strongest LN marker. It works because it is a manga panel in prose: a sound first, then the world. Use it for school-routine openings.

### 4.2 Dialogue in media res

```
"Kuze. You're on class duty with me today, so don't you dare run home."

Tachibana Rin had both hands flat on his desk before he'd even set his bag down.
```

The speaker can stay unidentified for a line or two. The reader works to place the voice, and that small work pulls them in. This depends on distinct character voices; see [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md).

### 4.3 Time/place stamp with a dash

```
The next day--

Kuze's desk was exactly where he'd left it. The paper taped to it was not.
```

```
Late May, second year of high school--
```

The dash is the house signal for "time has jumped, pay attention". It pairs well with a reveal in the second line.

### 4.4 The protagonist alone, thinking

```
On the way to school, a boy walked with one hand on his chin, frowning at nothing.

Minato Kuze. Second-year. Go-home club. As ordinary as a boy could get.
```

This is the "resting state" opening. Use it after a big chapter so the story can exhale, and to restate the POV's core question.

### 4.5 The cold open from another POV

```
In a room lit only by a desk lamp, a girl wrote five names on a sheet of paper.

Then, very carefully, she drew arrows between them.
```

A brief omniscient cut-away (see [Narration & POV](../core/narration-and-pov.md)) that shows the reader something Kuze doesn't know. Keep it under 300 words, then cut to Kuze with a numeral.

### 4.6 Opening don'ts

- **No weather-report openings** ("The sun shone brightly over the city as another day began.") unless the weather is immediately contradicted or used ironically.
- **No recaps.** Web-novel readers just read the last chapter. At most one clause of recap, folded into action.
- **No thesis statements** ("Kuze had always believed that people were fundamentally selfish."). Show the belief in a petty observation instead.

## 5. Numbered micro-sections

### 5.1 What a section is

A section is **one continuous scene** or **one continuous thought-spiral**. When the scene changes location or time significantly, or the POV shifts, start a new numeral.

Typical chapter layout (3,500 words):

| Section | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | 700-1,000 | Situation set up; the POV observes |
| 2 | 800-1,200 | Contact: dialogue-heavy scene, a small complication |
| 3 | 600-1,000 | The turn: the POV re-reads the situation |
| 4 | 300-800 | Aftermath or cut-away; the hook |

A short chapter (1,800 words) can be two or three sections. Never go above seven sections in a chapter, because the pulse turns into a flicker.

### 5.2 Every section tilts

Each section must **end on something that changes the reading** of what came before. That tilt can be small, such as a look, a line, or a silence. Section endings are a lighter version of chapter hooks; see [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md) for the full taxonomy.

```
Amamiya took the CD from Kuze's hand and passed it to Matsuri himself.

"Here, Hina. From Ogata-sensei."

Kuze had said her name. He'd been standing right there.

Why pass it through himself?

2
```

### 5.3 Sections as rhythm devices

- **Short sections (200-400 words)** after a long one act like a slammed door. Use them for reveals and cut-aways.
- **A section that is one line** is a nuclear option. Use it at most once per arc:

```
6

Tachibana Rin did not come to school that day.

7
```

- **Alternate texture:** a dialogue-heavy section, then a monologue-heavy section, then dialogue again. Two monologue sections back-to-back sag.

## 6. Scene skips inside a section

When time jumps but the section's emotional thread continues, skip without a numeral:

**Option A: blank line plus time phrase**

```
Kuze handed over the daily report and bowed.

After school--

The Literature and Broadcast Club room was louder than a club room had any right to be.
```

**Option B: centered asterisks**

```
* * *
```

Use Option A by default. It is lighter and more web-novel. Use asterisks only if the story's formatting already does.

Common skip phrases (vary them): *After school-- / The next day-- / An hour later-- / That evening-- / Monday came, as Mondays do-- / By the time the bell rang--*.

**Don't** skip with a summary paragraph ("The rest of the day passed uneventfully, with classes and lunch and more classes."). One clause is fine ("The rest of the day passed without incident."), but then cut.

## 7. Section and chapter endings

### 7.1 Good endings (tilts)

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Reveal | *Kuze's real stop was the same station as theirs.* |
| Arrival | *Tachibana stood in the doorway.* |
| Reframe | *Behind them, Shirasagi smiled, just slightly, at her phone.* |
| Silence | `"..."` after a devastating line |
| Verdict | *Hypocrite. It was the only word that fit.* |
| Question | *So why had she lied?* |
| Sound | *Ring, ring, click.* |

### 7.2 Bad endings

- **Emotional summary:** "He felt a complex mix of emotions about everything that had happened." Delete it and end one line earlier.
- **Moral:** "Maybe, he thought, people weren't so bad after all." Too neat, and it closes the question the reader wanted to keep open.
- **Scheduling:** "Tomorrow would be another day." That's a weather report for time.
- **Fade to sleep** ("He closed his eyes and drifted off.") unless the sleep itself is ominous or ironic.

The rule: **the last line should make the reader want the next line.** If the next line would be "and then nothing happened", cut back until it isn't.

## 8. Chapter length and the output-limit problem

| Request | Target |
|---|---|
| "A chapter" | 2,500-4,500 words |
| "A short chapter" | 1,500-2,500 |
| "A long chapter" / "volume-style chapter" | 5,000-8,000, with 6-7 sections |
| "A scene" | 800-1,500, one or two sections |

If your output limit is smaller than the request:

1. Write **complete sections** only. Never stop mid-sentence or mid-section.
2. End on a section-ending tilt, which you should have anyway.
3. After a `---` separator, state briefly which section number to continue from.

## 9. Chapter-level shape: situation, turn, hook

Every chapter, however small, has a three-part motion. Plan it with the [Chapter Plan template](../templates/chapter-plan.md).

1. **Situation.** One concrete, ordinary thing: class duty, an errand for Ogata-sensei, sharing a table at a family restaurant, a Chirp post.
2. **Turn.** The moment the POV's reading changes. Kuze realizes he was invited only as an alibi; a kind act looks staged; a cruel girl turns out to be scared.
3. **Hook.** The last beat reopens the question, pushing past the turn.

Worked example: *Chapter 3: Do You Like Family Restaurants? 1*

- **Situation:** Amamiya insists Kuze and Tachibana "make up" over dinner.
- **Turn (section 3):** Kuze notices Amamiya seats himself beside Tachibana, orders food made for sharing, and never once asks Kuze a question. Kuze realizes he's the alibi that lets Amamiya eat alone with one girl without the others getting jealous.
- **Hook:** That night on Chirp: `Haruto / Got these two to make up today, I'm kind of a genius lol / (Tap to view photo)`. The photo includes Kuze's hamburger. He never saw it being taken.

## 10. Formatting details that preserve the look

- Paragraphs are **separated by blank lines**, never indented.
- Dialogue lines each get their own paragraph, even rapid-fire ones.
- The silent `"..."` gets its own paragraph.
- Sound-effect lines get their own paragraph.
- Numerals are bare digits: `2`, not `2.` or `**2**`.
- In-world posts are formatted as blocks (see [Social Media & Texts](../setting/social-media-and-texts.md)).
- No Markdown headings inside the prose. The only heading is the chapter line, if your output medium supports one at all. Otherwise, plain text `Chapter 12: ...` is fine.
- No bold or italics for thoughts. Free indirect thought is plain text (see [Narration & POV](../core/narration-and-pov.md)). Italics are reserved, rarely, for a remembered line or a sign.

## 11. Prologues, interludes, and extras

- **Prologue:** a 500-1,500 word cold open, often from the end of the arc or from another POV. Label it `Prologue` or `Prologue: <Title>`.
- **Interlude:** a short chapter (800-2,000 words) from a non-POV character, placed between arcs. This is how the mastermind thread gets fed (see [Series Planning](series-planning.md#4-the-mastermind-thread)).
- **Side story / SS:** light, comic, and non-canonical-feeling. It can break the fourth wall a little. It is good for holidays and for rewarding the reader after a heavy arc.
- **Afterword:** never inside the chapter. It goes outside the `---`, and only if the user asks.

## 12. Chapter types and their shapes

Not every chapter has the same job. Name the chapter's job before you plan it, because the job changes the section layout.

| Chapter type | Job | Typical shape | Section count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Routine** | Show the POV's normal day and restate his core question | Walk to school, then classroom chatter, then a small observation, then a tilt | 3-4 |
| **Contact** | Force the POV into a scene with a heroine | Errand or duty, then private conversation, then an unexpected side of her, then the others notice | 3-5 |
| **Social** | Group outing; the POV as outsider | Invitation, then the "little world" forms, then the POV reads the asymmetries, then the verdict | 4-5 |
| **Pressure** | Rumor, photo, or social-media event | Discovery, then the class atmosphere shifts, then confrontation, then the POV's rational retreat | 4-6 |
| **Climax** | Outburst, confession, breakdown | Escalation, then the break (long section), then fallout, then the silent aftermath | 4-6, with one very long section |
| **Aftermath** | Staff room, apology, loose ends | Resolution on the surface, then unanswered questions, then a cut-away | 3-4 |
| **Interlude** | Another POV; feed a thread | One or two sections only | 1-2 |
| **Breather** | Comedy, found-family warmth | Gag setup, then escalation, then callback punchline, then a quietly warm final beat | 3-4 |

**Rhythm across chapters:** don't run two Pressure chapters without a Contact or Breather between them unless you are deliberately in the climax run of an arc (see [Arc Design](arc-design.md#4-pacing-across-10-20-chapters)).

### 12.1 Worked skeleton: a Contact chapter

*Chapter 5: Do You Like Class Duty? 2*, 3,200 words

- **(1), ~800 words.** Opening type: dialogue in media res. Matsuri Hina drops into the seat in front of Kuze: "Kuze-kun! You're on duty with me today, so no escaping!" Kuze's inner ledger notes that she said "no escaping" as if he'd tried before. He hadn't. Portrait of Hina (see [Description & Portraits](../core/description-and-portraits.md)). Tilt: she uses polite speech with Amamiya and none with Kuze. Why?
- **2, ~1,000 words.** The duty itself: blackboard, trash, the daily report. Banter in snap-back rhythm. Hina scolds him for "-san". Kuze's monologue on the cost of first names. Tilt: Hina stops wiping the board and asks, very quietly, whether Kuze thinks Amamiya "likes anyone."
- **3, ~900 words.** Kuze answers with logic. Hina laughs too brightly. The analytical engine runs: was that laugh relief, or hurt? He suspects his own jealousy for suspecting. Tilt: Amamiya appears at the door with two cans of juice, one for Hina, and none for Kuze.
- **4, ~500 words.** Walking home alone. A Chirp post from Hina: `Class duty done~! Thanks for the juice ♪ / (Tap to view photo)`. The photo is cropped so that only Amamiya's hand is visible. Final line: *Kuze had carried the trash. He wasn't in the photo either.*

Notice the rhythm: dialogue-heavy, then mixed, then monologue-heavy, then short and quiet. The chapter ends on a verdict-tilt that also serves as a hook, because the reader now wants to know what Hina really thinks.

## 13. Common structural failures and fixes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The essay chapter** | Pages of monologue with no scene | Put the monologue *inside* a scene; let dialogue interrupt it every 150-250 words |
| **The transcript chapter** | Pure dialogue, no reading of it | After every 5-8 lines of banter, give one or two lines of the POV's interpretation |
| **The flat line** | Every section is the same length and texture | Vary section length and alternate dialogue-heavy and monologue-heavy sections |
| **The early exit** | The turn happens in section 1 | Push the turn to the second half; section 1 is for the situation |
| **The double ending** | A hook, then another paragraph explaining it | Delete everything after the hook |
| **The recap tax** | Each chapter opens by restating the last one | One clause at most, folded into action |
| **The orphan cut-away** | Another POV appears with no payoff | Every cut-away plants something the POV discovers later (see [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md#6-cut-away-teasers)) |

## 14. Quick checklist

- [ ] Heading on one line, in the story's title pattern
- [ ] Opening type differs from the last two chapters
- [ ] First 200 words contain a concrete situation and a character voice
- [ ] 3-5 numbered sections (numerals bare, on their own lines)
- [ ] Every section ends on a tilt, never a summary
- [ ] At least one dialogue-heavy and one monologue-heavy section
- [ ] Scene skips use a time phrase, not a summary paragraph
- [ ] Chapter has a situation, a turn, and a hook
- [ ] Last line makes the reader want the next line
- [ ] Nothing after the last line except an optional `---` note
