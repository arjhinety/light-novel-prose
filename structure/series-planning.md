# Series Planning

> **Purpose:** Plan a multi-arc light novel or web novel. This covers the school-year spine, cast rotation, the mastermind thread, parallel scenes, callbacks, and the release cadence that serialized readers expect.
> **Load when:** Starting a new long story, planning past the first arc, or picking up a series where the author has lost track of threads.
> **Related:** [Arc Design](arc-design.md), [Chapter & Section](chapter-and-section.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [Character Design](../cast/character-design.md), [School Life](../setting/school-life.md), [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md), [Harem](../genres/harem.md), [Story Bible template](../templates/story-bible.md)

## 1. The series as a school year

The school year is the most useful scaffold a school-drama series has, because it gives you a natural, readable calendar of events that readers already understand. (Full details in [School Life](../setting/school-life.md).)

| Month | Event | Natural arc use |
|---|---|---|
| April | New class, self-introductions, seating chart | Arc 1 opening: new cast, POV's resting state |
| May | Golden Week, first midterms | Breather or study-group arc |
| May-June | Sports festival (some schools hold it in autumn) | Team rivalry, public humiliation, or triumph |
| June | Rainy season | Umbrella scenes, melancholy, a heroine-focus arc |
| July | Finals, then the start of summer break | Arc climax before break |
| August | Summer break: festivals, fireworks, beach, cram school | Out-of-school arcs; heroines seen out of uniform |
| September | Return to school; the cultural festival is prepared | Festival arc (the big one) |
| October | Cultural festival, school trip (2nd years) | Mid-series climax |
| November | Student council election | Politics, the mastermind's move |
| December | Christmas, winter break | Romance arc, confessions |
| January-February | New Year's shrine visit, Valentine's Day | Romance, jealousy, harem pressure |
| March | White Day, graduation of 3rd years, class reshuffle | Series finale or volume finale |

A **one-year series** is 6-10 arcs. A long web novel may run two years (2nd and 3rd year), using the class reshuffle in April as a structural reset.

**Rule:** anchor every arc to at least one calendar event. It makes the timeline legible and gives you ready-made set pieces.

## 2. Multi-arc structure

### 2.1 Layers

A series runs on three layers at once:

1. **Arc plots**, closed in 10-20 chapters (the Tachibana bullying arc, the festival film arc).
2. **Relationship threads**, which progress across arcs (Kuze and Tachibana: "you", then "Kuze", then something more; Kuze and Kujou: tool, then friend, then something unreadable).
3. **Series mysteries**, answered over 3+ arcs (who is manipulating the class? what does Amamiya actually know?).

Every arc must advance **at least one** relationship thread and **touch** the series mystery, even if only in a single cut-away.

### 2.2 A sample series outline (default cast)

| Arc | Months | Foreground | Pressure | Series-mystery touch | POV change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apr-May | Tachibana | Hoshino's bullying | Who leaked the station photo? Kujou's diagram cut-away | Takes the malice for her |
| 2 | May-June | Kujou | Film vs. play rivalry | Kujou edits the script's meaning in secret | Grabs her hand: "I'd rather not." |
| 3 | June-July | Matsuri | Rumor that Kuze is dating Hina | Amamiya's Chirp posts contain things only an insider knew | Calls her "Hina" once |
| 4 | Aug | Shirasagi + Enomoto | Summer festival; Shirasagi's two faces | Shirasagi and Kujou are in open cold war | Admits misjudging someone |
| 5 | Sep-Oct | Ensemble | Cultural festival | The screening reveals Kujou's hidden message | Takes the lead publicly |
| 6 | Oct | Amamiya | School trip; the harem cracks | Amamiya confronts Kuze: "You're doing what I do." | Understands Amamiya instead of judging him |
| 7 | Nov | Kujou | Student council election | The mastermind's reveal | Chooses to trust anyway |
| 8 | Dec-Feb | Tachibana / whoever the story chose | Confession season | Aftermath of the reveal | Says what he wants, out loud |

Notice the rhythm: dark, then lighter, then medium, then light, then big, then dark, then dark, then warm. Never put two dark arcs back-to-back without a lighter one between them, except in the final approach to the series climax.

## 3. Cast rotation

### 3.1 The foreground/background rule

Each arc foregrounds **one** heroine (or relationship). The rest stay alive in the background with **signature beats** (see [Character Voices](../dialogue/character-voices.md)): one tic-bearing line each every 2-3 chapters.

| Heroine | Background beat (reminds readers she exists) |
|---|---|
| Tachibana | Punches Amamiya; the canine grin; a kendo bag bumping a doorframe |
| Kujou | "My." "Indeed." "As his lawful wife..." A cool glance at Kuze a beat too long |
| Matsuri | "Kuze-kun! No polite speech!" Hands formed into an X |
| Shirasagi | "Fueee~" Her phone camera, always out |

### 3.2 Rotation order

- **Arc 1: the most abrasive heroine** (tsundere, delinquent). Friction generates the fastest story, and redeeming her in the reader's eyes is a strong opening payoff.
- **Arc 2: the most mysterious** (ojou, kuudere). This builds the series mystery.
- **Middle arcs: the warmest** (genki, childhood friend). These give the reader warmth before the big turns.
- **Late arcs: the most dangerous** (two-faced, yandere). The reveal lands harder once the reader trusts the cast.

See [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md) and [Character Tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md) for what each type brings.

### 3.3 Supporting cast lifespans

| Role | Frequency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Harem lead (Amamiya) | Most chapters | He is the POV's mirror |
| Confidant (Enomoto) | 3-4 chapters per arc | Rarity makes his speeches land |
| Otaku friend (Gon) | Breather arcs, comic relief | Tonal release |
| Teacher (Ogata-sensei) | 2-3 per arc | Authority and perceptiveness |
| Bully (Hoshino) | Heavy in her arc, a cameo after | A later redemption arc option |

## 4. The mastermind thread

A long school drama benefits from **one character quietly manipulating events** across arcs. In the default cast, this is Sayo Kujou: the elegant, sharp-tongued "lawful wife" who draws diagrams of everyone's affections at night.

### 4.1 Seeding schedule

| Arc | Seed | Reader's reaction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cut-away: Kujou draws arrows between names and smiles | "Ooh, creepy." |
| 1 | She steps forward in the staff room to explain the bullying, and the account favors Kuze | "Is she helping him?" |
| 2 | She joins Kuze's film team and secretly alters the script's meaning | "What's she planning?" |
| 3 | Kuze notices a timeline inconsistency: she condemned someone for something she did herself | "Wait..." |
| 4 | She and Shirasagi are in a cold war; each knows the other's secret | "Two schemers?" |
| 5 | The festival screening reveals her hidden message | Partial reveal |
| 7 | Full reveal and confrontation | Payoff |

### 4.2 Rules for the thread

1. **Cut-aways are short** (100-300 words) and end on a smile, a line, or an object. They should never explain her plan.
2. **She must also be genuinely helpful.** A mastermind who only harms is a villain; one who helps *and* manipulates is a character. Readers should debate whether she is good.
3. **The POV must almost notice** at least twice, then talk himself out of it ("Doubting her would be shameful."). This is dramatic irony and it also characterizes him: the cynic's one blind spot is the person who called him a friend.
4. **The reveal must be re-readable.** When it lands, earlier scenes should gain a second meaning. Plan the reveal first, then plant backward.

See [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md) for manipulation mechanics.

## 5. Parallel scenes

**Parallel scenes** repeat an early scene's setup later with something changed. They show growth through structure instead of statements.

### 5.1 Types

| Type | Early | Later |
|---|---|---|
| **Same place, different company** | Family restaurant: Kuze alone across from Amamiya and Tachibana, eating a hamburger | Same restaurant, same booth: Tachibana across from Kuze, and she orders the hamburger |
| **Same line, different speaker** | Amamiya: "You don't want bad blood, right? Let's make up." | Kuze says a version of it to two feuding first-years, and hates himself a little |
| **Same act, reversed role** | Amamiya grabs Kujou's hand to pull her back | Kuze grabs her other hand |
| **Same opening** | Arc 1, chapter 1: Kuze walks to school, hand on chin, alone | Arc 5, chapter 1: same walk, and Tachibana falls into step beside him without a word |

### 5.2 Callbacks

Callbacks are smaller: a detail, not a scene. Examples: **black coffee** (Kuze's order; later Tachibana orders it and grimaces), **the kanji of a name** (Kuze once lectured Tachibana about her name's meaning; arcs later she asks what "Minato" means), **"No polite speech!"** (Matsuri's running gag, until one day she uses "-san" on him, and it hurts).

**Rule:** log every plantable detail in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md) under "Callbacks". Reuse at least two per arc.

## 6. Serialization and web-novel cadence

### 6.1 How web-novel readers read

- **Daily or near-daily chapters** of 2,000-4,000 words.
- Readers comment per chapter, so every chapter needs something to react to (a line, a reveal, a gag).
- Readers binge after a break, so arcs must also read well in one sitting. Avoid recaps that would be redundant when binged.

### 6.2 Cadence rules

1. **Every chapter hooks** (see [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md)).
2. **Weekly shape:** if publishing daily, aim for one big moment per 5-7 chapters. Treat the week as a mini-arc.
3. **Arc boundaries** get a prologue or interlude to reset the reader. Label them clearly.
4. **Side stories (SS)** go between arcs or on holidays. They're light and they reward loyalty.
5. **Never end an arc on the weekend-release chapter with a question the reader must wait days for** unless you want engagement spikes. It's a deliberate tool, not a default.

### 6.3 The volume view

If the series is collected into volumes (roughly 70,000-100,000 words), each volume is **one or two arcs** with:
- a prologue (a cold open, often the climax's first line),
- the arc(s),
- an epilogue (the reset beat and a teaser),
- optional SS.

Plan arcs so they divide cleanly into volumes. The volume's final line is the strongest hook in the series.

## 7. Keeping threads straight (the ledger)

Maintain a running **thread ledger** in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md):

```
THREADS
- [open, arc 1] Who sent the station photo to Hoshino?  -> planned answer: arc 3 (Shirasagi)
- [open, arc 1] Why did Amamiya look away? -> planned: arc 6
- [open, arc 1] Kujou's diagram -> planned: arc 7
- [closed, arc 2] Who changed the script? -> Kujou (revealed to reader, not Kuze)

ADDRESS FORMS
- Tachibana -> Kuze: "you" (arc 1), "Kuze" (arc 1 end), ...
- Kuze -> Matsuri: "Matsuri-san" -> "Matsuri" (arc 3) -> "Hina" (once, arc 3 climax)

CALLBACKS
- Black coffee (ch 3)
- Kanji of "Kyoko"-style name lecture (ch 5)
- Family restaurant booth by the window (ch 3)
```

Update it after every chapter. A single inconsistent honorific does more damage to immersion than a weak chapter. See [Honorifics & Address](../dialogue/honorifics-and-address.md).

## 8. Endings

The rom-com trap: *there is no happy ending for a harem* (the POV himself notes this in-world). Decide your ending type early, because it shapes everything:

| Ending | Implication for planning |
|---|---|
| **One heroine chosen** | Seed her from arc 1; give the others their own closure arcs *before* the choice |
| **POV chooses no one, but changes** | The series is about his growth; the romance stays ambiguous and the friendships carry the finale |
| **The harem lead chooses** | The POV's story is about watching and understanding the choice; the final arc can mirror arc 1 |
| **Open ending (web-novel hiatus)** | Avoid unless the user wants it; close at least the series mystery |

See [Harem](../genres/harem.md) for the heroine-choice problem in detail.

## 9. Theme across the series

Each arc's confidant speech states one theme (see [Arc Design](arc-design.md)). Across a series, those themes should **build on one another** toward one series thesis instead of repeating.

For the default cast, the series thesis might be: *"You can't understand people from the back row."*

| Arc | Arc theme (stated by someone other than Kuze) | How it builds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You're not as logical as you think." (Enomoto) | He has feelings; they drive him |
| 2 | "You read people like tests you're trying not to fail." (Enomoto) | His analysis is self-protection |
| 3 | "You call it honesty. It's just being scared first." (Matsuri) | Cynicism is also a performance |
| 5 | "You judged him for doing what you're doing now." (Tachibana) | He is more like Amamiya than he admits |
| 6 | "Do you think I *like* being like this?" (Amamiya) | The harem lead has his own cage |
| 7 | "I trusted you to see through me. You didn't want to." (Kujou) | His blind spot was chosen |
| 8 | Kuze finally says a version of it himself, briefly, to someone younger | The thesis is earned |

**Rules:**
1. **Rotate who delivers the theme.** If the confidant delivers it every time, he becomes a mouthpiece. Later themes should come from heroines, rivals, even the antagonist.
2. **Themes can contradict.** Arc 6's Amamiya should complicate Arc 1's judgment of him. A series that only confirms the POV's first views has no growth.
3. **The POV voices the thesis only once**, near the end, and briefly. When he does, it should sound like him: dry, understated, and slightly embarrassed.

## 10. Genre-shifting across a series

Long web novels often drift in genre: a school drama gains a mystery layer, a rom-com gains a melodrama arc. That's fine if it's **planned** and the voice holds.

- **Keep the POV's voice constant** even when the genre changes. Kuze in a mystery arc still notices who got offered a drink. See [Style Bible](../core/style-bible.md).
- **Signal a shift with an arc opening.** A prologue in a different register (a cut-away with dread, or a flashback) tells the reader the rules are changing.
- **Don't shift in the middle of an arc.** A rom-com arc that turns into a murder mystery at chapter 9 breaks trust; a *new arc* that opens as a mystery doesn't.
- **Ratio drift.** Track the comedy-to-drama ratio per arc. If three arcs in a row are more than 70% drama, the next should be a breather. See [Genre Index](../genres/genre-index.md) for dial settings.

Common healthy drifts:

| From | To | Bridge |
|---|---|---|
| School drama | Mystery | An unanswered "who" from an earlier arc becomes the next arc's spine |
| Rom-com | Melodrama | A heroine's hidden circumstance (a move, an illness, a family) surfaces in a quiet cut-away first |
| Harem comedy | Psychological | One heroine's cheerful mask cracks on-page |
| Slice of life | Sports/club | A club event gives the cast a shared goal |

## 11. Continuing someone else's series

When the user hands you an existing story:

1. **Read everything available** before writing. If it's too long, read the first chapter, the last three chapters, and any chapter the user flags.
2. **Extract the ledger** (see §7): cast, address forms, running jokes, open threads, leitmotifs, and chapter title pattern.
3. **Identify the current beat** in the current arc (see [Arc Design](arc-design.md)). Don't restart the arc; continue from the beat you're in.
4. **Match their hooks.** If they end chapters on verdicts, keep verdicts in the rotation; don't suddenly switch to cold cut-aways every chapter.
5. **Honor their mistakes lightly.** If they've been inconsistent (a heroine's eye color changed), pick the most recent version and stay consistent from here. Mention it to the user in the notes after `---`, not inside the prose.
6. **Don't resolve their mysteries** unless asked. Advance them one step.

## 12. Worked example: planning Volume 1 from a one-line premise

**Premise from the user:** *"A loner guy keeps wondering why the popular guy in his class has five girls around him."*

1. **Bible:** Kuze (POV), Amamiya (lead), four heroines of distinct [dere types](../cast/dere-types.md) and [tropes](../cast/character-tropes.md) (tsundere, ojou, genki, two-faced), a confidant, a bully queen, and a teacher.
2. **Series thesis:** *"You can't understand people from the back row."*
3. **Volume 1 = Arcs 1 and 2**, from April to early June.
4. **Arc 1 (Tachibana, 14 chapters):** the 9-beat arc from [Arc Design](arc-design.md#2-the-9-beat-arc-house-default) with the bullying climax.
5. **Arc 2 (Kujou, 14 chapters):** the festival film arc from [Arc Design](arc-design.md#9-worked-outline-the-festival-film-arc-arc-2-14-chapters).
6. **Mastermind seeds:** diagram cut-away (Arc 1), staff-room testimony (Arc 1), script annotation (Arc 2).
7. **Parallel scene planned:** the family restaurant (Arc 1 ch 3, Arc 2 ch 13).
8. **Volume-ending hook:** a transfer student in September who calls Kuze "Mina", a name only Enomoto uses, which opens the childhood-friend arc.
9. **Open threads at volume end:** the station photo leak (to be answered in Arc 3), Amamiya's look-away (Arc 6), and Kujou's line (Arc 5).

Write this plan into the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md) before drafting chapter 1.

## 13. Launching a web novel: the first ten chapters

Serialized readers decide in the first 3-5 chapters. The launch has jobs the rest of the series doesn't:

| Ch | Launch job | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establish voice and core question within 300 words | Routine opening, an asymmetry noticed, a verdict or reveal hook |
| 1-2 | Introduce 3-4 characters with full portraits | Don't dump all five heroines at once; bring in two, then the others as a group burst |
| 2-3 | First real conflict | The pulled-in beat: a quarrel, a duty, a misunderstanding |
| 3 | First "that's the hook" moment | The alibi realization or the social-media post that reveals character. Readers who stay past this usually stay |
| 4-5 | First warmth | A small bond, so readers know the cynic can feel |
| 5 | First cut-away | Sets up the mystery layer and promises a longer game |
| 6-8 | Escalation | Pressure starts |
| 9-10 | First climax or a strong midpoint turn | Readers who reach ch 10 need a payoff to commit |

**Launch pitfalls:**
- **Too much setup.** No chapter without a scene; no worldbuilding lectures, even for school life.
- **Too many names.** Every new name must arrive with a portrait and a tic, or it won't stick. Cap it at around 6 named characters in the first 3 chapters.
- **Cynicism without warmth.** A narrator who only sneers for five chapters loses readers. Give him one flicker of self-doubt or kindness per chapter from the start.
- **Hiding the premise.** If the story is "why is he popular?", ask it in chapter 1, in those words or close to them.

## 14. Midseries fatigue and how to refresh

Around arcs 4-6, long series sag. The cast is known, the POV's tricks are familiar, and the reader can predict each chapter. Refresh without breaking the voice:

1. **Change the POV's position, not his voice.** Put Kuze in charge (festival director), in the crowd (someone else's bullying), or in the spotlight (a rumor about him).
2. **Promote a background character.** Gon or Ogata-sensei gets an arc; the reader sees the familiar world through a sideways angle via cut-aways.
3. **Break a status quo.** A heroine leaves the harem publicly, or Amamiya and Kuze are forced to share a room on the school trip.
4. **Pay off the oldest open thread.** Nothing re-energizes readers like an answer to a question from arc 1.
5. **Change the season's texture.** Moving from rainy-season melancholy to summer-festival light, or from the classroom to a school-trip inn, refreshes the prose itself: new sounds, new clothes, and new scenery lines.
6. **Introduce one new character with a direct link to the past** (a transfer student who knew Kuze before he became cynical), never a random newcomer.

## 15. Series planning checklist

- [ ] School-year calendar mapped to arcs
- [ ] 6-10 arcs outlined, each with a foreground character, pressure, POV change, and mystery touch
- [ ] Dark and light arcs alternate
- [ ] Rotation order chosen deliberately (abrasive, then mysterious, then warm, then dangerous)
- [ ] Background beats defined for every heroine
- [ ] Mastermind thread seeded with a schedule and a planned, re-readable reveal
- [ ] At least three parallel scenes planned
- [ ] Callback list started
- [ ] Thread ledger and address-form ledger created in the Story Bible
- [ ] Ending type decided
