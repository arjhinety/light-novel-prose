# Arc Design

> **Purpose:** Build 10-20 chapter story arcs with the rise, break, and quiet reset of a serialized school-drama light novel. This covers the 9-beat arc, its variants, pacing tables, and how to leave questions that pull readers into the next arc.
> **Load when:** Planning an arc, outlining a new story's first volume, or diagnosing why a run of chapters feels aimless or rushed.
> **Related:** [Chapter & Section](chapter-and-section.md), [Series Planning](series-planning.md), [Hooks & Cliffhangers](hooks-and-cliffhangers.md), [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md), [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md), [Archetypes](../cast/archetypes.md), [School Life](../setting/school-life.md), [Story Bible template](../templates/story-bible.md), [Sample climax chapter](../examples/sample-chapter-climax.md)

## 1. What an arc is in this genre

An **arc** is a run of 10-20 chapters (roughly 30,000-70,000 words, or about one published volume) organized around:

- **one foregrounded heroine or relationship** (the others stay in the background with a line or two each),
- **one social pressure** (a rumor, a photo, an election, a festival project, a bully queen),
- **one change in the POV character**, usually small. He does something the Kuze of chapter 1 would never have done.

The arc is the unit readers remember ("the Tachibana arc", "the festival arc"). Chapters are how they experience it; arcs are how they recall it.

The core tension in this voice is **observation versus involvement**. The cynical POV wants to stay a spectator, analyzing from the back row. An arc is the story of the world refusing to let him stay there, how badly he handles it, and the one moment he chooses to act.

## 2. The 9-beat arc (house default)

Worked throughout with the default cast: **the Tachibana arc** (Rin Tachibana, tsundere; social pressure from Reika Hoshino; POV Minato Kuze).

### Beat 1: Normal

**Chapters:** 1-2 of the arc (about 10%)
**Job:** Show the POV's resting state and restate his core question.
**Kuze's state:** Observes Amamiya's harem from the back row and files every asymmetry in his ledger. *Why is he the one everyone likes?*
**Texture:** Routine openings, dry comedy, and dense analytical monologue. Short sections.
**Plant here:** one tiny detail that will matter later (Tachibana hides a bruised knuckle from kendo; Hoshino laughs a little too loud when Tachibana walks past).

```
Tachibana punched Amamiya's shoulder, hard enough that he yelped.

Violence as affection. Kuze had seen it a hundred times and still didn't understand the exchange rate.
```

### Beat 2: Pulled in

**Chapters:** 2-4 (about 10-15%)
**Job:** Force contact. Something external drags the POV into the heroine's orbit: a teacher's errand, class duty, cleaning duty, a misunderstanding, being the only one who saw something.
**Kuze's state:** Irritated, and determined to minimize involvement. "Tell me what you want and let's be done with it."
**Texture:** Contact chapters (see [Chapter & Section](chapter-and-section.md#12-chapter-types-and-their-shapes)). Banter in snap-back rhythm.
**Key rule:** the pull must come from **outside** the POV. If he chooses to approach her this early, he isn't a spectator any more and the arc has nowhere to go.

### Beat 3: Small bond

**Chapters:** 4-6 (about 15%)
**Job:** Private moments where the heroine shows a side nobody else sees. For a tsundere, this is honesty without an audience. See [Dere Types](../cast/dere-types.md).
**Kuze's state:** Reluctant interest, which he rationalizes as "networking" or "avoiding trouble". He is lying to himself, and the reader sees it before he does.
**Texture:** Quieter scenes: the walk to the station, the shared umbrella, the conbini after cram school. Fewer people on the page. The comedy softens.

```
"Don't tell anyone I cried at the movie."

"Who would I tell?"

Tachibana looked at him for a moment, then laughed. Not the sharp, canine-flashing laugh she used on Amamiya. A smaller one.

"...Yeah. Fair."
```

**Plant here:** the thing the bully will weaponize. It might be a photo of the two of them, a line she said that sounds bad out of context, or a promise he made.

### Beat 4: Social pressure

**Chapters:** 6-9 (about 20%)
**Job:** The outside world turns. Rumors, a Chirp photo, desks covered in paper, a classroom where laughter stops when you walk in. The bully queen (Hoshino) steers public opinion.
**Kuze's state:** He analyzes instead of acting. He tells himself it's not his problem, and he repeats it.
**Texture:** Pressure chapters. Scenes are crowded, and the dialogue is group chatter with cruel undertones. Kuze's monologue gets **repetitive on purpose**: "Not my problem. Not my problem." Use the [leitmotif](../core/inner-monologue.md): the sediment is settling faster now.
**Key rule:** make the pressure **plausible**. It grows from things planted in beats 1-3 and from real social mechanics, such as envy, a heroine's genuinely abrasive habits, and a harem lead who won't take sides. It doesn't come from cartoon villainy.

### Beat 5: The POV fails

**Chapters:** 9-11 (about 10%)
**Job:** The moment the POV chooses "rationality" and stays silent while someone suffers. This is the arc's moral low point, and the reader must feel it.
**Kuze's state:** He hangs up on Tachibana. He ignores her call. He watches Amamiya look away and does the same.
**Texture:** Short, closed sentences. Loss of comedy. Food tastes like nothing. A single line ends a chapter here ("That evening's dinner tasted like sand.").
**Key rule:** **don't soften this.** The later break only works if this failure is real and specific. If he "almost" helps, the climax deflates.

### Beat 6: The break

**Chapters:** 11-13 (about 10%). Usually one long climax chapter, sometimes two.
**Job:** The dam bursts. The quiet POV explodes, takes all the malice in the room onto himself, and makes himself the villain so the heroine isn't alone. It is irrational, costly, and human.
**Texture:** See [Emotional Climaxes](../scenes/emotional-climaxes.md) and [Emotional Dialogue](../dialogue/emotional-dialogue.md). A crowd chant in triple quotes, intercut with his thoughts one line at a time, then the crack, then long run-on accusations. The outburst works *because* he has been terse for the whole arc.
**Structure:** The outburst is the longest section of the arc. It ends with the POV fleeing, not winning.

### Beat 7: The low point and the confidant

**Chapters:** 12-14 (about 10%)
**Job:** Alone behind the gym, or on a stairwell, or on the riverbank, the POV collapses. The confidant (Daichi Enomoto) finds him, because he always knows where Kuze hides, and delivers the **thesis speech**: the theme of the arc, stated plainly and kindly.
**Texture:** The monologue-and-silence duet. Daichi speaks in gentle paragraphs, and Kuze answers only `"..."` five or six times until something physical breaks the silence (tears, a cough, a laugh at a bad joke).

```
"You're not as logical as you think, Mina."

"..."

"You've got a lot of logic, sure. But you've also got a lot of other stuff. Anger. Guilt. Whatever made you stand up today."

"..."
```

**Key rule:** the confidant states the theme; the POV does **not** agree out loud. His agreement is his tears. Never let him summarize his growth.

### Beat 8: Aftermath

**Chapters:** 14-16 (about 10%)
**Job:** Surface resolution in the staff room: apologies, a warning from Ogata-sensei, the bully's reluctant apology. **Underneath, questions stay open.**
**Texture:** Aftermath chapters. Brisk summary sentences are allowed here ("With Hoshino's apology, the incident appeared closed."), and the word "appeared" does the work. End with 2-3 unanswered questions stated as the POV's thoughts, then a **cut-away**: Sayo Kujou at her desk, drawing arrows between names and smiling.

### Beat 9: Reset with change

**Chapters:** 16-18+ (about 5-10%)
**Job:** A new event begins (the cultural festival, exams, summer break), and the POV does one small thing differently. He answers a greeting. He grabs someone's hand before she's pulled away. He says, "I'd rather not," out loud.
**Texture:** Lighter. The comedy returns, the sections get shorter again, and the opening mirrors Beat 1 (see [Series Planning](series-planning.md#5-parallel-scenes)).
**Key rule:** the change is **small and concrete**. No speeches about growth. The reader notices it; he barely does.

## 3. Arc variants

The 9 beats are a spine, not a cage. Here are variants for other arc types, with the beats they bend.

### 3.1 The comedy arc (Breather arc)

Use after a heavy arc. It is 6-10 chapters.

| Beat | Comedy version |
|---|---|
| Normal | Same |
| Pulled in | An absurd obligation: the indie film needs a heroine and nobody volunteers |
| Small bond | Found-family warmth among misfits (Kuze, Gon, and a reluctant heroine) |
| Social pressure | Low-stakes rivalry: the play team vs. the film team |
| POV fails | A comic failure (the script is terrible, the camera battery dies) |
| Break | A triumphant or absurd set piece (the screening) |
| Low point | Skipped or played for a laugh |
| Aftermath | A callback gag |
| Reset | A warm final beat plus one serious seed for the next arc |

### 3.2 The heroine-focus arc (romance-leaning)

The POV's relationship with one heroine deepens. Pressure comes from **inside** the relationship, such as a misunderstanding or her feelings for the harem lead, instead of from a bully. The Break is a confession or a refusal instead of an outburst. See [Romance & Melodrama](../genres/romance-and-melodrama.md).

### 3.3 The mystery arc

Who leaked the photos? Who wrote the note? Beats 2-4 become investigation, Beat 5 is a wrong accusation, Beat 6 is the reveal confrontation, and Beat 7 is the guilt of having been wrong. See [Mystery, Psychological & Horror](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md).

### 3.4 The event arc (festival / trip / election)

The school calendar provides the structure (see [School Life](../setting/school-life.md)). Chapters map onto the event's days: prep, day one, day two, and the post-festival bonfire. Social pressure comes from logistics and rivalries. The Break often lands at the event's climax (the screening, the relay, the vote count).

### 3.5 The redemption arc (antagonist focus)

The bully queen or a hostile heroine becomes the focus. Beat 3's "small bond" is the POV seeing her alone and vulnerable. This is powerful after a bullying arc, because it complicates the villain. Use it only once the reader has had time to hate her properly.

### 3.6 The mirror arc

Late in a series, repeat an early arc's shape with the POV in a different position: now **he** is the one mediating, or the one with a crowd around him. It shows growth through structure instead of statement (see [Series Planning](series-planning.md#5-parallel-scenes)).

## 4. Pacing across 10-20 chapters

### 4.1 Beat-to-chapter maps

**12-chapter arc (tight):**

| Ch | Beat | Chapter type |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal | Routine |
| 2 | Pulled in | Contact |
| 3 | Pulled in | Social |
| 4 | Small bond | Contact |
| 5 | Small bond | Breather |
| 6 | Social pressure | Pressure |
| 7 | Social pressure | Pressure |
| 8 | POV fails | Pressure |
| 9 | Break | Climax |
| 10 | Low point / confidant | Climax (quiet) |
| 11 | Aftermath | Aftermath + cut-away |
| 12 | Reset | Routine (mirrored) |

**18-chapter arc (volume-length):**

| Ch | Beat |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Normal |
| 3-4 | Pulled in |
| 5-7 | Small bond (one Breather chapter among them) |
| 8-11 | Social pressure (escalating; one quiet chapter at 10) |
| 12-13 | POV fails |
| 14 | Break |
| 15 | Low point / confidant |
| 16-17 | Aftermath + interlude |
| 18 | Reset |

### 4.2 Pacing rules

1. **The midpoint turn.** Around 45-55% of the arc, something the POV believed turns out wrong. He learns the harem lead invited him only as an alibi, or that the heroine defended him to someone. This keeps the middle from sagging.
2. **Escalation is stepwise.** Each Pressure chapter must be worse in a *different way*: first whispers, then paper on desks, then a public chant. Don't repeat the same humiliation at a higher volume.
3. **One breather per four chapters.** Even in dark arcs, give the reader a warm or funny chapter every four or so, except during the climax run (Beats 5-7), where the tension must stay unbroken.
4. **The climax run is contiguous.** Beats 5, 6, and 7 happen within 2-4 chapters of each other, with no side stories in between.
5. **Aftermath is short.** One or two chapters. Readers forgive a quick wrap-up; they don't forgive a slow one.

### 4.3 Word-count pacing inside the climax

The Break chapter is often the arc's longest (5,000-7,000 words). Inside it, the outburst section can run 2,000+ words, while the sections around it are short:

```
Section 1: 600 words, morning, the air feels different
Section 2: 800 words, the chant begins
Section 3: 2,200 words, the break
Section 4: 400 words, he runs; the class is silent
Section 5: 300 words, cut-away: Kujou steps forward to explain to the teacher
```

## 5. Unanswered questions (the engine between arcs)

Every arc **closes its emotional loop** and **leaves its factual loops open**. The reader gets catharsis (Kuze stood up for Tachibana) but not full information.

### 5.1 How many and which kind

Leave **2-3** open questions per arc, drawn from different categories:

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Who took the photo of Kuze and Tachibana at the station and sent it to Hoshino? |
| **Why** | Why did Amamiya look away when Tachibana ran past him? |
| **What does X feel** | Why did Kujou step forward in the staff room, and why did she smile? |
| **What will happen** | Tachibana calls him "Kuze" now, without "you". What does that change? |

### 5.2 How to state them

State them as the POV's own thoughts, in the Aftermath beat, as a short question chain. See [Inner Monologue](../core/inner-monologue.md) for hypothesis chains.

```
Who had sent Hoshino those photos? Why had Amamiya looked at the floor? Why had Kujou, of all people, been the one to explain everything to Ogata-sensei?

Nothing had been answered. The curtain had simply been drawn, and everyone had agreed to pretend the play was over.
```

### 5.3 Payoff timing

- Answer **one** question early in the next arc (a reward).
- Keep **one** alive across 2-3 arcs (the mastermind thread; see [Series Planning](series-planning.md#4-the-mastermind-thread)).
- Let **one** be answered by implication and never stated. Readers enjoy working it out.

## 6. The POV's change (arc by arc)

The POV must change in **one small, visible way** per arc, and it must cost him something.

| Arc | Before | After (visible act) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Tachibana) | Spectator who analyzes everything | Screams at the class to draw fire from Tachibana | Whole class sees him as unhinged |
| 2 (Festival) | Lets others take what he values | Grabs Kujou's hand: "Sorry, Amamiya. I'd rather not." | Open conflict with the harem lead |
| 3 (Matsuri) | Refuses first names | Calls her "Hina" once, at the worst possible moment | Hina's confusion; Amamiya's suspicion |
| 4 (Shirasagi) | Trusts his cynical reads | Admits he misjudged someone | His self-image as the one who "sees through" people |

Track this in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md). The change **accumulates**: later arcs reference earlier ones ("The Kuze of April would have let her go.").

## 7. Heroine rotation inside an arc

- **Foreground heroine:** POV scenes, a portrait refresh, and interiority through the POV's reading of her.
- **Background heroines (2-4):** one or two lines per appearance, each with her signature tic, so readers don't forget them. One of them plants the **next** arc (Matsuri glances at Kuze a beat too long; Shirasagi's phone camera is always out).
- **Harem lead:** present in most chapters. His behavior is the mirror the POV judges himself against.
- **Confidant:** appears in 3-4 chapters. Don't overuse him, because his rarity makes the thesis speech land.

## 8. Failure modes

| Failure | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The fake low point** | The POV "almost" fails, or fails offscreen | Show the failure in scene, with specific acts (hung up, ignored the call, looked away) |
| **The unearned outburst** | The POV was already talkative and emotional | Keep him terse for the whole arc; the outburst's power is contrast |
| **The explained theme** | The POV states what he learned | Give the theme to the confidant; the POV answers with silence and tears |
| **Villain in a vacuum** | The bully has no plausible motive | Root her cruelty in envy, a real grievance, and class hierarchy |
| **Everything resolved** | All questions answered in the aftermath | Leave 2-3 open, in different categories |
| **Samey escalation** | Each pressure chapter is the same humiliation, louder | Change the *kind* of pressure each time |
| **Sagging middle** | No midpoint turn | Put a belief-reversal at 45-55% |
| **Heroine vanishes** | Background heroines forgotten | One tic-bearing line each per 2-3 chapters |

## 9. Worked outline: the festival film arc (Arc 2, 14 chapters)

This is a complete chapter-by-chapter outline so you can see the beats, chapter types, hooks, and seeds working together. The foreground is Sayo Kujou. The pressure is the film-team-versus-play-team rivalry plus Kujou's sudden isolation among the girls. The POV change is that Kuze publicly refuses to let Amamiya take something from him.

| Ch | Title | Beat | Type | Content | Hook / tilt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do You Like Indie Films? 1 | Normal | Routine | Late May. Class 2-3 splits into a play team (Amamiya, most of the girls) and a film team (Kuze, Gon, a few quiet boys). Kuze wrote the script because nobody else would. | No girl will play the heroine. The film team's girls stare at their phones. |
| 2 | ... 2 | Pulled in | Contact | Kuze, Gon, and a third boy debate cross-dressing. Enomoto drops by in haunted-house makeup; the girls notice *him* and reconsider Kuze. A girl is about to volunteer, and then the door bangs open. | Arrival: Kujou, hands on hips: "Bow to your savior." |
| 3 | ... 3 | Pulled in | Social | Kujou joins the film team. Kuze suspects her motives: *why leave Amamiya's play?* The film team is baffled and delighted. | Reframe: Kujou underlines a line of the script when no one is looking. |
| 4 | ... 4 | Pulled in → Break (mini) | Climax (small) | Amamiya bursts in: "Come back to the play! We started together!" He grabs Kujou's hand. Kuze grabs the other. | Action begun: "Sorry, Amamiya. I'd rather not." |
| 5 | ... 5 | Small bond | Contact | Aftermath of the hand-grab. Kujou is amused. Filming in the park on the weekend; she's unexpectedly bad at acting cute and brilliant at acting cold. | Line out of character: she laughs, really laughs, at a blooper. |
| 6 | ... 6 | Small bond | Breather | Gon's archaic-speech directing, a dog stealing the prop bento, Kujou buying everyone ice cream "as a noblewoman's obligation." | Quiet serious line: "They stopped talking to me, you know. The girls." |
| 7 | ... 7 | Social pressure | Pressure | Rumors: Kujou is "stuck up," "walks around like she owns the school," "looks down on us." Girls move seats away from her. Shirasagi is quietly at the center. | Question: who started it, and why now? |
| 8 | ... 8 | Midpoint turn | Contact | Kuze learns Kujou sabotaged Shirasagi's date plans two weeks ago by calling out her behavior in public. Kujou isn't purely a victim. His belief flips. | Verdict: *She'd done it to herself. Hadn't she?* |
| 9 | ... 9 | Social pressure | Pressure | Kuze's old habit returns: not his problem. He avoids her at lunch. Kujou eats alone on the roof. | Reveal: Kuze finds her script annotations; one of the heroine's lines is addressed to *him*. |
| 10 | ... 10 | POV fails (soft) | Pressure | He confronts her about the annotations in the wrong way, analytically, like an accusation. She goes cold. "I see. So that's how you read people." | Silence: `"..."` from Kuze. |
| 11 | ... 11 | Low point / confidant | Climax (quiet) | Enomoto at the swimming pool after hours. The thesis: "You keep reading people like tests you're trying not to fail." | Kuze sends one message: `Filming's Saturday. We still need our heroine.` |
| 12 | ... 12 | Break (positive) | Climax | Saturday. Will she come? She does, late, with the tripod he forgot. Filming the final scene; she delivers the line addressed to him, perfectly, in front of everyone. | Line out of character: "Minato-kun. Did you hear it?" |
| 13 | ... 13 | Aftermath | Aftermath | Editing night. Gon sleeps on the desk. Kuze and Kujou talk about nothing. The festival approaches. | Cold cut-away: Shirasagi scrolling a folder of photos of Kujou. |
| 14 | ... 14 | Reset | Routine (mirrored) | Morning walk to school; the opening mirrors Arc 1 ch 1, but Kujou falls into step with him without a word. | Question: *What had the line meant?* He still doesn't know. |

What to notice:

- **Beat 6 happens early** (ch 4) as a *mini-break*, and then the real Break (ch 12) is positive: showing up rather than exploding. Arcs don't need to repeat Arc 1's outburst; the beat's *function* (the POV acts irrationally, at a cost) is what matters.
- **The midpoint turn** (ch 8) complicates the heroine instead of just the villain. The reader, like Kuze, has to decide whether to keep caring.
- **The POV's failure is softer** than in Arc 1 (a cruel analysis, not silence during bullying), because the stakes are softer. Scale the failure to the arc.
- **Seeds for later arcs:** Shirasagi's photo folder (Arc 4) and the hidden line's meaning (the Arc 5 screening).

## 10. Adapting arcs for other genres

The 9 beats are built for school drama but transfer cleanly. Map the social pressure to the genre's native pressure:

| Genre | "Pulled in" becomes | "Social pressure" becomes | "Break" becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Fantasy](../genres/fantasy.md) | Assigned to a party or a dorm | Guild politics, noble rivalry, a failed quest blamed on the POV | A battle or a public duel where the POV reveals what he's hidden |
| [Isekai](../genres/isekai-and-reincarnation.md) | Summoned or reincarnated; a villager asks for help | The kingdom distrusts outsiders; a hero party's jealousy | Using modern knowledge or the hidden skill publicly |
| [Villainess](../genres/villainess-and-otome.md) | The game's heroine enrolls | Doom flags, the ballroom, the prince's suspicion | The condemnation scene, rewritten |
| [Action](../genres/action-and-battle.md) | A tournament entry | Losing, rival mockery | The rematch |
| [Rom-com](../genres/romcom-and-comedy.md) | A misunderstanding (fake dating) | The lie snowballs | The confession or the public correction |
| [Mystery](../genres/mystery-psychological-horror.md) | A first incident | Wrong suspicion, the POV suspected himself | Reveal confrontation |

Keep the **emotional** spine (spectator, pulled in, bond, pressure, failure, costly act, confidant, quiet change) even when the external plot changes.

## 11. Arc planning checklist

- [ ] Foreground heroine and social pressure named
- [ ] POV's resting state and core question restated in Beat 1
- [ ] The pull into the story comes from outside the POV
- [ ] The small bond plants what the pressure will weaponize
- [ ] Each pressure chapter escalates in a different way
- [ ] Midpoint belief-reversal placed at 45-55%
- [ ] The POV's failure is shown in scene, unsoftened
- [ ] Break, low point, and confidant within 2-4 contiguous chapters
- [ ] The confidant states the theme; the POV does not
- [ ] 2-3 open questions from different categories
- [ ] One small, concrete, costly change in the POV
- [ ] Cut-away that feeds the next arc or the mastermind thread
- [ ] Beat-to-chapter map written in the [Story Bible](../templates/story-bible.md)
